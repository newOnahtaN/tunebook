# Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford, FL: the folk scene (as of October 2026)

*Metro #20 (2.96M people). Research for Nate's tune book project, compiled from four parallel research threads (trad, dance and traditional singing; song, listening and media; heritage traditions; history, festivals and the fiddle angle). Machine-readable data: `orlando.json`.*

## The scene in a nutshell

Orlando's folk scene is small and spread out, and three forces shape it: Puerto Rico, theme parks and Florida's cattle-country past. The trad core is one owner-run pub, Claddagh Cottage. Its fiddler and banjo-player owners have hosted the Every-Other-Wednesday Irish session for 25 years and made the pub the Southeast's uilleann-pipe hub. Around it run a monthly circuit of Sunday-afternoon Irish sessions, a Friday bluegrass jam held in an Ocoee parking lot since about 1990, an invisible old-time jam in members' homes, a young, gender-neutral contra rebuilt in 2022, and a shape-note singing revived in 2024. Listening happens at Timucua's house-concert hall and in Central Florida Folk's library concerts, and no FM station airs a folk show. The heritage layer is bigger: bomba taught by Rafael Cepeda's great-granddaughter in Kissimmee, Venezuelan gaita, Haitian twoubadou, Eatonville's Hurston legacy, horseback quadrille at the Silver Spurs Rodeo, and Epcot heritage musicians since 1982.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **The session hosts bought the pub.** Vicki Gish and Scott Vocca met at Claddagh Cottage's session, ran it from 2001, bought the pub in 2016, lost the building to demolition within three months, and rebuilt "every detail" at a new address in 2018. The Every-Other-Wednesday Session turned 25 on Sep 9, 2026. The pub hosts the six-state South East Tionól, "Try the Pipes!" days, "Irish C" sessions pitched a whole step down for pipers, and since 2025 a monthly gypsy-jazz jam.
2. **A bluegrass jam in a parking lot.** Florida's best-known weekly bluegrass jam happens behind a Pizza Hut in an Ocoee strip mall, on the site of an old Piggly Wiggly, every Friday since about 1990. One 86-year-old regular has built 77 mandolins.
3. **Cattle drives had fiddlers.** Clayton Raulerson (1906-2000) of Narcoossee said every cattle team driving from Lake Okeechobee to Orlando carried a fiddler, because the fiddle "was the only thing back then we had that you could move around." The metro's only WPA fiddle recording is a cowhunter string band at Caruja Ranch, Kenansville (Osceola County), on July 1, 1940, playing "Shear 'Em" and "Pastime Annie".
4. **The rodeo began as a square dance.** Kissimmee's Silver Spurs Rodeo, which sells out an 8,300-seat arena, began in 1940 as Osceola ranch kids riding a Quadrille on horseback. Quadrille teams still practise weekly, and Osceola schools close for "Rodeo Day".
5. **The Kissimmee Kid.** Vassar Clements, Bill Monroe's fiddler (1949-56) and the "Father of Hillbilly Jazz", grew up in Kissimmee. His tune "Kissimmee Kid" was played by Old & In the Way with Jerry Garcia.
6. **Theme parks imported tradition bearers.** Epcot's Mariachi Cobre has played the Mexico pavilion since opening day in 1982; Disney recruited a hula troupe for 1971; SeaWorld brought hula master Henry Ohumukini in 1983; the Canada pavilion's Off Kilter played for 17 years. Several of these musicians later won Florida Folk Heritage Awards, and Off Kilter alumni now play the Claddagh session.
7. **A Puerto Rican metro with bomba but little jíbaro.** Orange County had the second-largest Puerto Rican population of any US county in 2018, behind only the Bronx. The great-granddaughter of bomba patriarch Rafael Cepeda teaches bomba on Tuesday nights in a Kissimmee elementary school. Yet no trovador club or weekly cuatro circle turned up, despite a 2006 NEA Fellow cuatro maker (Diomedes Matos) just over the line in Deltona.
8. **A 2026 Heritage Fellow in Ocoee.** Conga master Giovanni Hidalgo (Dizzy Gillespie's United Nations Orchestra, Planet Drum) lives in the metro.
9. **Eatonville, Hurston and a Slovak choir.** Zora Neale Hurston staged an Eatonville folk concert at Rollins College in 1933. The 1935 Lomax-Hurston-Barnicle trip recorded 104 songs in Eatonville, and in 1939 the WPA recorded a Slovak Lutheran youth choir in Slavia, near Oviedo, from a 1911 colony of Cleveland Slovaks.
10. **No folk radio; a folk website instead.** No FM station in the metro carries a folk, bluegrass or Celtic show. Arguably the most widely heard local folk "program" is Orlando resident Roger McGuinn's Folk Den: a traditional song online every month since 1995 ("Little Brown Jug" on Oct 1, 2026).

## Character

Orlando's scene is **small, suburban and Sunday-afternoon shaped**. Instead of nightly pub sessions there is a rotating monthly circuit: Irish sessions on the 1st Sunday in Maitland, the 3rd in Apopka and the 4th in College Park, plus a 2nd-Sunday old-time jam, all in the afternoon, with Claddagh Cottage's alternate-Wednesday session as the one weeknight anchor. Bluegrass's roots are rural and in Lake County (Cassia's 1940s community club near Eustis) and cattle-country Osceola. The tourism economy is a quiet engine: Epcot, Disney, SeaWorld, Church Street Station and Cirque du Soleil have paid heritage and roots musicians since 1971, and their alumni feed local sessions and listening rooms. Retirees and snowbirds sit at the metro's edges (The Villages, the coast), where modern western square dancing and dulcimer clubs thrive. The fastest-growing layer is Latin American and Caribbean: Puerto Rican bomba and plena, Venezuelan gaita, Colombian carnival music, Peruvian marinera, Haitian twoubadou and Brazilian forró and capoeira. It is spread across Kissimmee, Azalea Park, MetroWest and Pine Hills rather than one neighbourhood.

## What a visiting fiddler should do

- **Tuesday:** a quiet night for trad. Visit the Escuela de Bomba y Plena Tata Cepeda's weekly bomba class in Kissimmee (as a listener or a learner), or a folk-leaning open mic (Copper Rocket, Maitland).
- **Wednesday (alternate weeks):** the Every-Other-Wednesday Session at Claddagh Cottage, the best trad night in the metro.
- **Friday:** the Ocoee parking-lot bluegrass jam; Orlando Contra (1st and 3rd Fridays, Winter Park Ballroom, $15); the private old-time home jam if you can get on the list.
- **Saturday:** the Cassia Community Club bluegrass jams near Eustis (2nd and 4th Saturdays) and the CFBA concert-jam (3rd Saturday); the Mount Dora Dulcimer Society jam at the library (1st Saturday).
- **Sunday:** the Irish session circuit (An Tobar, Maitland, 1st Sunday; Stephie's, Apopka, 3rd; the Castle, College Park, 4th), the Old-Time Second Sunday at Broken Strings Taproom, the monthly Claddagh slow session, and the monthly shape-note singing (usually the 2nd Sunday, Grace Covenant Presbyterian).

## Part I. Trad sessions, dance and jams

*Thread: trad_dance. As of October 2026. Covers the Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford MSA (Lake, Orange, Osceola and Seminole counties). Nearby Volusia, Brevard and Hillsborough events are marked "outside the MSA".*

### Character

Orlando's participatory trad scene is small, suburban and shaped by Sunday afternoons. Its anchor is one neighborhood pub, the [Claddagh Cottage](https://claddaghcottageirishpub.weebly.com/about.html) in the Hourglass District. Its fiddler and banjo-player owners have run the same Every-Other-Wednesday Irish session since 2001, and in 2016 they bought the pub where they met. Around it, a quieter network keeps things going:

- a monthly circuit of Sunday-afternoon Irish sessions that moves between Maitland, Apopka and College Park;
- an old-time jam in a taproom west of downtown, plus a Friday-night old-time jam that rotates through private homes via an email list;
- a bluegrass jam that has met every Friday for about 35 years in a strip-mall parking lot in Ocoee.

Bluegrass has deeper roots in rural Lake County, where the Cassia Community Club's jams were legendary in the 1990s and were revived in 2026. It also goes back to Kissimmee, where [Vassar Clements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_Clements) grew up.

Contra dancing was rebuilt after COVID as an explicitly young, gender-neutral, sometimes-techno dance in Winter Park. Shape-note singing grew to an annual all-day singing only in 2024. The large retiree square-dance world exists, but at the edges: in The Villages, Seminole County and along the Volusia and Brevard coast, not in Orlando proper. Over all of this sits a professional Celtic layer from the tourist economy, including Epcot's long-running [Off Kilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off_Kilter_(band)) and Raglan Road at Disney Springs. Its alumni regularly turn up at the neighborhood pub.

**In numbers:** I count about **44 recurring participatory events a month** inside the MSA. That breaks down as:

| Category | Events per month |
|---|---|
| Irish sessions | ~6.2 |
| Old-time jams | ~4.5 |
| Bluegrass jams | ~7.3 |
| Contra dances | 2 |
| Shape-note singings | ~2 |
| International and Israeli folk dance | ~8 |
| Modern western square dances | ~12 |
| Other (dulcimer jam, gypsy-jazz jam) | 2 |

Without square dancing, the total is about 32 a month. Inside the MSA I could verify **no** current English country dance, Scottish country dance, céilí or set-dance class, morris team, sea-shanty sing, Scandinavian, klezmer or Balkan session. For those, Orlando dancers drive to the Space Coast or further.

### Irish sessions

#### Claddagh Cottage and the Every-Other-Wednesday Session (EOWS)

The **Every-Other-Wednesday Session** at Claddagh Cottage Irish Pub (2421 Curry Ford Rd, Hourglass District) is the oldest and steadiest trad event in the metro.

- **Hosts and history.** It was listed on The Session in November 2002 as "every other Wednesday… hosted by Vicki Birdfeather-Gish and Scott Vocca" ([thesession.org/sessions/176](https://thesession.org/sessions/176)). The pub says Scott and Vicki "have run the bi-weekly traditional Irish music session at the Claddagh since 2001" ([about page](https://claddaghcottageirishpub.weebly.com/about.html)).
- **The pub's story.** The two met at the original Claddagh location "playing music together at the traditional Irish music session." They bought the pub in 2016, and "were in business for less than a year when their building was sold and demolished." They "painstakingly recreated" the pub at its current address ([home page](https://claddaghcottageirishpub.weebly.com/)), which reopened in April 2018. A "1-year anniversary party" was on 20 April 2019, with The Bronx Boys and the house band Murphy's Slaw. During the move, the session met temporarily at the Harp & Celt downtown (Jason Van Steenwyk, The Session, Dec 2016).
- **Schedule.** The pub's public Google Calendar shows a biweekly Wednesday recurrence at 7:30 pm. It marks "25th Anniversary of the Every-Other-Wednesday Irish session!" on **9 September 2026**.
- **What it's like.** Visitors on The Session have called it "intermediate to strong… a little flute heavy… almost all tunes" (2004) and said it is "just like a session in Ireland" (a Dublin visitor, 2012). In 2012 the Kerry accordion player Brendan Begley dropped in after a gig.
- **Specialties.** The calendar shows an occasional **"Irish C session,"** played "one whole step down, so the key of D becomes C." That is a nod to the pub's uilleann pipers, who often play flat-pitched pipes.

The Claddagh has built a whole learners' and side-session ecosystem around the EOWS:

- **Monthly slow session.** One Sunday a month at 3 pm, "melody instruments, guitar and Irish bouzouki only." It first appears on the calendar on 4 September 2022.
- **"Learn a tune" class.** An hour before the slow session, with each month's tune named on the calendar (e.g. "The Earl's Chair," 11 October 2026; "Drops of Brandy" earlier). Vicki Gish announced the tune class in November 2024: "we are trying something new were we are teaching a tune starting at 3:00 pm before the pub opens" ([The Session](https://thesession.org/sessions/176)).
- **Gypsy Jazz Jam.** Monthly on a Sunday (usually the 3rd) at 5 pm, since March 2025.
- **House band, Murphy's Slaw.** Justin Murphy (flute, whistle, guitar), Kathleen Cavanagh (uilleann pipes), Vicki Gish (fiddle) and Scott Vocca (banjos, octave mandolin) play most other weekends, with no cover.
- **Uilleann-piping hub.** The pub hosts the **South East Uilleann Pipers Club**, whose members come from six states. Its annual **South East Tionól** has been held at the Claddagh each late January or early February since 2024 (next: 29–31 January 2027). The club also holds **International Uilleann Piping Day** there each November, with "history, demonstration, and 'Try the Pipes!'" ([southeastpipersclub.org](https://www.southeastpipersclub.org/)).

#### The sessions that didn't last

The Session's archive shows how hard it has been to keep a second Orlando session alive:

- **Before 2007:** a monthly Sunday session hosted by Rick Norry at **Fiddler's Green** in Winter Park (open since 1996).
- **2007:** the session moved to the new **Harp & Celt** downtown, then was "on hold" by October ([sessions/1692](https://thesession.org/sessions/1692)).
- **2008–2011:** a 4th-Sunday session at **Orlando Brewing**, described as "the latest of several attempts to find a stable venue for a one Sunday a month session." It had "dropped by the wayside" by November 2011 ([sessions/2053](https://thesession.org/sessions/2053)).
- **Summer 2025:** Jason Van Steenwyk ran a Monday session at the **Lucky Leprechaun** off International Drive, pitched as "convenient… for convention travelers and tourists." It ended in August 2025 when he moved to Hawaii ([sessions/9291](https://thesession.org/sessions/9291)).
- **c.2004:** a session at a café in Eustis is mentioned once.

Fiddler's Green now advertises a Thursday open mic ([openjamevents](https://openjamevents.com/open-mic-fiddlers-green-irish-pub)) rather than a trad session. I treat that as a lead for the Layer 2 thread.

#### Nearby, outside the MSA

- **Ormond Beach:** Fletcher's Pub holds a first-Sunday session (4–7 pm, since May 2018; "still going strong" in November 2024).
- **Brevard County:** Melbourne and Cocoa have a dense Irish cluster. Meg O'Malley's runs every Tuesday 6–9 pm ("still active as of November 2024"). Ann Dillon runs sessions at The Cottage Irish Pub (1st and 3rd Thursdays) and Hogan's in Cocoa (alternate Thursdays), per [FSFA](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/regular-jams/) and [The Session](https://thesession.org/sessions/3084).

For a visiting fiddler on a **Tuesday night**, the honest answer is to drive an hour to Melbourne. Orlando itself has nothing that night.

**Comhaltas:** Central Florida has no Comhaltas branch. Yet Orlando hosted the **2025 Comhaltas North American Convention** (3–6 April 2025, DoubleTree by Hilton Orlando at SeaWorld), headlined by the John Whelan All-Star Céilí Band with Dylan Foley, Larry Nugent and others ([Irish Echo](https://www.irishecho.com/2025/2/feile-oriel-to-honor-flanagan)). This is a good example of Orlando's convention-and-tourism economy bringing the trad world in briefly.

### Old-time

Old-time music in Orlando is small and partly invisible, because much of it happens in people's homes.

- **2nd-Sunday jam, Broken Strings Taproom** (1012 W Church St). FSFA lists it as "Old-Time Second Sunday… 2–5 pm," and an EventHound listing for an "Old Timers Jam Sesh" on Sunday 13 September 2026 matches it. The taproom is now operated by Three Odd Guys Brewing ([site](https://www.brokenstringsbrewery.com/)).
- **Friday home jam.** FSFA lists a "Friday night Old Time Jam at different homes, email Jaybrad44@yahoo.com to get on the notification list." This is the descendant of a long-running list. A 2015 archived page of the local band site folkfiddle.com described "MOST Fridays there is a fiddle tunes jam at various homes… write to Jay Bradbury and ask him to put you on the Orlando Area Tunes Jam Location email list" ([Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://folkfiddle.com/tunes.htm)). The same page shares chord charts "for the tunes we play at the Orlando Wednesday Tunes Jam."
- **Wednesday Tunes.** That earlier Wednesday jam was largely hosted by **Bruce and Sharon Hartmann**. The Orlando contra dance's memorial page for Bruce (c.2009) calls him "a main pillar of the Orlando Contra scene." It says Bruce and Sharon "hosted the largest percentage of the Wednesday Tunes old time jam sessions in recent years," calls him "a friend to, and student of, Stu Jamieson," and remembers "Sharon and Bruce and their hats at FOTMC in Dade City" ([Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/2010id_/http://orlandocontradance.org/bruce.shtml)). Sharon Hartmann is still active: she plays fiddle in the contra band McIntyre ([orlandocontra.org](https://orlandocontra.org/dances-and-events/)). By inference only, she is probably the "Sharon" contact on the FSFA list.
- **Barberville Pioneer Settlement** (Volusia County, outside the MSA, about 50 minutes north). This is where Central Florida old-timers gather in daylight. It has an old-time jam on 1st Saturdays (10 am–3 pm) and free old-time instrument tutoring on 4th Saturdays. Neither runs in summer or on festival days, and there is a $6 park fee ([FSFA](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/regular-jams/)). The settlement was founded in 1976 by Volusia County teachers ([pioneersettlement.org](https://www.pioneersettlement.org/)).
- **Florida State Fiddlers Association.** Statewide, the FSFA runs the official Florida State Fiddle Contest. Its 45th annual convention was in October 2026 at O'Leno State Park, which implies it started around 1981 ([convention page](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/2025-florida-fiddlers-convention/)).

A 2015 Fiddle Hangout thread sums up the regional feel: "Old time I'm not sure… Florida is pretty solid Bluegrass country pickin wise, always has been" ([fiddlehangout](https://www.fiddlehangout.com/archive/42213)).

### Bluegrass

#### The Ocoee Parking Lot Bluegrass Jam

Every Friday at about 6:30 pm, weather permitting, pickers set up lawn chairs in a strip-mall lot behind a Pizza Hut and Starbucks at West Colonial Drive and Maguire Road. There is no amplification and no drums, and in bad weather the jam may move to a member's house ([jamfinder](https://www.jamfinder.org/jams/ocoee-fl-parking-lot-bluegrass/)).

- **Origins.** It was started around 1990 by **Jack and Judie Lewis** and a friend. It began outdoors "near Tanner Hall on the shore of Lake Apopka," moved to Thursday nights in a Winter Garden firehouse until "a liability concern from the city" ended that, and then moved to the lot behind a service station. It switched to Fridays "because people could stay late" ([Orange Observer, Dec 2020](https://www.orangeobserver.com/news/2020/dec/16/pickin-it-up-locals-celebrate-30-years-of-bluegrass-jams/)). The lot once held a Piggly Wiggly.
- **People.** Johnny Adams of the band Moonlite Express (later The Freightliners) began building mandolins in 1994. "The first one took 1,000 hours." By 2023, at 86, he held his 77th ([ClickOrlando, 2023](https://www.clickorlando.com/insider/2023/11/30/youre-invited-florida-parking-lot-bluegrass-jam-plays-every-week-32-years-and-counting/)). A Belgian couple with a house in Winter Garden told reporters they come "most Fridays, when we are in the U.S." That is a small window onto the tourist and second-home overlay.
- **Status.** A current 2026 directory listing exists, but I found no dated 2025–26 news story, so I rate it medium confidence.

#### Cassia Community Club (Lake County)

The club is a 1940s schoolhouse on SR 44 near Eustis, "known though out Central Florida for our Country/Bluegrass jams… under the majestic oak trees" ([club site](https://cassiacommunityclub1.com/)).

- **Heyday and decline.** CFBA president John Parrish "cut his teeth playing bluegrass at Cassia" and recalls "massive bluegrass jams in the 1990's" with "hundreds" of players. Regulars included **Vassar Clements' cousins Carroll and Gerald**, Marty Rabon's father Kenny (as the article spells it; probably Marty Raybon), "a walking songbook of fiddle tunes," and Nathan Livers of Michael Cleveland & Flamekeeper. The jams then "dwindled" as the founders died.
- **2026 revival.** The Central Florida Bluegrass Association has rebuilt them. Jams run on the 2nd and 4th Saturdays at 4 pm, and a free **3rd-Saturday Jam & Concert** has a featured act at 5 pm followed by jams "until the last case closes." A CFBA Expo was held on 18 April 2026 ([Bluegrass Today, March 2026](https://bluegrasstoday.com/saturday-jam-in-eustis-fl-coming-back/); [jamfinder](https://www.jamfinder.org/jams/eustis-fl-cfba-3rd-saturday-jam-concert-cassia/)).

#### Vassar Clements and Kissimmee

Vassar Clements was born in 1928 in Kinard, Florida, and "grew up in Kissimmee." He taught himself fiddle at 7 and formed a local string band with his cousins (Wikipedia names them Red and Gerald). He met Bill Monroe through his stepfather's friendship with Chubby Wise, and replaced Wise in the Blue Grass Boys in late 1949 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_Clements)). He later briefly worked as a plumber at the Kennedy Space Center. This is the strongest "fiddle angle" for the metro. I found no local commemoration of him.

#### Nearby, outside the MSA

- **Rose Bay Campground, Port Orange:** a Monday-night jam (6–8 pm, "except during NASCAR and Bike Week"), backed by the band Salty Grass, has run for "upwards of twenty years" ([bluegrassjamsnearme](https://bluegrassjamsnearme.com/events/rose-bay-jam/)).
- **DeLand:** a Friday jam in a shopping center was reported in 2015 but is unconfirmed now.

Orlando's bluegrass associations are based outside the city (CFBA in DeLand). Bluegrass here lives in the rural and exurban ring rather than in the city.

### Dulcimers and other jams

- **Mount Dora Dulcimer Society.** Meets on 1st Saturdays (10 am–noon) in the community room of the W.T. Bland Public Library. "All acoustic instruments are welcome," and the library lends ukuleles ([NewsBreak/LibCal](https://www.newsbreak.com/florida-daily-brief-373179613/4868802771520-monthly-acoustic-jam-at-w-t-bland-library)).
- **Central Florida Dulcimer Festival.** Held every February at First Baptist Church of Mount Dora, with workshops in mountain and hammered dulcimer, fiddle, autoharp and bowed psaltery, jams on Thursday and Saturday nights, RV parking and scholarships ([festival site](https://centralfloridadulcimerfestival.net/); 2025 instructor Linda Weber Collins via [Cabin Hill Studios](https://www.cabinhillstudios.com/events/central-florida-dulcimer-festival-1)).

Mount Dora's antiques-and-retirees character suits a February festival with RV parking.

### Contra dance

**[Orlando Contra Dance](https://orlandocontra.org/)** dances on the 1st and 3rd Fridays at the Winter Park Ballroom Dance Co. (6904 Aloma Ave).

- **Format.** Doors open at 6:30 pm. A beginner lesson and an intermediate workshop both start at 7 pm. Beginner-to-intermediate dancing runs from 7:30 and intermediate-to-advanced from 9:00, followed by "dessert at Jeremiah's Italian Ice."
- **Cost.** $15, "Students and out of work: FREE! Your second contra ever: FREE!"
- **Who comes.** It calls itself "Florida's only gender-neutral contra series" and is unusually explicit about its audience: "young adults, roughly ages 18-30… a modern, high-tempo… dance party," typically at 120 bpm ([FAQ](https://orlandocontra.org/faq/)). Yet it also says "We have dancers from 7 to 70+… Typically 25+ people come, including 5+ first-timers."
- **Music.** Mostly live local bands:
  - That's a Plenty (Jonathan Hodge, fiddle; Ted Strauss, guitar)
  - McIntyre (Lisa McIntyre, George Houston, Terry Henson, Sharon Hartmann)
  - Sandy Back Porch (Freddy Burky)
  - visiting bands such as Kettle of Fish (Space Coast), Coral Riff (Tampa) and The Four O'Clocks (Atlanta)

  Some nights are "TECHNO!" contras with DJ'd pop-rock and contra-fusion mixes ([schedule](https://orlandocontra.org/dances-and-events/)).
- **Callers.** Joe Harrington, Sam Sharpless and JJ Luxe locally, with guests from Gainesville, Tampa, Berea KY and Bloomington IN.
- **Funding and listings.** It is listed on CDSS ([event](https://cdss.org/event/orlando-contra-dance-2-2/2026-06-05/)) and supported by a United Arts of Central Florida grant ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/greater-orlando-contra-dance/)).
- **Origins.** The series grew from **Contra Knights**, the UCF student club. Its first dance was "at UCF in Fall 2022. Almost nobody knew how to dance 90 minutes earlier." The on-campus dances are now dormant while the organizers seek new student officers. The defunct University of Florida contra club "gave us all their decorations, including the blacklights!" ([What's Contra](https://orlandocontra.org/whats-contra/)).

#### The older lineage

The current series has a predecessor. From at least 2005, **orlandocontradance.org** ran the **Orlando Last Saturday Contra Dance** at the Secret Lake Park Dance Hall in Casselberry. That building, at 200 N Triplet Lake Dr, is now the Casselberry Recreation Center, where the Hoedowners square dance.

- **Format.** A half hour of free instruction from 7:30, then dancing 8–11 pm. Admission was $8 in 2010 and $9 by about 2013.
- **Traditions.** Potlucks, a "Mad for Plaid" dance, a "Faux Fur Frolic," a tie-dye July, an ice-cream social, and a New Year's Eve dance in place of December ([Wayback 2010](http://web.archive.org/web/20100113062656/http://orlandocontradance.org/)).
- **Bands and callers.** Contradancelinks listings show bands such as Barnstorm (with Bruce and Sharon Hartmann), Celery City String Band, Rafter Ringers and the Flying Turtles String Band, and callers Pat Czar, Eric Harris and others.
- **English dances.** The series also organized occasional English dances (e.g. "The English are coming! To Casselberry," June 2013, with Pam Russo).
- **The end.** By 2020 contradancelinks listed an Orlando dance at **Lakeside Community Hall**, 4005 N Orange Blossom Trail. Then COVID cancelled every Florida dance ([contradancelinks](http://contradancelinks.com/schedule_FL.html)).

The post-2022 series is therefore a generational reset rather than a continuation. It moved from an older potluck-and-plaid Saturday dance to a youth-targeted, gender-neutral Friday dance party.

**Nearby:** Space Coast Contra now runs "5th Saturday" double dances in Rockledge ([spacecoastcontra.org](https://spacecoastcontra.org)). The 2025 Florida Folk Dance Council (FFDC) directory still says "usually the 2nd Saturday," so the sources disagree. Gainesville and Tampa have their own series.

### Square and round dancing

Before researching, I expected modern western square dancing to be huge in a retiree-heavy region. The data say otherwise for Orlando proper.

The [Florida Federation of Square Dancers](https://www.floridasquaredance.com/) counts "more than 60 clubs" statewide. Its **Central Florida Square & Round Dancers Association** (founded 15 February 1957 at the home of Mary and Hack Chilton, with a "Charter Jamboree" on 3 March 1957; [history](http://www.dancergram.com/aa-FWS/central/association.html)) covers nine counties. Inside the MSA, its weekly calendar ([dances](http://www.dancergram.com/aa-FWS/central/dances.html)) shows only these clubs:

- **Casselberry Hoedowners.** Sundays at the Casselberry Recreation Center: early rounds 1:30, workshop or class 2:00, club dance at Mainstream and Plus levels 3:00. Caller Rod Barngrover. Founded about 1984 (35th anniversary in 2019) ([site](https://www.squaredancesites.com/casselberry_hoedowners/)).
- **Whirl & Twirl, Oviedo.** "Most Saturdays": lessons at 3, dance 4–5:30 ([site](https://whirlandtwirloviedo.com)).
- **Geneva Round Dance Club.** A Federation member; no schedule was found.
- **Orange Blossom Squares, The Villages.** Sundays 6:45–8:45 pm at La Hacienda Recreation Center, Lady Lake, the Lake County part of The Villages. Founded 1986, "one of the largest dance clubs in Florida," with 154 members in 2015 ([about](https://orangeblossomsquares.org/about); [Villages-News, 2015](https://www.villages-news.com/2015/08/30/its-cool-to-be-square-at-least-on-the-dance-floor/)). Everything else is outside the MSA:

- other Villages clubs in Sumter County (Village Squares, Stars N Stripes);
- Ocala and Marion County clubs (Pilot Squares, New Circle Squares, Sternwheelers, Celebrity Squares);
- coastal clubs: Granada Squares Denim & Lace (Ormond Beach), Spindrifters (Port Orange), Space Trackers (Merritt Island), Palm Bay Promenaders;
- Wrangler Country Squares, which dances on Fridays from September to May at the Barberville Pioneer Settlement.

### Traditional singing (Layer 3)

#### Sacred Harp and shape-note

The **[Central Florida Shape-Note Singing](https://sites.google.com/view/orlandofasola)** group is organized by Rachel Speer, with Dean Jens as secretary and arranger. About 30 leaders are named in the minutes. **David Ivey** taught the singing school and "recounted his experiences singing in Central Florida in the 1960s" ([minutes 2024](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=Orlando&n=6735)). Sunday is at **Bethlehem Primitive Baptist Church, Old Chicora/Lithia**, near Tampa and outside the MSA, singing from the *Cooper Book* (2012). ### Age mix, snowbirds and the post-COVID rebound

- **Age.** Ages vary sharply by tradition:
  - Square dancing is retiree-run, including resident-only clubs in The Villages.
  - The Ocoee jam's founders and regulars were 80–86 in 2020–23.
  - Orlando Contra deliberately targets 18–30-year-olds.
  - The Sacred Harp group is a mixed-age revival crowd.
- **Seasons.** I have no attendance data, but the calendar shows a strong winter tilt:
  - The Highland Games, the Sacred Harp weekend, the Tionól and the dulcimer festival all fall in January and February.
  - The international folk dance club runs September to June, Wrangler Country Squares September to May, and Barberville's jams skip summer.
  - Melbourne regulars insist "Winter, summer, spring and fall… we are there!"
- **COVID.** In 2020–21 contradancelinks assumed "all dances and events are canceled." Fletcher's Pub noted in 2020 that "due to Covid, we don't have the usual players." The Claddagh's recurring calendar series was reset in March 2021. The rebound has been institutional rather than a return to old forms: a new contra organization (2022), a slow session (2022), the all-day Sacred Harp singing (2024), the Tionól (2024), a gypsy-jazz jam (2025) and the Cassia revival (2026).

## Part II. Song, listening rooms, traditional singing, instruction, radio and makers

*Thread: song_listening · As of October 2026 · Metro: Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford, FL (Lake, Orange, Osceola, Seminole counties)*

### Character

Orlando's folk song scene is small, mostly retired-age and run by volunteers. Its center is Winter Park, not Orlando proper. It has no folk coffeehouse district, no folk radio and no club that owns a room. What it has instead is a few unusually durable institutions:

- a 30-year-old singer-songwriter concert series that has moved through at least six venues;
- a church-hall open mic that has met on second Fridays since 1994;
- a song circle that calls itself the oldest in Central Florida;
- one of the country's most distinctive "house concert" venues, a family home built around a 90-seat hall with an active acoustic system.

Around these sit a public library that hosts folk concerts, a ukulele society, a guitar circle and kids' instrument days, plus a dulcimer club led by a nationally known player. The scene's emotional compass points north. Its touchstones are the Florida Folk Festival in White Springs, the Will McLean and Gamble Rogers festivals, and the Florida songwriting tradition those two men stand for. Gamble Rogers himself was born in Winter Park. A visiting fiddler won't find much fiddle in this layer. They will find a listening culture that is polite, inexpensive ($2 to $15 at the door), and happens mostly on Sunday afternoons and Friday evenings.

### The anchor: Central Florida Folk, Inc. and its "End of the Month" concerts

**Central Florida Folk, Inc. (CFF)** is a 501(c)(3) "Founded May 2001" and a group member of Folk Alliance International ([CFF personnel page](https://sites.google.com/site/susiecoolsongs/central-florida-folk/cffinfo)). Its concert lineage is older than the corporation. CFF's own archive, "thanks to Stuart Hall for documentation," lists every **"Live @ Leu"** concert at Harry P. Leu Gardens starting with **"The first Live @ Leu Gardens!!!" on July 30, 1995** (David Dowling, opened by Wednesday Tunes) ([Leu Gardens archive](https://cffolk.org/leu_gardens/performer_archive_list/past_leu_gardens_shows.htm)). The archive is a rare month-by-month record of a Southern singer-songwriter circuit:

- **Florida regulars:** Bob Rafkin (the most-booked artist, appearing at least seven times), Mindy Simmons ("Florida's own Songbird"), Tammerlin, Sam Pacetti, the Ashley Gang, Amy Carol Webb, Sno Rogers, Cyd Ward, Grant Livingston.
- **National folk touring names:** Steve Gillette & Cindy Mangsen (almost every January), Bill Staines, Jack Williams, Rod MacDonald, Harvey Reid, David Mallett, Small Potatoes, Roy Book Binder, Michael Smith (the series' "10th Anniversary" show, July 31, 2005), Carla Ulbrich, Don White, Magda Hiller and the 24th Street Klezmer Band.
- **Quirks:** the archive records no concert each March because of the Leu Gardens Flower Show. Each May reads "No Live @ Leu Concert, go to FFF @ White Springs". Most Septembers there was a "No Guitars Allowed" show (piano, hammered dulcimer, storytelling). Every July there was a free "Summer Sampler" of local performers, the first on July 15, 2001.

After 2008 the series kept moving ([concerts page](https://cffolk.org/concerts/concerts.htm)):

| Years | Venue |
|---|---|
| 2010 | Osteen Diner (Volusia County) |
| 2011–2016 | Villa ConRoy |
| 2016–2019 | Lake of the Woods |
| Intermittently | Fodor's Grove (2218 Vincent Rd, Orlando), the backup venue |
| Now | **Winter Park Public Library**, 1052 W. Morse Blvd |

That library was designed by Sir David Adjaye with HuntonBrady ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Adjaye)).

**Current season.** The site's schedule (updated 10-20-2026) runs:

- Nov 15: Dave Rudolf, opened by the Hoochie Digs
- Dec 13: Hat Trick, with Emmett Carlisle
- Jan 31, 2027: Rupert Wates, with Frank Critelli
- Feb 28: Grace Morrison & Marc Douglas Berardo
- Mar 21: David Singley, with Aaron English
- Apr 25: Bing Futch
- May 23: Dell Suggs, with Logan Belle
- Dec 12, 2027: Danny Schmidt

The page still says CFF hosts concerts "on the Last Sunday of the month", but several of these dates (Nov 15, Dec 13, Mar 21) are not last Sundays. Treat the cadence as roughly monthly, about 0.9 a month. The November 2026 flier gives the economics and the format ([flier](https://www.cffolk.org/concerts/winter_park_library/2026/november/EOM-NovDaveHoochie2026_720.jpg)):

- start time 2 pm;
- suggested donation $15, $10 for library and CFF members;
- "Jam Session Starts after the Show".

CFF also runs free kids' **"Instrument Petting Zoo"** afternoons at the library (next: Saturday Nov 21, 2026, 2–4 pm). There, "a professional performer will be available to explain each musical instrument" ([special events](https://cffolk.org/special_events/special_events.htm)).

**How small CFF is.** CFF publishes its membership list online with names and expiry dates. On Oct 17, 2026 it showed 19 numbered entries, of which 13 were unexpired. Dues are $25 individual and $40 family ([membership](https://cffolk.org/membership/membership.htm)). The volunteers are:

- President Barry Brogan;
- VP Sigi Nagys and Secretary Liz Nagys;
- Treasurer/webmaster John M. Cunningham;
- media contact Sheila Anderson;
- flier-maker and workshop leader Susan "SusieCool" Foster-Trewick;
- sound engineer Brett Cammack;
- song-circle host Brad Thompson.

The website "went on-line March 7, 2007" ([home](https://cffolk.org/)). Very few people sustain this series.

**The Song Circle.** CFF's song circle bills itself as **"Central Florida's oldest continuous song circle,"** where musicians bring "guitars, violins, bass fiddles and so on" and play "everything from homemade music to OPMs (other people's music)" ([Audubon Park Garden District legacy calendar](https://www.helloapgd.com/legacy-calendar/folk-music-song-circle)). As of SusieCool's open-mic directory (last updated Aug 1, 2026), it meets **First Thursdays on Zoom only** ([directory](https://sites.google.com/site/susiecoolsongs/open-mic-directory-central-florida)). No founding date was found. Its "oldest" claim is plausible given the people involved, but unverified.

**Early history in the press.** A 2002 *Orlando Weekly* piece fills in the era ([Orlando Weekly, Apr 4, 2002](https://www.orlandoweekly.com/music/friendly-folk-warms-museum-2258590/)). Central Florida Folk was then:

- sponsoring "seasonal concerts in Langford Park from October through March";
- running "at least 10 Sundays out of the year" of indoor shows at Leu produced by Stuart Hall;
- booking monthly "Folk Fridays" (since November 2001) at the **Albin Polasek Museum & Sculpture Gardens** through volunteer Annie Phelps.

The same article points readers to the statewide **Friends of Florida Folk** calendar and to "Orlando's independent spin-off group" at `members.aol.com/OTownFOFF`. That spin-off was likely a precursor of, or adjacent to, CFF. This is my inference and is unverified. Stuart Hall's line in that article still describes the scene: folk is "much more communal than rock & roll."

### Listening rooms and house concerts

#### Timucua Arts Foundation: "the white house"

Visit Orlando's 2026 profile tells the story ([Visit Orlando](https://www.visitorlando.com/blog/post/timucua-arts-foundation-orlando/)). In the late 1990s **Benoit Glazer**, conductor and musical director of Cirque du Soleil's *La Nouba* at Disney Springs, and his wife **Élaine Corriveau** began hosting concerts in their home. They soon built a custom three-story house in Orlando's SODO district around a concert hall that doubles as the family living room. Its features:

- 90 seats "over three floors";
- an active acoustic control system Glazer calls "the only system like it in Florida," able to create "ideal acoustics for 65 diverse setups";
- tickets "$30 or less," with free parking;
- a request that each guest bring a bottle of wine to share;
- "about half our events have no fixed ticket price";
- a 75-minute average show length.

Timucua turned 25 in 2025 ([Orange County newsroom](https://newsroom.ocfl.net/2025/05/timucua-arts-foundation-celebrates-25-years-of-bringing-art-to-life/)). Visit Orlando's text also says Glazer "founded Timucua out of his own home in 1990," which conflicts with its own "established in the year 2000" and with the 25th-anniversary framing. I use 2000.

Folk is a minority strand here, beside jazz, classical and poetry. Timucua's event feed does have **"Folk Series"** and **"Americana Series"** categories ([events](https://www.timucua.com/events)). Recent and upcoming entries:

- **Cassie and Maggie MacDonald**, the Cape Breton fiddle-and-piano sisters, in the International Chamber Music Festival (Jan 30, 2026; [event](https://timucua.com/event/icmf-cassie-and-maggie/));
- Walt McClements (Apr 2, 2026);
- Danny Davis and the Nashville Brass;
- Minnesota bluegrass band **Monroe Crossing** (Jan 16, 2027);
- the **Andrew Collins Trio** (Feb 6, 2027).

That adds up to perhaps four or five folk/roots nights a year out of about 150 shows. That is not a folk club, but it is a superb room for one. A fiddler visiting during the January–February chamber festival should check it.

#### First Unitarian Church of Orlando: Black Cat Café and the Milk District Salon

The **Black Cat Café Open Mic** dates to "one fateful Friday the 13th in May of 1994," which gave it its name and mascot. It has met monthly in Gore Hall on the **2nd Friday** "for over 30 years and still going strong," led by Chip D. (Chip Dockery) with a crew of about seven volunteers ([1U page](https://orlandouu.org/worship-overview/music-and-choir/black-cat-open-mic/)). It is free, with a $2 donation welcome. It describes itself as "one of the very few true 'listening room' venues in Central Florida," with a "very supportive audience that likes first-time performers." It also says "some of the best local professional acts" drop in to try out new material.

Sources disagree on the time. The church page says doors at 4:30 pm with performances 5–9 pm. Facebook event copies for 2025 say doors at 6:30 pm and 15-minute slots from 7 to 10 pm ([event copy](https://stayhappening.com/e/black-cat-open-mic-live-andamp-in-person-E2ISWOGTTT4)). An old cffolk.org page has 7:30–11:30 pm and a $2 cover ([cffolk Black Cat page](https://cffolk.org/open_mics/black_cat/black_cat.htm)). That page's 2007 photo log shows the cast of the era: Jeri Simpson, Stephen Grayce, "Old Guys on the Porch," Blues Matters, Stuart Hall and SusieCool, Chip Dockery and others.

The same church runs the **Milk District Salon at 1U** series ([page](https://orlandouu.org/worship-overview/music-and-choir/milk-district-salon-concert-series/)). The 2026–27 season is six events, with a $125 season pass (about $21 a show). It includes the veteran women's-music singer-songwriter **Tret Fure** (Mar 20, 2027) and a Korean *gayageum*/*piri* concert-lecture that includes folk songs (Feb 27, 2027). The church's music program also lists a "Folk Ensemble."

#### Blue Bamboo, Judson's Live, the Plaza Live, Casa Feliz, Tuffy's

- **Blue Bamboo Center for the Arts.** This Winter Park listening room, mainly jazz, reopened on June 22, 2025 in the *old* Winter Park Library building at 460 E. New England Ave. It has a ~200-seat Encore Stage and a ~60-seat Bravo Stage ([Winter Park Magazine, Mar 2026](https://winterparkmag.com/2026/03/31/yes-the-boo-is-here-to-stay/)). *Orlando Weekly* readers voted it 2025's "Best Comeback" ([Best of 2025](https://community.orlandoweekly.com/best-of/2025/local-music/best-comeback-blue-bamboo-center-for-the-arts-40217520)). So Winter Park's old library is now a listening room and its new library hosts the folk club.
- **Judson's Live** (the listening room at the Dr. Phillips Center). This is where most touring roots acts land. Undiscovered Music's acoustic listings for Nov 2026–Jan 2027 show Redd & the Paper Flowers (an Appalachian string band, Dec 12), Sam Morrow, Damon Fowler and Carolyn Wonderland there ([Undiscovered Music, Orlando](https://undiscovered.music/places/orlando_fl)). The same page lists **The Plaza Live** with Tommy Emmanuel (Dec 11) and Judy Collins (Jan 14), and **Tuffy's Music Box** in Sanford with Carbon Leaf (Jan 14). Tuffy's also booked Canadian indie-folk band Great Lake Swimmers for Oct 17, 2026 (Bandsintown, via search; Tuffy's own site blocked automated fetching).

It presents "Music at the Casa" (jazz, Spanish guitar, "acoustic music") and a "Parlor Series." On Nov 5, 2026 that series held "Sunshine & Shadows: An Evening of Florida Poetry & Music," with musical interludes by Orlando singer-songwriter Bob Carr and emcee Rollins professor Maurice "Socky" O'Sullivan ([Music at the Casa](https://casafeliz.us/music); [Parlor Series](https://casafeliz.us/parlor-series)). ### Florida's songwriter tradition and Orlando

**Gamble Rogers (1937–1991)** is Orlando's own. He was born in Winter Park, named for two architects in his family (father James Gamble Rogers II and great-uncle James Gamble Rogers), and chose music after walking into a Serendipity Singers audition ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamble_Rogers)). His early stories placed his characters in "non-fictional Winter Park, Florida" before he invented *Oklawaha County*. He was a Florida Folk Festival headliner in the 1970s and appears in *Heartworn Highways* (1976). He died in 1991 at Flagler Beach trying to save a swimmer. He entered the Florida Artists Hall of Fame in 1998.

The **Gamble Rogers Folk Festival** (St. Johns County, outside the MSA) held its 31st edition on April 10–12, 2026 ([festival](https://www.gamblerogersfest.org/gamblerogersfolkfestival)), and the 32nd is set for April 9, 2027 ([Visit St. Augustine](https://www.visitstaugustine.com/event/gamble-rogers-music-festival)). *Old City Magazine* says it has run "every year since 1995" ([article](https://www.oldcitymagazine.com/31st-annual-gamble-rogers-folk-festival-2026)), but "31st in 2026" implies 1996. CFF still lists it under "May."

**Will McLean (1919–1990)**, the "Father of Florida Folk" and "Black Hat Troubadour," wrote 3,700+ songs and stories, played Carnegie Hall with Pete Seeger in 1963, and entered the Florida Artists Hall of Fame in 1996 ([Florida Division of Arts and Culture](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-artists-hall-of-fame/will-mclean/)). His ashes were scattered in the Ocklawaha River at Gore's Landing ([Will McLean Foundation](https://www.willmclean.com/will-mclean-foundation/about-will-mclean)), in Marion County, outside the MSA. I found **no documented Orlando residence** for McLean. His Central Florida tie is the river country north of the metro and the foundation's circle.

The **Will McLean Foundation** was established in 1990 and has its contact number in Orlando's 407 area code. Its key founder was **Margaret Longhill (1921–2018)**, the "Matriarch of Florida Folk" ([tribute](https://www.willmclean.com/will-mclean-foundation/honoring-margaret-longhill)). It runs the **Will McLean Music Festival**: about 80 folk acts on three covered stages, a young musicians' program, a "Best New Florida Song" contest and a Saturday-night contra dance. The 2027 edition is March 12–14 at Florida Sand Music Ranch between Brooksville and Dade City; the foundation took over the 80-acre former Sertoma Youth Ranch in 2022 ([festival](https://www.willmclean.com/music-festival/will-mclean-music-festival); [home](https://www.willmclean.com/home)). The first festival was at the Rainbow River headsprings in 1990, though the foundation also says it has produced the festival "since 1991."

The **Florida Folk Festival** (White Springs, since 1953; [Florida Memory](https://www.floridamemory.com/discover/historical_records/folklife/festival.php)) shapes Orlando's calendar. For years the Leu Gardens and CFF schedules marked May: "No Show - See You at the 6Xth Annual Florida Folk Festival." Its organizer Tom Shed is quoted on Bing Futch's site saying Futch "plays the dulcimer like Jimi Hendrix." Futch also won "Best Guitarist (Solo/Duo)" at the **2016 International Blues Challenge** on mountain dulcimer.

## Part III. Heritage and diaspora traditions

*Thread: heritage. Metro: Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford MSA (Lake, Orange, Osceola, Seminole counties). As of October 2026. Structured data: `heritage.json`.*

### Character

Orlando's heritage scene is young, Caribbean and Latin American, and mostly south of downtown. Its center is the Puerto Rican diaspora of Orange and Osceola counties. In 2018, after Hurricane Maria, Orange County had 209,151 Puerto Rican residents, second only to the Bronx, and Osceola had the highest Puerto Rican share of any county outside the island, 33.7% ([Orlando Sentinel](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2019/09/27/census-bureau-puerto-rican-population-in-orange-osceola-jumps-125-after-hurricane-maria/)). That community has made Afro-Puerto Rican bomba and plena, Christmas parrandas, and huge parades and SanSe festivals into living practice. Jíbaro music (cuatro, seis and trova) is quieter than you would expect.

Around that core is a thick ring of newer diasporas, most built since the 1990s: Venezuelan gaita and llanero music, Colombian Barranquilla carnival and vallenato, Brazilian capoeira and forró, Haitian twoubadou, Caribbean carnival and steelpan, Mexican folklórico among Apopka's farmworker families, Ecuadorian and Peruvian dance, Vietnamese Tết, and Indian classical dance and garba.

Two older layers persist. In Osceola County, a Florida Cracker cattle culture still rides square dances on horseback (Quadrille) at the Silver Spurs Rodeo. In Eatonville, one of the oldest Black-incorporated towns in the US, the ZORA! Festival honors Zora Neale Hurston, who recorded her neighbors' songs there with Alan Lomax in 1935.

A distinctive Orlando twist is that the theme parks imported tradition bearers. Disney, SeaWorld and Epcot hired hula dancers, a zheng soloist and a Mexican folk ensemble, several of whom later won the state's Folk Heritage Award. Most participation happens at classes, church halls, restaurants and festival lawns rather than in pubs. There is no single "folk neighborhood": the Puerto Rican and Latin scene clusters in Kissimmee/Buenaventura Lakes, south and east Orlando and MetroWest, while the Scottish, Greek and Irish scenes are in the northern suburbs (Winter Springs, Maitland, Apopka, Ocoee).

### In a nutshell for a visiting fiddler

- **Tuesday:** watch or join a beginner bomba percussion or dance class at Boggy Creek Elementary in Kissimmee (6 pm basic, 7 pm intermediate) during the school's 5-week courses ([Puerto Rico es Bomba](https://www.puertoricoesbomba.com/service-page/clases-de-baile-kissimmee-orlando)).
- **Monday:** sit in on Rosie O'Grady's Highlanders' pipe band practice in Apopka ([Instagram, May 2025](https://www.instagram.com/p/DJNTlNtxyDJ/)).
- **Wednesday:** a free marinera class in east Orlando.
- **Friday:** a capoeira music-and-roda night in east Orlando, or, from October to January, Venezuelan gaita at Nando Grill.
- **Saturday:** Barranquilla carnival classes in the afternoon, forró in MetroWest at 7:30 pm, then Haitian twoubadou at Kalalou on John Young Parkway from 9 pm.
- **Best seasons:** January (SanSe, Highland Games, Three Kings, ZORA!), February (Silver Spurs Rodeo, Dominican Independence, Tết), late May (Orlando Carnival and its steelpan jamboree) and December (parrandas).

There is no recurring Irish-session-style jam among the heritage traditions. The fiddle-adjacent heritage is mostly historical: cow-camp fiddlers of the cattle drives and Puerto Rican cuatro lutherie.

### Puerto Rican traditions

#### Scale and migration
The migration to Central Florida started with Disney's opening in 1971 and never stopped. It spiked after Hurricane Maria in September 2017. In 2018 alone the Puerto Rican population of Orange and Osceola rose 12.5% (about 37,000 people), with Osceola up 22% ([Orlando Sentinel, 2019](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2019/09/27/census-bureau-puerto-rican-population-in-orange-osceola-jumps-125-after-hurricane-maria/)). By 2016 about one in four Osceola residents was already of Puerto Rican origin ([El Sentinel, 2017](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2017/12/26/a-son-de-plena-boricuas-paralizaron-un-walmart-en-kissimmee/)).

#### Bomba: one school, deep roots (active)
The **Escuela de Bomba y Plena Tata Cepeda** was founded in Central Florida in 2018 by Barbara Liz Cepeda (Ortiz). She is the eldest daughter of Margarita "Tata" Cepeda and great-granddaughter of Don Rafael Cepeda, patriarch of the best-known bomba family in Puerto Rico ([state grant application](https://files.floridados.gov/media/707652/traditional-arts-98500.pdf)). WUSF called it the first school in Central Florida dedicated to bomba and plena ([WUSF, 2021](https://www.wusf.org/arts-culture/2021-01-14/one-dance-teacher-goes-virtual-and-brings-bomba-and-plena-to-a-new-stage)).

- **Classes:** in 2026 the school ran Tuesday courses at Boggy Creek Elementary School in Kissimmee. Percussion meets in the library and dance in the cafeteria, in 5-week blocks (Feb 3-Mar 3 and Mar 31-Apr 28). It costs $125 per course for adults; drums are supplied, and dancers buy their own skirts.
- **Public events:** its June 2023 grant application plans four quarterly "Toques de Bomba/Bombazos" open to all at the *batey*, three workshops, and a free annual multicultural drum show, **La Magia de los Tambores**. The 2025 edition doubled as a *Parranda Navideña* on Dec 13 ([Facebook event](https://www.facebook.com/events/afrotainment/la-magia-de-los-tambores-parranda-navide%C3%B1a/25462498283389183/)).
- **Reach:** the application projected 3,837 people engaged in a year.
- **COVID:** Liz Cepeda moved classes to her living room and started a Saturday Zoom book study on Afro-Puerto Rican history that drew participants from the island too.
- **Performing group:** Kalindá appeared at SanSe Orlando 2026 and with Mexico Lindo at community events.

#### Plena and parrandas (active)
**Plenamar Latino** was founded in Puerto Rico in 2005 and is now Orlando-based ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/plenamar.latino.94/)). It made national news when its *asalto navideño* at the Vine Street Walmart in Kissimmee went viral in December 2017, three months after Maria. Five musicians with *panderos*, *güiro* and a trumpet got shoppers singing "Dame la mano paloma", and the video passed 2 million views. Member Jimmy Castro said they had done the same the previous year ([El Sentinel](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2017/12/26/a-son-de-plena-boricuas-paralizaron-un-walmart-en-kissimmee/)). The group was still on the SanSe Orlando bill in January 2026 ([Telemundo 31](https://www.telemundo31.com/noticias/local/guia-de-las-sanse-2026-en-orlando-esto-debes-saber-sobre-la-fiesta-de-la-diaspora/2393444/)).

A newer group, **Proyecto Plena**, registered as an LLC in January 2024 and plays parties in Florida and Puerto Rico ([registry](https://www.flcompanyregistry.com/companies/proyecto-plena-llc/)). Parranda season, roughly mid-November through Three Kings Day, is well documented every year. In 2025 it included a senior-center "Gran Parranda Navideña" in Kissimmee (Nov 14) and the Tata Cepeda school's parranda (Dec 13). Colombians hold their own "parranda vallenata" at Aguardiente restaurant ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DR-QnghkUPm/)).

#### Música jíbara, cuatro and trova (niche)
Given the size of the community, the jíbaro string tradition is surprisingly thin in the public record.

- **Cuatro lessons:** New World Music School of Orlando in Winter Park offers Puerto Rican cuatro lessons and $25 all-levels workshops led by string teacher Tito García ([school](https://newworldmusicschool.com/cuatro-lessons/)). The Latin Music Institute, also in Winter Park, teaches Puerto Rican cuatro alongside the Venezuelan cuatro and Cuban tres ([Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/the-latin-music-institute-winter-park)).
- **Trova:** trovadores trade improvised *décimas* "pico a pico" at Puerto Rican restaurants rather than at a standing society. Examples: Edgardo Rivera and Félix Nieves, "el Caballero de la Trova", at El Chinchorreo de My Friend, Antojos Boricuas 2 and Pa'l Callejón on Apr 5-6 2025 ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/edgardoriveratrovador/posts/sabado-y-domingo-de-trovadores-en-kissimmee-y-orlando-estar%C3%A9-en-tarima-en-pico-a/1230539545747545/)), and a "2do Evento de Trovadores" at Antojos Boricuas in Kissimmee on Dec 22 2024.

I found no Orlando trovador association, cuatro orchestra, or regular *seis* jam. That gap is worth checking with the community (see curiosities).

The cuatro's greatest living maker lives just over the line. **Diomedes Matos**, the "master's master" of the cuatro and a 2006 NEA National Heritage Fellow, is listed in Deltona (Volusia County, outside the MSA). He built cuatros for Yomo Toro and an instrument for Paul Simon's *The Capeman* ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/diomedes-matos)). In Winter Garden, Puerto Rican-born guitar maker **Manuel Velázquez** (2004 Florida Folk Heritage Award) built classical and flamenco guitars played by Segovia, Julian Bream and Sabicas ([FFHA](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/manuel-velazquez/)).

#### Mundillo lace and vejigante masks (active / niche)
Puerto Rican bobbin lace has the strongest state recognition of any Orlando tradition.

- **Aida Etchegoyen** moved from San Juan to Poinciana in 1988. She became "matriarch" of a group of Orlando-area lacemakers who met monthly at her home, and won the 2006 Florida Folk Heritage Award ([FFHA](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/aida-etchegoyen/)).
- Her apprentice **Aida Rodríguez** of Winter Garden later took apprentices of her own in 2012 and 2016 ([apprenticeship roster](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/apprenticeship-program/apprenticeship-participants/)).
- **Julia Belén Alverio** trained in Moca, "the cradle of mundillo". She founded **Las Damas del Mundillo Orlando**, whose dozen-plus members meet weekly in her converted-garage studio, and won the 2024 Folk Heritage Award ([FFHA](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/julia-bel%C3%A9n-alverio/)). The group demonstrated at the 2025 Florida Folk Festival.

**Lilly Carrasquillo** of Ocoee makes *vejigante* masks and has taught folk arts to migrant children through Orange County Public Schools since 1987. She shared the 2017 award with Mexican folk-dance educator Rey David Peñaflor ([FFHA](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/david-pe%C3%B1aflor-and-marilia-carrasquillo/)).

#### Parades and festivals (thriving)
- **Florida Puerto Rican Parade & Festival** (organization founded September 2016) marches through downtown Orlando every April. 2026 (Apr 25) marked its 10th anniversary and honored Yabucoa ([site](https://floridapuertoricanparade.org/)).
- **Kissimmee parade:** Osceola's own parade began in July 2011 and reached its 10th year in downtown Kissimmee on Sept 24 2023 ([El Osceola Star](https://www.elosceolastar.com/desfile-puertorriqueno-de-la-florida/)).
- **La SanSe Takes Orlando** recreates Old San Juan's Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián at the Central Florida Fairgrounds. It expected 10,000+ people on Jan 24 2026, with plena from Plenamar Latino and Kalindá ([Telemundo 31](https://www.telemundo31.com/noticias/local/guia-de-las-sanse-2026-en-orlando-esto-debes-saber-sobre-la-fiesta-de-la-diaspora/2393444/)). An Orlando SanSe was already running in January 2018.
- **Three Kings Day:** the Osceola Chamber holds a celebration in Old Town Kissimmee (Jan 11 2026, with camels), and the Alianza Center runs a separate evening event ([Positively Osceola](https://www.positivelyosceola.com/osceola-chambers-three-kings-day-brings-culture-joy-and-community-together-at-old-town-brought-xto-you-by-experience-kissimmee/)).
- **Pan-Hispanic festivals:** Viva Osceola (October, about 32 years old) and Fiesta Calle Orange (October, 27th year in 2025) are big, mostly commercial stages. They are worth noting because they book local folk troupes such as Mexico Lindo ([Osceola Chamber](https://www.theosceolachamber.com/post/viva-osceola-celebrates-hispanic-heritage-and-the-osceola-community); [Downtown Orlando](https://www.downtownorlando.com/Fun/Events/Fiesta-Calle-Orange)).

### Other Latin American diasporas

#### Venezuelan: gaita (active), joropo (niche)

#### Mexican and Central American (active)
**Mexico Lindo Orlando** was founded in 2015 by Gizela Maldonado, a veterinary pathologist who danced with the UNAM folklórico ballet. It celebrated its 10th anniversary in 2025. - **Dominican:** the Festival Independencia Dominicana fills Southport Community Park each February (Feb 23 2025, Feb 22 2026) and draws on merengue típico ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/festivaldominicanoorl/)). No perico ripiao ensembles or classes surfaced.

### Caribbean and Afro-Atlantic traditions

#### Haitian: twoubadou and kompa (active); rara not found
Orlando's Haitian community (Pine Hills, Apopka, south Orlando) has a weekly acoustic **twoubadou** night. Savalou Twoubadou played "every Saturday" at Kalalou Orlando Bar and Grill, 5160 S John Young Pkwy, from 9 pm with free entry, in spring 2025 ([Kalalou](https://www.kalalourestaurant.com/event-details-registration/savalou-twoubadou-every-saturday-2025-03-29-21-00)), and again in August 2025.

#### Caribbean carnival and steelpan (thriving / active)
**Orlando Carnival Downtown** began in 1987 as a "Carnival Party" organized by Johnson Gilbert with Gloria Chandler, Mark Modeste and Tina Stubbs ([AnydayGuide](https://anydayguide.com/festival/873-orlando-downtown-carnival)). It reached its 38th year in 2025, with a Memorial Day parade of mas bands from Tinker Field ([Caribbean American Passport](https://caribbeanamericanpassport.com/orlando-carnival-downtown/)). Its free kickoff, the **Pantastic Steelpan Jamboree**, puts 6-8 steel bands on Luminary Green (May 3 2025; May 2 2026) ([Downtown Orlando](https://www.downtownorlando.com/Fun/Events/Pantastic-Steelpan-Jamboree-25)). The home band is **New Generation Branches Steel Orchestra**, led by StClair Balthazar. It had been "training kids and teens for 25 years in Orlando" by 2012, rehearsing in an industrial yard off John Young Parkway ([Orlando Sentinel](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2012/06/19/steel-orchestra-is-all-about-the-kids-2/)). Pan has also entered the public schools: the Blankner School Steel Drum Band played the Orange County Public Schools district office in March 2026 ([OCPS](https://www.instagram.com/p/DVg5KqfoAC0/)).

**Junkanoo:** Lake Mary's DeVaughan Woodside apprenticed three times with Nassau master Percy "Vola" Francis and then taught Orlando apprentices as a master in 2017-18. His group, Junkanoo Near You, brings goatskin drums and cowbells to Orlando Carnival ([Florida Folklife](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/florida-folklife-40th-anniversary/the-stories-that-speak-for-us/devaughan-woodside/)).

### Native American and Seminole

The metro's main powwow is the **Thundering Spirit Family Pow Wow** at Renninger's Twin Markets in Mount Dora (Lake County). Its 23rd annual edition was Mar 6-8 2026, so it began about 2004. Admission was $10 a day in 2025, with Aztec dancers, flute players and hoop dancing ([ClickOrlando](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2025/02/24/celebrate-native-american-culture-at-mount-doras-thundering-spirit-family-pow-wow/); [PowWows.com](https://calendar.powwows.com/events/categories/pow-wows-in-florida/)). Just outside the MSA, the two-weekend Spirit of the Buffalo powwow in Auburndale (Polk) reached its 30th year in 2026.

The Seminole Tribe's reservations are in South Florida, and Seminole presence in the metro is now historical: county and town names (Osceola, Seminole; Apopka from "Ahapopka") and Seminole-life demonstrations at Osceola History's Pioneer Day ([Osceola History](https://osceolahistory.org/pioneer-day/)).

### African American heritage: Eatonville and the lined hymn

**Eatonville**, incorporated in 1887, was the home town of Zora Neale Hurston. In June 1935 she, Alan Lomax and Mary Elizabeth Barnicle recorded there for the Library of Congress, including the guitarist Gabriel Brown ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/2007660096/); [AFC Florida sampler](https://wwws.loc.gov/folklife/sampler/FLaudio.html)).

In 1987, N.Y. Nathiri, a third-generation Eatonville native, founded the Association to Preserve the Eatonville Community to fight a road-widening plan. In January 1990 it held the first Zora Neale Hurston Festival, with Alice Walker as keynote and about 10,000 visitors ([Orlando Sentinel, 1990](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/1990/01/07/her-eatonville-pride-led-to-hurston-festival/)). It drew 20,000 by 1992. The **ZORA! Festival** is now a season-long program: the 2026 outdoor festival ran Jan 30-Feb 1 alongside the 37th academic conference ([ZORA!](https://zorafestival.org/)).

The metro's most remarkable African American folk practice was documented in the state apprenticeship rolls. **Deacon Troy Demps** of Mt. Pleasant Missionary Baptist Church lined out hymns for more than 50 years. He "raised" a line of "What a friend we have in Jesus" and the congregation answered unaccompanied, its "voices glide and swoop", stretching two syllables over six or more ([Robert Stone essay](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/apprenticeship-program/apprenticeship-publications/troy-demps-african-american-hymn-liner/)). He served as master artist six times between 1995 and 2009, training at least eight Orlando men, and won the 2003 Folk Heritage Award. Yahoo filmed him at 86 in 2013. I found no later evidence, so the tradition is rated *fading*.

Sacred steel, Florida's other great Black church tradition, centers on Fort Pierce. I found no Orlando congregation.

### Florida Cracker cattle culture

Osceola County was cattle country long before it was Disney's back lot, and its ranch culture is the metro's oldest living tradition with participatory dance. In May 1940 young ranchers gathered for a barbecue on Geech and Connie Partin's ranch and began riding **Quadrille**, a square dance on horseback, at one another's ranches. After they rode in Gov. Spessard Holland's 1941 inaugural parade they formed the **Silver Spurs Club**. Its first rodeo, in March 1944 in the Partin pasture, charged a war bond for admission ([Silver Spurs: Quadrille](https://www.silverspursrodeo.com/rodeo-101/quadrille); [history](https://www.silverspursrodeo.com/history-of-silver-spurs-rodeo)).

Today the club has 300+ members and an 8,300-seat indoor arena (2003). Peewee, junior and adult Quadrille teams practice weekly, and the February 2026 rodeo sold out. Pete Clemons won the rodeo's all-around title nine times (2009 Folk Heritage Award), and saddle maker Mike Wilder makes its prize saddles (2008 award).

The **music** of Cracker cattle culture is now historical:
- **Clayton Raulerson** (1906-2000) of Narcoossee said every cattle team driving from Lake Okeechobee to the Orlando area carried a fiddler, because the fiddle "was the only thing back then we had that you could move around." He later repaired fiddles for Nashville players and built Stradivari-pattern fiddles (1999 FFHA, [bio](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/clayton-raulerson/)).
- **Richard Seaman** (1904-2002), born in an orange grove near Kissimmee Park, learned from grove fiddlers who played for "hours of square dancing" (2001 FFHA, [bio](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/richard-seaman/)).

Osceola History's free **Pioneer Day** at the relocated-building Pioneer Village at Shingle Creek (33rd on Nov 8 2025; 34th on Jan 30 2027) is the main public stage for that heritage today. It has live music, crafts and Seminole demonstrations ([Osceola News-Gazette](https://www.aroundosceola.com/news-photo-gallery/step-back-time-pioneer-day-2025)). Turpentine and citrus-camp music is historic only; its documentation (Hurston, Lomax) is mostly from Polk County and the Everglades towns outside the MSA.

### Surprises

1. A sold-out rodeo grew from a **square dance on horseback** (1940), and the Quadrille teams still practice weekly.
2. **Theme parks as folk importers:** Disney (hula, 1971), SeaWorld (hula, 1983) and Epcot (zheng, Mexican folk ensemble) brought in tradition bearers who later won state folk awards.
3. **A 2026 NEA Heritage Fellow in Ocoee:** Giovanni Hidalgo. The cuatro's "master's master" lives one county north.
4. **Rafael Cepeda's great-granddaughter** teaches bomba in an elementary-school cafeteria in Kissimmee on Tuesday nights.
5. **A Walmart parranda went viral** three months after Hurricane Maria.
6. **Orange County is the #2 Puerto Rican county in the US by population and Osceola is #1 by share**, yet jíbaro trova lives mainly in restaurant afternoons.
7. **Orlando was the state's hymn-lining apprenticeship hub**, with at least eight apprentices under one deacon.
8. **Gaita keeps Maracaibo's season** in exile: Fridays only from October to January.
9. **Cracker cattle drives always had a fiddler**, according to Clayton Raulerson.

## Part IV. History, people, festivals, archives and the fiddle angle

*Thread: history_festivals. Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford MSA (Lake, Orange, Osceola, Seminole counties). Researched Oct 30, 2026. Items outside the four counties are flagged.*

### The short version

Orlando's folk history is better documented than its present-day folk scene. In three rural spots inside today's metro, the Library of Congress and the WPA caught three very different musical worlds between 1935 and 1940:

- the Black town of **Eatonville**, through Zora Neale Hurston;
- a **Slovak Lutheran colony at Slavia**, near Oviedo;
- a **cowhunter string band with fiddles at a ranch in Kenansville**, in Osceola County.

After the war the region turned into the world capital of *staged* folk-flavored entertainment:

- Church Street Station's banjo, bluegrass and country rooms (1974-2001), including a saloon that broadcast country music to the nation every week;
- Disney's Hoop-Dee-Doo Revue (since 1974);
- Epcot's mariachi, taiko and bagpipe residencies (from 1982).

The participatory scene lives in the gaps: a volunteer folk society that has run a monthly concert series since 1995, a thin ring of Sunday-afternoon Irish and old-time sessions, and an annual pilgrimage north to White Springs and Barberville for festivals. No fiddle contest and no bluegrass or folk festival operating inside the four counties could be verified for 2024-2026. The two that existed (Kissimmee Bluegrass, 1977 to about 2016, and the Lake County Folk Festival, 1998 to 2022) are gone.

### Timeline

#### Before 1900: Seminole country, cattle and citrus

The 1823 Treaty of Moultrie Creek put most of central Florida, including the future Orlando, inside a Seminole reservation. The Second Seminole War and removal followed. The Armed Occupation Act of 1842 opened the land to white settlers, and Orlando was laid out in 1857 ([Wikipedia: Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida)).

No Seminole or Creek music recordings tied to the metro turned up. The Library of Congress's Seminole material, such as Harry Jumper's 1954 songs, comes from the South Florida reservations to which the Seminole were pushed ([AFC Florida finding aid](http://international.loc.gov/folklife/guides/Florida.html)). In the metro, Seminole music is a matter of place names (Seminole County, Osceola County, Lake Apopka, Kissimmee), not a living local practice.

The settler economy that followed was cattle and citrus, and both had fiddlers. **Richard Seaman** (1904-2002), born in an orange grove near Kissimmee Park, remembered local fiddlers at community gatherings that "would result in hours of square dancing" ([Florida Folk Heritage Award bio](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/richard-seaman/)).

**Clayton Raulerson** (1906-2000) of Narcoossee, Osceola County, drove cattle from Lake Okeechobee to the Orlando area as a young man. "In those days, all cattle teams had a fiddle player who kept the teams entertained at night around the camp fire," and he was that fiddler. The fiddle "was the only thing back then we had that you could move around." He later became a master fiddle maker and received a 1999 Florida Folk Heritage Award ([bio](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/clayton-raulerson/)). This is the clearest first-hand evidence of a Florida *cracker cow-camp fiddle* tradition in the metro.

#### 1887-1911: Black towns and a Slovak colony

**Eatonville** was incorporated on August 15, 1887, six miles north of Orlando, as one of America's first self-governing Black towns ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eatonville,_Florida)). The town and its festival often bill it as *the* oldest. Wikipedia notes that Brooklyn, Illinois (1873) is older. Sanford had its own Black town, **Goldsboro**, incorporated in 1891 and annexed by Sanford in 1911 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsboro,_Florida)).

In 1911 Lutheran Slovaks from Cleveland founded **Slavia**, on SR 426 at the edge of Oviedo ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavia,_Florida)). Slavia matters a great deal for the archive story below.

#### 1933-1940: Hurston, Lomax and the WPA

**Zora Neale Hurston** grew up in Eatonville and made it the center of American folklore collecting in the 1930s.

- **1933.** Working with Rollins College professors Edwin Osgood Grover and Robert Wunsch, she staged *From Sun to Sun: A Day in a Railroad Camp* in Winter Park, with an all-Black Eatonville cast singing work songs and performing dances before a white-only audience. Wunsch had argued to the college president that there was "no better way to introduce the students to the honest-to-the-soil material at their own doorsteps than to present to them in a program of folk songs and dancers a group of Eatonville negroes, headed by Zora Hurston" ([Rollins blog citing the Rollins archives](https://blogs.rollins.edu/pathwaytodiversity/2018/12/20/from-sun-to-sun-zora-neale-hurston-and-rollins/)).
- **1935.** *Mules and Men* was published, drawing on Eatonville and on the Loughman lumber and turpentine camp in Polk County ([PBS American Experience](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/swamp-zora-neale-hurston-and-polk-county-blues/)).
- **June 1935.** Alan Lomax, Hurston and Mary Elizabeth Barnicle recorded **104 songs on 21 discs in Eatonville**. Performers included Laura Alexander, Gabriel Brown, Hattie Fitzgerald, John and Rochelle French, Rev. Haynes, Pa Henry, Elder Sykes Jones, Henrietta Marshall, a Methodist congregation, and children playing singing games ([AFC finding aid](http://international.loc.gov/folklife/guides/Florida.html)). Gabriel Brown's "John Henry", "a long blues instrumental, with only one verse of the ballad sung", is online ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/ihas.200196392/)), and there are about 30 Lomax photographs of Eatonville from that June ([e.g.](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007660101/)).
- **1939.** Hurston, now on the Federal Writers' Project, sang her Eatonville repertoire for Herbert Halpert and Stetson Kennedy in Jacksonville on June 18. That included "Mule on the Mount", which she "originally learned … from George Thomas in Eatonville," and the jook song "Uncle Bud" ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000008/)).

The WPA team also worked *inside* today's metro, and querying the Library of Congress's [Florida Folklife from the WPA Collections](https://www.loc.gov/collections/florida-folklife-from-the-works-progress-administration/) by county gives a precise picture:

- **Seminole County, 30 items, all from Slavia.** On August 31 and September 1, 1939, Alton C. Morris recorded the youth chorus of St. Luke's Lutheran Church singing Slovak folk songs ("Orala, orala", "Kuklienka, kde si bola"), a Slovak Easter song, and the Slovak and Czech national anthems. Pastor Stephen M. Tuhy introduced the singers: the Jakubcin, Mertán, Duda, Mikler and Stanko families ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000283/)).
- **Osceola County, 11 items, all from Caruja Ranch, Kenansville, July 1, 1940.** Carita Doggett Corse and Robert Cornwall recorded Bob Hall, Walter Van Bass, Ned Hugh Bass and J. C. King on "banjo, guitar, and violins". They played the instrumentals **"Shear 'Em"** and **"Pastime Annie"** and sang "On the Old Kissimmee Prairie" and "Give Me a Ranch Down in Florida" ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000364/)). At the same session the Black singers **James Brown and Rufus Bland** recorded spirituals and the jook song **"Uncle Bud"**, the same song Hurston had sung from the Eatonville tradition. James Brown Jr. sang "Hurricane", a ballad of the 1926 Miami storm, with the Bass brothers on banjo and guitar ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000371/), [LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000365/)).
- **Orange and Lake counties: no items** by location facet. The Eatonville material is in the 1935 Lomax collection and Hurston's Jacksonville session.

So the metro's only documented pre-war fiddle band is a ranch string band in what is still cattle country south of St. Cloud. It was recorded in a mixed Black and white session that is unusual for the Jim Crow South.

#### 1944-1953: rodeo and the Kissimmee Kid

The **Silver Spurs Rodeo** started in Kissimmee on July 4, 1944 as a war-bond benefit on land given by rancher Henry O. Partin. It became the official state rodeo in 1994. Osceola County schools still close for "Rodeo Day" instead of Presidents' Day ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Spurs_Rodeo)).

**Vassar Clements** (1928-2005) was born in Kinard and raised in Kissimmee. He taught himself fiddle at seven and formed a string band with his cousins Red and Gerald. At 21, in 1949, he took a bus to Nashville and replaced Chubby Wise in Bill Monroe's Blue Grass Boys ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_Clements); [BluegrassBios](https://bluegrassbios.com/clements-vassar/)). The nickname stuck as a tune title: "Kissimmee Kid".

The **Florida Folk Festival** began in 1953 at White Springs, outside the metro ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Folk_Festival)). It became Central Florida's annual folk pilgrimage. Central Florida Folk still cancels its May concert so members can go ("See You at the Florida Folk Festival") ([CFF](https://cffolk.org/concerts/concerts.htm)).

#### The 1960s: the Carrera Room and Winter Park's folk revival

The Great Folk Scare reached Orlando through Winter Park and Rollins College. Pete and Barbara Hodgin turned their diner at 534 S. Park Ave into the **Carrera Room**, a folk coffeehouse open Fridays and Saturdays from 9 pm to 1 am, with coffee and frappés for 40-65¢ and drawings of Joan Baez and Pete Seeger on the menu. When rents rose they moved it to 643 N. Orange Ave by Rollins ([Winter Park Magazine, "Fun and Folkie"](https://winterparkmag.com/2018/10/04/fun-and-folkie/)).

It was "the epicenter of Central Florida's folk music scene in the '60s":

- **Gamble Rogers** (1937-1991), Winter Park-born son of the architect James Gamble Rogers II, was the house hero.
- **Gram Parsons** played there with his folk quartet, the Shilos.
- A 17-year-old **Bernie Leadon**, later of the Flying Burrito Brothers and the Eagles, played banjo there.
- **Fred Neil** came through.
- In the summer of 1965 Rogers and Alan Stowell persuaded Rollins to sponsor **Don Reno and the Tennessee Cut-ups**, followed by a jam at Rogers' house until the small hours.
- Orlando teenager **Jeanie Fitchen** was paid $8 a night to host the open mic at 14 and first played the Florida Folk Festival at 15, in 1966. She received a Florida Folk Heritage Award in 2001 ([bio](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/eugenia-sisinni-fitchen/)).
- Lakeland's Rick Norcross named his own coffeehouse the "Other Room" as a tribute.

The Carrera Room closed with the restaurant in **1970**. Rogers went on to headline White Springs, appear in *Heartworn Highways*, receive a Florida Folk Heritage Award (1993), and die in 1991 in the Flagler Beach surf trying to save a stranger ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamble_Rogers)).

#### 1971-2001: Disney, Church Street and staged folk

Walt Disney World opened in 1971, and the region's music economy became a tourism economy. Over the next three decades much of the "folk" a visitor heard in Orlando was performed for pay in themed rooms.

- **Rosie O'Grady's Good Time Emporium** opened July 19, 1974 (one source says 1972). Bob Snow built it into **Church Street Station** with Dixieland bands, banjo and can-can ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Street_Station)). Its piper Jamie Holton later co-founded Off Kilter.
- **Apple Annie's Courtyard** opened in July 1976 as the folk and bluegrass room. Its house band **Mother's Choice** (Sam and Susan Barnes, Ron Cole, Charlie Lesink and Jeff Pattishall) played on the 1982 LP *Sights and Sounds of Church Street Station*. Their Disney monorail parody of "Charlie on the M.T.A." survives as an MP3 on Orlando Memory ([Orlando Signal](https://orlandosignal.com/news/apple-annies/); [Orlando Memory](https://orlandomemory.org/organizations/mothers-choice/)).
- The **Cheyenne Saloon & Opera House** opened May 28, 1982 with house band the Cheyenne Stampede. From March 10, 1984 The Nashville Network filmed a weekly "Church Street Station" concert series there (1984-87 and 1989-92) with George Jones, Tammy Wynette, Roy Clark, and a pre-fame Garth Brooks, Alan Jackson and Travis Tritt. In 1985 Church Street drew **1.7 million visitors**, behind only Disney, SeaWorld and Busch Gardens in Florida ([Orlando Signal](https://orlandosignal.com/orlando-history/cheyenne-saloon-opera-house/)).
- **Disney's Hoop-Dee-Doo Musical Revue**, a country and vaudeville dinner show at Fort Wilderness's Pioneer Hall, started as a summer stopgap in June 1974 and went full-time on September 5, 1974. It turned 50 in 2024 ([WDWNT](https://wdwnt.com/2024/07/original-hoop-dee-doo-cast-reunites-50th/)).
- **Epcot** opened in October 1982 with **Mariachi Cobre** (founded in Tucson in 1971) on the Mexico pavilion steps, and they are still there in 2026 ([Disney by Mark](https://disneybymark.com/2026/09/mariachi-cobre-kidcot-station-move-to-new-locations-in-mexico-pavilion-at-epcot/)). Taiko drummers started at Japan in 1983, as **Matsuriza** from 1988 ([WDWMagic](https://www.wdwmagic.com/attractions/matsuriza/news/15mar2020-matsuriza-drummers-final-performance-today-at-epcot.htm)). The bagpipe-led **Off Kilter** debuted at Canada on July 1, 1997 and played there for 17 years ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off_Kilter_(band))).

Snow sold out in 1988-89. Disney's Pleasure Island and later Universal's CityWalk copied the one-ticket club-hop model, and Church Street faded. Most of its rooms were shut by 2001. The Cheyenne briefly reopened in 2008-09 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Street_Station)).

#### 1977-2000s: bluegrass, Highland Games and the folk-society era

- **1977:** first **Kissimmee Bluegrass Festival** (details under Festivals).
- **c. 1978-79:** first **Central Florida Scottish Highland Games** in Winter Springs. The 2026 games were billed as the 48th ([Orlando Sentinel](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2026/01/14/central-florida-scottish-highland-games-return-this-weekend-for-48th-year/)).
- **1981:** the **Florida State Fiddlers Association** held its first convention and state contest. Central Florida fiddlers start appearing on its winners list in 1990.
- **1990:** first **ZORA! Festival** in Eatonville.
- **1995:** two volunteer-run singer-songwriter series began: **"Live @ Leu"** at Harry P. Leu Gardens (from July 1995) and the **Langford Park Folk Concert Series** in Winter Park (October-March seasons). Both were documented show by show by volunteer Stuart Hall ([Leu archive](https://cffolk.org/leu_gardens/performer_archive_list/past_leu_gardens_shows.htm); [Langford archive](https://sites.google.com/site/susiecoolsongs/central-florida-folk/langford-park-archives)). They were the core of **Central Florida Folk, Inc.**, a 501(c)(3) incorporated in May 2001 ([CFF info](https://sites.google.com/site/susiecoolsongs/central-florida-folk/cffinfo)).
- **1996:** **Fiddler's Green Irish Pub** opened in Winter Park ([Orange Observer](https://www.orangeobserver.com/news/2016/nov/24/fiddlers-green-celebrates-20-years-winter-park/)).
- **1998:** first **Lake County Folk Festival** in Eustis.
- **2000:** **Timucua Arts Foundation** began house concerts in a SoDo home ([Orange County](https://newsroom.ocfl.net/2025/05/timucua-arts-foundation-celebrates-25-years-of-bringing-art-to-life/)).

#### 1980s-2020s: Puerto Rican Orlando

Since the early 1980s metro Orlando has absorbed a large Puerto Rican migration, from the island and from the Northeast ([Southern Spaces](https://southernspaces.org/2014/puerto-ricans-live-free-race-language-and-orlandos-contested-soundscape/)). By 2015 about **400,000** Puerto Ricans lived in the Orlando area ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/08/25/us/central-florida-emerges-as-mainland-magnet-for-puerto-ricans.html)). Buenaventura Lakes in Osceola County was 44.5% Puerto Rican in 2010, and Kissimmee is nicknamed "Little Puerto Rico". Hurricane Maria in 2017 drove a second surge.

In this slice the clearest traces are honors:

- **Giovanni Hidalgo**, the conga master, lives in Ocoee and was named a **2026 NEA National Heritage Fellow** ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/giovanni-hidalgo)).
- Cuatro maker **Diomedes Matos** (NEA Fellow 2006) lives in Deltona, just outside the MSA ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/diomedes-matos)).
- Luthier **Manuel Velazquez** of Winter Garden received a 2004 Florida Folk Heritage Award.
- Mundillo lacemaker **Julia Belén Alverio** received a 2024 Florida Folk Heritage Award.

Living bomba, plena and música jíbara practice belongs to the heritage thread.

#### 2020-2026: COVID and after

- Matsuriza played its last Epcot show on **March 15, 2020**, the day Disney closed.
- The Central Florida Folk Song Circle moved to Zoom.
- The state fiddle contest returned in person only in 2022 ([FSFA](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/florida-fiddle-contest-preregistration/official-florida-state-fiddle-contest-winners-by-year/)).
- The **Lake County Folk Festival** did not come back; organizers announced its end in January 2022.

Since then:

- Central Florida Folk concerts have resettled at the **Winter Park Public Library**, booked into 2027.
- Timucua marked 25 years and its 1,000th show in 2025 ([Orlando Weekly](https://www.orlandoweekly.com/music/orlandos-timucua-arts-foundation-celebrates-their-landmark-1000th-house-show-29903333/)).
- Mariachi Cobre was moved to a new spot in the Mexico pavilion in September 2026.
- The **Barberville Fall Country Jamboree** (just outside the metro) celebrates its 50th in November 2026.

### Festivals and camps

**Inside the MSA**

- **ZORA! Festival**, Eatonville. Founded 1990; active.
  - Presented by the Association to Preserve the Eatonville Community. It has grown from a weekend into a season running from Hurston's birthday (January 7) to October.
  - The 2026 Outdoor Festival of the Arts ran January 30 to February 1 on East Kennedy Boulevard, with tickets at $20-$100 ([ZORA!](https://zorafestival.org/zora-outdoor-festival-events/); [Orlando Weekly](https://www.orlandoweekly.com/arts/zora-festivals-outdoor-festival-of-the-arts-is-the-place-to-be-in-eatonville-all-weekend/)).
  - Its music is soul, R&B and gospel (2026: a Frankie Beverly tribute by Maze's Jubu Smith, plus Brick), with a line-dance day. Folk content is in the humanities programming rather than the bandstand.
  - A secondary source puts the 1990 attendance at more than 10,000 (not confirmed).
- **Central Florida Scottish Highland Games**, Central Winds Park, Winter Springs. Since about 1979; held in January; "thousands" attend ([flascot.com](https://flascot.com/); [WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/thousands-expected-2026-central-florida-highland-games-winter-springs/QPOYXEK4IVB33PRM6QVY46OUHU/)). Pipe bands and Highland dance. No fiddle competition was confirmed.
- **Kissimmee Bluegrass Festival**, Silver Spurs Rodeo grounds. Founded 1977; probably defunct.
  - A Kiwanis Club fundraiser. The first year lost $26,000.
  - By 1993 (the 16th) it had about 25 professional acts (the Lewis Family, Chubby Wise, Bill Harrell) and over 6,000 expected fans. Organizer Tom Rex called it "the kickoff of the nine-month bluegrass festival season," and said "most of the fans are accomplished bluegrass musicians themselves," jamming in the RV field ([Orlando Sentinel 1993](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/1993/03/05/fiddle-fest-bluegrass-plays-again-in-kissimmee/)).
  - It was still running in 2006 at Heritage Park, and a "Kissimmee Bluegrass Festival" was reviewed in December 2016 ([Bluegrass Today](https://bluegrasstoday.com/review-of-kissimmee-bluegrass-festival/)). No 2024-26 evidence was found.
- **Lake County Folk Festival**, Eustis. 1998 to about 2021; ended.
  - Free, on porches, in tents and in downtown venues around Ferran Park. Founded by Jeff Friberg and run by Public Arts and Music, Inc.
  - Its 20th edition was in 2017 ([Visit Florida](https://www.visitflorida.com/listing/lake-county-folk-festival/28700/)). Organizers announced in January 2022 that it had "reached the end of its life cycle" ([site](https://lakecountyfolkfest.com/)).
  - Lake Eustis Folk carries on with 3rd-Saturday house concerts.
- **Celtic and St. Patrick's events.** The Crooked Can Celtic Festival in Winter Garden (March 13-15, 2026) and Raglan Road's St. Patrick's festival at Disney Springs. Secondary listings only, low confidence.
- **Mount Dora and Sanford:** no folk, bluegrass or fiddle festival found. Mount Dora's spring event is the Blueberry Festival. The Orlando Fringe was not investigated.

**Outside the MSA but treated as the scene's own**

- **Florida Folk Festival**, White Springs. Since 1953; Memorial Day weekend. About 300 performances and roughly 10,000-20,000 attendees. Hosts the **official Florida State Fiddle Contest**, held on May 23 in 2026.
- **Will McLean Music Festival**, Florida Sand Music Ranch, Brooksville. Since 1990; March. About 80 acts on three stages, an Old-Time Gathering jam tent, a Saturday contra and a youth program ([site](https://willmclean.com/festival)).
- **Barberville Pioneer Settlement Fall Country Jamboree**, west Volusia. 50th edition on November 7-8, 2026. Nine stages, 230+ performances, two jam tents, an old-time barn dance with live callers, and the children's "Lil' Abner Fiddle Hunt". The Spring Frolic is in April ([site](https://www.pioneersettlement.org/fall-country-jamboree)).
- **FSFA Florida Fiddlers Convention**, O'Leno State Park. October; the 45th was October 15-18, 2026, with guest artists Rafe Stefanini and Karen Celia Heil and $50-60 registration ([FSFA](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/2025-florida-fiddlers-convention/)). It is the state's de facto old-time camp.
- **Florida Old Time Music Championships** at the Stephen Foster park. Sponsored partly by Gold Tone (Titusville) ([FOTMC](https://www.fotmcinc.com/)).
- **Deltona Celtic Festival** (November) and the **Gamble Rogers Folk Festival** in St. Augustine (May). The latter is listed by Central Florida Folk as an "area folk festival".

### Fiddle contests and contest fiddlers

The Florida State Fiddle Contest started in 1981 at Santa Fe High School in Alachua. FSFA's own record says a 1989 bill made it the official state contest; one secondary source says 1990. State statute places it at the Stephen Foster Folk Culture Center ([FSFA](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/florida-fiddle-contest-preregistration/official-florida-state-fiddle-contest-winners-by-year/); [Fla. Stat. 15.048](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._15.048)).

- **Divisions:** Junior (12 and under), Youth (13-17), Twin, Open (formerly "Contemporary") and Old Time (formerly "Rustic"). Contestants play two tunes in four minutes, with acoustic string accompaniment only.
- **Where it is held:** **no fiddle contest operating inside the Orlando MSA was found.** The state contest sits about 170 miles north.

Orlando-area names on the winners list:

- Jason Thomas (Orlando): 2nd Contemporary and 1st Twin, 1990.
- Sharon McGinnis (Lake Mary): 3rd Rustic 1998, 2nd Rustic 1999.
- **Sharon Hartmann** (Winter Park): 1st Rustic and 2nd Twin, 2000.
- **Jonathan Hodge**: Contemporary champion in 2001 (from Ocoee) and 2009 (from Eustis); 2nd in Twin in 2026.
- Gaien Kaup (Winter Springs): 2nd Contemporary, 2002.
- **Lauren Mayeaux** (Orlando/Winter Park): Rustic placings in 2003 and 2008, Twin in 2004.

### The fiddle angle: tunes

- **"Kissimmee Kid"** (Vassar Clements). The one real fiddle tune named for a metro town. It is on *Old & In the Way* and Vassar's 1975 Mercury LP, and has a Traditional Tune Archive annotation ([TTA](https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Kissimmee_Kid)).
- **"Shear 'Em"** and **"Pastime Annie"**. Fiddle instrumentals recorded at Kenansville in 1940 ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000364/), [LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000366/)). They are well worth a transcription project for Nate's tune book, since they are the metro's only documented local fiddle repertoire. They come with the songs **"On the Old Kissimmee Prairie"** and **"Give Me a Ranch Down in Florida"**.
- **"The Trip to Orlando"**. A G-major jig by English musician John Boniface (2007), written "in anticipation of a work trip to Orlando" ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/6693)). The Session's "Orlando's Return" (a march) probably refers to a person, not the city.
- **"Orange Blossom Special"** (Ervin T. Rouse, 1938). Florida's great fiddle tune, but its story belongs to the Jacksonville terminal and Rouse's South Florida swamp life, not Orlando ([NCpedia](https://www.ncpedia.org/rouse-ervin-t)). The Orlando connection is that it was Chubby Wise's showstopper at the Kissimmee festival.
- **"Florida Blues"** (Fiddlin' Arthur Smith, 1937; also recorded by Vassar) and **"Florida Rag"** (attributed to John Hartford). Florida titles with no Orlando link ([TTA](https://tunearch.org/wiki/Florida_Blues)).
- **"Mule on the Mount"** and **"Uncle Bud"**. Eatonville songs in Hurston's repertoire. "Uncle Bud" also turns up at the Kenansville ranch session.
- **Not found:** tunes titled "Lake Monroe", "Sanford", "Eatonville", "Apopka" or "Seminole", on The Session or in the indexes checked. Note that tunearch and slippery-hill blocked direct searching.

### Character

Orlando's folk history is unusually **archival and theatrical**. The deepest roots are captured on 1930s acetate: Eatonville's work songs and singing games, Slavia's Slovak hymns, and Kenansville's cowhunter fiddles. The most famous chapters, meanwhile, were *staged for visitors*: Hurston's concert for a white Rollins audience, Apple Annie's bluegrass, a TV saloon, and Epcot's mariachi and bagpipes.

The **tourism economy** pays heritage musicians more steadily than almost anywhere, yet it has drained the participatory scene:

- Festivals tied to local clubs have died (Kissimmee Bluegrass, Lake County Folk).
- There is no fiddle contest in a metro of three million.
- The living scene is volunteer-run, ageing and suburban: library concerts in Winter Park, a song circle in a café, and house concerts.

Geography is **sprawl along the I-4 corridor**: sessions are scattered across Maitland, Apopka, College Park and Eustis rather than concentrated in one neighborhood. The big trad gatherings are **north**, in Barberville, White Springs, High Springs and Brooksville, and the scene treats a two-hour drive as normal.

Two demographic engines reshape it. **Retirees and transplants** (snowbird bluegrass, Celtic societies) and the **Puerto Rican and Latin American boom** (now the metro's heritage-music heavyweights, from Hidalgo to the cuatro makers). Cracker cattle culture survives in rodeo, not fiddling.

**Visiting fiddler (leads, verify first).** Orlando trad runs on **Sunday afternoons**: Irish sessions on the 1st Sunday (AnTobar, Maitland), 3rd Sunday (Stephie's, Apopka) and 4th Sunday (The Castle, Orlando), and an old-time jam on the 2nd Sunday (The Broken String), all 2-6 pm. There is also a Friday-night old-time house jam by email list.

- **Tuesday:** nothing verified in-metro. The nearest Tuesday Irish session is Meg O'Malley's in Melbourne, about an hour east.
- **Saturday:** the Eustis Cassia Community Club jams (bluegrass on the 3rd Saturday), or drive to Barberville for its 1st-Saturday old-time jam ([FSFA jam list](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/regular-jams/)).
- **November:** go to the Barberville Jamboree.
- **May:** go to White Springs, where the state contest and the whole of Central Florida Folk will be.
