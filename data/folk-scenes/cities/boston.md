# Boston-Cambridge-Newton, MA-NH: the folk scene

Boston-Cambridge-Newton MSA (5.03 million people): Essex, Middlesex, Norfolk, Plymouth and Suffolk counties (MA) plus
Rockingham and Strafford counties (NH). As of October 2026. Desk research in four threads (trad and dance, song and
singing, heritage, history) from organization pages, calendars, the BBU jam list, thesession.org, state folk-arts
records and Internet Archive copies. No audio or video was played.

## Scene in a nutshell

Greater Boston has one of the deepest, most institutional folk scenes in the country: about 390 participatory events a month in 160+ verified series. Irish music is the engine, with roughly 140 sessions a month, a big share at one family's pubs in Somerville and Cambridge (The Burren, McCarthy's, The Bebop), plus Comhaltas and the Irish Cultural Centre in Canton. Three other pillars stand beside it: contra and English dance (CDS Boston 1915, Pinewoods Camp, NEFFA 1944, BIDA, and the gender-free contra movement born in Jamaica Plain in 1988); Cape Breton and Scottish fiddling from the 'Boston States' migration, centred on Watertown's Canadian American Club; and the Cambridge listening-room line of Club 47 and Club Passim. Shape-note, chantey and pub sings, Berklee's roots program, WUMB, Lowell's free festival and living diaspora traditions (Cape Verdean, Armenian, Cambodian, Greek, Haitian) fill it out. It is old, volunteer-run and strung along the Red Line.

## 10 most interesting things

1. **One family's pubs carry a big share of the trad scene.** Fiddler Tommy McCarthy and Louise Costello opened The
   Burren in 1996 ([ITMA](https://www.itma.ie/people/tommy_mccarthy_louise_costello/)), then The Bebop and McCarthy's
   (opened April 2025, [CS Indie](https://csindie.com/the-music-runs-all-april-5-as-mccarthys-and-toad-celebrate-their-first-year-in-porter-square/)).
   Together they host about 80 sessions and jams a month in five traditions, and McCarthy's has an Irish session
   [every night of the year](https://www.mccarthystoad.com/calendar).
2. **Gender-free contra started in Jamaica Plain.** A "Gay & Lesbian Contra Dance" began at First Church JP in
   September 1988 with 12 dancers and became the Lavender Country & Folk Dancers
   ([LCFD](https://www.lcfd.org/lcfd-history.html)). Every major Boston contra now calls Larks and Robins.
3. **The dance society is from 1915 and its camp was a Girl Scout camp.** Cecil Sharp and Helen Storrow founded CDS
   Boston in spring 1915 ([CDS](https://www.cds-boston.org/history/organization-history/)). Pinewoods began as
   Storrow's Girl Scout camp on Long Pond in Plymouth, inside the MSA ([Pinewoods](https://pinewoods.org/about/history/)).
4. **One Watertown club hosts about 17 trad events a month in five traditions**, from Cape Breton Gaelic ceilidhs to
   Comhaltas céilís and a Québécois session: the [Canadian American Club](https://canadianamericanclub.com/events),
   chartered 1937.
5. **Boston has its own Cape Breton dance forms.** The "Boston Set" is a Cape Breton square set that took shape in
   Boston in the 1950s ([Mass Folk Arts](http://www.massfolkarts.org/object_detail.asp?objectid=8500255)), and the
   Mass Cultural Council recognizes a distinct Boston branch of Cape Breton step dance
   ([MCC](http://www.massfolkarts.org/search.asp)).
6. **Dudley Street's dance halls were a "marriage bureau".** Saxophones were standard in the 1920s–50s Irish bands
   ([Global Boston](https://globalboston.bc.edu/index.php/home/immigrant-places/roxbury/dudley-street/)). Joe Derrane
   quit the button box for about 40 years when the halls died, then won an NEA Heritage Fellowship (2004) after a 1993
   reissue ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/joe-derrane)).
7. **The Burren's Monday session descends unbroken** from the Village Coach House (Brookline, to 1987) and the Green
   Briar (Brighton, 1990–2019), whose first hour was for slower players
   ([Boston Irish](https://www.bostonirish.com/arts/2026/30-years-burren)).
8. **"Michael, Row the Boat Ashore" came through a Boston folk society.** Tony Saletan of the Folk Song Society of
   Greater Boston reconstructed it from an 1867 slave-song book in Harvard's Widener Library and taught it to Pete
   Seeger in 1954 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Saletan)).
9. **NEFFA was founded over coffee and ended up in a hotel.** It grew from a 1944 wartime Tuesday square dance at the
   Boston YWCA, and in 2023 moved into a Marlborough hotel because no eastern Massachusetts school would host it
   ([NEFFA](https://www.neffa.org/history-of-neffa/)).
10. **For a fiddler: the bowed strings go far beyond Celtic.** State folk-arts apprenticeships fund Carnatic violin
    nearly as often as Celtic fiddle, and list a Gandharva-caste Nepali sarangi master in Watertown and Cretan and
    Arabic violinists ([MCC](https://blog.massfolkarts.org/index.php/category/apprenticeships/)).

## Layer 1: Trad instrumental and dance

### Irish sessions

The core is a walk along the Red Line from Inman Square to Porter and Davis Squares, the route that the new Tunes on
the Charles festival used for its 2026 "session trail"
([Boston Irish](https://www.bostonirish.com/arts/2026/tunes-charles-scenes-festival)).

- **The Burren** (Davis Square): Monday 8:30 pm with Helena Delaney, Tommy Sheridan and Terry O'Shea, after a 6 pm
  back-room slow session; Friday 9:30 pm (Callum Bell's closed session); Saturday 9:30 pm; Sunday 3–6 pm with Laura
  Feddersen and Nathan Gourley ([calendar](https://www.burren.com/music.html)). A regular warns that Friday and Saturday
  are "amplified sessions in a very loud, very crowded bar". Go Monday or Sunday to play.
- **McCarthy's** (Porter Square): Irish session 7–10 pm every night and a Sunday noon Scottish brunch session.
- **The Bebop** (by Berklee): Friday 6–9 pm ([Bebop](https://www.thebebopboston.com/new-events)).
- **The Druid** (Inman Square), a nook for 8–10 players: Saturday 4 pm (confirmed 2026), plus Wednesday night and
  Sunday noon sessions last confirmed in 2024 ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/2919)).
- **Brendan Behan** (Jamaica Plain), Saturday 5:30 pm, revived in 2006 after noise complaints had silenced it
  ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/142)); **Scobie's** (Dorchester), Friday and Saturday 6 pm; **the
  Dooliner** (Newton), Tuesday 7 pm; **Powers Music School** (Belmont), a monthly learners' session.
- Suburbs: the Irish Cultural Centre in Canton (Friday, "for over 15 years",
  [ICC](https://www.irishculture.org/whats-on/sessions/)), the Snug in Hingham (Monday), O'Neill's of Salem (Friday to
  Sunday), the Bunratty in Reading (Saturday 1 pm), Backbeat Brewing in Beverly, and in New Hampshire Cara in Dover and
  Dwyer's in Portsmouth.

**Comhaltas.** The Reynolds-Hanafin-Cooley branch (1975 per the branch, 1976 per comhaltas.ie) has about 220 active
members and held its 50th gala on 10 October 2026
([Boston Irish](https://www.bostonirish.com/arts/2026/comhaltas-boston-branch-celebrates-50-years)). It runs Tuesday
set and céilí workshops ($7), a first-Sunday céilí with a live band ($10–15), a monthly seisiún and a music school
([CCÉ](https://cceboston.org/activities)). A New York fiddler at the 2026 festival recalled coming up "to play with
Larry Reynolds … Now there's a whole new generation". **Piping:** the Patsy Touhey Memorial Weekend runs each April at
the Can-Am Club (Joey Abarta, Michael Stribling).

### Scottish and Cape Breton

The Canadian American Club in Watertown is the hub. The **Boston Scottish Fiddle Club** (1981) holds a third-Wednesday
Scottish and Cape Breton session in its downstairs pub ($5), eight Sunday workshops a year and a weekly fiddle
orchestra under Neil Pearlman ([BSFC](https://www.bsfc.org/our-activities/wednesday-night-sessions/)). The **Cape
Breton Gaelic Club** ceilidh (third Sundays, 3:30 pm, $7) promises "live fiddle music, Gaelic singing, and probably a
square set too" ([Can-Am](https://canadianamericanclub.com/event/gaelic-club/2026-09-20)). Add McCarthy's Sunday
Scottish brunch, Cape Breton step classes with Jennifer Schoonover and Mary MacGillivray, and occasional dances with
players like Kimberley Fraser. In March 2026 the Globe asked, "Boston has a reputation as a hub for Irish music. What
about Cape Breton?" ([PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-boston-globe/20260313/282291031743228)).

### Old-time and bluegrass

The BBU [jam list](https://bbu.org/resources/jams/) is the guide. Old-time is a Sunday affair: Brookline at 1 pm
(Andrew Tanis; weekly or twice monthly, sources differ), the Burren at 6 pm (Alan Kaufman's long-running jam, once at
the Skellig) and the Bebop at 7 pm. Juliana Bernardi's second-Wednesday jam at Remnant Satellite is "VERY chill … everyone
has the opportunity to call a tune" ([BBU](https://bbu.org/event/remnant-old-time-jam/2026-09-09/)). Bluegrass is
bigger: **Bluegrass Tuesdays** at the Smoot Standard in Central Square runs four jams by level
([BBU](https://bbu.org/event/bluegrass-tuesdays-jam-at-russell-field-2/2026-09-01/)); the Burren has Frank Drake's
Saturday 2 pm jam; the Bebop has Wednesday and Sunday jams; Toad has an advanced first-Tuesday jam; and Handsome
Ladies is a women's jam in JP. Sandy's Music in Central Square hosted a weekly old-time jam for 25-plus years until
2013 ([Cambridge Day](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2013/01/12/sandys-music-shuts-with-founder-old-time-music-host-in-ill-health-benefit-concert-is-jan-22/)).
No standing old-time square-dance series was found.

### Klezmer, Balkan, Québécois, Cajun

A second-Wednesday **Québécois session** meets at the Can-Am Club. **Electric Hora** is a monthly klezmer dance at the
Boston Synagogue ([BFNJM](https://www.bostonjewishmusic.org/)), with occasional klezmer workshop-jams there and at
Boston Workers Circle. Toad has an open-band **Cajun** night on fifth Tuesdays. Balkan music lives inside the dances
below; no weekly Balkan jam was found.

### Contra

**The big three**, all gender-free: **NEFFA Thursday Contras** (weekly, Concord Scout House, $10–15,
[NEFFA](https://www.neffa.org/thursday-contras/)); **Monday Contras** (2nd, 4th and some 5th Mondays, same hall,
[site](https://mondaycontras.com/); NEFFA's FAQ says weekly); and **BIDA** (1st and 3rd Sunday evenings and 4th Sunday
afternoons at the Cambridge Masonic Hall in Porter Square, $5–20, [BIDA](https://www.bidadance.org/)). BIDA formed in
2008 to make contra intergenerational; its Beantown Stomp weekend sold out in 2026. The two flagship weekday series
share a scout hall 20 miles out, with "one of the best and biggest wooden dance floors in the region"
([Scout House](https://concordscouthouse.org/)).

Smaller series ([NEFFA FAQ](https://www.neffa.org/contra-dancing-in-greater-boston/)): the Boston Gender-Free Contra in
JP (two Saturdays a month), Roaring Jelly in Lexington, Contra Dance for a Cause in Arlington, Carlisle (with a potluck
supper), Groton Grange, and Seacoast Contra in Newfields NH. Facebook-only dances in Dover, Durham, Bridgewater and
Cape Ann are low confidence.

### English country dance

CDS Boston runs a weekly **Wednesday English** dance in Arlington with Bare Necessities and friends, a beginner-friendly
**Harvard Square** dance on 2nd and 4th Fridays ($5–15), monthly **ACE** (Advanced Challenging English) and summer
dances with ice cream in West Newton ([CDS](https://www.cds-boston.org/events/)). LCFD runs gender-free English in JP on
Tuesdays ([LCFD](https://www.lcfd.org/bgfe.html)), and caller-fiddler Cammy Kaynor leads Sunday dances in Malden.

### Céilí, set and Scottish country dance

CCÉ's Tuesday workshops and first-Sunday céilí are at the Can-Am; the ICC in Canton has Monday set dancing. **RSCDS
Boston** (1950) calls itself the Royal Scottish Country Dance Society's "first overseas branch"
([NEFFA](https://cgi.neffa.org/public/showperf.pl?P=1746)). Its Monday Cambridge class at the Can-Am has live music
every week ($10, [RSCDS](https://rscdsboston.org/class-cambridge.html)); other classes meet in Beverly, Stow,
Chelmsford, Bedford and Greenland NH, and the Somerville class dances gender-free in a science-fiction fan club's
library.

### International folk dance and Scandinavian

The **Folk Arts Center of New England** (1975) grew from Conny and Marianne Taylor's Cambridge YWCA classes of 1955–56
([FAC](https://www.facone.org/about-our-founders.html)). It runs Thursday and Friday dances in Arlington ($8–12), a
monthly live-band party in Belmont, Mostly Waltz in Concord, and **Balkan Music Night**, whose 37th edition (one listing
says 36th) filled Arts at the Armory in February 2026 with about 15 acts
([Armory](https://artsatthearmory.org/events/balkan-music-night-2/)). Its *Folk Dancing 'Round Boston* is the best dance
listing now that *The Dance Gypsy* is gone. **Tuesday Night Revival** (Balkan, 1st Saturdays in Waltham) keeps the name
of MIT's old Tuesday Balkan nights ([TNR](https://tuesdaynightrevival.com)), and the band **Zdravets** plays a monthly
Bulgarian party in the same hall. The ex-MIT Israeli dance meets Wednesdays in Waban; MIT's own folk dance club is still
"on hold since COVID" ([MIT FDC](https://mit.edu/activities/fdc/home.html)). **Scandia Boston** returned on 25 October
2026 "after a 6 year hiatus" ([FAC](https://www.facone.org/programs/scandinavian-sundays-dance.html)).

### Morris, sword and rapper

NEFFA's 2026 program is effectively the team census ([NEFFA](https://cgi.neffa.org/public/showperf.pl?INDEX=ALL)):
**Pinewoods Morris Men** (1964, "by all accounts, the oldest morris team in North America",
[site](https://www.pinewoodsmorris.org/)); **Muddy River Morris** (1975, historically a women's side, 30 members);
**Ha'Penny** (1978, Monday practices in Lexington); **Banbury Cross** ("North America's oldest youth Morris side");
Great Meadows and Hop Brook (youth, Sudbury); **WhistlePig** (queer-led Border morris); Mystic Garland; Mystic Valley;
and the rapper and longsword teams No Apologies, Charles River and Still River. Most don't publish practice times, so
only three are in the census.

## Layer 2: Song and listening

**Club Passim** is the emblem. Joyce Kalina (later Chopra) and Paula Kelley opened Club 47 on Mount Auburn Street in 1958
as a jazz club, and 17-year-old Joan Baez got Tuesdays, the jazz players' night off
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Passim)). The club moved to Palmer Street in 1963 (the city agreed to
number it "47"), closed in 1968, reopened as Passim in 1969 and went nonprofit in 1994
([MMONE](https://www.mmone.org/club-47-passim/)). It now presents "more than 400 shows per year to an audience of over
30,000" in a room of about 100–110 seats (medium confidence), at $27–35 plus $5 in fees
([Passim](https://www.passim.org/live-music/experience-passim/)). It also runs a School of Music (2000), BCMFest, the
holiday Campfire festival, Down Home Up Here (a bluegrass and old-time weekend whose 2026 workshops included
"Clawhammer for the Clueless") and the Brian O'Donovan Legacy Fund, six $2,500 grants a year for touring Celtic acts
([fund](https://www.passim.org/mission/grants/brian-odonovan-legacy-fund/)).

**Church coffeehouses** are the quiet backbone, all-volunteer and older than most commercial folk clubs:

| Coffeehouse | Where | Since | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Nameless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nameless_Coffeehouse) | First Parish, Harvard Sq | 1966 or 1967 | 2024–26 activity not verified |
| [Me&Thee](https://web.archive.org/web/20260527090919/https://www.meandthee.org/history-of-the-me-and-thee-coffeehouse/) | UU Church, Marblehead | 1970 | Twice monthly, Sept–May; 300+ seats |
| [Circle of Friends](https://www.circlefolk.org/) | Franklin | c.1990 | 37th season in 2026 |
| [Linden Tree](https://lindentreecoffeehouse.org/) | Temple Emmanuel, Wakefield | not found | Sunday 2 pm, $25 |

When the Me&Thee opened in 1970, its church board banned the word "coffeehouse" for fear of "beatniks"; admission was
50 cents, and Bill Staines played there from its first week. Other rooms: the Burren back room (the O'Donovan Legacy
Series), Tupelo Music Hall in Derry NH (700 seats, multi-genre, [Tupelo](https://www.tupelomusichall.com/about-us/)) and
the Spire Center in Plymouth. Framingham's Amazing Things Arts Center has become a youth arts hub; whether it still books
folk is unverified.

**The Folk Song Society of Greater Boston** has about 300 members, a quarterly *Folk Letter*, a library at the New
England Conservatory and monthly singing parties in members' homes, open to all
([FSSGB](https://web.archive.org/web/20250804234911/https://fssgb.org/)). Co-founder Molly Lynn Watt dates it to 1957
([In Memoriam](https://web.archive.org/web/20261012004729/https://fssgb.org/in-memoriam/)); other sources say 1958 or
1959. Its Woody Guthrie tribute, "Bound for Glory", has run since the 1970s.

**Open mics** are few: Passim's monthly open mic ($10, [Passim](https://www.passim.org/live-music/club-passim/openmic/))
and the Can-Am Club's Friday pub night. **House concerts: no standing series found.** The only trace was a one-off FSSGB
house concert by Franco-American singer Josée Vachon in 2025. That may be a data gap, since house concerts often
advertise by private mailing list.

## Layer 3: Traditional singing

**Sacred Harp.** About 5.8 free singings a month, all with loaner books and the new 2025 Denson edition
([Boston Sacred Harp](https://sites.google.com/site/bostonsacredharp/home/local-singings)):

| Singing | When | Where | /mo |
|---|---|---|---|
| JP / Brookline | Sat before 1st and 3rd Sun, 3 pm | Jamaica Pond bandstand; Brookline in winter | 2 |
| Charlestown | 2nd Sun, 3 pm | St. John's Episcopal | 1 |
| Norumbega | 2nd Mon, 7:30 pm | Church of the Redeemer, Chestnut Hill | 1 |
| Somerville | Sat before 4th Sun, 10:30 am | Nathan Tufts Park; church in winter | 1 |
| Ipswich | 3rd Sun, 2:30 pm | Our Lady of Hope | 0.8 |

The local twist is the **Norumbega Harmony**, founded at Wellesley College in 1976 by Stephen Marini, who learned at
Primitive Baptist singings in the South. It has 50-plus singers and its own tunebook of New England singing-school
music ([Norumbega](http://www.serpentpublications.org/~ijs/norumbega.html)). That repertoire goes back to Boston's
William Billings and his *New-England Psalm Singer* (1770). The Boston All-Day Singing was on 9 May 2026.

**Chantey and pub sings.** The volunteer site [Boston Song Sessions](https://www.bostonsongsessions.org/) lists free
chorus-song sessions, about eight a month: the Armory Pub Sing in Somerville (2nd Thursdays), an outdoor sing at the
Somerville Growing Center (1st and 3rd Tuesdays), the Boston Area Chantey & Maritime Sing (3rd Sundays, rotating), the
Belmont pub sing, the Violet Folk Sing at the Sea Hag in Cambridge, a Salem pub sing and the Port Session in
Newburyport. The Violet sing asks singers to change lyrics that "could hurt or exclude others"
([Violet](https://sites.google.com/view/violet-folk-sings/home)).

**Revels.** John Langstaff brought his Christmas Revels to Harvard's Sanders Theatre in 1971; the 2026 Midwinter Revels
is the 56th ([Revels](https://revels.org/about/)). It is invented ritual turned community practice: an amateur chorus,
morris dancers, and an audience made to sing. Cambridge seeded Revels in eight other cities.

**Gospel.** No Black gospel quartet circuit was documented, which is a research gap, not proof of absence. Visible
practice: *Black Nativity*, staged by the National Center of Afro-American Artists since about 1970 (56th season in
2025, [NCAAA](https://ncaaa.org/blacknativity/)); the city's Boston Gospel Fest (25th in 2025) and its new
intergenerational choir, CityWide City Voices ([UniteBoston](https://uniteboston.com/boston-gospel-fest-turns-25-a-night-of-unity-song-and-celebration/));
and Berklee's spirituals ensemble.

## Layer 4: Heritage and diaspora traditions

Three immigrant waves sit on top of each other, and the older ones never left. Most evidence is from the Mass Cultural
Council's folk-arts records ([Keepers of Tradition](http://www.massfolkarts.org/search.asp)) and community event pages.

| Tradition | Community | Vitality | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|

**The Canadian layer** matters most for a fiddler. Cape Breton and PEI immigrants came from the 1920s to the 1940s.
Fiddler John A. Campbell, who arrived in 1963, remembered playing "for square dances at the Canadian-American Club in
Watertown every Saturday night… Now, it's dropped off, even the Gaelic Club"
([MCC](http://www.massfolkarts.org/object_detail.asp?ObjectID=8130106)). By 2010 non-Cape Bretoners made up more of the
dance crowd ([Boston Irish](https://www.bostonirish.com/arts/it%E2%80%99s-transition-time-cape-bretoners)). The music
survives as a chosen tradition more than an inherited one. A "100,000 Cape Bretoners" figure is unverified.

**Lowell** is a heritage capital in miniature: a Franco-American Day committee since 1971, the second-largest Cambodian
community in the US (Angkor Dance Troupe turned 40 in 2026 with 7,000-plus students trained), a Southeast Asian Water
Festival, a Dia de Portugal parade (45th in 2025), an African festival and the free Lowell Folk Festival.

**Big crowds** come to the Caribbean Carnival (53rd in 2026, [Boston 25](https://www.boston25news.com/news/local/franklin-park-packed-brim-bostons-53rd-annual-caribbean-carnival/UXFHSAM3NREL7N5UYXRRPRF634/);
an 800,000 figure is unverified), the North End feasts (the Fisherman's Feast, whose "Flight of the Angel" lowers a girl
from a third-story window to the Madonna, and Saint Anthony's, with a 10-hour procession behind the Roma Band of Boston)
and the Puerto Rican Festival (since 1967). Cape Verdeans are about a quarter of Brockton, yet no batuku group was found
online. Jean Appolon teaches Haitian folkloric dance with live drums every Saturday in Cambridge. Belmont oud maker
Peter Kyvelos (NEA 2001) has built 175-plus ouds. The Native observance with the deepest roots is the National Day of
Mourning on Cole's Hill in Plymouth, held every Thanksgiving since 1970; the 56th went ahead in 2025 after the town
tried to restrict it ([WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2025/11/26/day-of-mourning-plymouth-restrictions-lawsuit)).

**Irish beyond the pub.** Step dance is the healthiest heritage practice by numbers: 29 CLRG schools statewide, most
in Greater Boston (the list may date from 2019). The Irish Cultural Centre in Canton sits on a 61-acre campus with Gaelic
games fields. The Boston Police Gaelic Column was founded in 1992 after a bomb-squad officer's funeral had to borrow New
York Transit Police pipers, because New England had no police pipe band at all
([BPGC](https://www.bostonpolicegaeliccolumn.com/about_us)).

**The newer waves.** ForróFest USA, run by the Os Forrozeiros dance group since 2017, bills itself as "the biggest forró
festival in North America", and Samba Bar in Somerville had live forró every Friday in 2025. Veronica Robles, who
trained with a mariachi in Mexico City's Plaza Garibaldi from age 15, runs the East Boston center behind Viva México
(about 10,000 people in 2024). The Haitian Flag Day parade runs from Mattapan Square down Blue Hill Avenue each May
([Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/05/18/metro/boston-haitian-flag-day-trump-mattapan/)). In
the older communities, Brookline's Panayotis League plays Kalymnian laouto and Cretan violin at Greek weddings, and
Watertown's Sayat Nova Dance Company lost its founder, Apo Ashjian, in May 2026 while new Gorani ethnographic dance
classes started nearby ([ACEC](https://www.acecwatertown.org/acec-and-hamazkayin-boston-launch-gorani-ethnographic-dance-classes)).

## History: from Increase Mather to McCarthy's

**Puritans and scholars (1684–1900).** In 1684 Increase Mather's *An Arrow Against Profane and Promiscuous Dancing* was
printed in Boston. It attacked mixed dancing, not dancing itself, and targeted a dancing master, Francis Stepney, who
was reportedly fined and left town (medium confidence; some sources date it 1685–86,
[UNL](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/etas/82/)). In 1770 William Billings published *The New-England Psalm Singer*. In
1812 the Boston-built USS *Constitution* gave the contra world "Hull's Victory". Irish and Maritime immigrants brought
Celtic music to house parties from the mid-19th century. At Harvard, Boston-born Francis James Child compiled the Child
Ballads (1882–98), and the Kerry fiddling Hanafin brothers arrived in the 1890s.

**Storrow and Dudley Street (1915–1943).** Cecil Sharp and Helen Storrow founded CDS Boston in 1915, and Storrow's
Plymouth camp became Pinewoods. In the 1920s the Celtic dance scene moved from the South End to Dudley Square in
Roxbury, at first dominated by Cape Breton and Acadian bands, then by Irish ones
([Global Boston](https://globalboston.bc.edu/index.php/home/immigrant-places/roxbury/dudley-street/)). Justus O'Byrne
DeWitt opened his House of Music there in 1926. The Canadian American Club formed in 1937.

**Ralph Page, NEFFA and the halls' peak (1943–1957).** In 1943 CDS hired New Hampshire caller Ralph Page for a Tuesday
square and contra dance (at the YWCA per NEFFA, the YMCA per Wikipedia). Over coffee after one of those dances in 1944,
Page, Mary Gillette and Grace Palmer planned the first New England Folk Festival, a wartime "recreation" that included
Wampanoag and Navajo music and spirituals but "nothing from Germany or Japan" ([NEFFA](https://www.neffa.org/history-of-neffa/)).
After the war Irish emigrants filled five halls around Dudley Square. In 1947–51 schoolboy Joe Derrane cut his
accordion 78s; in 1948 Copley Records and *Hillbilly at Harvard* began; Paddy Cronin arrived in 1949; and around 1950
Frank Segalini opened the Hillbilly Ranch (some sources say late 1950s). Cape Breton fiddler Bill Lamey and Galway
fiddler Larry Reynolds both arrived in 1953. Lamey ran dances at the Orange Hall in Brookline and brought Angus
Chisholm and Winston Fitzgerald to town ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Lamey)), and from 1956 to 1977
Herbie MacLeod's house in Arlington hosted taped Cape Breton sessions that Rounder later issued as *From Cape Breton to
Boston and Back*. In 1954 Tony Saletan found "Michael, Row the Boat Ashore" in Widener Library; in 1955 he was the first
person to appear on WGBH-TV.

**Club 47 and the end of the halls (1958–1969).** Club 47 opened in 1958 and launched Joan Baez; coffeehouses such as the
Unicorn and Cafe Yana followed; Dylan played between sets in 1961. By 1968 "the folk boom had run its course" and Club
47 closed. Dudley Street died first: "By 1960, the whole ballroom scene had died," Derrane said (others say the
mid-1960s), as Irish immigration fell, Roxbury changed and rock and roll arrived. Irish music retreated to pubs; Cape
Breton music to the Watertown hall the club bought in 1969, the year Passim opened.

**Rounder, Comhaltas and the contra renaissance (1970–1989).** Rounder (1970), the Me&Thee (1970) and the Revels (1971)
came first. Comhaltas Boston followed in 1975, with Larry Reynolds as chair and Séamus Connolly newly arrived, along with
the Folk Arts Center; the BBU and Norumbega Harmony in 1976. The late-1970s contra renaissance filled the Cambridge YWCA
and the Concord Scout House ($2 at the door in 1978, [Lenk](https://www.configular.com/DanceHistory/timeline_4.pdf)).
Then came the Klezmer Conservatory Band (1980), BSFC (1981), WUMB (1982), *A Celtic Sojourn* and the Joe Val festival
(1986), the National Folk Festival in Lowell (1987) and, in 1988, gender-free contra in JP.

**Institutions and closures (1990–2019).** Larry Reynolds restarted his Monday session at the Green Briar in 1990.
Passim went nonprofit and Derrane made his comeback in 1994. When his 78s were reissued on CD in 1993, critic Earle
Hitchner asked, "When did this guy die?"; at Wolf Trap in 1994 his grown children heard him play the button box for the
first time ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/joe-derrane)). The Burren opened in 1996; BCMFest (2004), BIDA
(2008) and Berklee's roots program (2009) followed. The losses: Rounder to Nashville (2010), Larry Reynolds (2012 or
2013), Sandy's Music (2013), Johnny D's (2016) and the Green Briar (2019). thesession.org holds about 60 dead Greater
Boston listings: Harrington's in Wakefield ended "after 14 years" in 2019, the Skellig in Waltham when the pub was sold
in 2014, and the Press Room in Portsmouth "after 30-something years" in 2022
([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/3520)).

**COVID and the rebound (2020–2026).** NEFFA went online for three years (its 2021 online festival still drew 2,000-plus),
Lowell cancelled twice and Revels missed its 50th. Pubs closed for good: Rí Rá and Beara in Portsmouth shut, Jalapeños
in Gloucester could not get its entertainment license back, and the Milton Club burned in 2021. Scandia Boston went dark
for six years. Scott Alarik and Bill Staines died four days apart in December 2021, and Brian O'Donovan in October
2023. The rebound has been pub-led: NEFFA returned in 2023 in a hotel; McCarthy's opened in April 2025 with a nightly
session; and 2026 brought the Burren's 30th, Joe Val's 40th, Tunes on the Charles, CCÉ's 50th and Scandia's return.
The year also took BBU co-founder Stan Zdonik and Newton-born caller Dudley Laufman.

**Who built it:** Helen Storrow (the camp), Ralph Page and Mary Gillette (the dance and festival), the Dudley Street
bandleaders and O'Byrne DeWitt, Bill Lamey (the Cape Breton bridge), Larry Reynolds (sessions and Comhaltas), Betsy
Siggins and the Donlins (the listening room), Rounder's founders, the Langstaffs, the Taylors, Ted Sannella, Chris
Ricciotti, Brian O'Donovan, and Tommy McCarthy and Louise Costello.

**Local styles.** The **Boston Irish dance-hall style** (1920s–60s) put Cape Breton, PEI, Acadian and Irish styles

([Global Boston](https://globalboston.bc.edu/index.php/home/immigrant-places/roxbury/dudley-street/)); hear it in

quadrilles, which the NEA calls New England's oldest continuous quadrille tradition (2026 status unchecked).
**Contests** are marginal: Lowell's Banjo & Fiddle Contest is the only one inside the MSA.

## Character

**What's distinct.** Continuity and stacking. CDS Boston (1915), Pinewoods, NEFFA (1944), *Hillbilly at Harvard* (1948),
FSSGB and Club 47, Revels, CCÉ and BBU all still run. Three traditions that live apart elsewhere share a city:
Anglo-collegiate contra and English dance, working-class Irish session music, and Cape Breton fiddling. Universities
(Harvard, BU, Tufts, MIT, BC, Berklee, NEC, UNH) are part of the ecosystem. **Signature:** Irish sessions and contra,
with Cape Breton third and Passim as the listening-room emblem.

**Who shows up.** Hard data is thin. CCÉ has about 220 active members, "building back" after COVID; the Cape Breton
community was graying by 2010; BIDA was founded to make contra intergenerational. Gender-free calling and
mask-required dances hint at a young, progressive dance crowd (an inference, not a survey).

**A visiting fiddler's Tuesday** (3 November 2026, a first Tuesday): Bluegrass Tuesdays at the Smoot Standard in Central
Square from 6:15 pm, then the Red Line to Porter Square for McCarthy's Irish session (7–10 pm) and Toad's advanced
first-Tuesday bluegrass jam next door. Alternatives: the Dooliner's 7 pm Irish session in Newton, CCÉ's set-dance
workshop at the Can-Am Club in Watertown (7:30 pm, $7) or the Somerville Growing Center pub sing.

**A visiting fiddler's Saturday:** Bunratty in Reading at 1 pm or the Burren's bluegrass jam at 2 pm, the Druid at
4 pm, the Brendan Behan at 5:30 pm, McCarthy's at 7 pm and the Burren at 9:30 pm (loud: better for listening). Singers
can catch Sacred Harp at Jamaica Pond at 3 pm on the right Saturdays; dancers have the JP gender-free contra, Balkan
dancing in Waltham or FAC's party in Belmont. Start with *Hillbilly at Harvard* on WHRB and end at Passim. On Halloween 2026 (a Saturday) the Burren back room had a
square dance.

**Costs.** Pub sessions, Sacred Harp and pub sings: free. Can-Am sessions $5–7, contra $5–20 (typically $15), English
dance $5–15, a CCÉ céilí $10–15, Passim $27–35 plus fees, Balkan Music Night $42. In 1978 the Scout House contra was $2.

**Geography.** The core runs along the Red Line: Harvard Square (Passim), Central (the Smoot), Porter (BIDA,
McCarthy's), Davis (the Burren), with Inman a walk away. A Maritime and Armenian belt lies west in Watertown, Belmont,
Arlington and Waltham; mainstream contra lives in Concord. The center moved from the South End to Dudley Square
(1920s–60s), Brookline and Brighton pubs (1970s–2019), then Somerville and Cambridge.

## Surprises (the rest)

- **thesession.org shows only about 17 active MSA sessions**, against the Irish Echo's eleven-plus on Saturdays alone.
- **The Child Ballads were compiled at Harvard** by a Boston-born literary scholar who cared little for the tunes.
- **Durham NH, home of UNH's dance archives, is inside the Boston MSA.**
- **Rounder started on $1,500** in a Somerville apartment; Rounder 0001 was a George Pegram banjo tape bought for $125.
- **About six free Sacred Harp singings a month**, one partly from Boston's own Norumbega Harmony.
- **Brian O'Donovan hosted A Celtic Sojourn for 37 years while running Foxboro Stadium.**
- **Scandinavian dance only restarted in October 2026** after six years, in New England's biggest international-dance
  scene.
- From the thread notes: New England had no police pipe band until 1992; the Newfoundland Club of New England is about
  dogs; and the region's old dance calendar, *The Dance Gypsy*, is now a flamenco blog.

## Data-quality notes

- Four threads were merged on 30 October 2026. Web search was heavily rate-limited, so many facts come from direct
  fetches of organization pages and Internet Archive copies, and some from search summaries (marked medium). Twelve
  duplicates were removed.
- 163 series add up to about 389 events a month, or about 191 counting only high-confidence entries. McCarthy's
  dominates the Irish count. Several pub sessions rest on one thesession comment or an undated page (O'Neill's, the Old
  Court, the Dooliner, the Druid). Facebook-only dances are low confidence. Few morris teams publish practices. Annual
  feasts count as 1/12 a month; two seasonal series were spread over 3 months.
- Sources disagree on CCÉ's founding (1975/76), Monday Contras' frequency, the JP gender-free Saturdays, the Brookline
  jam, the Nameless Coffeehouse (1966/67), the Hillbilly Ranch's opening, Larry Reynolds' death (2012/13), Johnny D's
  closing (2015/16), FSSGB's founding and Page's YWCA vs YMCA.
- Thin or unverified: house concerts (none found), the Cantab Lounge, the Nameless's current activity, Revels beyond
  December, the New Hampshire counties, North and South Shore suburbs, batuku and rara groups, 2020s state
  apprenticeships, the tune list (two tune archives unreachable) and NEA Fellows (checked by name only).
