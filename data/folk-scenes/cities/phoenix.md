# Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler, AZ: a folk-scene dossier

*Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler MSA (Maricopa and Pinal counties, Arizona; 5,228,938 people in 2025), as of October 2026. Merged from four web-research threads run on Oct 30, 2026 (trad and dance; song and singing; heritage; history), working mostly from organizer sites and calendars, the thesession.org API, tribal newspapers and Library of Congress catalog records. No audio or video was played.*

## 1. Scene in a nutshell

Phoenix is a huge metro with a small, scattered, seasonal trad scene and a rich heritage layer. About 105 participatory events a month were verified in 45 series, roughly a quarter of Boston's count. Bluegrass dominates (about 28 jams a month), mostly on weekday mornings in senior centers, 55+ communities and RV resorts; they swell with snowbirds from November to April and go quiet in the heat. There is one weekly Irish session (Saturday afternoons in a Chandler strip-mall pub), about two contras a month, morning English country dance, strong international folk dancing and one monthly Sacred Harp singing. The city-owned Irish Cultural Center downtown hosts nearly every Anglo-Celtic group. Fiddler's Dream (1987), a 49-seat volunteer listening room, anchors the song scene, alongside free municipal festivals. The Valley's own traditions are Indigenous and Mexican: O'odham waila ('chicken scratch'), Yaqui pascola violin and harp in Guadalupe, Piipaash bird singing, mariachi and ballet folklórico.

## 2. The 10 most interesting things

1. **One Irish session for 5.2 million people.** The fifth-largest US metro has exactly one active listing on thesession.org, Fibber Magee's in Chandler ([API](https://thesession.org/sessions/nearby?latlon=33.45,-112.07&radius=80&format=json)). Tucson, a fifth the size, has three. The archive holds nine dead Phoenix-area sessions since 2002.
2. **Football set the session's clock.** The survivor meets Saturdays 2–4pm because NFL Sundays kept bumping it. In 2013 a pub's NFL package evicted it outright; in 2020 it settled on Saturdays "so we won't have to change the session day when football starts" ([thesession #913](https://thesession.org/sessions/913)).
3. **Bluegrass outnumbers Irish about 5 to 1, and it happens before lunch.** Most of the ~28 monthly jams meet on weekday mornings in senior centers, retirement campuses and mobile-home resorts ([ABA calendar](https://arizonabluegrass.com/calendar?EventViewMode=1&EventListViewMode=1)). High season is winter, the reverse of northern cities.
4. **Waila is alive inside the metro.** Waila Style 87, formed in 2023 by players from Gila River, Salt River and the Tohono O'odham Nation, is named for State Route 87, the road they drive to gigs ([Gila River Indian News](https://www.gricnews.org/index.php/grin-articles/2025-articles/waila-style-87-brings-high-energy-performance-to-kalka-park)).
5. **A town that fines sketching.** Guadalupe, a one-square-mile Yaqui and Mexican town between Tempe and Phoenix, fines anyone who photographs, records or even draws its Lenten ceremonies, where pascola dancers dance to violin and harp ([Town of Guadalupe](https://www.guadalupeaz.org/1201/History-Traditions)).
6. **One State Balladeer for 60 years.** Dolan Ellis, a founding New Christy Minstrel, has held the post since 1966. His Arizona Folklore Preserve went silent in July 2026 when the University of Arizona moved to sell its building ([AFP](https://arizonafolklore.com/reservation/the-music-falls-silent-at-the-arizona-folklore-preserve/)).
7. **A state-fair tip founded a label.** Canyon Records, often called the oldest Native American label, began at a 1951 Arizona State Fair booth, where a Hopi jeweler next door suggested recording Hopi music ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_Records)). It later issued the first commercial recording of O'odham fiddle-band music ([Gu-Achi Fiddlers](https://canyonrecords.bandcamp.com/album/old-time-oodham-fiddle-music)).
8. **Fiddles tuned a whole step low.** The Gu-Achi O'odham fiddlers tuned about a whole step down, "probably" to make strings last, and one of their tunes is the "Hohokum Polka", named for the Phoenix basin's ancient people ([Lamancusa, *Fiddle Tunes of the American Southwest*](https://www.oldtimefiddletunes.net/pdf/SWTunesBook.pdf)).
9. **Cotton-camp field recordings exist.** In 1938–39 FSA worker Margaret Valiant recorded migrant ballads in Casa Grande, a yodeling family at a Coolidge co-op farm and a Mexican violin-and-guitar group in Tempe ([LOC guide](https://guides.loc.gov/arizona-folklife); [Tempe card](http://www.loc.gov/item/afc9999005.31062/)).
10. **An Irish castle hosts everyone.** The castle-styled Irish Cultural Center downtown hosts contra, English country dance, Balkan and Israeli dance, Scottish societies, a Bulgarian heritage group and an Irish collar-and-elbow wrestling club ([ICC affiliates](https://www.azirish.org/affiliates/)).

How to read these numbers:

- "Other" contains about eight Fiddler's Dream concerts a month. Those are listening events, so the participatory total is nearer 97.
- The bluegrass count is conservative: the weekly Beatitudes jam is counted once, as a folk open mic. It does include one unconfirmed Casa Grande jam (2 a month).
- Resident-only jams in RV resorts and 55+ communities are excluded. There are probably many.

**In perspective.** At about 105 events a month for 5.2 million people, Phoenix runs about 20 events per million residents per month, roughly a quarter of Boston's count. The mix matters more than the total. More than a quarter of all events are bluegrass jams, and seven of their ten series meet in daylight. Much of the Valley's most distinctive music (waila, pascola, bird singing, powwow) happens at annual or seasonal community events that barely move a monthly count.

## 4. Layer 1: Trad instrumental and dance

### Irish: one survivor and a graveyard

**Fibber Magee's, Chandler (Saturdays 2–4pm)** is the only weekly session. Its [thesession listing](https://thesession.org/sessions/913) reads like a 20-year diary. It began around 2004–05 as a Sunday slow session drawing "20-25 on any Sunday." An NFL package evicted it in 2013, and it swapped days with each football season until 2020. After a COVID pause it resumed in May 2021, and it was still weekly in September 2026 ([Signals AZ](https://www.signalsaz.com/events/performing-arts/irish-session-fibber-magees-3/)).

At the **Irish Cultural Center**, the free **First Friday Seisiún** runs during the art walk. The first hour (6–7pm) is a slow session, and the second is a session "hosted by Four Peaks Irish Arts" ([ICC](https://www.azirish.org/first-friday/); [dtphx](https://dtphx.org/do/first-friday-seisiun-1)). A second slow session precedes each Third Friday Céilí. Four Peaks Irish Arts is a Chandler nonprofit whose website has lapsed ([Cause IQ](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/four-peaks-irish-arts,800366021/)). It may be Arizona's Comhaltas branch, but that is medium-low confidence.

**The graveyard** (all on thesession.org):

- O'Connor's (c. 2002–04), where the owner preferred the jukebox ([#127](https://thesession.org/sessions/127)).
- Seamus McCaffrey's (2007–09); Lis Doon Varna, Peoria, and Monks, Chandler (both c. 2009).
- Robbie Fox's, Tempe (2010–13): "Fate conspires against Phoenician Traddies."
- Rosie McCaffrey's (to 2015), Fiddler's Dream (2016–17) and The Dubliner (2018–19). All three were hosted by fiddler Sheila "Fiddles" Maguire ([#128](https://thesession.org/sessions/128)).
- An Eloy park session that ran six days a week for a month in 2023, then posted: "This session is no more. Or if you lived in Eloy you might say no mas" ([#7987](https://thesession.org/sessions/7987)).

No weekly evening pub session exists in Phoenix proper.

### Old-time: a slow jam, and no fiddlers' chapter

The **Phoenix Old Time Music Society** (POTMS, "over 20 years") meets on Saturday mornings. The 1st and 3rd Saturday jams are at Glendale Foothills Library (10:30), the 2nd at North Mountain Visitor Center (10:00), and the 4th in the East Valley, except in summer ([POTMS](https://www.azoldtimejam.com/)). Everyone plays together, at moderate tempo, with no solos. The ~70-tune list mixes Appalachian standards with "Inisheer," "Farewell to Tarwathie," Shetland's "Da Slockit Light" and Canadian tunes ([tune list](https://www.azoldtimejam.com/music-jam-details)). Dulcimers, harps and rhythm bones are welcome. Think Pacific Northwest slow jam crossed with a dulcimer club. No hard-driving Round Peak-style jam turned up.

The **Arizona Old Time Fiddlers Association** (Payson, 1975) has chapters in Tucson, Yuma, Cottonwood and dormant Payson. It has **none in the Phoenix area**, though its mailing address is in Surprise ([chapters](https://arizonaoldtimefiddlers.org/chapters.htm)). Its form asks for summer and winter addresses and offers an "Over 80" dues tier.

### Bluegrass: the biggest thing going, mostly before noon

The **Arizona Bluegrass Association** (1981; PO box in Mesa) keeps the jam calendar ([calendar](https://arizonabluegrass.com/calendar?EventViewMode=1&EventListViewMode=1)). Weekly jams:

- Tempe's Pyle Adult Recreation Center, twice: Monday noon, and Friday 10am ("bluegrass instruments only").
- Scottsdale's Via Linda Senior Center, Wednesday 10am ("Bluegrass Acoustic Only (Harmonica Too)").
- Cave Creek's Mountain View Pub, Wednesday 5:30pm, the only one in a bar.

Smaller jams meet in a Knights of Columbus hall, a Mesa 55+ community and an Apache Junction mobile-home resort (BYOB). There is also a winter-only Sun Lakes country-club jam: "contact Bernie before heading out as sometimes gets cancelled." The ABA's old domain now serves Indonesian lottery spam.

Its **Bluegrass Roundup** at Apache Junction Rodeo Grounds (5th edition Feb 5–7, 2027) has a band contest, workshops, a beginner course with mandolinist Billy Parker and a free barn dance ([Roundup](https://aba18.wildapricot.org/roundup)). The youth counterweight is **Jam Pak**, started by Anni Beach in her Chandler front yard in 1994 with six children and homemade "canjos." It now has about 30 players ([VoyagePhoenix](https://voyagephoenix.com/interview/life-work-with-anni-beach-of-old-central-chandler/)).

### Scottish and Cape Breton

- **Scottish country dance.** **RSCDS Phoenix** dances Tuesdays 7–9pm from September to May, since Sept 2026 at Kaleidoscope Dance Space in Mesa. It costs $10, has certified teachers, and draws winter snowbird dancers ([RSCDS](http://www.rscdsphoenix.com/p/classes-2.html)).
- **Scottish or Cape Breton session:** none found.
- **Highland Games.** The **Phoenix Scottish Games** skipped their 60th edition in 2025 over costs, falling ticket sales and "lessening member resources." Whether they returned in 2026 is unverified ([Mesa Independent](https://www.yourvalley.net/mesa-independent/stories/phoenix-scottish-games-postpone-annual-event-until-2026,558948)).

### Contra

**Phoenix Traditional Music & Dance** (PHXtmd; 1983; CDSS affiliate; [about](https://phxtmd.org/about-us)) publishes its whole-year volunteer roster as a public Google Sheet. That makes every dance countable: about 20 a year, plus specials.

- **Regular dances:** Second Saturdays at St. Katherine Greek Orthodox Church in Chandler or the Phoenix Conservatory of Music, and Fourth Fridays at the Irish Cultural Center. Each night has a lesson at 6:30 and dancing 7–10. There is no July dance ([contra](https://phxtmd.org/contra-dance)).
- **Specials:** a Halloween dance in Mesa, a day-after-Thanksgiving "Burn the Calories" dance, and New Year's Eve.
- **Cost:** a $10–25 sliding scale; "our cost to put on a dance averages $17.50 per dancer."
- **Callers and bands.** Calling is gender-neutral. Callers include Peg Hesley, Christine Merryman, Suze St. John and Abbie Sorg; bands include Big Fun, Rave Review, Chakra, Wild Irish Fiddle, the Gilbert Town Fiddlers and Tucson's Cat Mountain Rounders.
- **Winter Frolic** (Jan 8–9, 2027): three contras plus ECD, with caller Jeremy Korr and the band Root System ([Frolic](https://phxtmd.org/winter-frolic-2027)). The Desert Spring weekend has been inactive since COVID.

Phoenix and Tucson share callers and bands. The Sharlot Hall Museum dates the state's contra revival to Tucson around 1975, with Phoenix "soon" after ([Sharlot Hall](https://archives.sharlothallmuseum.org/articles/days-past-articles/1/arizonas-latest-dance-craze-is-300-years-old)).

### English country, international, squares, clogging

- **English country dance.** **Phoenix English Country Dancers** (Peg Hesley) meet 9–11am on 2nd and 4th Saturdays, to recorded music, for $10 ([ECD](https://peghesley.com/english-country-dancing)). The morning start dodges the heat.
- **International folk dance.** **Phoenix International Folk Dancers** dance about 13 times a month ([PIFD](https://www.phxfolkdancers.org); [events](https://www.phxfolkdancers.org/special-events.html)).
  - Tuesday nights at the ICC (Wednesdays in Tempe in October 2026).
  - Thursday-morning Israeli and Friday-midday international sessions in Mesa.
  - An annual festival since about 1988.
  - Bulgarian teacher Dr. Anton Andonov most Januarys; the live Bulgarian band Bulgarika in November 2025.
  - Members report 20 to 50 years of dancing.
- **Modern Western squares.** The state association lists four clubs in 2026, one in the MSA (Sun Lakes) ([GCSDA](https://www.azsquaredance.us/club_list.html)). Mesa Checkmates still dances Wednesdays. Seven more clubs appear on Ceder.net without dates. The federation claims square dancing is "the State Dance of Arizona since 1990" by Gov. Rose Mofford's proclamation; it is not a statute ([AZ Federated](https://azsquaredancers.com/)).
- **Clogging.** The **Maricopa 4-H Cloggers** (1980) claim to be Arizona's longest-running clogging group.
- **Old-time squares.** The 2010s urban dances (Rhythm Room, Lost Leaf, Dusty Boots) left no trace after 2020. The best one now is the Roundup barn dance with **Pick & Holler** (Carolyn Camp, fiddle; Matt Minjares, banjo) and caller **Grace Rolland** ([ABA](https://aba18.wildapricot.org/Dance)).

### Klezmer, Balkan, Scandinavian, morris

- **Klezmer.** **Little Chef Klezmer Band** formed on a diner patio during First Friday in 2022. It has been resident band of the Phoenix Yiddish Culture Festival since 2024 ([LCKB](https://www.littlechefklezmer.com/about)). No klezmer jam exists.
- **Balkan.** Balkan dance lives in PIFD and the Bulgarian Heritage Center (2023).
- **Scandinavian:** nothing found.
- **Morris.** NAMDO lists **Fairhaven Morris** as active: Fieldtown, Border and Bampton, "a drinking team with a Morris Dancing problem," seen at the Arizona Renaissance Festival ([NAMDO](https://morrisdance.org/california-and-arizona/); [RenFest](https://www.renfest.org/directory/fairhaven-morris-dancers)). No practice schedule is public.

## 5. Layer 2: Song and listening

### Fiddler's Dream

[Fiddler's Dream](https://fiddlersdream.org/) (1702 E Glendale Ave, Sunnyslope) has been "all-volunteer and acoustic since 1987" ([FAQ](https://fiddlersdream.org/faqs/)). It has 49 seats, $10 admission, homemade coffee and no alcohol. It is "completely totally absolutely acoustic (except for our electric Yamaha piano)." In October 2026 its calendar ([calendar](https://fiddlersdream.org/calendar/)) had:

- a Thursday open stage for music, poetry and spoken word;
- about eight Friday and Saturday concerts, including a free matinee with Irish fiddler **Kate Rose**, guitarist Armand Ramirez and **Arvel Bird**;
- a monthly **Phoenix Folklore Friday** of "traditional stories and songs," hosted by Chrissy and Rachelle Dart ([event](https://fiddlersdream.org/events/phoenix-folklore-friday-the-family-corvidae/)).

It still requires KN95 masks for everyone but the performer, as inclusion for immunocompromised listeners.

### The Lon Austin network

Much of the song scene was built by one Phoenix Parks employee. In 1965 Lon Austin hitchhiked to San Francisco and found Faith Petric's Folk Music Club: "the decisive opening," a place "for people that were on every level of participation." He came home convinced "some programs should be free" ([petition](https://www.gopetition.com/petitions/induct-lon-austin-into-amehof.html); [Glendale Daily Planet](https://glendaledailyplanet.com/glendale-folk-heritage-festival-celebrates-years-p1732-96.htm)). What he started:

- **Glendale Folk & Heritage Festival** (c. 1989, first at Encanto Park). It is free. The 2026 edition, Feb 28–Mar 1 at Sahuaro Ranch Historic Park, had "hundreds of performers across seven stages" ([Glendale](https://www.glendaleaz.gov/News/Whats-New/Folk-Heritage-Festival-Returns-To-Downtown-Glendale)). Workshops cover spoons, saws, jug band and autoharp, and POTMS hosts a big jam.
- **Beaded Lizard Music Gathering** (c. 1990, first as the Encanto Park Coffee House). It meets Wednesdays at the Beatitudes retirement campus: an open jam 6–7pm, then a featured performer, free ([Sunnyslope Historical Society](https://sunnyslopehistoricalsociety.org/event/alisa-phoenix-featured-performer-at-the-beaded-lizard-music-gathering/)).
- **Monday Night Melodies** (1993), free concerts at Peoria's library, now twice monthly.
- **Arizona Songwriters Gathering** (1996). It runs one January Saturday at Glendale Main Library, with song-sharing circles and a teen stage ([library](https://library.glendaleaz.gov/Beyond-Books/Events-Programs/Arizona-Songwriters-Gathering)). The West Valley Country Music Association is among its sponsors; country and folk songwriting blur here.

### Songwriters, big rooms, a farm festival

- **Songwriter groups.** The **Arizona Songwriters Association** (1977) was founded on the Los Angeles Songwriters Showcase model ([Hall of Fame](https://www.azmusichalloffame.org/inductees/jon-iger/)). It runs a monthly Zoom critique. The **NSAI** chapter meets 3rd Tuesdays ([ASA](https://azsongwriters.org/)). No Seeger-style song circle was found.
- **MIM.** The Musical Instrument Museum has a 300-seat theater and books "more than 300 artists" a year ([MIM](https://mim.org/upcoming-concerts/about-mims-music-theater/)). Fall 2026 brought The High Kings (sold out), Judy Collins, Altan and John McEuen, at $44.50–97.50 ([events](https://mim.org/events/)). Its March **Experience Ireland** weekend puts local trad groups such as the Gilbert Town Fiddlers and Dé Máirt Ceòl on stage ([MIM](https://mim.org/events/experience-ireland-2026/2026-03-13/)).
- **Other rooms.** The Irish Cultural Center books occasional Irish concerts, such as Karan Casey in 2023 ([ICC](https://www.azirish.org/international-concerts/)). Bob Corritore's **Rhythm Room** (1991) keeps blues alive as a local tradition ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Corritore)).
- **Laveen Folk Festival.** Bea Graves's free festival puts five stages on a working dairy farm near South Mountain, "goats, geese" included. Full acoustic drum kits are banned and performers are unpaid. The 2026 dates are Nov 21–22 ([Desert Vibes](https://www.thedesertvibepodcast.com/post/laveen-folk-festival-the-free-family-friendly-folk-music-festival-on-a-working-farm-near-south-mou); [Undiscovered Music](https://undiscovered.music/venues/laveen_folk_festival)).
- **House concerts:** no standing series was found, only a duo offering to play living rooms ([Duo Ocotillo](https://www.duoocotillo.com/house-concerts)). This finding is low confidence, since house concerts hide on private lists.

## 6. Layer 3: Traditional singing

- **Sacred Harp.** **Fasola Phoenix** "normally meet[s] 11–1 the third Saturday of each month." The venue is shared by email, and the group has 61 Facebook followers ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/fasolaphoenix/)). The time comes from an undated page, but Tucson's site confirms 2026 joint sings with "Phoenix Fasola."
- **Halfway singings.** On fifth Saturdays the two cities meet halfway, at Central Lutheran Church in **Arizona City**, Pinal County. The 2026 dates were Jan 31, May 30, Aug 29 and Oct 24 ([Tucson Sacred Harp](https://www.tucsonfasola.org/)). Tucson is the region's center, with singing schools and the Elder Gerald Hand convention.
- **Shanties and ballads.** No shanty sing exists. MIM's monthly **World Choir Workshop** taught "Drunken Sailor" beside the Japanese fishermen's "Sōran Bushi" in Sept 2026 ([MIM](https://mim.org/events/world-choir-workshop-sea-shanties/)). Most residents hear shanties at the Arizona Renaissance Festival, which draws about 250,000 visitors a season ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Renaissance_Festival)). No ballad or unaccompanied-singing group was found.
- **Cowboy poetry and Western song**, Arizona's own folk song:
  - **Dolan Ellis** arrived in 1959 and played the Scottsdale coffeehouses. His own folk club, Dolan's, was "even hiring a young John Denver." He left the New Christy Minstrels in 1963 ([Hall of Fame](https://www.azmusichalloffame.org/inductees/dolan-ellis/)). In 1966 he became the nation's first Official State Balladeer, and ten governors have reappointed him. He has written 300+ Arizona songs and is alive in his 60th year in office ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolan_Ellis)).
  - **Marshall Trimble**, now state historian, toured with the folk trio Gin Mill Three in 1963 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Trimble)).
  - **Gatherings.** Wickenburg's **Cowboy Christmas Poetry Gathering** holds its 35th on Dec 5, 2026, for $30–40 ([Out Wickenburg Way](https://outwickenburgway.com/events/35th-annual-cowboy-christmas-poetry-gathering/)). Its ordinal implies 1992; an aggregator says 1988. Glendale's festival includes cowboy poetry, and Prescott's big gathering is outside the MSA.
- **Gospel.** The **Southwest Gospel Music Festival** at Mesa Arts Center (Jan 2026) brought Legacy Five and Triumphant Quartet to snowbird audiences at $39 a session ([IMC](https://imcconcerts.com/event/southwest-gospel-music-festival-2026/)). Bluegrass gospel is in the ABA's mission.
  - **Not covered:** RV-resort hymn sings are plausible but unlisted. Black gospel and LDS hymnody were not researched.

## 7. Layer 4: Heritage and diaspora

This is the richest layer. Four Native nations sit inside the metro's own counties: the Gila River Indian Community (Akimel O'odham and Pee Posh), the Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community (Onk Akimel O'odham and Xalychidom Piipaash), the Fort McDowell Yavapai Nation and the Ak-Chin Indian Community. The Tohono O'odham Nation's San Lucy District is also in Maricopa County. Add one of the country's largest urban Native populations, the Yaqui town of Guadalupe and a very large Mexican-American community, and you get traditions found almost nowhere else in a top-20 metro.

### Waila, the fiddle band's descendant

Waila (from Spanish *bailar*) is O'odham social-dance music: polka, chote (schottische), mazurka, two-step and now cumbia, danced counterclockwise as a smooth walking polka. It grew out of fiddle bands, and accordion and saxophone came in during the 1950s. Two of its three homelands, Salt River and Gila River, are inside this MSA ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_scratch)).

The best evidence that it is alive here is an Oct 2025 Gila River Indian News story ([GRIN](https://www.gricnews.org/index.php/grin-articles/2025-articles/waila-style-87-brings-high-energy-performance-to-kalka-park)).

- **The night.** **Waila Style 87** packed Kalka Park in Komatke for Native American Day.
- **The band.** It has eight members from Hashan Kehk, Salt River, the Tohono O'odham Nation, District 4 and Casa Blanca. Leader and accordionist Kirkland Chico: "We play country, Tohono and Waila. Waila is chicken scratch. Tohono is Mexican-style, like cumbia."
- **The lineage.** Chico names his teachers (Ronnie Juan, Archie Miguel, Arnold Paul and others) and legacy bands such as Pima Express, Cisco Band and The Molinas.
- **Still dancing.** Salt River held a free waila dance with the band on Feb 14, 2026 ([SRPMIC](https://srpmic-nsn.gov/event/waila-chicken-scratch-dance/)).

The contest stage is Gila River's **Mul-Chu-Tha** fair in Sacaton (since 1962). It began as a fundraiser for a swimming pool, and its battle of the bands is a key waila showcase ([site](https://mul-chu-tha.com/)). The 2025 fair was cancelled over "safety concerns within the Community" ([GRIN](https://www.gricnews.org/index.php/grin-articles/2025-articles/march-14-2025-articles/61st-annual-mul-chu-tha-fair--rodeo-cancelled)). A Facebook post found by search says it returned in 2026. Gertie Lopez's **Gertie and the T.O. Boyz** won that battle four times between 2012 and 2018 ([awards](https://www.gertieswailamusic.com/awards.html)). The band comes from Chuichu, in the Pinal County part of the Tohono O'odham Nation, and calls itself "the only waila band that travels with a violin" ([BorderLore](https://borderlore.org/woman-waila-gertie-lopez-plays-music-remember/)).

### O'odham fiddle bands

The ancestor of waila is the fiddle band: two violins, guitar, snare and bass drum. The Gu-Achi Fiddlers (Elliott Johnson and Lester Vavages, Tohono O'odham, outside the MSA) made its first commercial recording for Phoenix's Canyon Records ([Bandcamp](https://canyonrecords.bandcamp.com/album/old-time-oodham-fiddle-music)). Inside the MSA, St. John's Mission at Komatke taught Western instruments; a 1916 photo shows a "Girls' Musical Club" ([St. Olaf](https://pages.stolaf.edu/americanmusic/2021/11/01/st-johns-mission-or-a-tricky-research-process/)). No active Akimel O'odham fiddle band turned up in 2024–26 sources, so here the tradition is rated historic-only (low-medium confidence).

### Yoeme ceremony in Guadalupe

Yaqui refugees from Sonora settled Guadalupe around 1907 to dig canals, and the town incorporated in 1975. Lent and Easter are "the most important occasion for the Yaqui in Guadalupe" ([Town](https://www.guadalupeaz.org/1201/History-Traditions)). Pascola dancers step to violin and harp, in Jim Griffith's words "to the music of the harp and violin" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/yoeme.htm)). Societies such as the Kohtumbre Ya'ura and the Oficio Achalim (deer and pascola) carry the rites, which a Lenten Society captain dates to the early 1600s ([Pascua Yaqui](https://www.pascuayaqui-nsn.gov/culture/); [KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/2022-04-15/content-1772470-how-pascua-yaqui-guadalupe-celebrate-easter-400-year-old-tradition)). This is worship, not a concert. Some parts are open to respectful visitors, but cameras, phones, recorders, sketchbooks and alcohol are barred ([etiquette](https://www.pascuayaqui-nsn.gov/culture/visitor-etiquette/)). Leave the fiddle at home.

### Bird songs, powwows, hoop dance

- **Bird songs.** Piipaash bird songs are Yuman song cycles sung with gourd rattles, shared "from the Havasupai all the way down to Southern California." Since 2015 the **Piipaash Matasheevm** has gathered singers at Lehi each April and October. It grew from the "Gathering of the Gourds" potluck started by Earl and Carolyn Stacey ([OAN](https://oan.srpmic-nsn.gov/annual-fall-piipaash-matasheevm-celebrates-the-people/)). The Oct 3, 2026 edition had open singer rotations and an O'odham give-away dance ([SRPMIC](https://srpmic-nsn.gov/event/piipaash-matasheevm-community-social-2/)).
- **Powwows.** The **Phoenix Indian Center** (1947) calls itself the oldest American Indian nonprofit of its kind ([PIC](https://phxindcenter.org/history-2/)). Recurring powwows inside the MSA: ASU (37th, Apr 2026; [State Press](https://www.statepress.com/article/2026/04/asu-pow-wow)); Roy Track Memorial in Mesa (41st, Oct 2025); Red Mountain Eagle at Salt River ($35,000+ prizes; [powwows.com](https://calendar.powwows.com/events/red-mountain-eagle-pow-wow-2/)); Orme Dam Victory Days at Fort McDowell (45th, Nov 2026), marking the defeat of a dam that would have flooded the reservation ([FMYN](https://fmyn.org/event/orme-dam-victory-days-2025/)); Mul-Chu-Tha; and a Huhugam Heritage Center social.
- **Hoop dance.** The **World Championship Hoop Dance Contest** at the Heard draws 100+ dancers each February ([Heard](https://heard.org/event/world-championship-hoop-dance-contest/)). The Heard counts from 1991. Wikipedia says the contest began at the New Mexico State Fair in 1991 and moved to the Heard in 1992 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Championship_Hoop_Dance_Contest)).

### Mexican and Mexican-American

- **Mariachi.** **Rosie's House**, a free community music school, teaches three levels for grades 5–12 ([Rosie's House](https://rosieshouse.org/programs/afterschool/mariachi/)); **Marcos de Niza High** in Tempe has had three ensembles since 2017 ([MdN](https://sites.google.com/tuhsd.k12.az.us/mdn-fine-arts-academy/performing-arts/instrumental-music/mariachi)). Festivals run at Phoenix College (since 2024), in Chandler (27th, Nov 1, 2026) and at the Herberger Theater, which went all-female in 2025 ([Herberger](https://herbergertheater.org/events/2nd-annual-mariachi-folklorico-festival/)).
- **Folklórico.** Ballet Folklórico Quetzalli-AZ in Mesa takes students from age 3 to adult ([BFQ](https://www.bfq-az.org/)). Ballet Folklórico Esperanza holds its Día de los Muertos festival in a historic farm cemetery in Avondale ([BFE](https://balletfolkloricoesperanza.org/)).
- **Matachines.** Danzas Misión de Guadalupe, led by María Luisa Loeza from a Durango family of danzantes, dances at Phoenix churches every Dec 11–12. Unlike Guadalupe's violin-led Yaqui matachines, it is drum-led.
- **Día de los Muertos.** At least ten events fill about three weeks ([New Times](https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/dia-de-los-muertos-celebrations-phoenix-2026-40707467/)). Mesa Arts Center's 20th (with Lila Downs) and MIKIZTLI downtown, with its giant Las Flacas skeleton puppets, are the largest.
- **Son jarocho:** no fandango or jarana class was found.
- **Norteño:** not researched.

### European diasporas

- **Irish: thriving.** The ICC's Third Friday Céilí dances to the live band Dé Máirt Ceòl, costs $8 and runs 11 times a year ([ICC](https://www.azirish.org/iclf-programs/ceili-dancing/)). Step schools include Celtic Steps, led by Dublin-born accordionist Christopher McGrory. The St. Patrick's parade held its 43rd in 2026 ([parade](https://stpatricksdayphoenix.org/)).
- **Scottish: fading.** The Caledonian Society was organized in 1953 and held its first Highland Games in 1964 ([COSCA](https://www.cosca.scot/associations/caledonian-society-of-arizona)). The Games postponed their 60th in 2025, and the society's website was last updated in 2020.
- **Greek.** The Phoenix Greek Festival marked 65 years in October 2026. Eight age-graded dance groups ("Little Lambs" to young adults) dance to the live band Synthesi and teach the public ([festival](https://phoenixgreekfestival.org/)).
- **Bosnian.** The Bosnia & Herzegovina Cultural Center (2012) rehearses KUD Izvor folklore ensembles weekly ([BHKC](https://bhkcarizona.com/)). **KUD Sevdah** reports 30+ members and a group singing *ilahije* devotional songs; its page is undated.
- **Eastern European.** The 2nd Eastern European Folk Festival (May 2026) taught Polish, Serbian, Bulgarian and klezmer dance ([EEFF](https://eefolkfest.wixsite.com/phoenix)).
- **Polish and polka.** The Polish Festival imports troupes from Chicago, Las Vegas and LA ([site](https://polishfestivalaz.com/)). Pulaski Club Arizona hosts a polka fest ([IPA](https://www.ipapolkas.com/events/way-out-west-polka-fest-3/)). Each winter the Midwest's "Dutchman" circuit comes to Mesa. In March 2025 the Zweifel Brothers and Matt Hodek & The Dakota Dutchmen played a 55+ resort clubhouse for $15, BYOB.

### Other diasporas

- **Arab.** The **Arab American Festival** at Steele Indian School Park calls itself "the largest Arab event in the nation" ([site](https://arabamericanfestival.org/)). A Lebanese festival (24th in 2025) features dabke.
- **Japanese.** **Arizona Matsuri** (1984, free) has taiko by Fushicho Daiko and Ken Koshio. The Arizona Buddhist Temple's Obon has bon odori open to all.
- **Chinese.** Phoenix Chinese Week held its 36th fair in 2026. The Chinese Cultural Center was sold in 2019 and stripped of its Chinese architecture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Cultural_Center,_Phoenix)).
- **Pacific Islander.** The **Arizona Aloha Festival** (1995) runs two free March days at Tempe Beach Park. Its 2026 dates differ between sources by a day.
- **West African.** **Kawambe-Omowale**, active for 40 years, teaches Mande drum and dance twice a month at Eastlake Park.
- **Juneteenth.** Phoenix first held it in 1911, after a visit by Booker T. Washington. It has been at Eastlake Park since 1921 ([Cronkite](https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2022/06/20/juneteenth-holiday-celebration-eastlake-park-phoenix/)).
- **Garba.** Phoenix Garba Mela bills itself as Arizona's largest indoor garba.
- **Not found** (absence of evidence only): Filipino, Vietnamese, Hmong, Afghan, Somali, Congolese and Ethiopian music.
- **State support.** The Tucson-based Southwest Folklife Alliance runs the state's Master-Apprentice awards: about 100 masters since 2015. Phoenix-area awardees in 2025 were David Olarte (Afro-Latin salsa) and Deborah Brooks (Passamaquoddy basketry) ([AZ Arts](https://azarts.gov/news/announcing-the-2025-master-apprentice-artist-awardees/)).

## 8. Venues and organizations

**The hub is an Irish castle.** The Valley has no folk-society hall. - the Irish session, céilí and step dancers;
- PHXtmd's Fourth Friday contra and New Year's Eve dance;

- affiliates including Daughters of Scotia, the Bulgarian Heritage Center and the Guardians of St. Brigid, who revive collar-and-elbow jacket wrestling ([affiliates](https://www.azirish.org/affiliates/)).

For a metro with almost no pub-session culture, this one building does a lot of stabilizing.

A band page called its 2022 edition the "41st" ([Hillbilly Fever](https://hillbillyfever.com/portfolio/41st-annual-wickenburg-bluegrass-festival/)), against the Chamber's numbering, which implies 1980. Payson merged into the county fair in 2025 and lists 2026–27 as TBA, though a schedule PDF gives Oct 3, 2026. Tucson's coordinator warned that contest could end without a successor. No folk or fiddle camp was found in the MSA.

## 10. Instruction, media, shops and archives

**Instruction.**

- **Gilbert Town Fiddlers** (2012) is the most dynamic young institution ([GTF](https://www.gilberttownfiddlers.com/organization.html)). Students write all their own arrangements. It added a middle-school program in 2022 and a second high-school group in 2025, reaches "1,500+ students in one day" on school tours, co-produces the ASTA Arizona Fiddle Fest, has learned from Hanneke Cassel, Brittany Haas and The Fretless, and plays in the contra-band rotation. Co-founder Taylor Morris toured with Canada's Barrage ([bio](https://www.taylormorrismusic.com/about.html)).
- **Other youth programs.** Jam Pak teaches bluegrass. School mariachi and folklórico academies form the biggest youth pipeline, entirely separate from the Anglo trad scene.
- **Adult classes:** ICC slow sessions, RSCDS, PIFD's Israeli beginner class, 4-H clogging, Kawambe-Omowale's drum class, ABA workshops (e.g. "Bluegrass Soloing Made Easy," Tempe, Dec 9, 2026; [ABA](https://www.arizonabluegrass.com/event-6887754)), MIM's choir workshops and ASA's online critiques. Sacred Harp singing school exists only in Tucson.

**Radio and media.**

- **No dedicated folk show on Phoenix FM** (medium confidence).
- **KJZZ 91.5** airs Bob Corritore's *Those Lowdown Blues*, five hours on Sunday nights since 1984. It is the Valley's longest-running roots show ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Corritore)).
- **Streaming:** KRDP Indie's online morning mix includes Americana ([schedule](https://indie.listen2krdp.com/krdp-indie-schedule/)), and listeners stream Tucson's KXCI shows *American Traditions* and *The Bluegrass Show* ([KXCI](https://kxci.org/programs/american-traditions/)).
- **Canyon Records** (1951) is the great local label; its waila and O'odham fiddle albums are on [Bandcamp](https://canyonrecords.bandcamp.com/).
- **Print:** AOTFA's bimonthly *The Arizona Fiddler* ([Sept/Oct 2026](http://www.arizonaoldtimefiddlers.org/images/Sept%20October%202026.pdf)) and PHXtmd's quarterly *Arizona Folk News*.

**Shops and makers.**

- **Boogie Music** (Phoenix, since 1979) teaches violin, banjo and mandolin ([Boogie](https://www.boogiemusic.com/info/About-Us-12.html)).
- **Violin shops** (from a search summary only): Andrew's Fine Violins and Arizona Fine Violins in Mesa, and Valley Violins in Scottsdale ([Valley Violins](https://valleyviolinsaz.com/)).
- **Historic:** Ziggie's Music sold Duane Eddy his 1957 Gretsch.

**Archives.**

## 11. History

**Before 1900.**

- **Hohokam and missions.** The Hohokam farmed the Phoenix basin for about 2,000 years, until around 1450, and dug some 135 miles of canals that Anglo settlers later reused ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona)). Their music is known only from archaeology. From **1687** the Jesuit Eusebio Kino worked the Pimería Alta. Missionaries are credited with bringing violins and European dance tunes to the O'odham ([No Depression](https://nodepression.org/the-gu-achi-fiddlers-old-time-oodham-fiddle-music/)). That is the standard account; no Gila River-specific source was found.
- **The earliest Anglo dance.** In **1866** the territorial governor's wife described contras and squares in Prescott ([Sharlot Hall](https://archives.sharlothallmuseum.org/articles/days-past-articles/1/arizonas-latest-dance-craze-is-300-years-old)).
- **Settlers.** Phoenix was recognized in **1868**. Mormon settlers founded Lehi in 1877 and registered Mesa in **1878**, and they danced to fiddles. No early Mesa fiddler is named anywhere ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesa,_Arizona)).
- **Schools.** The Phoenix Indian School opened in **1891** and ran a marching band ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Indian_School)). St. John's Mission at Komatke followed in **1894**, and its band toured the East in 1922–23.

**1900–1945: fieldwork, dance halls, migrants.**

- **Guadalupe.** Yaqui refugees settled the town around **1907**.
- **Ethnographers.** Frank Russell documented Pima songs at Sacaton in **1901–02** ([Archive](https://archive.org/details/pimaindians01russgoog)). George Herzog recorded 116 cylinders there in **1929**, the same year Densmore's *Papago Music* appeared and the Heard Museum was founded.
- **Clay Ramsey.** He arrived from Texas in a Model T in **1924**. By the early 1930s his Old Timers played fiddle and guitar every morning on KOY. He bought the open-air **Orangewood Dance** in the orange groves for about $1,300. It had canvas flaps, stoves and no liquor. About 300 dancers came on Saturdays at 35¢, and the dance lasted "about thirty-five years." His baby son Floyd slept there in a basket ([interview](https://azmusicdude.com/clay-ramsey-interview/)).
- **Viola "Mom" Ruth.** She won state fiddle titles in 1926 and 1945 and taught square dance for the WPA to up to 900 pupils a week ([Heritage Tunes](https://heritagetunes.com/books/pioneer-western-folk-tunes/)).
- **Dust Bowl.** Buck Owens's family reached Mesa in **1937**.
- **Field recordings.** In **1938–39** Margaret Valiant recorded the cotton country. She caught the McGee family yodeling at the Casa Grande Valley Farms co-op, cowboy singer Cal Boies in Phoenix, a Típica orchestra at Pueblo Grande, a Tempe violin-and-guitar group playing "Rancho grande," and old ballads from Casa Grande migrants ([LOC](https://guides.loc.gov/arizona-folklife)). Russell Lee photographed the Agua Fria camp's "pianist of the camp orchestra" in 1940 ([LOC](http://www.loc.gov/item/2017786276/)).
- **War.** The **1942** Gila River incarceration camp held Japanese Americans on Gila River land.

**1945–1975: records, radio, twang and folk.**

- **Studios, labels, stars.** Buck and Britt played the Romo Buffet in **1945**. The Boleys opened Phoenix's first studio in **1948**, the year Ruth published *Pioneer Western Folk Tunes*. **Canyon Records** followed in **1951**, the year the Old Timer label cut square-dance 78s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_Records)). Glendale-born Marty Robbins hosted KPHO's *Western Caravan* in the early 1950s.
- **The Coolidge launchpad.** Little Coolidge's KCKY launched Lee Hazlewood, Duane Eddy (with a weekly show in **1957**) and Waylon Jennings. Eddy's twang hits were cut at Floyd Ramsey's studio ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duane_Eddy)).
- **Retirees arrive.** **1960**: Sun City opened to 100,000 opening-weekend visitors and seeded today's retiree jam geography ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_City,_Arizona)).
- **Native milestones.** **1962**: the first Mul-Chu-Tha fair, and Peter La Farge's "Ballad of Ira Hayes," about a Pima Marine from Sacaton ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Ira_Hayes)).
- **Folk coffeehouses.** Scottsdale's Mews held Wednesday hootenannies around **1963**, alongside the Lumber Mill and Foggy Mountain (forum memories, low-medium confidence; [city-data](https://www.city-data.com/forum/phoenix-area/2740881-1960s-folk-music-scene.html)).
- **JD's.** In **1964** JD's opened with Jennings as house band. Wikipedia places it in Tempe; others say Scottsdale.
- **Balladeer and fiddlers.** **1966**: Dolan Ellis became State Balladeer. **1975**: AOTFA formed in Payson, and Tucson started contra dancing.

**1975–2019: institutions.**

- **Late 1970s and 1980s.** Wickenburg's festival began around **1980**, the ABA in **1981** and PHXtmd in **1983**. **1984** brought Matsuri, Corritore's KJZZ show and The Dubliner pub, with Irish music "five nights a week" ([New Times](https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/food-drink/seamus-mccaffreys-phoenix-irish-pub-st-patricks-day-parties-andrew-mirtich-11537156/)). **1987**: Fiddler's Dream. **1988**: the Gu-Achi Fiddlers LP (year medium confidence).
- **Lon Austin's era.** **c. 1989–96**: Austin built the free festival, the coffeehouse, the library series and the songwriters' gathering.
- **1990s.** **1990**: the Indian School closed. **1991–92**: the hoop dance contest arrived at the Heard. **1994**: Jam Pak.
- **2000s.** **1999–2012**: the Irish Cultural Center was built in stages. **2002–2019**: nine Irish sessions came and went.
- **2010s.** **2010**: MIM opened. **2012**: the Gilbert Town Fiddlers. **2015**: the Piipaash Matasheevm.

**2020–2026: COVID and after.**

- **COVID.** Fibber's session paused until May 2021. PHXtmd ran a Jamulus server, and the hoop dance contest went online in 2021.
- **Rebound.** **2023**: Waila Style 87, the Roundup and the Bulgarian Heritage Center.
- **Losses.** **2025**: the Scottish Games skipped their 60th, Mul-Chu-Tha was cancelled, and Payson's contest merged into a county fair.
- **2026.** Ellis marks 60 years in office while his Folklore Preserve goes silent in July ([AFP](https://arizonafolklore.com/reservation/the-music-falls-silent-at-the-arizona-folklore-preserve/)). Casa Grande's fiddle contest takes the year off. Fiddler's Dream turns 39, still masked.

## 13. The fiddle angle

**Tunes.** Place-named tunes here are mostly false friends.

- **"The Phoenix"** (reel, by Cork accordionist Dave Hennessy) is named for a Cork pub ([#1105](https://thesession.org/tunes/1105)).
- **"Arizona"** (reel, by Keith Murphy) shows no sign of being about Arizona ([#4174](https://thesession.org/tunes/4174)).
- **The real local finds** are O'odham: the Gu-Achi "Hohokum Polka," plus "Cababi Polka" and "Memories in Ajo" from farther south ([Lamancusa](https://www.oldtimefiddletunes.net/pdf/SWTunesBook.pdf)).
- **Viola Ruth's "Beaver Creek"** was "arranged to fit the call 'Take a Peek'."
- **A retitling.** AOTFA's newsletter notes some Arizona fiddlers call "Black Velvet Waltz" the "Cowboy Waltz" ([newsletter](https://arizonaoldtimefiddlers.org/images/May%20June%202025.pages.pdf)).
- **Not fiddle tunes:** Duane Eddy's guitar instrumental "South Phoenix," and "Big Iron," whose "Agua Fria" may be New Mexico's ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Iron)).

**Styles.**

1. **Contest fiddling** on the Texas/Weiser model, from AOTFA's Small Fry to Senior divisions.
2. **O'odham fiddle-band style:** two fiddles tuned about a whole step low, playing polkas, mazurkas and two-steps.
3. **Yaqui pascola violin** with harp, heard only in ceremony.
4. **Mexican violin-and-guitar bands**, recorded in Tempe in 1939, whose violin lives on in mariachi.
5. **Western square-dance fiddling**, preserved in Ruth's book and Ramsey's 78s.
6. **Today's slow-jam pan-Celtic-Appalachian mix.**

**Contests.** **Wickenburg** (November) is the one inside the MSA. Casa Grande's Grande Fiddle Championship took 2026 off. The rest of the winter circuit is outside: Payson, Salome, Yuma, Ajo, Tucson and Safford ([AOTFA events](https://arizonaoldtimefiddlers.org/events.htm)).

## 14. Character

**What's distinct.** A trad scene built by transplants and snowbirds, running beside Indigenous and Mexican traditions with little crossover. Its deepest commercial legacy is country and Western, plus Canyon Records.

**Who shows up.** No survey exists, but the evidence skews old: jams in senior centers and 55+ resorts, AOTFA's "Over 80" dues tier, folk dancers with 20 to 50 years on the floor. The young are in school mariachi, folklórico, Jam Pak and the Gilbert Town Fiddlers.

**Tuesday vs. Saturday.**

- **Tuesday is thin.** Dance at PIFD (ICC, 6:30pm; Wednesdays in Tempe in October 2026) or RSCDS (Mesa, 7pm). There is no Tuesday session; the nearest is Tucson's Hotel McCoy, 6–8pm ([#8846](https://thesession.org/sessions/8846)). Wednesday is better: Via Linda at 10am, Mountain View Pub at 5:30, and the Beaded Lizard at 6.
- **Saturday is the day.**
  - Morning: ECD at 9 (2nd and 4th Saturdays), or a Sun Lakes or Mesa jam; then a POTMS old-time jam at 10 or 10:30.
  - Late morning: Sacred Harp at 11 (3rd Saturday).
  - Afternoon: Irish at Fibber Magee's at 2.
  - Evening: on 2nd Saturdays, contra at 7 in Chandler or Phoenix; otherwise a $10 Fiddler's Dream concert.

**Winter vs. summer.**

- **November–April is high season.** Snowbird jams fill, RSCDS and the East Valley jam run, and festival weekends pile up: Wickenburg and Laveen (November), the Roundup and hoop dance (February), Glendale (late February), then Mul-Chu-Tha, Guadalupe's Lent and the powwows.
- **Summer is quiet.** There is no July contra, Sun Lakes and East Valley jams stop, and little happens outdoors before sunset. Fibber's, the ICC and Fiddler's Dream carry on indoors, and players escape to Flagstaff.

**Costs.** Most of it is free: jams, the First Friday session, Sacred Harp, library concerts and the Glendale festival. Contra is $10–25 sliding; ECD and RSCDS $10; céilí $8; Fiddler's Dream ~$10; MIM $44.50–97.50; Wickenburg $25 a day; the Roundup $110–130 with camping; polka dances $15, BYOB.

**Geography.** You need a car. The scene is spread across downtown (ICC, Heard), north Phoenix (Fiddler's Dream, MIM), an East Valley cluster (Chandler, Mesa, Tempe, Sun Lakes, Apache Junction), the West Valley (Glendale, Peoria, Sun City), reservation communities, Guadalupe, the Pinal farm towns and far-off Wickenburg.

## 15. More surprises

- **No fiddlers' chapter.** AOTFA has no Phoenix-area chapter, though its mailing address is in Surprise and its form asks for summer *and* winter addresses ([AOTFA](https://arizonaoldtimefiddlers.org/chapters.htm)).
- **A parks worker built the free folk scene.** Lon Austin's festival, gathering and library concerts trace to Faith Petric's San Francisco club in 1965 ([petition](https://www.gopetition.com/petitions/induct-lon-austin-into-amehof.html)).
- **Still masked.** Fiddler's Dream requires KN95 masks in 2026 and is still fully booked ([FAQ](https://fiddlersdream.org/faqs/)).
- **The Dutchmen winter in Mesa.** The Dakota Dutchmen play $15 BYOB dances in a 55+ clubhouse.
- **A boarding school turned festival ground.** Steele Indian School Park, once a federal assimilation boarding school (1891–1990), now hosts Matsuri and the Arab American Festival ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Indian_School)).
- **Halfway singings.** Phoenix and Tucson Sacred Harp singers meet halfway, in Arizona City ([Tucson Sacred Harp](https://www.tucsonfasola.org/)).
- **Scottish down, Irish up.** The Scottish Games skipped their 60th year while the Irish Cultural Center thrives ([Mesa Independent](https://www.yourvalley.net/mesa-independent/stories/phoenix-scottish-games-postpone-annual-event-until-2026,558948)).

## 17. Data-quality notes

- **How it was researched.** Four threads ran on Oct 30, 2026 under heavy search rate limits, so most facts come from direct reads of organizer sites, calendars, thesession's API and tribal news.
- **What the numbers mean.** 45 series and ~105 events a month (~67 high-confidence only), with seasonal series annualized; "other" includes ~8 listening concerts a month, and the bluegrass count is conservative.
- **Couldn't read.** Facebook groups (AZ Irish Music, ContraDancePhoenix), resident-only resort jams, tunearch.org, the Arizona Memory Project and Discogs.
- **Unverified.** The 2026 Scottish Games, Four Peaks Irish Arts' status, Black Wren Morris, the Casa Grande airport jam, KUD Sevdah's current activity and the Wickenburg poetry gathering's age.
- **Not found** (which is not proof of absence): house concerts, a shanty sing, a ballad group, a Phoenix FM folk show, son jarocho, an NEA Fellow in the MSA, and Filipino, Vietnamese or East African music.
- **Where sources disagree:** Wickenburg's founding (1979 or 1980), JD's location (Tempe or Scottsdale), the hoop dance start (1991 New Mexico or 1992 Heard), the Scottish Games start (1964 or c. 1965) and Payson 2026 (TBA or Oct 3).
- **Search summaries only:** Fiddler's Dream's Thursday time, the violin shops and the 1988 Gu-Achi LP year.
- **Inferred.** Founding years marked "c." come from edition numbers.
- **Etiquette.** Yaqui and O'odham ceremonies are religious practice. Recording is banned in Guadalupe.
