# Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario (the Inland Empire): folk scene dossier

*As of October 2026. Metro population 4.77M (2025); Riverside and San Bernardino counties, about 27,000 square miles, from Ontario to the Colorado River. Built from two research threads (sessions, dance and song; heritage, history and people) that were then merged. Structured data: `riverside.json`. Claremont, Pomona and San Dimas are in LA County: they are flagged `outside_msa` here and counted in the Los Angeles dossier.*

## Scene in a nutshell

The Inland Empire has one of the thinnest Anglo-Celtic trad scenes of any big US metro. There are about 13 verified recurring jams, sessions, open mics and dances, roughly 40 meetings a month (8 per million residents, against LA's 20). That means one Irish session (biweekly, snowbird season, Saturday lunch at a Palm Desert golf course), three bluegrass jams, a coffeehouse open mic, two seniors' folk-dance groups and a cluster of modern club square-dance clubs. There is no contra, no old-time jam, no shape-note singing and no fiddlers' association district. Look past fiddles, though, and the region has one of the most distinctive living traditions in any US metro: **Cahuilla bird singing**. Revived from near-extinction in the 1980s, it now fills its own festival in Palm Springs, opens every regional powwow, and in 2022 opened Coachella. Around it are a national-champion youth mariachi, a 50-year-old folklórico company, Basque Chino, a century-old Californio fiddle band at the *Ramona* pageant, and the ghost of a 1970s bluegrass festival boom. For contra, Sacred Harp or a weekly session, IE players drive west into LA County.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **Bird singing is the signature tradition.** A line of men with palm-seed gourd rattles, women dancing as one body, and a 300+ song migration cycle sung dusk to dawn. It nearly died out by the 1980s; in 2023 the Torres Martinez chairman counted "over 200 Bird Singers" across four sister bands ([Joshua Tree Voice](https://joshuatreevoice.com/cahuilla-bird-singers/); [Palm Springs Life](https://www.palmspringslife.com/arts-culture/singing-the-birds-brings-ancient-cahuilla-songs-to-palm-springs/)).
2. **A Coachella land acknowledgment, and a refusal.** Desert Cahuilla bird singers opened Coachella in 2022 and 2023. One singer stayed away: "One of the things he taught us was not to glorify what we're doing. It's for our people" ([Joshua Tree Voice](https://joshuatreevoice.com/cahuilla-bird-singers/)).
3. **One Irish session for 4.8 million people**, at a Palm Desert golf course on 2nd and 4th Saturdays, October to April. It plays Irish, Scottish, some Cape Breton and Norwegian tunes and is run by "some professionals and a lot of retired musicians" ([thesession 9622](https://thesession.org/sessions/9622)).
4. **The fiddlers' association never came south.** In 1970 the Southern California Old-Time Fiddlers' Association declined to join the state association, then split into old-time and "amplified country and Western" factions. Today CSOTFA has no IE district, and there is no contra or old-time jam ([CSOTFA history](https://www.csotfad8.org/history-of-csotfa); [districts](https://www.csotfa.org/districts.htm)).
5. **Square dancing is the biggest scheduled trad dance form**, coordinated by the delightfully named [Cow Counties Hoedown Association](https://cowcountieshoedownassociation.com/), after the 19th-century nickname for these ranching counties. Riverside has a dedicated [Cowtown Square Dance Center](https://www.cowtownsquaredance.com/).
6. **The region's longest fiddle lineage is Californio.** The Arias Troubadours' twin violins have played the *Ramona* pageant in Hemet since 1924, with a repertoire that "has not changed in nearly 90 years" ([Press-Enterprise, 2011](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2011/04/28/hemet-ramona-dedicated-to-memory-of-longtime-troubadour/)).
7. **A lost bluegrass belt.** Golden West in Norco brought Monroe and Flatt in 1973. Calico Ghost Town drew 24,000 in 1976, where 8-year-old Stuart Duncan started playing festivals ([LA Times, 1988](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-05-07-vw-2460-story.html)). Huck Finn Jubilee in Victorville was IBMA Event of the Year in 2001. Huck Finn is now in San Dimas (LA Co.) and paused for 2026 ([huckfinn.com](https://huckfinn.com/)), and Calico's festival was renamed ["Spring Jam"](https://calicoattractions.com/event-schedule) in 2026.
8. **One Indigenous people's dance on another's land.** P'urhépecha families from Ocumicho, Michoacán rehearse the *Danza de los Ancianos* every December in trailer camps on the Torres Martinez Cahuilla reservation, then process to the church in Mecca ([David Bacon, New Labor Forum](https://newlaborforum.cuny.edu/2011/10/04/how-the-purhepechas-came-to-southern-californias-coachella-valley/)).
9. **A national mariachi title.** Riverside Arts Academy's youth Mariachi Campana won the 2026 Mariachi Nationals ([Press-Enterprise](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2026/09/01/riverside-youth-mariachi-group-wins-national-title/)). A "first California ensemble ever" claim was retracted a day later ([Raincross Gazette](https://www.raincrossgazette.com/mariachi-campana-becomes-first-california-ensemble-to-win-nationals/)).
10. **Basque Chino.** Among the warehouses survive two Basque clubs (1946 and 1968), a family fronton, a *Klika* drum-and-bugle corps and an *irrintzi* (Basque yell) contest ([Champion Newspapers](https://www.championnewspapers.com/community_news/article_084891e1-0ea0-4d04-9bb1-0cc0250e166f.html)).

A visiting fiddler's best bets: **Saturday** is the IE's day, with the Fairmount Park bluegrass jam in Riverside (9 am to noon) and, in season, the Palm Desert session at lunch. **Wednesday** is the Back to the Grind open mic in Riverside. ## Overlap with Los Angeles

The IE works partly as a suburb of LA's folk scene. Its iconic bluegrass festival (Huck Finn) now lives in San Dimas; its nearest contra moved from Claremont to South Pasadena; and its nearest weekly Irish session (The Back Abbey), folk shop and concert series (Folk Music Center) and dormant shape-note singing are all in Claremont, a mile over the county line. Those items appear in this file flagged `outside_msa` and are counted only in the LA file. Traffic runs mostly one way: only the desert (Pappy & Harriet's, the Joshua Tree Music Festival, GramFest, historically Calico and Huck Finn) draws LA players east.

## Part 1. Sessions, jams, dance, song and singing (layers 1-3)

### Character

The IE's folk life is **dispersed, daytime, older and car-dependent**. Look at when things happen:

- Saturday 9 a.m. bluegrass in a Riverside park;
- Saturday 12:30 p.m. Irish tunes after lunch at a Palm Desert golf club;
- Tuesday 2 p.m. international folk dance at a Palm Desert senior center;
- Thursday 2 p.m. folk dance in a Crestline seniors' club;
- Sunday 3 p.m. English country dance at a Riverside senior center (if it still runs).

This is a schedule built around retirees, snowbirds and people who commute to LA or Orange County on weekdays. The only regular weeknight "folk" fixture with youth in the room is the [Wednesday open mic at Back to the Grind](https://riversidescene.com/articles/concerts/backtothegrindopenmicmusicnight), a Riverside coffeehouse open since 1996.

The second defining trait is the **pull of Los Angeles**. When IE residents want contra, Sacred Harp, Scottish country dancing, a weekly Irish session or an old-time jam, they drive west: to Claremont, South Pasadena, Covina, Burbank, Fullerton, Mission Viejo. The California Bluegrass Association's statewide [jam calendar](https://californiabluegrass.org/calendar/category/jam/) lists jams in Covina, Mission Viejo, Long Beach and North Hollywood, but no city in either IE county. The RSCDS Los Angeles branch [lists no IE classes](http://www.rscds-losangeles.org/Classes.html). The Folk Dance Federation of California, South [lists](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/clubs.htm) no member club in Riverside or San Bernardino, just two associate groups, in Palm Desert and Crestline.

Third, the IE's roots identity is **Western and desert-flavored rather than Celtic or Appalachian**. Its iconic music places are a movie-set cantina in Pioneertown, a ghost-town silver camp at Calico, and the Joshua Tree motel room where Gram Parsons died. The signature participatory form is the **bluegrass jam**, and the signature dance is **modern Western club square dancing**, not contra.

### Geography, by subregion

#### Riverside / Corona / Norco (western Riverside County)
The densest cluster:
- **[Inland Empire Bluegrass Jam](https://www.startjamming.com/jams/inland-empire-bluegrass-jam-at-fairmont-park)**, every Saturday 9–noon at Fairmount Park. Beginner-friendly and free. Startjamming says it was "checked Mar 26, 2026," and it is also on the SWBA list ([June 2025 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20250621024446/https://www.southwestbluegrass.com/2024/07/29/jams/)). It is the *only* IE jam in startjamming's database; the next nearest are about 30 miles away in Orange County.
- **Back to the Grind open mic**, Wednesdays from 5:30 sign-up to 9 p.m., no cover, hosted by Patrick Maloney ([listing](https://allevents.in/riverside/riverside-open-mic-music-series-back-to-the-grind-oct-28th-2026/200030913515347)). A four-piece folk group, "Looking Up," plays almost every week.
- **Square dancing:**
  - [Riverside Single Squares](https://www.riversidesinglesquares.com/): dances on 2nd and 4th Fridays at Wesley United Methodist, plus a Tuesday class at the Janet Goeske Senior Center for the 2026–27 season.
  - [Cowtown Singles & Couples](https://www.cowtownsquaredance.com/): a 1st-Friday Mainstream/Plus dance with rounds at the Cowtown Square Dance Center, plus Wednesday classes.
- **English country dance (unverified):** [englishcountrydancing.org](http://englishcountrydancing.org/) lists 1st and 4th Sundays at the Janet Goeske Center, $5 ("Fleur de Lis" demo & community team). The site is live but undated, and I found no 2024–26 confirmation, so it is not counted.
- **History:** Norco hosted the [Golden West Bluegrass Festival](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-05-07-vw-2460-story.html) from 1973 (the 1988 edition was the "35th," so there were several a year at its peak). Wikipedia also says the Huck Finn story "began in Norco in the early 70's" ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huck_Finn_Jubilee)).

#### San Bernardino / Redlands / Yucaipa / Oak Glen
- **[Sunkissed Squares](https://www.sunkissedsquares.com/)** runs a 14-week square & line-dance class on Tuesdays at the Redlands Community Center (started Sept 1, 2026). The first two classes are free, then $7 each.
- The **[Redlands Bowl](https://redlandsbowl.org/)** has given free "Music Under the Stars" for over a century, but its programming is general rather than folk.
- **[Riley's Farm](https://rileysfarm.com/index.php/colonial-faire/)** in Oak Glen runs a 1774-themed Colonial Faire ($18–22, July; the 10th was in 2026). It is the region's main colonial living-history event, but I found no evidence of a regular English country dance there.
- Yucaipa and Redlands had no jam, session or folk club I could verify.

#### West End (Ontario / Upland / Rancho Cucamonga / Chino) and the Claremont question
Ontario hosted the **Huck Finn Jubilee** at Cucamonga-Guasti Regional Park after Don Tucker's death: from 2013 per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huck_Finn_Jubilee), or 2014 per [huckfinn.com](https://huckfinn.com/who-are-we/). The Greater Ontario Convention & Visitors Bureau dropped it after 2016. New owners revived it in 2018, and it has been in [San Dimas (LA County) since 2022](https://bluegrasstoday.com/new-home-for-huck-finn-jubilee-announced-in-california/).

The West End's folk life in practice is **Claremont, just inside LA County**. Everything below is flagged `outside_msa` and not counted:
- the [Folk Music Center](https://www.folkmusiccenter.com/): shop, repair, lessons, museum (a nonprofit since 1976), a concert series ($10 in Oct 2026) and a last-Sunday open mic ($3) ([events](https://www.folkmusiccenter.com/events));
- a weekly Thursday Irish session at [The Back Abbey](https://thesession.org/sessions/9611) since early 2026;
- Monday Israeli dancing;
- the former 2nd-Saturday contra (now in South Pasadena);
- a shape-note singing at Claremont Presbyterian, which [FolkWorks lists "on hiatus"](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/shape-note-sacred-harp/).

The Folk Music Center founders, the Chases, also ran the [Golden Ring](https://www.folkmusiccenter.com/museum) cafe from 1965 for about five years. It booked Rev. Gary Davis, Doc Watson, Hedy West, John Fahey and the New Lost City Ramblers. It was the Folk Scare's nearest outpost to the IE.

#### Temecula Valley / Hemet / Menifee / Lake Elsinore
- A **bluegrass jam** on 2nd and 4th Saturdays, 1–3 p.m., at Bastards Canteen in Temecula appears on the SWBA list ([June 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250621024446/https://www.southwestbluegrass.com/2024/07/29/jams/)). I could not confirm it for 2026 (low confidence).
- The wineries book plenty of acoustic acts, but those are performances, not participatory music.
- Hemet and Sun City/Menifee are big retirement areas where club square dancing should thrive. I could not read the Cow Counties member list (JavaScript-only), so whether they have clubs is an **open gap, not a finding**.

#### Coachella Valley (Palm Springs, Palm Desert, Indio)
Snowbird country, with a schedule to match:
- the [Shadow Mountain session](https://thesession.org/sessions/9622). It moved to 2nd and 4th Saturdays from Oct 10, 2026, and its posted season runs to April 24, 2027;
- the [Desert International Folk Dancers](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/clubs.htm), Tuesdays 2–4 p.m., November–April, at the Joslyn Center.

Richard Gilbert's scraped [SW folk concert calendar](https://nwfolk.com/rgswfmcc.html) for the valley and Pioneertown lists just four shows for Nov–Dec 2026: three at Pappy & Harriet's plus Celtic Woman at Fantasy Springs casino. **Stagecoach** (Indio, April) is a country megafestival, not folk; I list it only for completeness.

#### High Desert (Victorville / Apple Valley / Hesperia / Barstow / Calico)
The historic bluegrass core:
- **Calico Ghost Town** ran the spring bluegrass festival. In 1988 organizer Don Tucker claimed "10,000 to 12,000" people a year and "24,000 that year" (1976). It held fiddle, banjo, guitar, band and clogging contests, with "$2,500 in prizes" ([LA Times, Apr 1988](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-04-03-tr-899-story.html)).
- The 2024 County Parks listing shows a one-day festival with Americana bands at $15 ([parks.sbcounty.gov](https://parks.sbcounty.gov/event/calico-bluegrass-festival-2/)). By 2026 the operator's calendar calls it ["Spring Jam"](https://calicoattractions.com/event-schedule) (May 9).
- **Huck Finn Jubilee** lived at **Mojave Narrows Regional Park** (Victorville) on Father's Day weekend until 2012. It drew up to 7,000 and booked Ralph Stanley, Alison Krauss, Del McCoury, Emmylou Harris and Steve Martin.
- Today the High Desert's bluegrass gathering is the **SWBA SuperJam** campout (Memorial Day weekend): Mojave Narrows in 2025 ($10/car day use, $20/night dry camping), and the Victor Valley Moose Lodge in Hesperia in 2026.
- I found no weekly High Desert jam.

#### Morongo Basin (Joshua Tree / Yucca Valley / Twentynine Palms / Pioneertown)
The IE's most vivid roots-music landscape, though mostly for listening:
- **[Pappy & Harriet's](https://pappyandharriets.com/history/)**, open since 1982;
- the **[Joshua Tree Music Festival](https://joshuatreemusicfestival.com/past-lineups/)**, since 2003, twice a year (May 14–17 and Oct 8–11, 2026). It is eclectic: Greensky Bluegrass, Trampled by Turtles and Valerie June among its alumni;
- **[GramFest](https://jtarts.org/tag/gram-fest/)**, a November tribute to Gram Parsons (2025 edition Nov 22).

For participation, a **Yucca Valley Bluegrass Jam** group on Meetup posted a "Bluegrass Practice / Jam" for Sunday Oct 25, 2026 at 6 p.m. ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/yucca-valley-bluegrass-jam/)). Its frequency is unconfirmed.

#### Mountains (Idyllwild / Big Bear / Lake Arrowhead / Crestline)
- **Idyllwild** is a remarkable small-town music village. The week of Oct 29–Nov 4, 2026 listed about 17 gigs at Cafe Aroma, Taryn's Place, Middle Ridge Winery, Lily's and others, including Idyllwild Arts student songwriting nights ([Good Morning Idyllwild](https://goodmorningidyllwild.com/live-music-page/)). Today's [summer programs](https://idyllwildarts.org/summer/) show no folk or fiddle track, and there is no session or jam.
- Its history is richer. ISOMATA was founded in 1946 and held its first summer session in 1950. Clark Noone's [2019 thesis](https://scholarship.claremont.edu/cgu_etd/374/) covers 1946–62; its keywords include "Seeger," which suggests a folk-revival connection worth tracing.
- **Crestline** has the [Crestline International Folk Dancers](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/clubs.htm): one hour, third Thursdays at 2 p.m.
- **Big Bear and Lake Arrowhead** had no bluegrass festival, jam or fiddle event that I could verify for 2025–26.

### Costs

Most participation is **free**: the Fairmount and Temecula jams, the Back to the Grind open mic, and the Palm Desert session (where you buy lunch). Square-dance classes run about [$7 a night](https://www.sunkissedsquares.com/), and the unverified ECD asks $5. Over the line in Claremont, the Folk Music Center open mic is $3 and concerts are about $10. Festivals:
- Calico: $15 for adults (2024);
- Riley's Colonial Faire: $18–22;
- SWBA SuperJam: $10 per car for day use, $20 a night to camp.

**There is no IE contra or house-concert series to price.**

### Who shows up

The evidence points to older players:
- The Palm Desert session describes itself as "some professionals and a lot of retired musicians," together for 5+ years.
- The international folk-dance groups meet in senior centers on weekday afternoons, and Desert IFD only in snowbird season.
- The square-dance clubs advertise "great for singles, couples, families" and recruit through community-center classes.

The coffeehouse open mic is the main youth-facing room. Idyllwild Arts students turn up in Idyllwild's gig listings, and Pappy's and Joshua Tree Music Festival draw a younger desert-tourism crowd that mostly listens. I found no demographic surveys; all of this is inferred from listings.

## Part 2. Heritage traditions, history, people, archives and the fiddle angle

### 1. Native traditions: bird songs, Salt Songs, Bighorn songs and powwows

#### Bird singing: what it is and how it came back

Bird songs are the social song cycle of the Cahuilla and their neighbours (Serrano, Cupeño, Luiseño, Kumeyaay, and in adapted form the Mojave and other Colorado River peoples). Singers perform in a line with the leader at the centre. They shake rattles made from gourds, cottonwood sticks and the seeds of the native California fan palm, while women and girls dance "as a unified group, emphasizing collective movement over individual expression" ([Palm Springs Life](https://www.palmspringslife.com/arts-culture/singing-the-birds-brings-ancient-cahuilla-songs-to-palm-springs/)). The full cycle runs to more than 300 songs, traditionally sung in order from dusk to dawn over several nights. A single song can be one word, such as the Cahuilla word for "cold" marking a wintry stretch of the migration. Bird singer Anthony W. Purnel quotes a verse: "On the wind, we came" ([Palm Springs Life](https://www.palmspringslife.com/arts-culture/how-bird-singing-preserves-the-culture-of-the-agua-caliente-band-of-cahuilla-indians/)).

**The near-death and the revival.** Agua Caliente's song keeper John Joseph (Joe) Patencio "was told not to carry these songs on", and when he died in 1974 his student Alvino Siva said he wished he had "sat up with him more and asked about the old words". By the 1980s, when "it wasn't cool to be Indian", bird singing had almost disappeared. A small group of elders won a California Arts Council grant to mentor younger singers, and "as those older guys passed away, these younger guys started their own groups, and it pyramided from there" (Gerald Clarke, in [Palm Springs Life](https://www.palmspringslife.com/arts-culture/how-bird-singing-preserves-the-culture-of-the-agua-caliente-band-of-cahuilla-indians/)). The three elders usually credited:

- **Robert Levi Sr.** (Torres Martinez, c.1918–2007). He was a Sherman Institute alumnus and staff member, a saxophone and clarinet player and a WWII veteran. He founded the **Intertribal Cahuilla Bird Singers** because he believed "an intertribal effort was needed to save the songs", recorded a 5-CD set of Desert Cahuilla bird songs, won the California Arts Council grant, and trained San Manuel's singers, including **James Ramos**, later the first Native American elected to the California State Assembly, who has sung bird songs before the Legislature ([Dorothy Ramon Learning Center](https://dorothyramonlearningcenter.substack.com/p/isyl-ipe-hiwen-coyotes-waiting); [Indianz obituary](https://indianz.com/News/2007/004783.asp)).
- **Anthony J. "Biff" Andreas Jr.** (Agua Caliente, 1938–2009) "brought bird singing back to Agua Caliente". He started a group of all ages that sang "at the Andreas Ranch… at fundraisers at the University of California, Riverside… in various parades" ([Joshua Tree Voice](https://joshuatreevoice.com/the-songs-of-our-people/); [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/72518880/anthony_joseph-andreas)).
- **Alvino Siva** (Los Coyotes Cahuilla/Cupeño, d. June 26, 2009, Banning), "the last Cahuilla Indian cowboy" and one of the last fluent speakers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Coyotes_Band_of_Cahuilla_and_Cupeno_Indians)).

The next generation includes **Bill Madrigal Sr.**, an ACTA master artist in 2002 who taught gourd-rattle making and the song cycle to Sean Bogner ([ACTA](https://actaonline.org/profile/bill-madrigal/)), and **Luke Madrigal** (1959–2020), "one of the first in California to revitalize the singing… in the 1980s" ([Indigenous Storytelling](https://indigenoustorytelling.com/luke-madrigal)). The current generation includes **Gerald Clarke**, a Cahuilla painter and UCR professor from Anza who learned from Bill Madrigal and Alvino Siva and prefers "all-nighters" with the "deep cuts", along with **Anthony J. Andreas III** and **John R. Preckwinkle III**.

**Vitality: thriving.** It has a festival of its own, contests with prize money, multiple named groups, a university pipeline (UCR, CSUSB) and young children dancing. The tension is now over *where* to sing, as the Coachella debate shows, not *whether* anyone can.

#### Serrano Bighorn Sheep songs

The *Paa'chucham* were sung before bighorn hunts in the San Bernardino Mountains by a singer wearing a rattle of bighorn hooves on his leg. They are "the only remaining song genre unique to the Serrano". Ernest Siva learned them from his cousin Lloyd Marcus, whose 1970s recording of the last 12 songs remains "the basis of Ernest's learning and teaching". He passed them to his grand-nephew Isaac Horsman Rodriguez in a 2008 ACTA apprenticeship ([ACTA](https://actaonline.org/profile/ernest-siva/)). Siva holds USC degrees in music education, taught American Indian music at UCLA, and founded the **Dorothy Ramon Learning Center** in Banning (2003), named for his aunt, the last fluent Serrano speaker ([DRLC](https://dorothyramon.blogspot.com/p/about-dorothy-ramon-learning-center-inc.html)). He has received the Heard Museum's Spirit of the Heard award ([Press-Enterprise 2014](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2014/10/19/morongo-tribal-elder-ernest-siva-earns-award/)) and a CSU honorary degree ([CSU](https://www.calstate.edu/impact-of-the-csu/alumni/Honorary-Degrees/Pages/Ernest-H-Siva.aspx)). **Vitality: niche.**

A small gem from Siva's archive: the Desert Cahuilla lullaby **"Isyl Ipe Hiwen" (Coyote's Waiting)**, which Robert Levi sang to him. Siva transcribed it and taught it to his UCLA classes, and it is now "a favorite of many people from many different cultures" ([DRLC](https://dorothyramonlearningcenter.substack.com/p/isyl-ipe-hiwen-coyotes-waiting)). Its words: "Coyote is waiting… and he will come and get you, and he will eat you."

#### The Colorado River edge: Mojave bird songs and Chemehuevi Salt Songs

San Bernardino County runs all the way to the Colorado River, so the MSA includes **Needles** (Fort Mojave) and the **Chemehuevi Reservation** at Havasu Lake.

- **Fort Mojave Indian Days** in Needles turned 50 in October 2025. It has a bird-singing competition, youth bird dancing and a parade ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/mojavedays/); [Las Vegas Weekly photo](https://lasvegasweekly.com/photos/2025/dec/23/939958/)). Groups include the touring **Rez Life Bird Singers**, who bring Mojave songs to other tribes ([Southern Ute Drum](https://www.sudrum.com/culture/2022/11/18/ft-mojave-bird-singers/)). **Vitality: active.**
- **Salt Songs** (*Asi Huviav*) are the sacred funerary cycle of the Nuwuvi (Southern Paiute/Chemehuevi). They are being revived by the Salt Song Trail Project, co-founded by **Matthew Leivas Sr.** of Havasu Lake ([Cultural Conservancy](https://www.nativeland.org/salt-song-trail); [Artbound](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/artbound/salt-songs-to-break-your-heart)). These are ceremonial, not public entertainment. **Vitality: niche.**

#### Powwows: the IE has a lot of them

Intertribal powwow culture is strong because of Sherman Indian High School (founded as Perris Indian School in 1892, Sherman Institute in Riverside from 1902), which drew students from 50+ nations ([OAC](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8fn1cfv)), and because of the gaming tribes' resources.

- **Morongo Thunder & Lightning Powwow**, Cabazon, late September. The 35th in 2026 drew 30,000+ ([Yahoo/press](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/30-000-gather-native-american-040000978.html)).
- **San Manuel Pow Wow** at Cal State San Bernardino, mid-September, since 1996 ([CSUSB](https://www.csusb.edu/inside/article/591368/san-manuel-pow-wow-returns-csusb-sept-19-21)).
- **UC Riverside Pow Wow**, late May; the 43rd was May 22–24, 2026, with Cahuilla bird singers and Danza Azteca Kalpulli Teuxihuitl ([News from Native California](https://newsfromnativecalifornia.com/event/43rd-annual-ucr-powwow/)).
- **Sherman Indian High School Youth Pow Wow**, the 40th on April 18, 2026, "after a year's absence" ([Raincross Gazette](https://www.raincrossgazette.com/noteworthy/sherman-indians-pow-wow-returns-after-year-off/)).
- **Pechanga Pow Wow**, Temecula, early January; the 24th is set for Jan 8–10, 2027 ([Pechanga](https://www.pechanga.com/entertain/pechanga-pow-wow)).
- **Soboba**: the tribe says the "next Pow Wow will be announced at a later date", so it is on hiatus ([Soboba](https://www.soboba-nsn.gov/powwow)).

Two museums anchor the Native scene. The **Malki Museum** (1964), on the Morongo Reservation, was conceived by Jane Penn and built with Katherine Siva Saubel and anthropologist Lowell John Bean ([Wikipedia: Saubel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Siva_Saubel)). The **Agua Caliente Cultural Plaza and Museum** opened in downtown Palm Springs on Nov 3, 2023, with bird singers and dancers at the opening ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/agua-caliente-cultural-plaza-museum-open-november-3-2023-301928308.html)).

### 2. Mexican and Mexican-American traditions

People of Mexican descent make up 86% of the IE's Latinos, and Riverside County has the sixth-largest Latino population of any US county ([Riverside Record citing Pew](https://riversiderecord.org/thousands-flock-to-downtown-riverside-for-annual-day-of-the-dead-festival/)). The heritage scene is correspondingly big, though it lives mostly in schools, festivals and dance companies rather than in a club or session culture.

**Mariachi: thriving, and school-driven.** The Riverside Arts Academy (founded 2012; 500+ students; [RAA](https://www.riversideartsacademy.com/)) runs Mariachi Juvenil and its flagship **Mariachi Campana de Riverside**, which rehearses Wednesday evenings and Saturday mornings ([RAA](https://www.riversideartsacademy.com/mcr)). It is directed by **Alex Treviño II**, formerly of Mariachi Los Camperos ([Press-Enterprise 2024](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2024/09/06/enrollment-is-open-for-riverside-arts-academys-music-ensembles/)). Campana placed third at the 2026 Tucson International Mariachi Conference and then won the **2026 Mariachi Nationals** in Anaheim. The Gazette ran a correction after the academy withdrew its claim to be the "first California ensemble" to win ([Raincross Gazette](https://www.raincrossgazette.com/mariachi-campana-becomes-first-california-ensemble-to-win-nationals/)). There are also university ensembles: **CSUSB Mariachi Los Coyotes** ([CSUSB](https://www.csusb.edu/music/ensembles/chamber-ensembles/mariachi-los-coyotes)) and UCR's mariachi courses, which "emphasize mariachi and son jarocho" ([UCR MUS 175A](https://www.coursicle.com/ucr/courses/MUS/175A/)). The festivals are the **Lake Elsinore Mariachi Fest** (6th annual, Sept 19, 2026; [Elsinore 247](https://elsinore247.com/2026/09/mariachi-fest-returns-to-lake-elsinore-for-its-6th-annual-celebration-sept-19.html)) and a free **Indio Mariachi Festival** each March ([Discover Palm Desert](https://discoverpalmdesert.com/event/mariachi-festival/); year not shown on the page).

**Ballet folklórico: thriving.** **Ballet Folklórico de Riverside** was founded in 1976 by Ana de la Tejera "with 13 children… practicing on driveways and in garages" and celebrates 50 years in 2026 ([bio](https://ideasforacts.com/ballet-folklorico-riverside/); [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/bfderiverside/)). Rialto's **Mi Lindo México** danced Sinaloan suites at the 2025 Day of the Dead ([Riverside Record](https://riversiderecord.org/thousands-flock-to-downtown-riverside-for-annual-day-of-the-dead-festival/)).

**Día de los Muertos: thriving.** Cosme Cordova's **Riverside Day of the Dead Festival** began around 2003 with about 200 people. Its 23rd edition (Nov 1–2, 2025) filled seven blocks of Market Street and White Park, drew "tens of thousands", and offered community altars, two stages, folklórico and danza ([Riverside Record](https://riversiderecord.org/thousands-flock-to-downtown-riverside-for-annual-day-of-the-dead-festival/)).

**P'urhépecha in the Coachella Valley: active.** This is the most interesting diaspora story in the region. From around the 1980s (Bacon's informant describes "three decades" of migration), families from Ocumicho, Michoacán, came to work the citrus and grape fields. About 2,000 live in the Duros and Chicanitas trailer parks on Torres Martinez land, rising to 5,000+ in harvest season. "In Duros or Mecca you can practically go anywhere and speak P'urhépecha with anyone." Every December young people practise the **Danza de los Ancianos**, "long lines of young people shuffle around the trailers to the music of guitars and horns", ahead of the Guadalupe procession to the church in Mecca ([David Bacon](https://newlaborforum.cuny.edu/2011/10/04/how-the-purhepechas-came-to-southern-californias-coachella-valley/)). La Comunidad Purépecha del Valle de Coachella "creates space to continue and preserve their traditions" ([Coachella Valley Farm Workers](https://www.coachellafarmworkers.com/Purepecha/8)). A Coachella-based Facebook page devoted to *pirekua* (Purépecha song), "Cultura Purepecha Pirekuas – CPP", has ~21,700 followers ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/CulturaPurepechaPirekuasCPP/)). The best journalism is from 2011–2019, so treat the details as medium confidence.

**Not found:** an IE son jarocho fandango series (the SoCal fandango scenes are in LA and Santa Ana), an IE Guelaguetza, or organized Mixtec/Zapotec music groups. Their absence online is not proof that they don't exist; these communities are often visible only on WhatsApp and Facebook.

### 3. Other heritage communities

**Basque Chino: active.** Chino became a Basque hub in the 1950s as Basques found work as dairymen, gardeners and servers. The **Centro Basco** boarding house dates from 1940. The **Chino Basque Club** was founded in 1968 by six men at the Pyrenees Bar, signed up 28 members in a week, and now has about 150 members and 10 events a year ([Champion](https://www.championnewspapers.com/special_section/article_ea518f44-f696-11ed-9d60-3bec468ecdb6.html)). Its dance groups are Xori Ttipiak, Eskualdun Izarrak and Dantzari Onestak ([CBC](https://www.chinobasqueclub.com/history-of-cbc/)). The older **Southern California Basque Club** (1946) held its 80th picnic on July 5, 2026, together with the NABO convention ([NABO](https://www.naboeuskara.org/events/nabo-convention-southern-california-basque-club-picnic)). A typical day: Basque Mass with dancers and *Klika*, a gâteau basque contest, an irrintzi contest, a lamb dinner for $30 and evening dancing to Amerikanuak ([Champion 2025](https://www.championnewspapers.com/community_news/article_084891e1-0ea0-4d04-9bb1-0cc0250e166f.html)). The Labor Day picnic weekend brought pelotaris and musicians (Iñaki Diéguez, Xabier Berasaluze "Leturia") over from the Basque Country in 2025 ([CBC](https://www.chinobasqueclub.com/2025-chino-basque-picnic-weekend/)).

**Others, briefly:**

- **German:** Big Bear Lake Oktoberfest, 56th season in 2026, in a convention center built in 1976 in the shape of a tent to house it ([Big Bear Events](https://bigbearevents.com/book-an-event/facility-information/); [GermanUSA](https://www.germanusa.com/events/california/big-bear-lake-1/big-bear-lake-oktoberfest-2026/16936/)). Active.
- **Japanese-American:** UCR's **Senryu Taiko** (1998, formed with ethnomusicologist Deborah Wong) is "one of the earliest intercollegiate taiko groups" ([Discover Nikkei](https://discovernikkei.org/en/taiko/groups/29/)). I found no Obon inside the MSA; the nearest are in LA County.
- **Scottish:** the Scottish Society of the Inland Empire points members to Scottish Fest in Costa Mesa (Orange Co., outside the MSA) ([SSIE](https://iescots.com/upcoming-events)). There are no IE highland games.
- **African American:** churches go back to 1911 (New Hope MBC) and St. Paul AME on San Bernardino's Westside ([SB Sun](https://www.sbsun.com/2011/01/02/black-church-marks-century/); [CSUSB Bridges](https://omeka-s.csusb.edu/s/bridges-social-media-archive/item/39)). A community choir sing is part of Riverside's Juneteenth ([Riverside UCC](https://riversideucc.com/events/juneteenth-celebration-riverside-gospel-ensemble-sings/)). The Pomona Valley Juneteenth Jazz & Arts Festival (36th in 2026) is just outside the MSA in LA County ([Press-Enterprise](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2026/06/11/heres-where-juneteenth-celebrations-are-being-held-in-the-inland-empire-this-year/)).
- **Historic only:** **Filipino** farmworkers struck the Coachella vineyards on May 3, 1965, months before Delano ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delano_grape_strike)), and Riverside's **Pachappa Camp** (1905) was the first Koreatown on the US mainland ([UCR](https://pachappacamp.ucr.edu/history-timeline/)). I could not verify any living rondalla or Korean folk music practice in the MSA today.

### 4. History: from Pahsitnah to Pappy & Harriet's

**Before and around contact.** The Ramona Bowl sits on the ridge of **Pahsitnah**, a large village "thriving when the Spanish first passed by in 1774". Smallpox later drove survivors toward what is now Soboba ([Wikipedia, landmark plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ramona_Pageant)). The bird-song cycle itself is the oldest "documented" music of the region: oral history set to song.

**New Mexicans and Mormons.** In 1842–43, 20–30 families of "Indian-Mexican ancestry from New Mexico" founded **Agua Mansa** and **La Placita de los Trujillo** on the Santa Ana River. Together they were the largest settlement between New Mexico and Los Angeles in the 1840s, and today only the cemetery survives ([SB County Museum](https://museum.sbcounty.gov/agua-mansa-pioneer-cemetery/); [StoryMap](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/d38d92b54a6044efad4fa0b0972bb7b2)). In 1851, 437 Latter-day Saints arrived from Salt Lake and built Fort San Bernardino, staying until 1857 ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/when-san-bernardino-was-a-mormon-colony)). I found no specific documentation of either community's music; that would be a good archival question for Phase 2.

**Boarding school, citrus and the *Ramona* industry.** The Sherman Institute band was photographed in 1901 ([Museum of Riverside](https://www.facebook.com/museumofriverside/posts/sherman-institute-band-circa-1901-the-perris-indian-school-moved-to-riverside-an/640072211451749/)). Marching bands were a core assimilation tool, the subject of a UC study, *A Dissonant Education: Marching Bands and Indigenous…* ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3kr5j3z2)). Yet Robert Levi, a Sherman sax player, became the bird-song revival's key teacher. Helen Hunt Jackson's 1884 novel launched a tourist industry. The **Ramona** outdoor play opened in 1923 and is "the longest running outdoor play in the United States", missing only 1933, 1942 and 2020, with a cast of ~375 local volunteers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ramona_Pageant)). The 1928 film theme "Ramona" (Mabel Wayne and L. Wolfe Gilbert) was "the first theme song written for the movies" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramona_(1928_song))). In 2025 the play got its first Native American director, Duane Minard (Paiute/Yurok), a former member of the **Redtail Spirit Dancers and Singers** who perform in the play's Elder Blessing scene ([Ramona Bowl](https://www.ramonabowl.com/ramona-returns-for-102nd-season-with-history-making-director-at-the-helm)).

**The Arias Troubadours.** José Arias Sr. and his brother-in-law Antonio Corral formed the band and were invited to play "authentic California music" for *Ramona* in 1924. A 1948 photo shows José Sr. with his sons Alfonso and José Jr. on violin, nephew Antonio Corral on guitarrón and Rafael Gama on accordion ([academia.edu article](https://www.academia.edu/29827810/Mexican_Folk_Music_and_Theater_in_Early_Twentieth_Century_Southern_California_The_Ramona_Pageant_and_the_Mexican_Players)). José Jr. joined on violin at 12 and played until 2009. Alfonso played every season from 1939, and the group was "a mainstay at the Mission Inn in Riverside during the late 1950s and most of the 1960s" ([Press-Enterprise 2011](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2011/04/28/hemet-ramona-dedicated-to-memory-of-longtime-troubadour/)). A fourth generation was still playing in 2014 ([Press-Enterprise 2014](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2014/04/03/hemet-arias-troubadours-continue-8216ramona8217-tradition/)). For a fiddler, this is the IE's most important lineage: twin violins playing a frozen early-California dance and song repertoire for a century.

**Western, country and the High Desert.** Pioneertown was built in 1946 as a movie set for Roy Rogers and Gene Autry. Its Cantina, in 1982, became **Pappy & Harriet's**, where Harriet sang country standards with Pappy and their granddaughter ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappy_%26_Harriet%27s)). The **Roy Rogers–Dale Evans Museum** was in Apple Valley and then Victorville (1976–2003) before decamping to Branson, where it closed in 2009 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Rogers-Dale_Evans_Museum)). San Bernardino's **Swing Auditorium** (1949–1981) hosted the Dillards among many others until a plane hit it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_Auditorium)). The **Brandin' Iron**, San Bernardino's country saloon, closed unexpectedly on Nov 27, 2023 ([SB Sun](https://www.sbsun.com/2023/11/30/taylor-swift-and-willie-nelson-played-there-but-san-bernardinos-brandin-iron-is-now-closed/)). Gram Parsons died at the Joshua Tree Inn in September 1973, and "Gram Fest" ran at Joshua Tree from 1996 to 2006 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_Parsons)). I found no published history of Dust Bowl migrant music specific to the IE; the Okie country story is told for Bakersfield and LA, not Fontana or Riverside. That gap is worth chasing.

**The bluegrass boom (1973–2016).** Southern California bluegrass grew out of UCLA's 1963 festival and the Topanga Banjo-Fiddle Contest, but its big campground festivals were in the IE. In 1973 Dick Tyner brought Bill Monroe and Lester Flatt to the first **Golden West Bluegrass Music Festival** in Norco. Don Tucker's **Calico Ghost Town Spring Bluegrass Festival** "traditionally draw[s] between 10,000 and 12,000 people" and had 24,000 in 1976, with clogging and instrumental competitions on 250 acres north of Barstow ([LA Times 1988](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-05-07-vw-2460-story.html)). The Tuckers' **Huck Finn Jubilee** (1976), held mostly at Mojave Narrows Park in Victorville, was named IBMA Bluegrass Event of the Year in 2001 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huck_Finn_Jubilee)). After Don Tucker died in 2012 it went to Ontario (2013–16), was dropped by the city, revived by new owners in 2018, moved to San Dimas in LA County in 2022, and announced "a one-year pause" for 2026 ([Huck Finn](https://huckfinn.com/)). What remains inside the MSA is a one-day **Calico Bluegrass Festival** in May ([SB County Parks](https://parks.sbcounty.gov/event/calico-bluegrass-festival/)) and Blythe's **Desert Bluegrass Festival** (March 13–15, 2026; [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/groups/blythebluegrass/)). The Temecula Valley Bluegrass Festival was revived in 2017–18 but shows no 2024–26 evidence ([Bluegrass Today](https://bluegrasstoday.com/temecula-valley-bluegrass-festival-returns-in-2017/)).

**Revival, recognition, rebound (1980s–2026).** The 1980s bird-song revival, the Malki Kéwet (since 1966) and the powwow circuit (UCR c.1984, San Manuel 1996) were built by Native people largely without outside folklore institutions. ACTA apprenticeships came later (2002, 2008). Lalo Guerrero, the "father of Chicano music", came to front a club in Rancho Mirage in 1973, settled in Palm Desert and became the IE's only NEA National Heritage Fellow in 1991 ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/eduardo-lalo-guerrero)). After COVID cancelled *Ramona* and moved Huck Finn online in 2020, heritage events came back strongly: the Agua Caliente museum opened in 2023, Morongo drew 30,000 in 2026, and Riverside's mariachi won a national title.

### 5. Archives and recordings

- **Hearst Museum, UC Berkeley:** Lucile Hooper's 1918 Cahuilla wax cylinders, including a "Bird Song" sung by Allejandro ([catalog](https://portal.hearstmuseum.berkeley.edu/catalog/51706f3e-1a72-48e6-85db-bb4842495294)).
- **Smithsonian NAA (J. P. Harrington):** aluminum discs including a Cahuilla "Bird Song, Quails Who Helped Blind Man" from Perfecto Segundo, collected by Adam Castillo and Blanche Seeley. Harrington's wax cylinders went to the Library of Congress folklife division in the early 1980s ([SI](https://www.si.edu/object/archives/components/sova-naa-1976-95-ref16176); [SOVA](https://sova.si.edu/record/naa.1976-95/ref16084)).
- **UCLA Ethnomusicology Archive:** *Cahuilla Bird Songs* (1987), from a student field class ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/calauem_000032)).
- **Bancroft Library:** *Mojave Indian Creation Songs* (Emmett Van Fleet, 1972), plus bird songs sung by Hubert McCord ([OAC](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8vm4m9g)).
- **Sherman Indian Museum:** 243 linear feet, partly digitized ([Calisphere](https://calisphere.org/collections/27124/)).
- **Malki Museum Library & Archive** ([Malki](https://malkimuseum.org/pages/library-archive)), **UCR Special Collections** (750+ collections; [UCR](https://library.ucr.edu/libraries/special-collections-university-archives/collections)), **CSUSB's Bridges** Black-history oral histories ([ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.lib.csusb.edu/bridges-project/)).
- **Community-made recordings:** Robert Levi's 5-CD *Desert Cahuilla Bird Songs*, Ernest Siva's *Voices of the Flute* book and CD, and the Arias Troubadours' *Music of the Ramona Pageant*.
- **Library of Congress / AFC:** API searches for Cahuilla, Serrano, Luiseño, Chemehuevi, Soboba and Morongo found **no** AFC field collections, and IE place-name hits were Veterans History Project interviews. There are no known Lomax or WPA California Folk Music Project recordings from the IE. The region's documentation came from anthropologists, not folk-song collectors.

### 6. The fiddle angle

**Contests.** A program survives for a **"California Annual Champion Fiddlers Contest & Square Dance Festival"** held Sept 7–8, 1951 at the Farmers Fair of Riverside County in Hemet ([eBay listing of the program](https://www.ebay.com/itm/257417090319); medium confidence). Calico's 1970s–80s festivals ran instrumental competitions ([LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-05-07-vw-2460-story.html)), and Blythe still runs a band contest. Today the **California State Old Time Fiddlers' Association has no district in either county**; the nearest are District 4 in Fullerton and District 3 in Tehachapi ([CSOTFA](http://csotfa.org/districts.htm)). I found no current fiddle contest in the MSA.

**Fiddlers.** The best-documented violin players in the IE's heritage music are the **Arias family** (above). **Stuart Duncan**, a Southern California native, "began playing at the Calico festivals when he was 8 or 9" and met his future Nashville Bluegrass Band partner Pat Enright at Calico in 1974 ([LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-05-07-vw-2460-story.html)). The folk duo **Mary McCaslin and Jim Ringer** lived in San Bernardino; McCaslin, an open-tuning pioneer, died in Hemet in 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_McCaslin)). Mariachi violin is the region's largest fiddle pipeline today, through Mariachi Campana, CSUSB and UCR.

**Tunes.**

- **"San Bernardino Waltz"**, Mary McCaslin, *Way Out West* ([recording listing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zl0Wl5smXKs)). It is a song, but a waltz a fiddler could pick up. It is not on The Session.
- **"Riverside" strathspey and "Riverside" jig** (Dan R. MacDonald) are **false friends**: they are named for Riverside, West Bay, Cape Breton ([TTA](https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Riverside_(1)); [The Session #17822](https://thesession.org/tunes/17822)). That's a fun one for a Cape Breton player.
- **"Ramona"** (1928) and **"Route 66"** (Barstow, San Bernardino) are popular songs, not trad tunes.
- I searched The Session's API for ~35 IE place names (Redlands, Cucamonga, Temecula, Mojave, Barstow, Joshua Tree, Big Bear, Cajon, Needles, Hemet, Chino and others) and found **no IE-named trad tunes**. The Traditional Tune Archive blocks automated search, and DuckDuckGo `site:` searches turned up nothing either.

### 7. What a visiting fiddler should actually do

- **A Saturday in late January:** Singing the Birds in Palm Springs (free, all day). Listen to the rattles: a rhythmic tradition as tight as any session.
- **Memorial Day Sunday:** Malki Kéwet in Banning, with bird singing, fry bread and veterans honoring.
- **Late September:** Morongo powwow (bird singing and dancing contest Saturday afternoon, peon hand game at dusk), the San Manuel powwow the weekend before, and Calico Days.
- **Early July or Labor Day:** a Basque picnic in Chino ($30 dinner).
- **Nov 1–2:** Riverside Day of the Dead; look for folklórico and Mariachi Campana.
- **Spring weekends:** *Ramona* in Hemet (about $32 in 2011), to hear what's left of the Arias tradition.
- **A Tuesday night:** heritage music doesn't run weekly here. For a session or jam, see the layer-1 thread; this thread found no weekly heritage-music event in the MSA.

**Geography:** scattered. The Native tradition is anchored in the San Gorgonio Pass (Banning/Cabazon), Palm Springs, Thermal, Anza, Riverside (Sherman, UCR) and Needles. Mexican traditions are everywhere, densest in Riverside, San Bernardino and the eastern Coachella Valley. Basque life is in Chino, and Western and bluegrass survivals are in the High Desert and Blythe.

## Surprises

- The region's signature living tradition isn't fiddling but Cahuilla bird singing: a line of men with palm-seed gourd rattles, women dancing as one body, and a 300+ song migration cycle sung dusk to dawn. It nearly vanished by the 1980s; in 2023 the Torres Martinez chairman counted 200+ singers in just four sister bands.
- Coachella opened in 2022 and 2023 with a Cahuilla bird-song land acknowledgment, and some bird singers refused to take part: 'not to glorify what we're doing. It's for our people.'
- A metro of 4.8 million has exactly one Irish session: biweekly, October to April, Saturday lunch at a Palm Desert golf course, mixing Irish, Scottish, Cape Breton and Norwegian tunes.
- There is no contra dance, old-time jam, shape-note singing or CSOTFA fiddlers' district anywhere in the two counties. In 1970 the Southern California Old-Time Fiddlers' Association declined to join the state association and then split into old-time and 'amplified country and Western' factions.
- The most-scheduled trad dance form is modern club square dancing, coordinated by the 'Cow Counties' Hoedown Association, named after the 19th-century nickname for these ranching counties.
- The region's oldest continuous fiddle lineage is Californio: the Arias Troubadours' twin violins have played the Ramona pageant in Hemet since 1924, with a repertoire that 'has not changed in nearly 90 years'.
- The IE was once SoCal's bluegrass festival capital. Golden West in Norco brought Monroe and Flatt in 1973, Calico Ghost Town drew 24,000 in 1976 (where 8-year-old Stuart Duncan started playing festivals), and Huck Finn in Victorville was IBMA Event of the Year in 2001. Huck Finn has since moved to San Dimas (LA Co.) and paused for 2026, and Calico's festival was renamed 'Spring Jam' in 2026.
- P'urhépecha migrants from Ocumicho, Michoacán rehearse the Danza de los Ancianos each December in trailer camps on the Torres Martinez Cahuilla reservation, then process to the church in Mecca: one Indigenous people's dance on another's land.
- Mojave bird songs at Needles were partly borrowed from the Cahuilla as the Mojave's own creation songs faded (Llewellyn Barrackman, Bancroft finding aid).
- Chino, now a warehouse suburb, still has two Basque clubs (1946 and 1968), a family fronton, a Klika drum-and-bugle corps and an irrintzi (Basque yell) contest.
- Riverside's youth Mariachi Campana won the 2026 Mariachi Nationals. A claim that it was the 'first California ensemble ever' to win was retracted the next day.
- AI search summaries confidently named a Cahuilla bird singer a 1995 NEA Heritage Fellow. The NEA's own list has exactly one IE-resident fellow, Lalo Guerrero (1991, Cathedral City), the 'father of Chicano music'.
- Big Bear built a convention center shaped like a beer tent in 1976 just to house its Oktoberfest, now in its 56th season.
- LA's shape-note group domain, still linked from the national directory, now redirects to a gambling spam site, so IE singers following the directory hit a dead end.

## Curiosities for phase two

1. Is the IE's density of about 8 recurring trad events per million residents per month the lowest of the 20 metros? Is the 'bedroom metro' effect (players commute to LA/OC/Claremont jams) visible in other exurban MSAs?
2. Do other metros have a revived Native song genre with its own festival (like Singing the Birds) rather than only powwows? Compare Phoenix (O'odham waila, Yaqui), San Diego (Kumeyaay bird songs) and Seattle (Coast Salish).
3. Why did the 1970s-2000s bluegrass festival belt (Norco, Calico, Victorville) leave the IE: organizer deaths, county-park policy, heat, or an aging audience? Did Phoenix-area festivals (e.g. Wickenburg) follow the same arc?
4. Do retirement and snowbird metros (IE, Phoenix, Tampa, Orlando) sustain modern club square dancing and senior international folk dance better than young metros, and do their sessions show the same Nov-Apr seasonality as Palm Desert?
5. Is school mariachi the real folk-instrumental pipeline in Latino-majority metros? Which cities' youth programs win Mariachi Nationals (Riverside vs San Antonio vs Tucson vs Phoenix)?
6. Why no contra or ECD revival in a metro with UC Riverside, the University of Redlands and Cal State San Bernardino? What did Sacramento or Phoenix have that the IE lacks?
7. Are there other century-old pageant bands like the Arias Troubadours (Santa Barbara Fiesta, San Gabriel's Mission Play), and is the Arias family still playing Ramona in 2026?
8. Do Basque communities elsewhere (Boise, Reno, Bakersfield, San Francisco) show the same two-club split and picnic calendar as Chino?
9. Why is there no Obon inside the IE when LA County has about 16 each summer? Where do IE Japanese-American families dance?
10. What did ISOMATA's 1950s Idyllwild folk workshops actually contain, and did they feed the LA revival?
11. Does 'mythic site' tourism (Gram Parsons in Joshua Tree, Pioneertown) ever produce participatory music, or only listening? Compare Okemah and Clarksdale.
12. Has the Calico contest tradition (fiddle, banjo, guitar, band and clogging, $2,500 in prizes in 1988) moved anywhere, or does SoCal now have no public fiddle contest south of Fullerton?

## Data quality and caveats

Web search was heavily rate-limited (several research threads shared a quota), and Facebook, Instagram and Meetup were mostly unreadable, so jams and clubs organized only on social media are probably undercounted, especially in Hemet/Menifee, the High Desert and the Coachella Valley. The Cow Counties Hoedown member roster is JavaScript-only, so square dancing in the retirement towns is undercounted. The Southwest Bluegrass Association site blocks fetches (a June 2025 Wayback capture was used). The Riverside ECD (englishcountrydancing.org, 1st/4th Sundays) has a live but undated site and is NOT counted. The Temecula and Yucca Valley bluegrass jams are low confidence. Sources disagree on Huck Finn's founding year (1974/1976/1977/1978) and its move to Ontario (2013 vs 2014). Calico attendance figures are 1988 organizer claims. Purépecha material is mostly 2011-2019 journalism, and the Arias Troubadours at Ramona are confirmed only to 2014. Annual heritage events are prorated at 1/12 a month in recurring_events_per_month_total. The NEA check scanned all 53 California fellows on arts.gov. No Library of Congress/AFC field collections from the IE were found by API search. Son jarocho, Oaxacan, Filipino, Vietnamese, Hmong, Armenian, Dutch and Obon traditions were NOT FOUND, which is not proof of absence. No dedicated acoustic/folk instrument shop or folk luthier was verified inside the two counties (the functional folk shop is Claremont's Folk Music Center, in LA Co.). Claremont and San Dimas items are flagged outside_msa and belong to the LA count. Overlap with LA: the Back Abbey session, Folk Music Center open mic and Claremont Israeli dance appear in both files; count them only for LA.
