# Miami–Fort Lauderdale–West Palm Beach (MSA 33100): a folk-scene dossier

*Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach counties. Compiled 30 Oct 2026 from four research threads (trad instrumental and dance; song, media, shops and instruction; heritage and diaspora; history and people). Public web sources only; nothing was played.*

## 1. Scene in a nutshell

For a fiddler, South Florida is thin, seasonal and tilted north. A metro of roughly 6 million people has one weekly Irish session ([Tim Finnegan's, Delray Beach](https://thesession.org/sessions/3406)), one seasonal one ([McCarthy's, Tequesta](https://thesession.org/sessions/9621)) and no Irish session at all in Miami-Dade or Broward. The best weekly fiddle night is an old-time jam that has run since about 1996 and now meets in a national violin shop in Pompano Beach ([Oldtime Jammers](https://www.oldtimejammers.com/)). Around that sit a 1971-vintage bluegrass association, one healthy monthly contra in Boca Raton, more modern western squares than contras, and plenty of Israeli folk dancing. Snowbirds set the calendar: sessions swell from November to April and some shut for the summer. But the region's real folk life is Caribbean and Latin American: Haitian konpa and folkloric drumming, Bahamian Junkanoo, Trinidadian steelpan, Afro-Cuban batá. Its fiddle claim to fame is a train song: "Orange Blossom Special" was written by a fiddler who lived and died here.

## 2. Ten most interesting things

1. **One Irish session within 60 km of Fort Lauderdale, none near Miami.** thesession.org's "nearby" search returns only Tim Finnegan's in Delray Beach ([API: Fort Lauderdale](https://thesession.org/sessions/nearby?latlon=26.1,-80.2&radius=60&format=json), [API: Miami](https://thesession.org/sessions/nearby?latlon=25.77,-80.19&radius=60&format=json)).
2. **Irish trad runs on snowbird time.** McCarthy's session "ended for the summer" on 31 May 2026 and restarted on 10 Sep ([thesession 9621](https://thesession.org/sessions/9621)). Tim Finnegan's tunes "crank up to speed… November to April" with visitors from Boston, Chicago, New York, Canada, Ireland and England ([thesession 3406](https://thesession.org/sessions/3406)).
3. **The longest-running trad jam is old-time, in a violin shop.** It started around 1996 in Boca and now meets every Thursday at the [Fiddlershop](https://fiddlershop.com/pages/about-us) in Pompano Beach, a national online violin retailer with 12 luthiers ([Oldtime Jammers](https://www.oldtimejammers.com/)).
4. **"Orange Blossom Special" is a Miami tune by residence.** Its writer, North Carolina fiddler Ervin T. Rouse, bought a Miami house in 1938 and died in Miami-Dade in 1981 after years "fiddling for tips in isolated taverns on the edge of the Everglades" ([NCpedia](https://www.ncpedia.org/rouse-ervin-t)).
5. **One of Ireland's leading fiddlers lives in Miami.** James Kelly moved from Dublin in 1984, won TG4's Gradam Ceoil in 2006 and has composed more than 800 tunes ([Museum of Miami](https://museumofmiami.org/south-florida-folklife-center/artist-in-residence-programs/james-kelly/)). None of the session listings found mention him.
6. **Squares beat contra four weekly nights to one monthly dance**, including challenge-level C-1/C-2 and the LGBTQ+ South Florida Mustangs (1977) ([Mustangs](http://www.southfloridamustangs.org/)).
7. **The 1930s collectors worked here.** Lomax, Hurston and Barnicle recorded in Belle Glade in 1935 ([LOC guide](http://international.loc.gov/folklife/guides/Florida.html)), and in 1940 the WPA recorded Bahamian "Conch" songs in Riviera, now Riviera Beach ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000080/)).
8. **Joni Mitchell was "discovered" in Coconut Grove**, when Bobby Ingram took David Crosby to the Gaslight South in 1967 ([Boca Mag](https://www.bocamag.com/when-coconut-grove-was-groovy/)).
9. **The heritage scene dwarfs the Anglo-Celtic one.** Sounds of Little Haiti draws 800–1,200 people a month ([Haitian Times](https://haitiantimes.com/2025/09/19/sounds-of-little-haiti-10-year-anniversary/)), and Carnival drew 100,000+ in 2025 ([jamaicans.com](https://jamaicans.com/miami-broward-carnival-2025-was-a-time-caribbean-culture-colour-and-unity-in-south-florida/)).
10. **No regular shape-note singing anywhere in South Florida.** The first seed singing was held in Miami Shores on 10 Oct 2026 ([listing](https://shapenotesingings.com/singings/miami-shores-half-day-singing)).

> Note: the final `miami.json` deduplicates slightly differently (name aliases plus venue/day/category matching) and counts **59 in-MSA recurring events, about 147 events a month** (23 high, 25 medium, 11 low confidence). Use the JSON `headline_stats` as the canonical figures.

**How to read it.** Strict fiddle-tune sessions (Irish, old-time and the Jupiter Celtic session) come to about **20 a month**, the same figure the trad-dance thread reached independently. Leave out the open mics (only 8 of the roughly 30 SFFAMC listings were captured), the generic song-list jams and the youth mariachi program, and the total falls to about 95 a month. Outside the MSA, but treated as local: Long Key Squares, the Florida Folk Festival and the FOTMC.

**Snowbird seasonality.** Winter is high season for the Anglo-American scene: McCarthy's runs September–May, Tim Finnegan's peaks November–April, the Broward song swap is outdoors only November–March ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/broward-folk-club/)), Ira Weisburd's dances are "seasonal only" ([gifd](https://www.gifd.org/event.aspx?idevnt=502)), and the big Anglo folk events cluster in December–February. The Haitian class runs September–June, and the Caribbean festivals take late spring to autumn. The Boca contra is the main thing that runs all year.

## 4. A week in South Florida for a visiting fiddler

**Geography first.** The MSA is a coastal strip about 100–110 miles long along I-95 and US-1. Trad activity sits in two belts:

- the **north belt**: Tequesta and Jupiter through Lake Worth Beach, Boynton, Delray and Boca;
- the **middle belt**: Pompano through Fort Lauderdale, Wilton Manors and Hollywood to North Miami / North Miami Beach.

Central and south Miami-Dade have almost no fiddle activity. Their folk life is heritage: Little Haiti, Little Havana, Coconut Grove and Homestead. Allow 60–90 minutes in traffic from Miami to Delray. If you're here for tunes, base yourself in Broward or south Palm Beach County.

**Monday.** Jupiter library old-time jam (11 am, free); A-Jacks Advanced squares in Boynton (2 pm, $6); the Boca Acoustic Jam (6 pm); on the 2nd Monday, the ukulele sing-along at the Broward Main Library; in season, Ira Weisburd's Israeli night at Kings Point ($10).

**Tuesday: the weak night for fiddlers.** There is no fiddle session in the MSA. Instead you can:
- do Scottish country dancing in Sunrise (7–9 pm) or Israeli dancing at Soref JCC (from 7:30);
- watch challenge-level squares in Wilton Manors;
- sing at My Mama's open mic in Dania Beach.

The nearest Tuesday Irish session is outside the MSA, at the Kilted Mermaid in Vero Beach (1st and 3rd Tuesdays). From January to March, Long Key Squares in the Keys stops dancing for every sunset ($10).

**Wednesday.** Penny Lane beginner bluegrass jam (6 pm; check whether it is weekly or April-only); the Luna Star jam (7 pm); on alternate Wednesdays, the Boca acoustic jam in Deerfield Beach.

**Thursday: the best fiddle night.** The Oldtime Jammers play at the Fiddlershop, Pompano Beach, from 7 to 9 pm, free, year-round; the shop's showroom takes walk-ins. In season, McCarthy's Irish session in Tequesta starts at 5 pm on the 2nd and 4th Thursdays, but it's roughly 50 miles north, so you can't comfortably do both. Also on Thursdays: Temple Beth Am Rikudim ($15), the Mustangs' squares, Luna Star's occasional "Celtic Thursdays" with Gaelica, and on the 4th Thursday Blanche Ely's $10 living-room concert.

**Friday**
- Castoffs squares in Boynton, 6–9 pm ($6).
- 3rd Friday: Sounds of Little Haiti (konpa).
- One Friday a month: Viernes Culturales on Calle Ocho.

**Saturday: the best all-round day.**
- 1–2:30 pm: Haitian folkloric dance with live drums in Little Haiti (September–June, $0–15).
- 1–5 pm: Rudy's Pickers in Lake Worth Beach.
- 2–4 pm: the SFBA jam at the Hollywood farm, or on alternate Saturdays the advanced bluegrass jam at Penny Lane.
- 1st Saturday: the Miami old-time jam at Lost City Brewing from 1 pm, then the Acoustic Underground concert at Luna Star ($8/$10).
- 3rd Saturday: the SFCDS contra in Boca at 7 pm ($15). The band is booked, so you'll dance, but ask about sitting in.

**Sunday: the Irish day.**
- 1–4 pm: Tim Finnegan's in Delray, best November–April.
- The Jupiter Celtic / old-time / Portland Collection session (ask Sheryl via FSFA).
- 1st Sunday: bluegrass festival and jam at Greynolds Park ($10 per car).
- 3rd Sunday: the Broward song swap (2–5 pm, by the lake in winter).
- 2nd Sunday: the Miami-Dade swap and potluck (5–9 pm).
- 3–5 pm: Michael Stock's *Folk & Acoustic Music* on WLRN 91.3 on the car radio.

**Saturday versus Tuesday.** On a Saturday you can play bluegrass, old-time (1st Saturday) or pickers'-circle music in the afternoon, hear Haitian drumming at lunchtime, and contra dance (3rd Saturday) in the evening. On a Tuesday there is no fiddle music at all, only dancing: Scottish, Israeli or challenge squares.

**Winter versus summer.** November–April is peak season: McCarthy's runs, Tim Finnegan's has visitors, the song swap is outdoors, Kings Point dances run, and the big events land (Everglades Bluegrass in December, the fiddle contest in January, the folk festival and Seminole Fair around February 1, SASSF's festival in March). In summer McCarthy's stops, the swap goes indoors and the Haitian class breaks, but the Oldtime Jammers, SFBA jams and Boca contra carry on, and the Caribbean festivals arrive (Goombay in June, Goombay Broward in July, IFE-ILE in August).

**The folk festival** began with one stage and one day at Easterlin Park, Oakland Park ([New Times 2025](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/music/south-florida-folk-music-festival-returns-with-sarah-lee-guthrie-22203722/)), then moved to Markham Park (2008, after a two-year gap; [Sun-Sentinel](https://www.sun-sentinel.com/2008/01/11/folk-festival-makes-a-comeback/)), Hugh Taylor Birch State Park, and Bergeron Rodeo Grounds (2020). It has two stages (Gator and Will McLean), workshops, jams and songwriter contests. Headliners were Mean Mary, Livingston Taylor and John McCutcheon in 2026, and Sarah Lee Guthrie in 2025.

**Fiddle contests.** The South Florida Fair contest is the only one in the MSA, with Junior, Youth and Adult divisions. Its [rules](https://www.southfloridafair.com/p/south-florida-fair/getinvolved/competitions/fiddle-contest) say "the Devil went down to Georgia" is not a traditional fiddle tune while "Soldier's Joy" and "Cumberland Gap" are, and they welcome Irish and other "culturally authentic" fiddling. The official state contest ([F.S. 15.048](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/15.048)) is run by the FSFA at the Florida Folk Festival, about 450 miles away.

**Camps.** There is no residential fiddle or folk camp in the MSA. The closest are Fiddle Heaven, the SSBF camp and the festival workshops.

## 10. Shops and makers

- **[Fiddlershop](https://fiddlershop.com/pages/about-us), Pompano Beach.** Founded in 2012 by Michael "Fiddlerman" Holstein and his son Pierre. A national online violin shop with a showroom that takes walk-ins, 12 professional luthiers and "over 6,000 instruments a year" set up. It is the metro's most important fiddle business and the home of its best jam.
- **[Penny Lane Music Emporium](https://pennylaneemporium.com/), Fort Lauderdale.** Claims South Florida's widest "selection of Banjos, Mandolins, Ukuleles", plus violins and violas. Also a school and concert room.
- **[Village Music Wellington](https://www.villagemusicwellington.com/).** Sales, repair, rentals and school; "Best New Dealer in the USA (2014)".
- **Luthiers.** Mike Long built guitars and donated some to the SFBA ([SFBA history](https://sfbahistory.org/)). No fiddle maker beyond the Fiddlershop's in-house luthiers was named.
- **Steelpan.** [Michael Kernahan](https://www.thebash.com/caribbean-steel-drum/michael-kernahan) (tuner and maker; 21st Century Steel Band), Steel Pan Maestro, and Steel Band Culture in Lake Worth (low confidence).
- **Drums and beadwork.** Ezequiel Torres builds batá and makes shekerés and beaded drum covers. Romeo Ragbir is a tassa drum maker. Mannolie DiSantos makes Lukumí ritual beadwork.
- **Gaps.** No Miami builder is in the [Cuban tres luthier directory](http://www.cubantresguitar.com/tres-luthiers.html) (the nearest is in Tampa), and no Haitian drum maker was verified.
- **Gone.** [Sam Ash](https://www.wpbf.com/article/florida-sam-ash-music-stores-to-close-stores/60683880) closed its Margate, Miami Lakes and other Florida stores in 2024.

## 12. Heritage and diaspora traditions

This is the heart of the metro. In 1987 Tina Bucuvalas wrote that migration had made reggae, plena, salsa, jíbaro, Cuban guajiro and charanga, compas, calypso and soca, norteño and mariachi "a dominant force" in the region's music ([Bucuvalas 1987](https://museumofmiami.org/south-florida-folklife-center/highlighted-projects/traditions-south-florida-folklife/)).

Only three NEA Heritage Fellows have ever been listed in the metro: Cachao (1995, [NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/israel-lopez)), the Epstein Brothers (1998, klezmer, Tamarac, [NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/epstein-brothers)) and Ezequiel Torres (2010, [NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/ezequiel-torres)). The Florida Folk Heritage Award (FFHA) is a better index: about 30 recipients are tied to the metro, including most of the 2024 class ([press release](https://dos.fl.gov/communications/press-releases/2024/press-release-secretary-of-state-announces-recipients-of-the-2024-florida-folk-heritage-award/)).

**Haitian.**
- **Konpa.** [Sounds of Little Haiti](https://haitiantimes.com/2025/09/19/sounds-of-little-haiti-10-year-anniversary/) has run on third Fridays since 2015. It draws 800–1,200 people a month and 3,500+ in May, about 250,000 in all. The [Compas Festival](https://www.haitinewstoday.com/haitian-compas-festival-2025/) returned in May 2026, and UNESCO inscribed konpa in Dec 2025 ([Haitian Times](https://haitiantimes.com/2025/12/11/unesco-konpa-intangible-heritage-haiti/)).
- **Folkloric dance.** Tradisyon Lakou Lakay's class keeps going "despite the funding challenges non-profits face". Louinès and Lucrèce Louinis (FFHA 2016) are the reference dancers.
- **Vodou.** Jude "Papaloko" Thegenus (FFHA 2024), an oungan (Vodou priest) in Miami since 1987, plays late nights at the Florida Folk Festival ([FFHA](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/jude-papaloko-thegenus/)).
- **Creole culture.** [Sosyete Koukouy](https://joneljuste.com/2026/02/27/sosyete-koukouy-celebre-60-ans-de-promotion-de-la-culture-haitienne/) marked 60 years in Dec 2025. Other bearers: Jan Mapou (FFHA 2007), storyteller Liliane Nérette Louis (2006), fè koupé (cut-metal) artist Almann Ulysse (2007) and sign painter Serge Toussaint (2016).

**Cuban.**
- **Afro-Cuban.** Ezequiel Torres builds batá and came on the 1980 Mariel boatlift. [IFE-ILE](https://ife-ile.org/about-3/), founded by Neri Torres, has held a festival every summer since 1998. The Museum of Miami calls the Lukumí community "thriving" ([DiSantos page](https://museumofmiami.org/south-florida-folklife-center/artist-in-residence-programs/mannolie-disantos/)).
- **Rumba.** Mostly staged on Calle Ocho; no street or park rumba on a fixed schedule was verified.
- **Décima.** Punto (UNESCO 2017) lived in 2017 at Rancho Bejucal's televised Sunday guateques and in 2023 as Lenier Mesa's improvisation "batalla". Controversias (improvised duels) in Hialeah are unverified.
- **Big events.** Calle Ocho (1978) set a conga-line record of 119,986 in 1988; also Viernes Culturales and CubaNostalgia. These are popular events rather than folk ones.

**Bahamian.** Goombay began in 1976 or 1977; Black and white Grove residents organized it together, including co-founder Herb Hiller (d. 2025). It peaked at about 500,000 visitors, left the main thoroughfare after 2014, and was revived in 2022 under vonCarol Kinchens-Williams, drawing about 15,000 in 2024. It now runs on City of Miami money ($150,000 in 2025, $143,000 in 2026) with no private sponsors ([Spotlight](https://coconutgrovespotlight.com/2026/06/01/can-a-revived-goombay-stand-on-its-own/)).
- **Troupes:** [Fatboi Junkanoo](https://coconutgrovespotlight.com/2026/06/04/the-family-behind-fatboi-junkanoo-entertainment/) (Nassau-born Jamal Joseph, with child moko jumbies, or stilt walkers), [Junkanoo Miami](https://www.instagram.com/junkanoomiami/) and the Bahamas Junkanoo Revue (Langston Longley, FFHA 2009).
- **Moving north:** as the West Grove gentrifies, Bahamas Goombay Broward started in Miramar in 2026.

**Anglophone Caribbean.** The Miami Broward Carnival (1984) now has 18,000+ masqueraders in 20+ mas bands. The 2024 Panorama had seven steelbands, with Lauderhill Steel Ensemble the 2023 champion ([jamaicans.com](https://jamaicans.com/an-evening-of-melody-and-rhythm-at-the-2024-miami-broward-carnival-panorama/)). Romeo Ragbir (FFHA 2009) has taught tassa to children for about 29 years ([SFLCN](https://sflcn.com/caribbean-american-heritage-month-cultural-icon-romeo-ragbir-master-of-tassa-drumming/)).

**Seminole and Miccosukee.** The free [Tribal Fair & Pow Wow](https://www.semtribefair.com/seminole-tribal-fair-pow-wow-2026-with-schedule/) at Hard Rock Hollywood has patchwork clothing contests and pow wow dance and drum; it held its 50th edition in 2023 ([SMP](https://seminolemediaproductions.com/50th-anniversary-seminole-tribal-fair/)). Tradition bearers include Betty Mae Jumper (FFHA 1994) and James Billie (2019).

**Latin American.**
- **Mexican:** Homestead's conservatory calls itself "Florida's sole mariachi school"; Tomás Granado (FFHA 2007) formed a norteño band there in 1970.
- **Guatemalan Maya:** Festival Maya has marimba and the deer dance, and Q'eqchi' weaver Concepción Poou Coy Tharin received the FFHA in 2023. The Palm Beach Post reported ICE detentions in 2026.
- **Others:** bomba and plena (Rhythm Foundation), Miamibloco, [Aires Panameños](https://museumofmiami.org/south-florida-folklife-center/artist-in-residence-programs/bfap/), cuatro player [Henry Linárez](https://museumofmiami.org/south-florida-folklife-center/artist-in-residence-programs/henry-linarez-ensemble/), and Garifuna organizer [Mirta Castillo](https://www.nbcmiami.com/discover-black-heritage/south-floridas-garifuna-community-preserving-their-history-and-increasing-cultural-visibility/3247577/).

**European, Jewish and others.**
- **Ukrainian:** the [Ukrainian Dancers of Miami](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/the-ukrainian-dancers-of-miami/) (about 40 members) dance Avramenko choreographies.
- **Jewish:** KCJO calls itself "the only professional Klezmer band in south Florida". Others include Alejandra Czarny (Yiddish song), Susana Behar (Sephardic) and Myriam Eli (FFHA 2023), but there is no community klezmer jam or Yiddish circle despite the large Jewish population in Boca, Delray and Aventura.
- **Greek and Lebanese:** church festivals, including the 47th Lebanese Festival in 2026 and the 49th St. Catherine Greek Festival in 2024.
- **West African:** [Delou Africa](https://museumofmiami.org/south-florida-folklife-center/artist-in-residence-programs/delou-africa-dance-ensemble/).
- **Scottish:** no Scottish or Cape Breton fiddle session exists.

**Black Southern and cracker.** Gospel is active: the Lee Boys (sacred steel, Perrine; FFHA 2020), the Miami Soul Stirrers (1952; FFHA 2002), the Florida Fellowship Super Choir, and the Overtown Music & Arts Festival, back in 2025 after six years. Florida's documented old-time fiddle lineages (George Custer, Wayne Martin, Richard Seaman, Chubby Wise) are North and Central Florida. The local cracker heritage survives as craft: Glen Simmons' Everglades skiffs and Robert Rudd's cypress furniture.

## 13. History: from the Tequesta to 2026

**Before Miami.** The Tequesta lived on Biscayne Bay from about the 3rd century BCE; few were left by 1763 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tequesta)). The Seminole Wars pushed Seminole and Miccosukee families into the Everglades.

**Bahamian Coconut Grove.** The Grove was established in 1825 ([Boca Mag](https://www.bocamag.com/when-coconut-grove-was-groovy/)), and Bahamians settled it in the late 1800s, before Miami existed ([FIU News](https://news.fiu.edu/2024/preserving-goombay-fiu-team-receives-library-of-congress-grant-to-document-iconic-miami-festival)). Miami incorporated in 1896 with a large Black share of the founding vote (168 of 362 per Wikipedia; 162 of 368 elsewhere). Black residents were then segregated into what became Overtown, whose Lyric Theater (1913) anchored the "Little Broadway of the South". I-95 later cut Overtown apart ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtown,_Miami)).

**The train and the tune.** The *Orange Blossom Special* ran from New York to Miami from 1925. Rouse wrote the song, bought a Miami house in 1938 and recorded it with his brother Gordon in 1939. Monroe and Chubby Wise popularized it in 1942, and Cash revived it in 1965 ([NCpedia](https://www.ncpedia.org/rouse-ervin-t)).

**Hurston, Lomax and the WPA.** In June 1935 Alan Lomax, Zora Neale Hurston and Mary Elizabeth Barnicle recorded in Belle Glade and Chosen in the Palm Beach County Glades, then went on to the Bahamas. It was a Library of Congress trip, not the WPA, whatever one AI summary claimed. John Lomax returned to Belle Glade in May 1936 to record prisoners. Stetson Kennedy and Robert Cook recorded for the Florida WPA in 1939–40 and reached Riviera in January 1940 ([LOC guide](http://international.loc.gov/folklife/guides/Florida.html)); no WPA-era recordings were made in Miami itself. In 1939 Hurston sang the WPA railroad spiking songs she had learned from Max Ford, a singing liner on a construction gang near Miami around 1930.

**1940s–50s.** The Ukrainian Dancers of Miami were founded in 1941 and first performed in 1949. The South Florida Emerald Society followed in 1949, the gospel trio the Miami Soul Stirrers in 1952, and the Florida Folk Festival began in White Springs in 1953.

**Cuban exile waves.** The "Golden Exile" came in 1959–62. The Freedom Flights of 1965–73 brought about 300,000 Cubans and cemented Little Havana ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flights)), and exiles started Calle Ocho in 1978. The 1980 Mariel boatlift brought about 125,000 Cubans and about 25,000 Haitians ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariel_boatlift)), among them batá master Ezequiel Torres. Cachao settled in Miami in 1983, and the 1994 balsero (rafter) crisis added about 35,000 Cubans.

**Haitian migration.** The threads trace it mainly through people and institutions rather than numbers:
- **1980:** Mariel-era arrivals.
- **1985:** Jan Mapou founded Sosyete Koukouy in Miami.
- **1987:** Papaloko settled in Miami.
- **1990:** Libreri Mapou opened in Little Haiti.
- **1998–99:** the Compas Festival began.
- **2015:** Sounds of Little Haiti began.
- **2025:** UNESCO inscribed konpa.

**The 1960s Coconut Grove folk scene.** Vince Martin settled in the Grove in 1960 and Fred Neil followed in 1962. That year David Crosby came down by Greyhound to sleep on Bobby Ingram's couch: "Fred Neil and Vince Martin became my mentors" ([New Times 2019](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/music/things-to-do-fort-lauderdale-david-crosby-at-parker-playhouse-may-21-11167661/)). The Flick in Coral Gables (1964–74) hosted Mitchell, Crosby, Buffett, Jerry Jeff Walker and Steve Goodman ([New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/music/the-flick-miamis-legendary-music-club-and-coffeehouse-celebrates-its-50th-anniversary-6449006/)). John Sebastian's "Coconut Grove" (1966) was a tribute to Neil, and in 1967 Crosby heard Mitchell at the Gaslight South. In December 1968 the Miami Pop Festival in Hallandale drew about 100,000 to hear Mitchell, Ian & Sylvia, Flatt & Scruggs and the Junior Junkanoos ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Pop_Festival_(December_1968))); a rained-out festival there in May had inspired Hendrix's "Rainy Day, Dream Away". The Doors' March 1969 concert was "the beginning of the end", and by 1970 "the Gaslight was closed". Neil played his last show at the Old Grove Pub in 1981.

**Institutions take root (1970s–80s).**
- **1970:** Tomás Granado's norteño band formed in Homestead.
- **1971:** the SFBA was founded.
- **Early 1970s:** the Seminole Tribal Fair began.
- **1974/75:** the Miccosukee festival began.
- **1976/77:** Goombay began.
- **1977:** the Mustangs were founded.
- **1977–78:** the first Everglades Bluegrass Convention was held "in the smack middle of the Glades" with Bill Monroe; Ralph Stanley headlined the next ([SFBA history](https://sfbahistory.org/70s/70s.htm)).
- **Late 1970s:** Stock opened his Deco Gecko folk club.
- **1981:** Stock's WLRN show began, and Rouse died.
- **1983:** SASSF was founded, with a first festival at the old Crandon Park zoo.
- **1984:** James Kelly arrived, and the Carnival started.
- **1986:** the museum folklife center opened, followed by the South Florida Folklife Festival (1986–88).
- **1987:** Delou Africa was founded.

**1990s–2010s.**
- **Early 1990s:** the South Florida Folk Festival began.
- **1996:** Luna Star, the Oldtime Jammers and IFE-ILE all started, and Calle Ocho was cancelled after the Brothers to the Rescue shootdown.
- **1999:** the Bamboo Room opened.
- **2004–13:** Irish sessions came and went at O'Shea's, Luna Star and the Frog & Toad.
- **2008:** the SFBA lost its Ives Dairy home, Cachao died, and the folk festival returned from a two-year gap.
- **2012:** the Fiddlershop opened.
- **2012–21:** the Masons' contra series wandered through Davie, the Barnacle, the Miami Beach bandshell ([WLRN 2016](https://www.wlrn.org/news/2016-07-20/leave-your-stillettos-at-home-for-miami-beachs-contra-dance-nights)), Coral Gables and Boca.
- **2014:** Goombay left the main thoroughfare, and Upstairs at the Van Dyke closed.
- **2019:** Bobby Ingram died; about 600 people came to his memorial.

**COVID and the rebound.**
- **2020:** the Tim Finnegan's session paused (it was back by April 2021). Calle Ocho was cancelled in 2020 and 2021.
- **2021:** the folk festival, newly moved to Davie, went virtual.
- **2022:** Goombay was revived, the West Grove became "Little Bahamas", and the fiddle contest began.
- **2023:** SFCDS dancing returned.
- **2024:** the Bamboo Room reopened, and Sam Ash closed.
- **2025:** Luna Star changed hands; Stock's show was cut by an hour before he entered the Hall of Fame; contra returned to Miami; UNESCO inscribed konpa; HistoryMiami became the Museum of Miami ([New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/arts-culture/historymiami-is-no-more-welcome-museum-of-miami-40546867/)).
- **2026:** McCarthy's started; the Highland Games became a Celtic festival; contra rose to $15; Bahamas Goombay Broward debuted; the LOC Goombay collection went online; the SSBF held its first camp; a Sacred Harp seed singing was held in Miami Shores; the 49th Everglades Bluegrass Festival is set for December.

## 15. The fiddle angle

- **"Orange Blossom Special"** is the region's tune. Rouse wrote it after getting to know the Miami train in Florida. Chubby Wise claimed co-authorship for years, and biographer Randy Noles (*Orange Blossom Boys*, 2002) settled it in Rouse's favor. Johnny Cash later brought Rouse to Miami to play it at a Cash concert. Rouse's last years, playing for tips in Everglades bars, are the region's most poignant fiddle story ([NCpedia](https://www.ncpedia.org/rouse-ervin-t)).
- **Tunes named for the region.** A thesession name search for Miami, Florida, Biscayne, Everglades, Okeechobee, Seminole and Hialeah returned nothing. "Key West" matches ["The Key West"](https://thesession.org/tunes/13091), a hornpipe from *Kerr's Second Collection*, but whether the title means Florida is unknown. The only clearly Florida-named tune found is ["Grayton Beach"](https://thesession.org/tunes/3610) (a 2004 Panhandle tune; outside the MSA). Tunearch blocked automated searching; James Kelly's 800+ compositions are worth checking for Florida titles.
- **Where the local fiddlers are.** Irish players are at Tim Finnegan's on Sundays and old-time players at the Thursday Oldtime Jammers and the Jupiter library on Mondays. The Boca contra bands include the Pompano Jammers and Lost City String Band, and Gaelica plays Celtic sets at Luna Star and The Field. At the 2016 Miami Beach contras, Cornbread played "old time Appalachian tunes with names like Jefferson's reel" ([WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/news/2016-07-20/leave-your-stillettos-at-home-for-miami-beachs-contra-dance-nights)).
- **Contests.** South Florida Fair (in the MSA, since about 2022); the state contest and the FOTMC at White Springs (outside).
- **Violin in Latin traditions.** Cachao came out of Arcaño y sus Maravillas, a Cuban charanga, the flute-and-violins format that Bucuvalas lists among Miami's living Cuban styles ([Wikipedia: Cachao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachao)). Miami charanga violinists are an open lead.
- **No local fiddle style.** Florida's documented old-time fiddle lineages are North and Central Florida. Down here the fiddle is a transplant: snowbirds, Irish emigrants, Appalachian migrants such as Rouse, and the Fiddlershop's customers.

## 16. Character

What makes South Florida different is that the Anglo-Celtic folk world is a small, retiree-heavy, seasonal subculture inside a metro whose living folk traditions are Caribbean and Latin American. The Sun-Sentinel called the folk scene "a marvel, considering the stereotypes arrayed against it" ([2008](https://www.sun-sentinel.com/2008/01/11/folk-festival-makes-a-comeback/)). WLRN pitched contra as "something truly exotic for Miami".

**Who shows up.** Retired educators at the Delray session; snowbirds from Boston, Chicago, New York, Canada, Ireland and England in winter; daytime jammers at the Jupiter library and Sunrise senior center; a gay square-dance club nearing 50; Kings Point residents doing Israeli dances; singer-songwriters at the swaps; and UM students playing newgrass.

**Signature traditions.** For Nate's purposes these are old-time (the Thursday jam), bluegrass (the 1971 association) and the singer-songwriter song swap. For the metro as a whole they are Haitian konpa and folkloric drumming, Bahamian Junkanoo, Trinidadian steelpan and Afro-Cuban batá.

**Shape of the scene.** Activity is scattered along about 100 miles of I-95, and no single neighborhood plays the role Coconut Grove did in the 1960s. Palm Beach County is the strongest area for tunes, Broward for bluegrass and song swaps, and Miami-Dade for heritage. Gentrification keeps pushing heritage communities outward: the Bahamian West Grove toward Miramar, and Coconut Grove's bohemia to boutiques ("It's all commercial now", Crosby, 1988).

## 18. Surprises

1. Only **one** active Irish session lies within 60 km of Fort Lauderdale and none near Miami, and the only other one in the MSA shuts for the summer ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/9621)).
2. "Orange Blossom Special"'s writer owned a Miami house from 1938 and died in Miami-Dade after years fiddling for tips at the edge of the Everglades ([NCpedia](https://www.ncpedia.org/rouse-ervin-t)).
3. TG4 Gradam Ceoil winner James Kelly has lived in Miami since 1984, yet there is no Irish session south of Delray ([Museum of Miami](https://museumofmiami.org/south-florida-folklife-center/artist-in-residence-programs/james-kelly/)).
4. The longest-running trad jam (about 1996) is old-time and meets in a national violin retailer's showroom ([Oldtime Jammers](https://www.oldtimejammers.com/)).
5. Broward's folk hub is an aquaponic tilapia farm, home to both the bluegrass jam and the song swap ([SFBA](https://www.southfloridabluegrass.com)).
6. Modern western squares outnumber contra, with challenge-level C-1/C-2 and a 1977 LGBTQ+ club ([caller4u](http://www.caller4u.com/)).
7. In 1940 the WPA recorded Bahamian "Conch" songs from an 84-year-old blind man in a Riviera Beach Gospel Hall ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000080/)).
8. Hurston's WPA railroad songs came from a Miami construction-gang singing liner ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000003/)).
9. There is no regular shape-note singing in South Florida; the first seed singing was on 10 Oct 2026 ([shapenotesingings](https://shapenotesingings.com/singings/miami-shores-half-day-singing)).
10. One two-hour Sunday show is the region's only folk radio. It was cut by an hour in 2025, months before its host entered the Folk Radio Hall of Fame ([ArtsPaper](https://palmbeachartspaper.com/folk-radio-hall-of-fame-inducts-longtime-wlrn-host-stock/)).
11. Only three NEA Heritage Fellows have ever been listed in the metro, and none is Seminole, Miccosukee, Haitian or Bahamian ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/ezequiel-torres)).
12. Two genres practised here are on UNESCO lists: Cuban punto (2017) and Haitian konpa (2025) ([Haitian Times](https://haitiantimes.com/2025/12/11/unesco-konpa-intangible-heritage-haiti/)).
13. Goombay once drew about 500,000 and now survives on about $143,000 of city money ([Spotlight](https://coconutgrovespotlight.com/2026/06/01/can-a-revived-goombay-stand-on-its-own/)).
14. Florida's only mariachi conservatory teaches 300 farmworkers' children a year free in Homestead ([WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/arts-culture/2024-11-06/making-mariachi-cool-floridas-only-conservatory-in-homestead-shares-music-mexican-american-pride)).
15. Jupiter and Tequesta have more trad-tune sessions than all of Miami-Dade ([FSFA](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/regular-jams/)).
16. There is no shanty sing in a maritime metro, no klezmer jam in a heavily Jewish one, and no Miami builder in the Cuban tres directory ([tres directory](http://www.cubantresguitar.com/tres-luthiers.html)).
17. Flatt & Scruggs and the Junior Junkanoos shared the 1968 Miami Pop Festival bill with Joni Mitchell ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Pop_Festival_(December_1968))).

## 19. Curiosities

1. Fiddle-tune sessions per million: Miami has about 20 a month for about 6 million people. How do DC, Boston and Seattle compare? Is this the thinnest large US metro for participatory trad?
2. Northern sessions slump in summer, while Miami's surge in winter. Could thesession comment dates measure how much snowbirds lift Florida's winter sessions?
3. The square-to-contra ratio here is four weekly nights to one monthly dance. Is that a retiree effect, compared with contra-dominated northern metros?
4. There are three old-time jams to two Irish sessions here, the reverse of most northeastern metros. Is the Fiddlershop the reason?
5. Why does the Irish session sit in Delray and Tequesta rather than Miami? Map Irish-American population against session locations across metros.
6. Which other big metros have no regular Sacred Harp singing, and will the Miami Shores seed take root?
7. Does heritage teaching (Haitian drum, pan, tassa, mariachi) outnumber fiddle teaching here more than in New York or Houston?
8. Miami's Panorama had seven steelbands. How does that compare with Brooklyn's Labor Day Panorama and Toronto's?
9. Little Haiti versus Flatbush: which keeps more weekly participatory Haitian practice?
10. Folk radio here is two hours a week for about 6 million people. How do Boston, Seattle and DC compare?
11. Both of the region's big folk and Celtic festivals use a rodeo ground. How common are livestock venues elsewhere?
12. Israeli dance thrives but there is no klezmer jam. How does that compare with New York or Philadelphia?
