# San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont: the folk scene (dossier, October 2026)

*Metro: San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont MSA (CBSA 41860), population 4.63 million: San Francisco, Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin and San Mateo counties. San Jose/Palo Alto (Santa Clara County), Santa Cruz, Sonoma, Napa and Solano are outside the MSA; things there that the scene treats as its own are noted and flagged `outside_msa` in the JSON. Research done with web search and fetch only, as of October 2026. Structured data: `san-francisco.json`. Part 1 is the synthesis; Part 2 has five detailed chapters (sessions, dance, song, heritage, history) with inline sources.*

## Scene in a nutshell

The Bay Area's folk scene is old, centred on the East Bay, and unusually plural. Berkeley is the hub: the Freight (1968, now a $5M nonprofit hall and school), the Starry Plough's Sunday Irish sessions (up to 25 players), Ashkenaz, a weekly Monday Sacred Harp singing, and BACDS contra and English dances. San Francisco adds Changs (1938, among the oldest folk dance clubs in the US), the country's oldest public chantey sing (1981), the Plough and Stars Irish pub (1975), Hardly Strictly Bluegrass's free weekend in Golden Gate Park, and a pioneering LGBTQ+ square dance lineage. Under that sit heritage traditions few metros match: Cantonese opera since 1852, the North American birthplace of taiko, bon odori at six temples, kulintang, zydeco brought by WWII shipyard migrants, son jarocho seeded by Los Cenzontles, West African dance and Balinese gamelan. The weak spots: contra shrank after COVID, old-time jams are few, and losing space is the constant threat.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **Americans' international folk dancing started here, with a Chinese-American artist.** In February 1938 Song Chang, who had learned Swedish dances in a Pacific Avenue basement, taught three couples schottisches and waltzes in a North Beach studio. Out of that grew the Folk Dance Federation of California (1942/43) and as many as 400 clubs statewide. Changs still dances every Friday in Miraloma Park for $6–8 ([Changs history](http://changsfolkdancers.blogspot.com/2008/01/history-of-folk-dance-movement-in.html)). Berkeley Folk Dancers (1941) dances seven nights a week and has been free for anyone under 30 since 2013 ([BFD](https://berkeleyfolkdancers.org/more-facts-about-us/)).
2. **The rutabaga contest.** Berkeley's first Old Time Fiddlers' Convention (1968, Provo Park) was "conceived in the back of a Volkswagen bus" and billed as the "35th Annual". First prize was 3 lb of rutabagas and second prize 5 lb; judges took bribes in booze; a 40-piece band was "disqualified for unlawful assembly." Mike Seeger came and recorded the Folkways LP *Berkeley Farms* ([BOTMC](https://www.berkeleyoldtimemusic.org/berkeley-farms-folkways-lp)). Suzy Thompson revived it in 2003/04 as today's Berkeley Old Time Music Convention. San Diego's 1970s Balboa Park contests also gave rutabagas (see the San Diego dossier).
3. **Cooley's Reel is a Bay Area tune.** Tune #1 on thesession.org is named for Galway box player Joe Cooley, who in his last American years adopted a crowd of long-haired Berkeley players ("the sunshine between the notes"). His partner Kevin Keegan played nightly at the Plough and Stars and died in SF on Dec 20, 1978, five years to the day after Cooley ([CCÉ](https://sfcooleykeegancce.org/misc/cooley-keegan_story.html); [Joe Cooley Tapes](https://joecooleytapes.org/)).
4. **thesession.org undercounts the scene by more than half.** It shows 9 Irish sessions inside the MSA; volunteer calendars (CelticSF, SFFMC, Bluegrass Signal) show 19 Irish session series and about 43 sessions a month. Four volunteer-kept calendars are the real infrastructure ([CelticSF](https://sites.google.com/site/davbrown2/celtic); [SFFMC calendar](https://www.sffmc.org/community-folk-calendar/)).
5. **The oldest public chantey sing in the US.** It has run at the SF Maritime National Historical Park since 1981. For decades it went 8 pm to midnight aboard the 1890 ferry *Eureka*, under three rules ("no drinking, no smoking, no falling off the pier"), and always opened with "Away Rio" ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/chantey-sing-san-francisco); [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/safr/learn/news/thirtysing.htm)). At the SFFMC's 50th Free Folk Festival in July 2026, "Sea Shanty was the best attended workshop."
6. **A cradle of LGBTQ+ square dancing.** Foggy City Squares was performing by 1981, "one of only a handful of openly gay square dance groups in the United States". The International Association of Gay Square Dance Clubs was started in SF in 1983. Western Star still dances Thursdays at the Harvey Milk Recreation Center ([Foggy City](https://foggycity.org/about-foggy-city-dancers/)). Today every BACDS dance uses gender-neutral calling, and a queer-led English dance (Improper English) and East Bay Square's "dance any role" policy are the scene's youngest edge.
7. **The 1938 WPA recordings.** Sidney Robertson's WPA California Folk Music Project, run from Berkeley, recorded Molokan hymns on Potrero Hill (she found the congregation by hearing children sing "She'll be driving six white horses" through a basement window), a Croatian winegrower's homemade gusle in San Mateo, and almost 40 Azorean songs from Oakland radio host Alice Lemos Avila ([AFC Story Map](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/29d82f51db8848d5b62790c0854ee102)).
8. **Woodstock paid for a folk label's building.** Country Joe gave Chris Strachwitz the publishing rights to the "Fixin'-to-Die Rag" as a courtesy, and the Woodstock royalties bought Arhoolie's El Cerrito building. Strachwitz became the first NEA "keeper of tradition" Heritage Fellow (2000) ([Arhoolie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arhoolie_Records); [NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/chris-strachwitz)).
9. **A state-funded heritage engine.** Eleven of the 20 mentors in ACTA's 2025 statewide apprenticeship cohort live in the five counties. One pair is a Cantonese opera grandfather teaching his grandson at Jing Ying Institute in Chinatown, where opera has been staged since the first Cantonese opera in America on Sansome Street on Oct 18, 1852 ([ACTA 2025](https://actaonline.org/2025apprenticeships/); [Hong Fook Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Fook_Tong_Chinese_Dramatic_Company)).
10. **Free, and fragile.** Almost all participatory folk here is free: sessions, Sacred Harp, the chantey sing, SFFMC song circles and festivals, and Hardly Strictly Bluegrass (an estimated 750,000 people in 2011, billionaire-funded and without sponsors). The institutions are under strain, though. The Freight ran a ~$940K deficit in FY2023 ([Berkeleyside](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2024/03/25/berkeleys-freight-salvage-attempts-rebound-1-million-dollar-deficit)), and the SFFMC, after losing its church hall, now meets in a member's flat with 12–16 people, down from the ~100 who crowded Faith Petric's house.

Runners-up: the 75th Kolo Festival (Thanksgiving 2026) came six months after its founder "Kolo John" Filcich died at 101. A Basque bertsolari session in South SF in Oct 2026 improvised verses on "using driverless taxis." Berkeley Morris's bear hobby-horse Lucy was stolen along with a member's car in 2024 and is still missing. Ashkenaz's maple floor came from a San Leandro pencil factory.

## Character: what makes the Bay distinct

**The East Bay is the centre of gravity, not San Francisco.** Berkeley holds the Freight, the Starry Plough, Ashkenaz, La Peña, the Hillside Club, Berkeley Folk Dancers, the Monday Sacred Harp singing, the BACDS Wednesday contra and English dances, Lark in the Morning's shop, the Berkeley Old Time Music Convention, and Arhoolie and Down Home Music in El Cerrito next door. Historically, alcohol limits within a mile of the UC campus "insured that jazz and nightclubs stayed in San Francisco, leaving folk music and strong coffee" to Berkeley ([Jabberwock history](https://www.chickenonaunicycle.com/Jabberwock%20History.htm)). San Francisco's strengths are its Irish pubs in the Richmond and Sunset, the maritime park, Changs, the Mission (Polish Club contra, Feral Friday squares, Carnaval, Día de los Muertos) and Chinatown/Japantown. Marin (San Rafael contra, Kopachka, Mill Valley bluegrass) and the Peninsula (Menlo Park folk dance, Half Moon Bay sessions, Basque South SF) are lively satellites, and Fremont, Hayward and the Tri-Valley carry the South Asian, Afghan, square dance and Scottish Games scenes.

**Longevity is the signature.** Changs (1938), Berkeley Folk Dancers (1941), the SFFMC (1948) and its *folknik* (1964), the Caledonian Club's Highland Games (1866, the 160th held in 2026), the Starry Plough's Monday céilí (1980), the Freight (1968), Kitka and Gamelan Sekar Jaya (both 1979), Berkeley Morris (1977), the chantey sing (1981) and Peter Thompson's *Bluegrass Signal* (on air since about 1975). Few US metros have this many 40-to-85-year-old participatory institutions still operating.

**The signature tradition is breadth.** If one tradition defines the Bay, it is international folk dance, which was born here. For a fiddler the core is the Irish session circuit built on Cooley and Keegan, plus a bluegrass network that is the busiest by monthly slots. On the heritage side, Japanese bon odori and taiko, zydeco and son jarocho are the most participatory.

**Who shows up.** No Bay Area demographic survey of dancers or session players was found. The legacy institutions skew older: the SFFMC's song circles draw 12–16 people, and the Freight's co-chair said in 2024 that "the real majority of young people are not necessarily listening to folk music right now." Several younger, queer-normative formats are growing: Improper English, East Bay Square's "dance any role" policy, the Balance the Bay urban contra weekend (sold out), the Lord of the Rings square dance, the Starry Plough's women-led FairPlé session and its kids' session, and the SFFMC shanty workshop crowds. The Freight has deliberately broadened its audience toward Black and Latino artists since 2016.

**COVID and the rebound.** Contra took the hardest hit. SF had no regular contra from 2020 until a new Wednesday series began in Aug 2024. The East Bay's founding 1980 contra is on hiatus, Hayward went independent and vaccination-only, and the Squirrelly Stringband's North Oakland square dance never restarted (replaced in 2024 by the East Bay Square). Sessions came back but shifted: the Plough's Sunday session moved from 8 pm to 1 pm via a pandemic parklet, and the UICC slow session started masked in Duboce Park. Zoom stuck, with SFFMC sings, AmeriCeltic sessions, BASH Christian Harmony over Jamulus, and the SF Maritime virtual chantey sing ($29K raised). The Peninsula's Monday old-time jam fell from 15–25 players to 8–10.

**Space is the existential issue.** The SFFMC lost St. Cyprian's. The Mandala Folk Dance Center reportedly closed. Nordic Footnotes pays $300 a night in rent. The Starry Plough survived COVID on a ~$67K GoFundMe and plans to become a nonprofit. The Lost Church argues that "far more artists can fill a 49 seat theater than a 490 seat theater." The counter-trend is ownership: Ashkenaz's community bought its building after David Nadel's 1996 murder, the Freight built a $12M hall in 2009, and Kularts and APICC bought a SoMa building in 2024.

6. Which metros have a permanent public chantey sing?
7. Do folk institutions that own their buildings survive better than renters?
8. Gold Rush and immigrant song culture left almost no living participatory trace here except Chinese opera, Portuguese festas and the Highland Games. Which 19th-century traditions survive in other metros?
9. Mariachi is surprisingly thin in the five counties compared with San Jose and Southern California. What determines where school mariachi takes root?

That works out to roughly four instrumental sessions or jams a day across the five counties. The busiest day is Sunday. Four Irish sessions run every week (Plough at 1pm, Starry at 5:30 and 7:30, Sláinte), and on a given Sunday they are joined by up to four of the monthly ones (Mad Dog, Mayflower, Alhambra, Starry kids, Duboce Park), along with the Lucky Horseshoe bluegrass jam, the Tam Jam and most of the monthly old-time and Balkan events. The thinnest day for a visiting fiddler is Thursday (set dancing at the Plough, the Atlas Cafe old-time jam and a couple of Marin bluegrass jams).

How confident are these numbers? Medium-high for Irish and old-time, which are cross-checked across thesession.org, venue calendars and two independent volunteer calendars. Medium for bluegrass, which leans heavily on one list (Peter Thompson's [Bluegrass Signal](https://www.bgsignal.com/jams--instruction.html)) plus the [CBA calendar](https://californiabluegrass.org/calendar/category/jam/). One coverage warning: [thesession.org](https://thesession.org/sessions/nearby?latlon=37.80,-122.27&radius=40&format=json) shows only 11 sessions within 40 miles of Oakland and just 9 inside the MSA, while local calendars give 19 Irish session series. Anyone counting from thesession alone would miss more than half of the scene.

### The calendars that hold the scene together

Four volunteer-maintained lists, all updated in October 2026, are the real infrastructure here:

- **The SF Folk Music Club's [community folk calendar](https://www.sffmc.org/community-folk-calendar/)**, edited by *folknik* calendar editor Shelby Solomon (updated 10/20/26). It covers every genre and is the only list that catches small things like the Duboce Park slow session or the La Peña family jam.
- **David Brown's [CelticSF](https://sites.google.com/site/davbrown2/celtic) and [OldtimeSF](https://sites.google.com/site/davbrown2/oldtimesf) calendars** (10/26/26), posted monthly to groups.io lists (CelticSF has 116 members). These are the best sources for session etiquette, contacts and what has died.
- **Peter Thompson's [Bluegrass Signal](https://www.bgsignal.com/)**, a monthly mailer with the fullest bluegrass jam list in Northern California.
- **Tony Becker's [AmeriCeltic](https://americeltic.net/)**, which also runs two weekly Zoom Irish sessions (Trans-Atlantic at noon and Pacific Evening at 7pm on Wednesdays).

Behind these are groups.io lists for Québécois music ([QueTradSF](https://groups.io/g/QueTradSF), 143 members), the [Dublin Irish Jam](https://groups.io/g/dublinirishjam) (37 members and a shared PDF tunebook) and BACDS's open-band practice group. The largest Meetup is ["Irish music trad session"](https://www.meetup.com/irish-music-trad-session/), with 572 members and 1,733 past events.

### Irish: two pubs carry it, with a ring of newer monthly sessions

**The Plough and Stars** (116 Clement St, Inner Richmond) calls itself the "Home of Traditional Irish Music in the Bay Area" and was voted SF Weekly's Best Irish Pub in 2004 ([site](https://theploughandstars.com/)). A city Legacy Business report, which surfaced in search but could not be opened, dates its opening to April 30, 1975, under Bob Heaney, who also founded Berkeley's Starry Plough ([SF.gov PDF](https://www.sf.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/item_3d._lbr-2017-18-030_plough_and_stars.pdf); medium confidence). Its October 2026 calendar shows a **Seisiún every Sunday 1–4pm**. That session runs while the Clement Street farmers market is on outside, "so much street traffic will pop in", and "first drink is on the house" ([thesession comment, Nov 2024](https://thesession.org/sessions/276)). It is led on rotation. A Spanish visitor in February 2026 praised "a well assorted set of slip jigs, slides, polkas and hornpipes" and the presence of banjo, several fiddles, flutes, whistle, accordion and concertina. A 1986 regular, Peter Kasin, writes that Marla, Jack, Suzanne and others "anchor the sessions on a rotating basis". The afternoon slot is a COVID legacy. Before 2020 the Sunday session ran 8–11pm and there was a Tuesday session too. In April 2022 the players were still "playing in the parklet outside starting at 1 pm". The Plough also hosts a **1st-Saturday 4pm trad session** (Meetup), **CCÉ set dancing most Thursdays** to live céilí bands (Pigs in the Rigging, Barry O'Connell & Vinnie Cronin, Ceo), and an old-time jam (see below).

**The Starry Plough** (3101 Shattuck, Berkeley), "a staple of the East Bay scene since 1973" with "deep roots as an Irish Revolutionary watering hole" ([site](https://thestarryplough.com/)), is the single busiest session venue in the metro. The venue itself counts "Irish music and dance, 12 events per month" ([Sunday session page](https://thestarryplough.com/sunday-irish-music-session/)). Its weekly Sunday structure:

- **Early Session, 5:30–7pm.** Moderate pace, with a different host each week, "above beginning … but below full speed". Chris Cornell keeps a Google Sheet of the tunes played ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/363)).
- **Late session, 7:30pm until after midnight.** The format rotates through the month. First Sundays are a **Guest Host** session that allows only one chordal instrument and asks drummers to "play very quietly". Third Sundays are a **FairPlé** session led by women, aligned with the Irish gender-balance movement. The other Sundays are the regular **Starry Session**, with song breaks "every hour or so" and a strict *ciúnas* (quiet) during songs. Tips go into "the Wooly Hat™" or a Venmo QR code on every table.
- **Kids & Beginners session** on 2nd and 4th Sundays, hosted by Darcy Noonan. Times conflict between sources: 2–4pm or 4–5:30pm on the venue's own pages, and "3rd Sunday 4:30pm" on CelticSF.
- A **Pop-up Gaeltacht** (Irish-language hour) on 2nd Sundays, and a **Monday céilí** with a dance lesson at 7pm and live music at 9pm.

The etiquette page is unusually explicit: "don't drone, harmonize, or noodle along." A thesession thread from July 2025 to January 2026 argues over whether an upright bass belongs in the session. A regular replied with a long essay ending in "no one wants a lot of splanging and discord." Still, by May 2026 a regular reported the Sundays "going from strength to strength, with up to 25 live musicians … and often 10 singers". A visitor who comes "from Rome every August" wrote in August 2026: "If you can make it to only one session in the Bay Area, make it the Starry Plough."

**The ring of newer and monthly sessions:**
- **United Irish Cultural Center, "Seisiún Saturday"**: 4–7pm "nearly every Saturday" in the Emerald Pub (2700 45th Ave), with free Eventbrite registration. Leaders include Cormac Gannon, Valerie Rose and Eric Owens, and it is listed through December 2026 ([UICC](https://irishcentersf.org/events/list/?tribe-bar-search=seisiun)). The same building hosts a monthly **"not full speed"** slow session (last or 4th Saturday, 2pm; [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/irish-music-sessions-not-full-speed/)). That group started during COVID in "some quiet corner of a park with mask wearing" and still sometimes plays outdoors in **Duboce Park**.
- **Mad Dog in the Fog** (Haight St): added in February 2026, first Sundays 6–9pm. "Players get free drinks ($100 limit)" ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/9633)).
- **O'Reilly's Pub** (1840 Haight): a monthly *full-speed* session, usually on 4th Saturdays from 12:30–3:30, RSVP required. It is hosted by **Ceo / Ceol sa Cheo**, Blake Ritter (fiddle and concertina; Humboldt County; "an All-Ireland champion in the slow airs tradition") and Alina Larson (uilleann pipes, which she "started playing … on May 21st 2020"). The duo spent 2024–25 hosting at Sandy Bell's in Edinburgh. Their name means "music in the fog", for the "atmospheric conditions shared between Ireland and the Northern California coastline" ([ceol-sa-cheo.com](https://www.ceol-sa-cheo.com/)). They also host the Starry early session on some Sundays.
- **Sláinte** (Jack London Square, Oakland): Sunday music with the band Buncrana, per the [SFFMC calendar](https://www.sffmc.org/community-folk-calendar/). Whether it is still an open session is unresolved; a January 2025 thesession comment asked exactly that. Its second-Thursday session has no evidence since 2023.
- **The RIC** (Rockridge Improvement Club, Oakland): "small, semi-closed", 1st and 3rd Saturdays at noon, intermediate to advanced, with "a nice selection of teas and a full bar" ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/9516)).
- **Marin:** the **Burren House** in San Rafael on Tuesdays at 7pm ([CelticSF](https://sites.google.com/site/davbrown2/celtic)). The **Mayflower Pub** in San Rafael is "now on the second and fourth Sundays" (comment of October 3, 2026), after years on first Tuesdays.
- **Peninsula:** the **Fret & Fiddle** crew runs sessions at **Hop Dogma** in Princeton-by-the-Sea (1st Wednesday) and **Molly O's** in San Carlos (2nd Saturday). **Alhambra Irish House** in Redwood City has a possible 1st-Sunday session and monthly céilí. The **Half Moon Bay Yacht Club** hosts a slow session on 2nd Mondays: "We use sheet music … around 8 regulars … a standard set of about 15 tunes and usually around 5 sing alongs". Because "the venue is technically a private club", visitors need a guest pass ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/9728)).
- **Dublin (Alameda Co.):** the **Dublin Irish Jam** plays Celtic and old-time in the historic Murray Schoolhouse at Dublin Heritage Park on the 1st, 3rd and 5th Tuesdays ([groups.io](https://groups.io/g/dublinirishjam)). A bluegrass jam uses the same schoolhouse on the 2nd and 4th Tuesdays, so the building has a jam every Tuesday of the month.

**Just outside the MSA**, people from the five counties regularly drive to O'Flaherty's in San Jose ("San Jose's only up-tempo seisiún", Tuesdays), the SF Pipers' Club session at St. Stephen's Green in Mountain View (paused in summer 2026 because "We've been displaced by the World Cup"; [thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/2284)), Redwood Cafe in Cotati, and Bruehol Brewing in Benicia.

**Comhaltas.** The **Cooley-Keegan Branch** of CCÉ was "started in the early 1970s" and is named for accordionists Joe Cooley and Kevin Keegan ([branch history](https://sfcooleykeegancce.org/misc/about_cceck.html)). Today it runs:
- Sunday-evening céilís at the UICC ($15 for members, $20 for others; November 8 and December 13 in 2026)
- Thursday set dancing at the Plough
- a **first-Friday singing session** in members' homes, running since 2002, with "about a dozen people … until 11 or midnight" and stories in Irish and English ([singing page](https://sfcooleykeegancce.org/music/singing.html))
- the Mallen Scholarship for junior members

In June 2024 it presented the **Rose Tree Youth Céilí Band**, led by Valerie Rose, with players "ranging in age from 6 to 14". The branch site claims "seisiúns nearly every night of the week". The current calendars do not support that: Tuesday, Saturday and Sunday are well covered, but Monday, Wednesday and Thursday are thin.

**Uilleann pipes.** The **SF Pipers' Club** was founded in 1979. It meets about monthly, gives members free piping, reed-making and pipe-maintenance lessons, and hosts the biennial **West Coast Piping Tionól** ([sfpipersclub.org](https://sfpipersclub.org/)).

### Old-time: few jams, deep roots

Old-time has only about 12 jam slots a month, but it has the oldest institution. The **Berkeley Old Time Fiddlers' Convention** held its first stringband contest in Provo Park in 1968. With a counterculture joke, it was "called the 35th Annual. The following year the 17th Annual was held and 22nd Annual the year after that" ([BOTMC about](https://www.berkeleyoldtimemusic.org/about)). The Folkways LP *Berkeley Farms* documents those years. Today's **Berkeley Old Time Music Convention** is a five-day September festival with a stringband contest, which is the metro's in-MSA fiddle contest of note. Its 2025 winners were Leah Wollenberg and Jeremy Reinhard. BOTMC also runs a free March "Spring Situation" at the Central Library: the March 7 2026 edition had an old-time jam, a teen jam, an Irish session led by Wollenberg, a song circle, a Karen Celia Heil fiddle workshop and a Stairwell Sisters reunion ([2026 page](https://www.berkeleyoldtimemusic.org/spring-situation-2026)).

Current jams:
- **Odd-Tuesday Old Time Jam at the Plough and Stars**, 1st/3rd/5th Tuesdays 7–9:30pm, led by Dinah and Noah Stroe ([OldtimeSF](https://sites.google.com/site/davbrown2/oldtimesf)). It is the in-person half of the long-running "Andronico's" jam; the even-Tuesday half lives on Zoom under new leadership. The Irish pub's own calendar lists it as "Old Time Jam, Odd Tuesdays @7 to 9:30pm".
- **Shred Sesh**, 3rd Sundays 2–4pm at the Starry Plough. It is a hosted "onion jam": "a core group that stays tight in the middle, so the groove is never compromised", with everyone else in layers around it, and the host fiddler may call every tune ([Starry](https://thestarryplough.com/free-daytime-shows-jams/)). Evie Ladin hosts on November 15 2026 and Nick Lofaro on December 20.
- **Berkeley Public Library**, Art & Music Dept on the 5th floor, 3rd Tuesdays 5:30–7:30pm, co-sponsored by BOTMC.
- **Pre-dance jams.** The Feral Friday square dance at the Polish Club in the Mission has a jam 6:45–8pm on 2nd Fridays, and the East Bay Square Dance at the Hillside Club in Berkeley has a free jam 6:30–8pm on 3rd Fridays. Old-time's growth area appears to be the square-dance revival, with callers Evie Ladin and Robin Fischer and house band the Round Top Stringband. Ashkenaz holds a "Lord of the Rings" square dance every Halloween (4th annual in 2026, music by "Denethor's Supper").
- **Bernal Heights**: Progressive Grounds Cafe, 4th Sundays 3–5:30pm ("bring warm layers"). Bluegrass Signal lists 2nd *and* 4th Sundays.
- **Atlas Cafe** in the Mission (2nd and 4th Thursdays) and **Winters Tavern, Pacifica** (3rd Wednesdays). Each has only one source (Bluegrass Signal), so confidence is lower.
- **Lucas Valley Old Time Jam**, 2nd Saturdays 11am–1pm at the Rotary Valley Senior Village in San Rafael.
- **CSOTFA District 9**, 4th Sundays 1:30–5pm at the United Methodist Church in Castro Valley: a stage in the hall plus "jam circles outside. Some are fast, some are slow." It costs $7, and first-timers get in free ([csotfa9.org](https://csotfa9.org/)). This is the local district of the statewide association, whose championships are held in Lodi. The 2025 Open champion was Lucy Crary ([csotfa.org](https://csotfa.org/)).

The big Monday old-time jam (Palo Alto) and the Santa Clara Valley Fiddlers' monthly jam (San Jose, about 200 members "from Gilroy to Berkeley") are both just outside the MSA ([showman.org](https://www.showman.org/jams/oti/index.htm), [fiddlers.org](https://www.fiddlers.org/)). Pete Showman reports his Monday jam fell from "15-25" players before COVID to "8-10" after. **West Marin Fiddle Camp** in Bolinas held its "6th & Final" camp in July 2026.

### Bluegrass: the most slots, plus an invitation-only circuit

Bluegrass has the most monthly slots (about 46), but about a third of them are invitation-only. These are the four "JamBot" jams: **Tam Jam** (Sundays at Taste of Rome, Sausalito; "more a performance than a jam", with one old-time night a month), **Point Jam** (Tuesdays at the Hotel Mac, Point Richmond), **Hop Jam** (Wednesdays at Hop Dogma, Half Moon Bay) and the **Taco Jam** (Saturdays at Baja Taqueria on Piedmont Ave, Oakland) ([jambot.org](https://jambot.org/JamBots/TacoJam/)).

Open jams, about 30 a month:
- **San Francisco:** the **Lucky Horseshoe** in Bernal Heights on Sundays 4–7pm, 21+, on stage into a mic; you "must be able to lead a vocal or instrumental song" ([CBA](https://californiabluegrass.org/event/horseshoe-jam/all/)). **Blondie's** on Valencia (Mondays, jam then band), **Hotel Utah** (Tuesdays) and **Barbary Bluegrass** at Belle Cora in North Beach (2nd Sundays).

The **Baygrass Collective** runs jams at the Starry Plough (2nd Sundays) and Cato's Ale House in Oakland (4th Tuesdays). - **Marin:** the **Marin Bluegrass Sessions** on the Sweetwater Music Hall stage in Mill Valley (4th Sundays, free, with a support band), **Papermill Creek Saloon** in Forest Knolls (3rd Thursdays) and **Marin Lutheran Church** in Corte Madera (1st and 3rd Thursdays).

### Scottish and Cape Breton: one big club

There is no dedicated Cape Breton session in the five counties. Its music lives inside the **San Francisco Scottish Fiddlers**, formed in 1986 "when Alasdair Fraser placed an advertisement in a local newspaper", which has "grown to over 300 members" with $30 household dues ([SFSF](https://www.sfscottishfiddlers.org/About-the-SFSF)). Members meet monthly "in private homes and community halls from San Jose in the south to Healdsburg in the north and as far as Nevada City to the east". The club says 3rd Sunday; CelticSF says 2nd Sunday, with Zoom on 3rd Sundays. The repertoire runs past Scotland to "Galicia and Brittany … Scandinavia … Cape Breton … Quebecois and even an Old Timey American tune or two." Fraser handed the music directorship in April 2025 to Caroline McCaskey, who "grew up in the SFSF". She is a U.S. National Scottish Fiddle Champion and a two-time International Musical Saw Champion. The club's big public moment is **Stravaig**, its spring concerts (April 16–18 2027 in Davis, Livermore and the Presidio Theatre), along with farmers-market gigs in Mountain View. The Freight's August **Fiddle Summit** with Valley of the Moon faculty is the local face of Fraser's camp in Boulder Creek, which is outside the MSA.

### History thread: Cooley, Keegan and the Plough

The Irish revival has a clear origin story. Joe Cooley, the East Galway accordionist and founder member of the Tulla Céilí Band, came to the US in 1954 and moved on to San Francisco. There he formed the **Gráinneog Céilidh band** with Kevin Keegan, fiddlers Sue Draheim (born in Oakland in 1949) and Will Spires, and Eric Thompson ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Cooley)). Keegan moved to SF in 1970. He played "first at the Carousel Ballroom, and then nightly at The Plough and Stars", and died in SF on December 20 1978, "five years to the day" after Cooley ([CCÉ tribute](https://sfcooleykeegancce.org/misc/cooley-keegan_story.html)). Mickie Zekley, who founded **Lark in the Morning** in 1974, was "first inspired by … Joe Cooley and Kevin Keegan, whom he performed with for many years". Lark in the Morning grew to stores in SF (Fisherman's Wharf), Seattle and Mendocino. All of them closed by 2010, and it reopened as a Berkeley storefront in November 2017 ([Lark](https://larkinthemorning.com/pages/about-us)). One of its staff bought her first fiddle there at 11 and busked beside the old Wharf shop. Its staff today includes the piper Alina Larson. The **Joe Cooley tapes** ([joecooleytapes.org](http://www.joecooleytapes.org/)) preserve the duo. Bay Area players still follow *Kevin Keegan's Waltz* with *The Orphan* jig, as Cooley and Keegan did; Cathie Whitesides wrote that "we used to play it with Kevin when he was living" ([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/13090)).

### Tunes named for the place

The pickings are slim but real: Charlie Lennon's reel **"The San Francisco"**, a companion to his "The Windy City" ([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/4387)); Alasdair Fraser's swing jig **"Dr. Angus & Emily MacDonald's Trip to San Francisco"** ([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/18427)); **Kevin Keegan's Waltz**; and **"Farewell to Joe Cooley"**. Marty Somberg's **"Trip to Berkeley"** is by a Michigan fiddler. "Frisco" is only an alias of the Sliabh Luachra hornpipe *An Gleanntán*, which prompted the poster's quip that "no matter what Jack Kerouac might tell you, no one in the SF Bay Area calls San Francisco 'Frisco'" ([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/11137)). "Golden Gate" is a Québécois waltz with no clear SF link. No tunes named for Oakland, Marin, Tamalpais, Sausalito or the Presidio were found on thesession. The Traditional Tune Archive blocked automated searches, so it was not checked.

### What a visiting fiddler should do

- **Sunday:** the Plough (1pm), then BART to Berkeley for the Starry Plough's 5:30 early session and stay for the 7:30. On a 3rd Sunday, catch the Shred Sesh at 2pm first; on a 4th, the accordion jam.
- **Tuesday:** odd weeks, the Plough's old-time jam (SF), Burren House (San Rafael) or the Dublin schoolhouse. On a 3rd Tuesday, the library old-time jam.
- **Wednesday:** East Bay Bluegrassers in El Cerrito; on a 1st Wednesday, the Hop Dogma Irish session on the coast.
- **Saturday:** UICC Seisiún Saturday (4–7pm). Add O'Reilly's (4th Saturday) or The RIC (1st and 3rd Saturdays, ask first).

Costs are almost always zero; tip the hat. The exceptions are CSOTFA's $7 door and the East Bay Bluegrassers' $5.

## Chapter 2: Participatory folk dance
## San Francisco Bay Area: participatory folk dance (census and organizations, Oct 2026)

*Thread: dance. Scope: the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont MSA (SF, Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, San Mateo counties). Things in Santa Clara, Santa Cruz and Sonoma counties are flagged as outside the MSA.*

### Summary

The Bay Area dance scene has two separate roots. The older one is the **international folk dance** movement, which began in San Francisco in 1938 and spread statewide through the Folk Dance Federation of California. Its clubs still meet most nights: Berkeley Folk Dancers alone dances seven times a week. The younger root is the **New England-style contra and English country dance** revival of the late 1970s, now organized by the Bay Area Country Dance Society (BACDS, founded 1981).

COVID hit the contra side hard:

- San Francisco had no regular contra from 2020 until August 2024.
- The East Bay's founding Friday contra is "on hiatus."
- Hayward split off as an independent dance.
- The North Oakland old-time square dance ended.

The international side mostly carried on, though the Mandala Folk Dance Center closed. Around both roots sits an unusually varied mix:

- weekly Irish céilí in a pub since 1980
- about eight weekly Scottish country dance classes inside the MSA
- three active morris teams
- weekly Israeli, Greek and Balkan dancing
- about 26 modern Western square dance clubs, including two of the oldest LGBTQ+ clubs in the country

#### Counts (Oct 2026, inside the MSA)

**Contra: about 5.8 dances a month**

| Series | Schedule |
|---|---|
| Berkeley | 1st, 3rd and 5th Wednesdays |
| San Francisco | 2nd and 4th Wednesdays |
| San Rafael | 2nd Saturdays |

**Traditional (old-time) squares:** one a month.

**English country dance: about 7.8 a month.** Berkeley 2nd and 4th Wednesdays, Berkeley 4th Saturday (experienced dancers), San Francisco 2nd Saturday, Mill Valley 2nd Saturday afternoon, plus a queer-centered English dance (Improper English).

**Everything else:**

- International and Israeli folk dance: about 76 club nights a month.
- Scottish and Irish dance classes and céilís: about 45 a month.
- Modern Western square dance clubs: about 100 club nights a month.

**Total:** about 257 recurring dance events a month, or about 155 if you leave out the modern Western square dance clubs. Club class nights and team practices make these numbers higher than a count of public dances would be.

**Admission:** a contra costs $20 at the door at BACDS (sliding from $6 to $25). An international folk dance night costs $5–10.

### 1. Contra and old-time squares

#### Today's census

**Berkeley Contra (BACDS).** 1st, 3rd and 5th Wednesdays at Christ Church East Bay (formerly Grace North Church), 2138 Cedar St.

#### How it got here

BACDS's own history articles, scanned from the *Bay Area Country Dancer* newsletter, are collected on [a Google Site](https://sites.google.com/view/bacds/history).

**East Bay contra (Kirston Koths, [20th-anniversary article](https://drive.google.com/file/d/19sBQ8pbOGdBzPwuOUv1HG4noqaD8rUHz/view), 2000).**

- Before BACDS there was "only one contra dance in the greater Bay Area, called by Brad Foster on the Stanford campus."
- The East Bay contra began in 1980 on first Wednesdays at First Congregational Church in Berkeley. "The admission was $2.50. The first dance attracted 50 dancers."
- Kevin Carr and Daniel Steinberg of the "yet-to-be-invented Hillbillies From Mars" played the first dance.
- The dance moved through the UC Berkeley gym ("where legend has it that the dancers were fond of skinny dipping in the pool during the break"), John Muir School, two Oakland schools, Finn Hall and Prospect Sierra School, then to Humanist Hall in Oakland in 2008.
- It is now listed as on hiatus.

**San Francisco contra (Charlie Fenton, [1997 article](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IiNj4uEUK_PKeKUdY6dM5U_QOcG3n49p/view)).**

- It began in 1981–82 as a Thursday series at Fort Mason run by the Plowshares coffeehouse of the SF Folk Music Center. The four Thursdays rotated: English, old-time squares, New England contras and an "Open Mike" dance.
- On Oct 2, 1982 it moved to California Hall at Polk and Turk, a 1909 German social hall once called Germania Hall and "hastily renamed during World War I." Sixty-seven dancers came.
- Fenton had to get a monthly one-day beer license and a police "Dance Hall Keeper's Permit, which made me feel like I was in the Old Wild West."
- On drag-performance nights, "security would be provided by several dozen men lining the street out front on motorcycles, and wearing black leather biker gear," who helped carry the sound equipment.
- The dance moved to St. Paul's Presbyterian in the Outer Sunset in November 1984.
- In 1986 it absorbed a Marin series. Attempts at the Point Reyes Dance Palace and in Mill Valley had failed because "the local interest just was not there."
- The 1987 Christmas Day dance led to the **No Snow Ball** (from 1988), which grew past 200 dancers. The 33rd was held in December 2019 ([calendar](https://bacds.org/calendars/2019/12/)), and none appears on the 2025 calendar.
- Fenton wanted "to parallel the established dances of the Boston area, including those at the Concord Scout House."
- In October 2019 the SF Saturday contra even put on a "Special Techno: Live contra w/black lights" ([Oct 2019 calendar](https://bacds.org/calendars/2019/10/)).
- No SF contra appears on the October 2022 or March 2024 calendars. The city went about four years without a regular contra.

### 3. International folk dance: the original Bay Area folk boom

#### Origin story

The Changs history, written by Larry Getchell and published by the Folk Dance Federation ([Changs blog](http://changsfolkdancers.blogspot.com/2008/01/history-of-folk-dance-movement-in.html)), dates the start to "a foggy evening in February, 1938." That night three couples, including **Song Chang** and his new bride Harriet, met at Bryl Wyneck's studio in the Montgomery Block. Song taught them "plain Schottische, Norwegian Mt. March, Ace of Diamonds and some waltzes."

- Chang, a Chinese American, had been struck in 1931 by European dancers' "disdain for racial barriers."
- He had danced Swedish dances in the basement of "Mama Gravandar's School of Swedish Applied Arts" on Pacific Avenue.
- The first night's group included "a coppersmith, a Chinese American and his Scandinavian bride, a commercial sandblaster, a puppeteer and several artists."
- They moved to the Green Lantern bar at 740 Kearny and lost that space the same year, probably because "they didn't spend for drinks." Then they moved to 415 Broadway, where Buzz Glass joined.
- The movement would grow to "as many as 400 folk dance clubs in the State of California."
- Changs celebrated its 85th anniversary in 2023. It still dances every Friday (7–10 pm, $6–8) at the Miraloma Park clubhouse, with a Family Night on fourth Fridays ([FDF listing](https://www.folkdance.com/community/where-to-dance/listing/changs-dancers/)).

**The Folk Dance Federation of California.** Its past-presidents list starts with Henry "Buzz" Glass in 1942–43 ([FDF history](https://www.folkdance.com/about/history/)). The RSCDS San Francisco Branch history says the Federation was formed in 1943, so the sources disagree. *Let's Dance!* magazine has been published since 1944 and is edited by Loui Tucker.

**Berkeley Folk Dancers** (1941) calls itself "America's largest folk dance club," dancing "seven times a week, fifty weeks a year. Now in our 85th year!" ([BFD](https://berkeleyfolkdancers.org/)).

- Membership is "over 100 people."
- There are four class levels, Monday to Thursday evenings at Live Oak Park, with a Friday all-request night and two afternoon classes at Northbrae Church.
- The repertoire has more than 200 dances, mostly danced to recorded music.
- Dues are $260 a year, but membership is **free for anyone under 30** through the 2013 Mel Mann Fund ([facts](https://berkeleyfolkdancers.org/more-facts-about-us/)).
- BFD's Festival of the Oaks reached its 77th year in 2026.

#### Kolo Festival and Kolo John

In 1952 **Ivan "John" Filcich** founded the Kolo Festival as a benefit for the folk dance teacher Vyts Beliajus ([Society of Folk Dance Historians](https://sfdh.us/encyclopedia/filcich_j.html)).

- Filcich was born near Rijeka in 1924 and was "dancing six nights a week" in Oakland by 1948.
- He ran the Slav-Art Music Center in Oakland, Festival Records and the Festival Folk Shop in SF.
- He was nicknamed "Kolo John" in *Let's Dance!* in 1957.
- He died on **May 11, 2026, aged 101**.
- The 75th Kolo Festival runs Nov 27–29, 2026 at the Croatian American Cultural Center in the Excelsior, with music from Albania to Sweden and Turkish dances taught by Ahmet Lüleci ([kolofestival.org](https://www.kolofestival.org/)).

#### Stockton Folk Dance Camp

## Chapter 3: Song, listening rooms, traditional singing, radio and shops
## San Francisco Bay Area: folk song, listening rooms, traditional singing, radio, shops

*Thread notes for the san-francisco dossier (SF, Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin and San Mateo counties). Research as of October 2026, from web sources only. Counts and dates come from the cited pages; where sources disagree I say so.*

### The short version

The Bay Area's song scene sits on the East Bay, specifically Berkeley, around three anchors that have lasted for decades: **the Freight** (Freight & Salvage), a 1968 coffeehouse that became a $5 million-a-year nonprofit concert hall and school; the **San Francisco Folk Music Club**, a volunteer song club founded in 1948 that still meets to pass the turn around a circle; and a strong **traditional-singing** layer: a Sacred Harp singing every Monday in West Berkeley, the country's oldest public chantey sing at the San Francisco maritime park (since 1981), Kitka's Balkan women's singing in Oakland, and Black gospel choirs with roots in the WWII migration. On top of that sit radio shows that have run for decades (Peter Thompson's *Bluegrass Signal* on KALW, on the air since about 1975) and Hardly Strictly Bluegrass, a free festival a billionaire paid for that draws hundreds of thousands to Golden Gate Park every October.

What's distinctive is less the size than how long things last and how cheap they are: almost everything participatory here is free (Sacred Harp, chanteys, SFFMC, both SFFMC festivals, Hardly Strictly). What's fragile is space. The SFFMC's in-person meeting moved into a member's flat in 2026 after losing its church hall, and is down to 12-16 people a month, against the ~100 who once crowded Faith Petric's house.

### 1. The Freight: from an 87-seat coffeehouse to a $5.3M institution

The Freight was founded in 1968 by Nancy Owens and named for the used-furniture store that had occupied its first space on San Pablo Avenue ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freight_%26_Salvage)). The *East Bay Express* puts the original at **87 seats** ([EBX, 2018](https://eastbayexpress.com/freight-amp-salvage-is-changing-with-the-times-ahead-of-its-50th-anniversary-2-1/)). Its first decade was bluegrass-heavy but eclectic: Elizabeth Cotten, Lightnin' Hopkins, Malvina Reynolds, Hazel & Alice, Kate Wolf and frequent shows by Vern & Ray. It incorporated in 1983 as the **Berkeley Society for the Preservation of Traditional Music**, moved to a 220-seat room on Addison Street in 1988, and on **August 27, 2009** opened its current $12 million LEED building in the downtown arts district, with classrooms built in ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freight_%26_Salvage)).

- **Size:** *the sources disagree on capacity.* Wikipedia says 490 seats; the *East Bay Express* issued a correction saying it seats 450.
- **Scale:** its FY2024 Form 990 shows **$5,279,422** in revenue: program services $2.93M, grants and contributions $2.0M ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/942887073), [Cause IQ](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/freight-and-salvage-coffeehouse,942887073/)). It "welcomed over 50,000 music lovers" in 2024 and hosts "shows almost every day of the year" (EBX).
- **School:** in 2024, two sold-out sessions of the **Freight Singers** community chorus, five sessions of instrumental and vocal classes, plus workshops and jams, reached about **2,000 students**. A 4th-grade program, *Golden State of Song*, started in Berkeley's public schools that year ([Cause IQ](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/freight-and-salvage-coffeehouse,942887073/)).
- **Change:** in 2016 the Freight hired Peter Williams (now artistic director) specifically to bring more Black and Latino artists to the main stage. At a 2018 Meshell Ndegeocello show, a board member estimated 80% of the audience were first-time visitors ([EBX](https://eastbayexpress.com/freight-amp-salvage-is-changing-with-the-times-ahead-of-its-50th-anniversary-2-1/)). In 2024 it launched a three-day **Deford Bailey Legacy Festival** and began working with the Black Banjo Reclamation Project. Its fall 2026 calendar mixes Solas, John Gorka, Chris Smither and Dirk & Amelia Powell with a Lalo Guerrero Legacy Festival, a Windham Hill 50th anniversary and a Monday-noon residency by Bobby McFerrin & MOTION ([SFFMC calendar](https://www.sffmc.org/community-folk-calendar/)).
- **Open mic:** first Mondays, with a lottery at 7:20; soloists get one song on the big stage ([Bay Area Open Mics](https://www.bayareaopenmics.com/OM-THEFREIGHT.html)).

When the Freight outgrew its scale, the room it left behind was effectively rebuilt: in 2016 pianist **Sam Rudin** opened **the Back Room** (1984 Bonita Ave), about 100 seats on thrift-store couches with no bar and all ages welcome, explicitly to recreate the old 87-seat Freight. It celebrated ten years with a ten-day festival in April 2026 ([48 Hills](https://48hills.org/2026/04/one-decade-in-the-back-room-still-holds-space-for-intimate-musical-encounters/)). This fall it has Joe Newberry & April Verch, Pierre Bensusan and Zoë Lewis.

### 2. The San Francisco Folk Music Club: a song circle since 1948

The SFFMC's own history is good reading ([sffmc.org/history](https://www.sffmc.org/history/)). Founder **Dave Rothkop** wrote: *"In 1948 I concocted the San Francisco Folk Music Club. We mostly met at our tiny apartment in North Beach"*, and called it *"the legitimate child of Hiroshima and the Cold War... We didn't end the cold war but have pursued our dream of mutual understanding through music."* It restarted in 1949 through a notice in SF State's student paper, was reorganized by Herb Jager in 1959, and from 1962 was run by **Faith Petric** (1915-2013). Petric was a Rosie the Riveter in WWII and marched from Selma to Montgomery ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Petric)). She hosted the club's musical meetings at her Haight-Ashbury house until she died at 98; turnout "often approached 100 people, sometimes so full that there was scarcely space to sit down." She started the *folknik* in 1964. Pete Seeger taught the club a song-leading workshop around 1980. The club incorporated as a 501(c)(3) in 1997, says it has "hundreds of members" and claims to have "inspired/mothered/fathered some five folk clubs in California" ([About](https://www.sffmc.org/about-us/)).

**What it does now (fall 2026):**
- **In-person musical meeting:** 3rd Sundays, 1-5pm, potluck, everyone takes a turn leading or requesting a song. After the club "had to leave" St. Cyprian's church, members Glen Van Lehn and Tes Welborn offered their flat near the Golden Gate Park Panhandle for 12 months ("there are 15 steps to get to their front door"; [Musical meetings](https://www.sffmc.org/musical-meetings/)). The board's notes in the Nov-Dec 2026 *folknik* count **16 people** at one meeting ("they stayed in one room even though two were available. 4 or 5 new people came") and **12-15** on average.
- **Zoom sings:** four a month (1st and 3rd Tuesdays 1-3:30pm; 1st and 3rd Fridays 7pm-midnight). Per the same notes, "around 15-20 people over a four-hour period (about eight at a time)."
- **Two free festivals:** the **San Francisco Free Folk Festival** (first held 1977 at the Hall of Flowers; the **50th** was on July 11, 2026 at the Golden Gate Bandshell area, co-sponsored since 2023 by Illuminate and SF Rec & Park; [sffolkfest.org](https://sffolkfest.org/)) and the **El Cerrito Free Folk Festival** (since 2009; Oct 10, 2026 at the El Cerrito Community Center, with two stages, workshops and a morris team; [ECFFF](https://elcerritofreefolkfestival.org/)). The board's verdict on the 50th: "Went well, big crowd, **Sea Shanty was the best attended workshop.**"
- **Camps:** **Camp Harmony**, a camper-led New Year's camp at Camp Newman in Sonoma County (outside the MSA), Dec 29, 2026-Jan 3, 2027. The board voted **$7,500** of club assets to keep prices down after Camp Newman raised its fees. Three summer holiday-weekend gatherings: the 2026 Labor Day gathering at the East Bay Center for the Blind cost $150 in rent and took in $422 in donations. Plus *Spring Harmony Online* in March ([Camp Harmony](https://www.sffmc.org/camp-harmony/)).
- **The *folknik*** is bimonthly; its **community folk calendar**, edited by Shelby Solomon, is the most complete single listing of the Northern California acoustic scene I found ([calendar](https://www.sffmc.org/community-folk-calendar/)).

SFFMC history also includes the **Plowshares Coffeehouse** concert series at Fort Mason (1977-1998). And Faith Petric's personal hoard of folk-music journals from around the world, nicknamed "the Fort Knox of folk music", went to **Northwestern University** in 2014, joining the Berkeley Folk Music Festival archive ([Northwestern foreword](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/foreword/)).

### 3. The earlier history: San Pablo Avenue, North Beach and the Berkeley festival

- **North Beach clubs.** The **hungry i** (1949 or 1950, 83 seats in the Sentinel Building basement) booked folk singers such as Stan Wilson before turning to comedy in 1953 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_i)). The **Purple Onion** (1952) gave stages to the Kingston Trio and the Smothers Brothers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Purple_Onion)).
- **KPFA's "Midnight Special" (1956)** was the scene's first radio hub. The Berkeley Historical Plaque Project dates it to 1956 ([plaque](https://berkeleyplaques.org/e-plaque/san-pablo-avenue-folk-music-clubs/)). Locals performed live on it, and Barry Olivier hosted it ([Northwestern](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/barry-olivier-festival-director/)). *Note: the brief associates the show with Gert Chiarito; I found no source for that in this pass.*
- **San Pablo Avenue, 1958-68** ([plaque](https://berkeleyplaques.org/e-plaque/san-pablo-avenue-folk-music-clubs/)):
  - 1958: the **Steppenwolf** (Max Scherr, who sold it to start the *Berkeley Barb*) and the **Blind Lemon** (Rolf Cahn, a refugee from Nazi Germany, with Barbara Dane; Odetta played opening night).
  - 1963: the **Cabale** at 2504 San Pablo, where Jerry Garcia played bluegrass. *Sources disagree on the date: one search summary says Jan 1963, a rock-history blog says the site was a club "going back to 1962."* The same address later housed seven different clubs ([Rock Archaeology](https://rockarchaeology101.blogspot.com/2024/07/2504-san-pablo-avenue-berkeley-ca-long.html)).
  - Mid-1960s: the **Jabberwock** on Telegraph.
  - 1968: when the Jabberwock closed, the Freight opened. "At the turn of the century, only the Freight remained."
- **Berkeley Folk Music Festival, 1958-1970.** Barry Olivier ran it at UC Berkeley. His own line: *"The Festival was Pete Seeger mesmerizing 6,000 people at the Greek Theater... It was the Red Crayola playing the strangest music I have ever heard, so loud that a third of the audience at the Pauley Ballroom left."* Northwestern bought his archive in **1973** through bookseller and Berkeley city councilman John Swingle. It holds about **33,500 artifacts**, all digitized with an NEH grant ([exhibit](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/); [foreword](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/foreword/)). *A search summary gave 1974 as the purchase year; the curator's foreword says 1973.* Early in his career, the university warned Olivier not to print folk concert posters on red paper because of the color's association with communism.
- **Berkeley Old Time Fiddlers' Convention (1968-c.1971).** It was "conceived in the back of a Volkswagen bus," and its first edition was billed **"The 35th Annual"**, the next the 22nd, then the 17th. First prize was **3 lbs of rutabagas** and second prize 5 lbs. A banjo player won first prize for being in Geneva at the time ("the ultimate thing a banjo player could do, in terms of good taste, was to be at least 8,000 miles away"). The organizers nearly cancelled once TV crews got interested, because "if a community function becomes too large, then rules must be made" ([Rita Weill's 1971 Folkways notes](https://www.berkeleyoldtimemusic.org/berkeley-farms-folkways-lp)). Suzy Thompson revived it as the **Berkeley Old Time Music Convention**. *Sources disagree on the year: 2003 per [Berkeleyside](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2026/09/17/around-berkeley-old-time-music-convention-coastal-cleanup-day-artisanal-comedy-show), 2004 per a search summary.* The 2026 edition ran Sept 16-20: a free string band contest capped at 12 bands in the Berkeley Central Library, a panel on Black old-time musicians, and Alice Gerrard reading from her memoir ([contest page](https://www.berkeleyoldtimemusic.org/contest)). The BOTMC also runs a monthly 3rd-Tuesday old-time jam on the library's 5th floor.
- **Other milestones.** Malvina Reynolds of Berkeley wrote **"Little Boxes"** (1962) about Daly City's tract houses, *in* this MSA ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Boxes)). The Edwin Hawkins Singers recorded **"Oh Happy Day"** (1967) at Ephesian Church of God in Christ in Berkeley ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Happy_Day)). Mimi Fariña founded **Bread & Roses** in Marin in 1974; it now puts on "over 500 shows a year" in hospitals, jails and senior homes ([B&R](https://www.breadandroses.org/mission-and-history)).
- **Earlier still:** San Francisco-born **Sidney Robertson Cowell** directed the WPA/Library of Congress/UC Berkeley **Northern California Folk Music Project** (1938-40), with a staff of 20, 35 hours of recordings and 168 photographs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Robertson_Cowell)).

### 4. Traditional singing

#### Sacred Harp
**Bay Area Sacred Harp (BASH)**, a 501(c)(3), sings "pretty much every week of the year" ([home](https://bayareasacredharp.org/); [local singings](https://bayareasacredharp.org/local-singings/)):
- **Berkeley, every Monday** 7-9pm at Good Shepherd Episcopal (West Berkeley, a flat 15-minute walk from North Berkeley BART). First Mondays open with a half-hour *Learner's Group* singing school. On 3rd and 5th Mondays the class sings from "other" books, currently the Cooper Revised Edition (2012).
- **San Francisco**, 1st and 3rd Sundays at noon in a Bernal Heights home. There's a 5th-Sunday *Shenandoah Harmony* singing, and a December "Cookham & Edom Singing & Cookie Exchange" ("bring 2 dozen cookies... or don't, and just eat cookies!").
- **Palo Alto** (outside the MSA), 2nd and 4th Sundays.
- A 4th-Wednesday **Christian Harmony** singing online over **Jamulus**, low-latency audio software that lets people sing together in near real time.
- The local books are already the new **Sacred Harp 2025 Edition**.
- All-day singings: the **Helwig-Coghlan Memorial Singing** (Nov 7, 2026, Berkeley, dinner on the grounds) and the **All-California Sacred Harp Convention** (Jan 16-17, 2027, Alameda), which alternates between the Bay Area and Los Angeles. The fasola.org minutes for January 1995 call it the "**seventh annual**" All-California convention, held that year at Lucie Stern Community Center in Palo Alto, which puts the start around **1989** ([minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=California%20Convention&n=10)). Carolyn Deacy, elected convention chair in 1995, is on BASH's three-person board in 2026 ([About BASH](https://bayareasacredharp.org/aboutbash/)).
- *Not found:* a documented founding date for regular Bay Area singings. An AI search summary claimed "late 1970s/early 1980s" Berkeley beginnings without a source; treat it as unverified.

#### Sea chanteys
The **San Francisco Maritime National Historical Park chantey sing** began in **1981** with ranger Dave Nettell. Ranger **Peter Kasin**, who attended his first sing in November 1989, took it over in 1996 ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/chantey-sing-san-francisco); [NPS 30th-anniversary release](https://www.nps.gov/safr/learn/news/thirtysing.htm)).
- **Format:** for decades it ran on first Saturdays from 8pm to midnight aboard the 1890 ferryboat ***Eureka***, with three rules ("no drinking, no smoking, no falling off the pier"), crowds "sometimes as many as several hundred," and always **"Away Rio"** as the opening song ([Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2015/12/the-chantey-sing-remembers-san-francisco-s-place-in-sailing-history/)).
- **Kasin:** also an Irish fiddler at the Starry Plough. His line: "Sailors weren't recruited from opera houses!"
- **Awards:** Best of the Bay from the *Bay Guardian* (1997, 2011) and Best of SF from *SF Weekly* (2005, 2010).
- **2026 status:** it continues on first Saturdays (Oct 3, Nov 7 and Dec 5 listed by the [SF Maritime National Park Association](https://maritime.org/events/)), now apparently at **6pm in the Maritime Museum building** (900 Beach St) per the SFFMC calendar, not late night on the *Eureka*. A **virtual sing** on a 3rd or 4th Saturday has raised **$29,480 from 714 supporters** ([Givebutter](https://givebutter.com/ChanteySing)).
- **Local songs:** the park notes chanteys about San Francisco's crimps **Shanghai Brown** and **Larry Marr**, and about hell ships and the Gold Rush ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/safr/learn/historyculture/chantey-sing.htm)).
- **Nearby:** Mare Island in Vallejo (Solano County, outside the MSA) holds its own sings. Sláinte in Oakland's Jack London Square hosted a shanty sing on Nov 5, 2026 (recurrence unknown).
- **People:** the Nov-Dec 2026 *folknik* memorializes **Allan MacLeod**, a Scottish singer who learned "under the critical eye of Ewan MacColl and Bert Lloyd," founded the Berkeley **Singers' Circle**, and made *Deepwater Shanties* (1997) with Dick Holdstock.

#### Balkan, Georgian, Slavic and Yiddish song
- **Kitka** (Oakland, 1979) grew out of the Westwind International Folk Ensemble and takes its name from the Bulgarian/Macedonian word for "bouquet" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitka)). Now in its "forty-five seasons," the ten-voice group learns songs from village elders across Eastern Europe and the Caucasus ([kitka.org](https://www.kitka.org/)). It runs public **Balkan singing workshops** (Nov 14, 2026 in El Sobrante), a December *Wintersongs* tour, and hosts the **Iavnana Georgian Ensemble**, a community choir that sang at Ashkenaz in Nov 2025 ([tickets](https://tickets.venuepilot.com/e/the-iavnana-ensemble-2025-11-07-ashkenaz-music-dance-community-center-berkeley-ffa7f8)).
- *Trio Kavkasia*, named in the brief, formed in 1994 per Wikipedia, but I found no evidence it is Bay Area-based today ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavkasia)).
- **Slavyanka Chorus**, founded in SF in 1979 as a men's chorus, still performs (Rachmaninoff's *All-Night Vigil*, Nov 2025, per a search summary; medium confidence).
- **KlezCalifornia** (22nd year, mailing list of 3,000) presents Yiddish song workshops, such as Daniel Kahn's "Yiddish song smuggling" on Nov 6, 2026 ([KlezCalifornia](https://klezcalifornia.org/)).
- The **CCE Cooley-Keegan** Irish branch holds a monthly 1st-Friday singing session in members' homes. It "averaged a dozen people," with sean-nós singers, storytellers and shared songbooks ([CCE](https://sfcooleykeegancce.org/music/singing.html)).

#### Gospel as community practice
The Oakland Public Library's African American Museum & Library (AAMLO) traces Bay Area gospel to the WPA-funded **Oakland Colored Chorus** (1930s, Elmer Keeton) and Maxine Blackburn's **Gospel Singers' Fellowship Circle** (1943, St. Paul's AME, Berkeley), which linked church choirs across the Bay ([AAMLO guide](https://oaklandlibrary.org/content/gospel/)). The **Oakland Interfaith Gospel Choir** (1986) grew out of a Jazz Camp West workshop, added a non-auditioned community choir in 2013 (peaking at 200 singers in 2019) and absorbed the Oakland Youth Chorus in 2022, for eight ensembles ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Interfaith_Gospel_Choir)). *Gap:* I could not verify a living small-group **quartet** circuit (anniversary programs and so on) with 2024-26 evidence; that needs church-bulletin or *Oakland Post* sources.

#### Improvised and world-music choirs
**CircleSing** holds monthly 90-minute circles in Oakland (3rd Wednesdays, First Congregational) in the circlesong tradition of **Bobby McFerrin**, led by David Worm ([circlesing.org](https://www.circlesing.org/)). McFerrin himself had a Monday-noon residency at the Freight in fall 2026. **World Harmony Chorus** meets weekly on Tuesdays in Berkeley ([Instant Harmony](https://instantharmony.com/concerts.html)).

## Chapter 4: Heritage and diaspora traditions
## San Francisco–Oakland–Fremont: heritage and diaspora traditions

*Thread: heritage and diaspora traditions (layer 4), five-county MSA (SF, Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, San Mateo). As of Oct 2026. Companion data: `sf-heritage.json`.*

### The short version

If you measure a folk scene by how many living traditions are actually taught, danced and handed down, the Bay Area is probably the richest metro in the country, and the data supports that. The Alliance for California Traditional Arts (ACTA), the state's folk-arts service organization, [funded 20 mentor–apprentice pairs statewide in 2025](https://actaonline.org/2025apprenticeships/). Eleven of those mentors live in the five counties. Their traditions: Cantonese opera, Lung Moon Taoist ritual, Tahitian drumming, Guinean balafon, Shona mbira, Haitian folkloric dance, Maya Mam weaving, Carnatic drumming, capoeira angola, Japanese shibori and Mexican folk harp. Across the [2022](https://actaonline.org/2022apprenticeships/), [2023](https://actaonline.org/2023apprenticeships/), [2024](https://actaonline.org/2024apprenticeships/) and 2025 cohorts, 39 pairs had a mentor or apprentice in the MSA.

What makes the Bay distinctive is less any single tradition than the infrastructure. Several institutions from the 1960s and 70s are still running:

- [La Peña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pe%C3%B1a_Cultural_Center) (1975)
- [Ashkenaz](https://www.ashkenaz.com/) (1973)
- [World Arts West's Ethnic Dance Festival](https://www.worldartswest.org/) (1978)
- [San Francisco Taiko Dojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Taiko_Dojo) (1968)
- [Gamelan Sekar Jaya](https://www.gsj.org/), [Kitka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitka) and [Carnaval SF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnaval_San_Francisco), all founded in 1979

Underneath them are much older immigrant institutions. The [Caledonian Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highland_Gathering_and_Games) dates from 1866, the [Half Moon Bay Holy Ghost brotherhood](https://www.halfmoonbayides.org/holy-ghost-festival/) from 1871, Chinatown's opera houses from 1852, and the [Buddhist Church of SF's bon odori](https://japantownculturaldistrict.org/community-events/buddhist-church-of-san-franciscos-94th-bon-odori) has been held 94 times.

One caveat on geography: several communities' centers of gravity sit just outside the MSA, in Santa Clara County. Vietnamese Tết, Persian Nowruz, the mariachi festival and the San Jose taiko lineage are all strongest in San Jose.

### Mexican and Chicano traditions

**Son jarocho has a precise origin story here.** In 1989 Eugene Rodriguez, then a California Arts Council artist in residence, met Gilberto Gutiérrez's group Mono Blanco from Veracruz. Rodriguez decided the improvised *fandango* could "reinvigorate traditional Mexican music in California which he felt had become increasingly arranged, choreographed, and staged" ([Los Cenzontles' own history](https://www.loscenzontles.com/fandango-project/)).

- **1989.** Chris Strachwitz of Arhoolie (El Cerrito) recorded Mono Blanco on that first visit for the album *Soneros Jarochos*.
- **1992–95.** A U.S.–Mexico Fund for Culture grant paid for a three-year "Fandango Project" residency at La Peña, the Exploratorium and the East Bay Center. It included instrument-building workshops and a 1993 fandango at SF's Cinco de Mayo festival.
- **1994.** Los Cenzontles incorporated in San Pablo. Its first faculty included Gutiérrez on son jarocho, Francisco "Pato" Díaz on mariachi, and trumpet and trombone teachers for the neighborhood's young *banda* players.

Today the [Cenzontles academy](https://www.loscenzontles.com/academy/) teaches son jarocho, son abajeño, Purépecha *pirekuas*, ranchera and corridos on jarana, vihuela, violin and harp. NPR called it "a factory of culture." Linda Ronstadt called it "the best community-based arts program I have ever seen."

The participatory jarocho scene is smaller and more informal than LA's:

- [Son de Fandango](https://actaonline.org/artist_roster_item/son-de-fandango/) (Claudio Vega and Lolis García, Richmond) was an ACTA 2023 pair.
- In Nov 2025 they played a free, jaraneros-welcome night at El Cerrito's [Natural Grocery annex](https://naturalgrocery.com/2025/11/05/the-annex-sessions-son-de-fandango-wednesday-november-12-2025/).
- EastSide Arts Alliance in Oakland hosted an open "[Fandango por la Vida](https://www.eastsideartsalliance.org/calendar/fandango-por-la-vida-son-jarocho-vs-megaprojects-of-death)" in Jan 2026.
- ACTA funded a zapateado jarocho pair in 2023 (Jesús Cortez, Daly City, with Cecilia Peña-Govea, Oakland) and a Mexican folk harp pair in 2025 (Nydia Algazzali González, Albany, with Belén Islas, San Pablo).

**Vitality: active.** There is no verified weekly fandango; it runs on events and classes.

**Mariachi** is, surprisingly, the thin spot. KQED's Aug 2025 commentary says Northern California schools are trying to "[catch up to more established offerings in places such as Southern California and Texas](https://www.kqed.org/news/12051704/mariachi-strikes-a-chord-with-bay-area-youth-preserving-tradition)." The region's academic anchor (San José State, which hired ethnomusicologist José Torres-Ramos in 2024) and its showcase, Fiesta del Mariachi, are both in San Jose, outside the MSA. Inside it, the youth infrastructure I could verify is:

- [Mariachi CMC](https://sfcmc.org/course/mariachi-cmc/) at the Community Music Center in the Mission
- [META / Mariachi Juvenil de Hayward](https://meta-mjh.org/), Tuesday nights, ages 7–17
- Los Cenzontles

I found no Oakland school mariachi program.

**Día de los Muertos is thriving.** The Mission procession dates to the late 1970s and early 1980s (Galería de la Raza, El Colectivo del Rescate Cultural). It has been organized since 1992 with the [Marigold Project](https://www.dayofthedeadsf.org/about-the-marigold-project), which also runs the Festival of Altars in Potrero del Sol Park. [El Tecolote's 2025 photos](https://eltecolote.org/content/en/dia-de-muertos-sf-mission-procession-2025/) show Aztec *danza* groups leading it. Oakland's Unity Council runs a Fruitvale festival as well. Carnaval's 2027 poster honors the late Mission Aztec dancer and healer Ricardo Peña ([Carnaval SF](https://carnavalsanfrancisco.org/about/)).

### Chinese: Cantonese opera since 1852

San Francisco is where Chinese opera entered the United States. The all-male [Hong Fook Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Fook_Tong_Chinese_Dramatic_Company) gave its debut on Oct 18, 1852, at the American Theater on Sansome Street, at one point with 123 actors. It played to "the delight of both American and Chinese audiences" before a doomed New York contract stranded the troupe. Chinatown's music clubs are chronicled in Ronald Riddle's *Flying Dragons, Flowing Streams* (1983), and the [Great Star Theater](https://www.greatstartheater.org/blog/2025/home-of-the-cantonese-opera) (1925) still stages opera.

The living center is the [Jing Ying Cantonese Opera Institute](https://www.windnewspaper.com/article/jing-ying-institute-shares-the-beauty-of-cantonese-opera-with-wider-communities-by-launching-its-first-talkback-show) at 129 Waverly Place. It was founded in 1997 by Xiu Ben Chen, an erhu concertmaster with 50 years' experience and the fifth generation of an opera family, and became a nonprofit in Aug 2024. On Aug 30, 2025, it staged its first show with English talkbacks and subtitles. In "The Flooding of Gold Mountain Temple" three generations of Chens sang together: Xiu Ben, his wife Ginger, his father Ziping and his son Kelan. That same year ACTA funded Ziping to mentor Kelan, a grandfather teaching his grandson through a state program. A separate 2025 ACTA pair, Ko Si Hing and Wai Ming Lee (both SF), works in "Indigenous Cantonese Lung Moon Taoism ritual arts," which is almost invisible online.

**Vitality: niche.** It is intergenerational and newly outward-facing, but it rests on one family institution.

### Filipino: kulintang, folk dance, Pistahan

- **Kulintang Arts (Kularts).** Founded in 1985 by Robert L. Henry, Marcella Pabros and Alleluia Panis ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kularts,_Inc.)). Maguindanao master Danongan Kalanduyan, NEA Heritage Fellow 1995 and listed in San Francisco, was its first kulintang director (1985–88). His lineage continues: Conrad Benedicto (SF) and Titania Buchholdt (Richmond) were both 2023 ACTA mentors in "kulintang music … from the Kalanduyan line."
- **A permanent home.** In Nov 2024 Kularts and the Asian Pacific Islander Cultural Center bought 262 7th Street in SoMa Pilipinas for a 120-seat theater, with a $5M city grant plus $3M from the state ([CAST](https://cast-sf.org/san-franciscos-soma-welcomes-apicc-and-kularts-to-262-7th-street/)). Kularts held a 40th-anniversary gala in Sept 2025.
- **Pistahan.** The [33rd parade and festival](https://www.pistahan.net/) ran Aug 8–9, 2026, billed as the "largest Filipino parade on the West Coast."
- **Rondalla.** I could not document a rondalla ensemble in the five counties. The 2021 NEA rondalla fellow, Tagumpay Mendoza De León, is in Burbank.

### Japanese: taiko and bon odori

Taiko in North America began with disappointment. Seiichi Tanaka attended SF's Cherry Blossom Festival in 1967 and found it missing the "noise" of a Japanese festival ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Taiko_Dojo)). He apprenticed with Daihachi Oguchi in Japan, came back to drum at the 1968 festival, and opened San Francisco Taiko Dojo that year. He became an NEA Heritage Fellow in 2001.

In 2025 the dojo was teaching at the Japanese Cultural and Community Center (JCCCNC) and in South SF while looking for a permanent home. It held a "Hishou" fundraiser in Nov 2025 ([Nichi Bei, via search](https://www.nichibei.org/2025/12/san-francisco-taiko-dojo-aims-for-the-skies-in-hishou/)). The [Cherry Blossom Festival](https://sfcherryblossom.org/) itself held its 59th edition in 2026 and plans a 60th in April 2027. Its 2026 community marshals were Roy and PJ Hirabayashi, San Jose Taiko founders and NEA 2011 fellows (outside the MSA).

Obon is unusually dense here. I verified 2025 bon odori at:

- Buddhist Church of SF (its 94th, on Octavia St)
- Berkeley Buddhist Temple and Berkeley Higashi Honganji, which alternate hosting
- Buddhist Church of Oakland
- Buddhist Temple of Alameda, with four public practice nights
- [San Mateo Buddhist Temple](https://sanmateobuddhisttemple.org/obon/), also with four practice nights

Southern Alameda County Buddhist Church in Union City also holds one. The 2025 NEA Heritage Fellowship went to bon odori choreographer Adrienne Reiko Iwanaga ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/adrienne-reiko-iwanaga)), who is listed in Saratoga, just outside the MSA. **Vitality: thriving.** This is one of the most participatory heritage traditions in the metro: anyone can join the circle.

### Scottish, Irish, Basque, Portuguese, Greek, Balkan, Jewish

**Scottish.** The Caledonian Club of San Francisco (Nov 1866) held its first Games on Thanksgiving 1866 at Hayes Park ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highland_Gathering_and_Games)). They ran through the 1906 earthquake, the Depression and both World Wars, with 2020 the only interruption. The [160th Games](https://thescottishgames.com/home) were held over Labor Day 2026 at the Alameda County Fairgrounds, Pleasanton; regular attendance is 30,000+, with close to 50,000 at the 150th in 2015.

The piping base is thinner than the Games suggest. The WUSPBA Northern Branch [lists only eight competing bands for 2026](https://wuspbanb.org/bands/), and three are in the MSA:

- the [Prince Charles Pipe Band](https://wuspbanb.org/bands/prince-charles-pipe-band/), founded 1967, sponsored by the Caledonian Club, world champions in 2000 and 2003, at the 2025 Worlds with a mostly under-18 juvenile band
- the Irish Pipers Band of SF
- the MacIntosh Pipe Band (Larkspur)

**Basque.** The [Basque Cultural Center](https://sfbasqueculturalcenter.org/) in South San Francisco is a restaurant, fronton and rehearsal hall for the [Zazpiak Bat Dancers and Klika](https://www.sfbasquefolk.org/groups/zazpiak-bat-dancers). It had the delight of the year. On Oct 24, 2026, the SF Basque Club and the North American Basque Organization staged a full bertsolari session honoring Xalbador (1920–1976), with renowned improvisers Andoni Egaña and Sebastian Lizaso plus US-based bertsolaris. Among the assigned topics were "using driverless taxis, staying at the bar for too long, and dealing with over-packed suitcases." Xalbador's granddaughters (Aire Ahizpak) sang his verses ([BCC](https://sfbasqueculturalcenter.org/blog/xalbador-gogoratuz)). The NEA's 2003 "Basque Poets" fellowship is listed in San Francisco. One countertrend: the club's June picnic [moved in 2025 to Penngrove](https://sfbasqueculturalcenter.org/events-page/basque-club-picnic-yzd52), in Sonoma County, outside the MSA.

**Portuguese.** Half Moon Bay's I.D.E.S. Society celebrated its 154th Holy Ghost festa in 2025, crowned 2026 queens, and keeps a formal *Chamarita* dance in the small IDES hall after the Sunday feast ([HMB IDES](https://www.halfmoonbayides.org/holy-ghost-festival/)). Search summaries list San Leandro (May 24–25, 2025) and Hayward IDES festas. **Vitality: active** but parish-bound.

**Greek.** Four parish festivals with dance troupes inside the MSA: [Oakland](https://www.oaklandgreekfestival.com/) (May; 2027 dates already posted), Novato (Memorial Day, Minoan Dancers), [Belmont](https://www.belmontgreekfestival.com/) (Labor Day) and Castro Valley (October).

**Balkan / international folk dance.** The [Kolo Festival](https://kolofestival.org/) turns 75 in 2026 (Nov 27–29), at the Croatian American Cultural Center. Its own decline is visible, though. The hall's [Tamburitza Festival](https://www.sfcv.org/events/croatian-american-cultural-center/san-francisco-tamburitiza-festival) was last evidenced in Feb 2020, and its website now partly serves slot-machine spam, so **tamburica is fading.** [Kitka](https://www.kitka.org/), Oakland's ten-voice women's ensemble (1979, an offshoot of the Westwind International Folk Ensemble), has spent 45 seasons collecting songs from elders in Eastern Europe and the Caucasus.

**Klezmer / Yiddish.** [KlezCalifornia](https://klezcalifornia.org/), in its 22nd year, has a 3,000-person list and runs the KlezCalYidFest (Nov 2026). It is deliberately handing over to a younger board.

**Russian Molokans.** Sidney Robertson Cowell recorded the Molokan service on Potrero Hill on Sept 14, 1938 ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017701287/)); her photo caption notes the pictured group was "less than a quarter of the total adult group." The church apparently survives, but I found no public 2024–26 evidence of the singing. It is private by nature. **Rated niche, low confidence.**

### Louisiana Creole zydeco, and the blues of 7th Street

Both came with the WWII shipyard migration. "Queen" Ida Guillory, NEA 2009 and still alive at 97 in Daly City, moved from Lake Charles to San Francisco at 18 when her family came "along with many other Louisiana emigrants to pursue work in the shipyards" ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/queen-ida-guillory)).

The zydeco dance scene is easier to count than almost anything else here, thanks to Ellen Papper's monthly [Cajun/Zydeco calendar](https://sfczcalendar.com/) (Oct 2026 edition, updated 10/21). It lists about 12 dances inside the five counties that month:

- **Ashkenaz, Berkeley:** three nights. Mark St. Mary, Creole Belles at the Bay Area Roots Fest, and Iko YaYa's Halloween dance, all with a 7:30 lesson and $25 at the door.
- **Eagles Hall, El Cerrito:** two dances at $20.
- **Others:** Oakland's Oaktoberfest, a Crockett Oktoberfest, a free Berkeley harvest festival, a Gilman Street block party, a San Rafael clubhouse, and an El Cerrito pasta joint.

Richmond-raised accordionist [Andre Thierry](https://www.andrethierry.com/) played at least three of them. **Vitality: active**, though the bands skew older.

**The blues** is now heritage more than a living community practice. West Oakland's 7th Street was the "Harlem of the West" from the 1930s to the 1960s. [Slim Jenkins' supper club](https://localnewsmatters.org/2020/05/06/harlem-of-the-west-oaklands-once-bustling-jazz-and-blues-scene-along-seventh-street/) opened in 1933, and Esther Mabry's Orbit Room ran from 1963 until 2011 and is now [being restored](https://www.kqed.org/news/12062057/historic-west-oakland-blues-clubs-restoration-reveals-layers-of-hidden-history). The NEA list shows how many blues masters retired to the Bay: Brownie McGhee (Oakland, 1982), John Lee Hooker (San Carlos, 1983) and Charles Brown (Berkeley, 1997). **Vitality: fading.**

## Chapter 5: History, people, archives and defunct venues
## San Francisco Bay Area (SF, Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, San Mateo): history, people, archives, defunct venues

*Thread notes for the san-francisco dossier, as of Oct 2026, from web search and fetch only. Structured data is in `sf-history.json`.*

### The short version

The Bay Area's folk history has three layers that rarely meet. First, a **Gold Rush and immigrant song culture**: "Old Put's" songsters (1855, 1858) printed "Sweet Betsy from Pike" in San Francisco, and the first Chinese opera in America was staged on Sansome Street in 1852. Second, **two great 20th-century documentary projects**: Sidney Robertson's WPA California Folk Music Project (1938–40, run from an office in Berkeley) and Chris Strachwitz's Arhoolie Records (1960, El Cerrito). Third, a **Berkeley-centred revival**: San Pablo Avenue's coffeehouses, the Berkeley Folk Music Festival (1958–70), the Freight & Salvage (1968), and the old-time and bluegrass string-band culture that grew around them. San Francisco itself was mostly the commercial "folk-pop" stage (the hungry i, the Purple Onion, the Kingston Trio) until the Fillmore era. In one historian's words, "San Francisco folk music disappeared in a puff of green smoke" ([Chicken on a Unicycle](https://www.chickenonaunicycle.com/Jabberwock%20History.htm)).

### 1. Gold Rush songsters and the first immigrant stages (1849–1930s)

- **John A. Stone ("Old Put")** crossed overland in 1850, failed at prospecting, and became "San Francisco's foremost minstrel composer and the singing voice of the California Gold Rush". He wrote more than fifty miner songs between 1853 and 1858 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Stone)). *Put's Original California Songster* (San Francisco: D.E. Appleton, 1855) "sold phenomenally well" ([archive.org scan](https://archive.org/details/putsoriginalcali00ston)). *Put's Golden Songster* (1858) contains his "Sweet Betsy from Pike" ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/putsgoldensongst00ston)). He died in 1864 and is buried at Greenwood in the Gold Country.
- **Chinese opera.** On **Oct 18, 1852**, the all-male Hong Fook Tong company (42 actors, financed by Canton merchants) gave the first Cantonese opera performance in the US, at the American Theatre on Sansome Street. It played continuously into March 1853, at one point with 123 actors. Lured to New York by a contract that was not honored, the troupe was stranded: a critic there compared the music to "distressed cats" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Fook_Tong_Chinese_Dramatic_Company)). Chinatown later built purpose-made opera houses, the Mandarin (1924, later the Sun Sing) and the Great China (1925). The Great China, now the **Great Star Theater** at 636 Jackson St, is described as "the last Chinese theater in any Chinatown in the United States" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Star_Theater)).
- **Jesse Fuller** arrived in Oakland in **1929** and worked as a Southern Pacific fireman and gandy dancer and then as a wartime shipyard welder. In the early 1950s he became "Lone Cat", a one-man band with his self-built foot-pedal bass, the *fotdella*. He recorded "**San Francisco Bay Blues**" in 1954, and it now has 88+ recorded versions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Fuller); [song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Blues)). Bob Dylan picked up his neck-rack harmonica habit from Fuller in Denver in 1959.

*Not found:* documentation of 19th-century Irish, Italian or Portuguese dance-music venues in SF; the Robertson collection is the earliest solid evidence for those communities.

### 2. California Gold: the WPA Folk Music Project (1938–40)

San Francisco-born **Sidney Robertson** (later Cowell) had worked under Charles Seeger collecting in Appalachia and the Midwest. She came home and talked the WPA, the Library of Congress and UC Berkeley's Music Department into sponsoring a Northern California survey. To qualify for WPA money she had to keep about 20 unemployed people busy, so she hired translators, draftsmen and photographers as well as recordists ([AFC Story Map](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/29d82f51db8848d5b62790c0854ee102)). The result is the Library of Congress's **"California Gold"** collection: 35–40 hours of recordings (sources differ) in 12 languages from 185 musicians, plus 168 photographs and scale drawings of instruments ([AFC blog, 2024](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2024/04/celebrating-california-gold-afcs-new-story-map-on-the-northern-california-folk-music-from-the-1930s-collection/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Robertson_Cowell)). Former AFC specialist Catherine Hiebert Kerst notes that Robertson "was remarkably uninterested in finding the very oldest songs, but rather wanted to document what people were actually singing in their homes".

Recordings made inside the five counties ([Story Map](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/29d82f51db8848d5b62790c0854ee102)):

- **San Francisco:** the **Molokan** congregation of **Potrero Hill**, Russian dissenters who had come from Soviet Armenia, "a town at the foot of Mount Ararat". She found them because on a Sunday walk she heard children in the church basement singing "She'll be driving six white horses when she comes", and she later called these recordings the "most extraordinary" of the whole project. Also Icelandic singers, and English-language songs "from the **Barbary Coast** district". One example: Byron Coffin Sr. sang and narrated Barbary Coast songs, with Mrs. Coffin at the piano, in **Alameda** on Apr 6, 1939 (LoC item [2017700892](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017700892/); *medium confidence, via search summary*).
- **San Mateo:** **Peter Boro**, a Croatian "small California wine grower" who made his own *gusle* (one string of 30 horsehairs) and *mišnice* goat-skin bagpipe, and who sang "Konjanik", part of the Kosovo epic.
- **Oakland**, the best-represented city in the collection, with six languages. Hungarian carols recorded at a New Year's Eve party in 1939. Puerto Rican singers Aurora Calderon, Elinor Rodriguez and Cruz Lozada ("Bolero sentimental"; LoC item [2017701673](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017701673/)). Above all, the **Azorean Portuguese**: **Alice Lemos Avila**, who hosted a Portuguese radio hour on Oakland's KRE, sang nearly 40 songs between Dec 1938 and Apr 1939 and also transcribed and translated for the project. Her 70-year-old guitar came from Faial.
- **Richmond:** another Portuguese version of the **Chamarrita** (LoC item [2017702019](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017702019/)), a figure dance Robertson believed was the first European dance performed in California.

Robertson's 1942 UC Press monograph *The Recording of Folk Music in California* and Kerst's 2024 book *California Gold: Sidney Robertson and the WPA California Folk Music Project* ([LoC](https://www.loc.gov/programs/general-publishing/publications/inside-the-collections/item/library-publications/california-gold/)) are the key texts. Her line to the Library's Harold Spivacke in Aug 1938: "…just let me touch folk songs and immediately everything in connection begins to act as if it were possessed – including me."

### 3. Folk dance, folk clubs and a listener-supported radio station (1938–1957)

- **Changs International Folk Dancers.** In 1938 the Chinese-American artist **Song Chang** turned Valborg "Mama" Gravander's Scandinavian dance circle into an open-to-all international folk dance group. It performed at the 1939–40 Treasure Island exposition's international folk dance exhibition, took its current name in 1942, and is widely called the oldest continuously operating folk dance club in the US ([folkdance.com history PDF](https://www.folkdance.com/LDArticles/FolkDancingM1975.pdf); [SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/entertainment/article/Changs-folk-dancers-celebrate-70-years-3207752.php)). *Medium confidence on the exposition detail.*
- **San Francisco Folk Music Club (1948).** It was not founded by "Faye and Jim", as the brief suggested. High-schooler **Dave Rothkop** started it in a North Beach apartment, calling it "the legitimate child of Hiroshima and the Cold War". After it fizzled he restarted it in 1949 through an ad in SF State's *Golden Gater*. Herb Jager reorganized it in 1959, and **Faith Petric** led it from 1962 until her death at 98 in 2013. Her Haight-Ashbury jams "often approached 100 people", and she started the *folknik* newsletter in 1964, which is still published ([SFFMC history](https://www.sffmc.org/history/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Petric)).
- **KPFA (Berkeley, Apr 15, 1949)** was the first Pacifica station ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPFA)). In 1956 21-year-old **Barry Olivier** started "The Midnight Special" there, a live folk show, and opened **The Barrel**, probably Berkeley's first folk instrument shop. UC administrators told him to stop printing concert posters on red paper ([Northwestern exhibit](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/barry-olivier-festival-director/); [Berkeleyside obituary](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2023/10/19/barry-olivier-berkeley-obituary)).
- **North Beach folk-pop.** The **hungry i** (1949/50, Kearny & Columbus, 83 seats) presented folk singers such as Stan Wilson before Enrico Banducci turned to comedy in 1953 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_i)). The **Purple Onion** (1952, 140 Columbus, 80 seats) is where the **Kingston Trio** got their break in late June 1957. **Phyllis Diller**, the Alameda housewife who had made her stand-up debut there, cancelled a week; manager Frank Werber talked the club into hiring the untested Trio, and the week became six months ([Wikipedia: Kingston Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kingston_Trio); [Purple Onion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Purple_Onion)). "Tom Dooley" went to No. 1 in 1958. Note that the Trio formed among Stanford and Menlo College students, which puts its origin in Palo Alto (Santa Clara County, outside the MSA).

### 4. Berkeley's San Pablo Avenue and the Berkeley Folk Music Festival (1958–1970)

The Berkeley Historical Plaque Project maps the strip ([plaque](https://berkeleyplaques.org/e-plaque/san-pablo-avenue-folk-music-clubs/)):

- **Steppenwolf** (2136 San Pablo, 1958). Max Scherr sold it to start the *Berkeley Barb*.
- **Blind Lemon** (2362 San Pablo, 1958). Run by Rolf Cahn, a German-Jewish refugee, and **Barbara Dane**; Odetta played opening night. (One source dates it to the "mid 1950s".)
- **Cabale** (2504 San Pablo). Founded by Cahn, Debbie Green, Howard Ziehm and Chandler Laughlin; opened **Jan 4, 1963**, renamed Cabale Creamery in Aug 1964 and folded in mid-1965. Jerry Garcia played bluegrass there, the Kentucky Colonels played it in April 1964, and Jorma Kaukonen recorded there ([berkeleyfolk blog](https://berkeleyfolk.blogspot.com/2009/08/cabale-creamery-2504-san-pablo-avenue.html)). The building later housed the Questing Beast, Babylon and the Long Branch.
- **Jabberwock** (2901 Telegraph, 1963–68). Bill "Jolly Blue" Ehlert bought it on Mar 23, 1965. It was "an anachronism by the end of 1967" once the Fillmore and Avalon paid real money ([history](https://www.chickenonaunicycle.com/Jabberwock%20History.htm)). The same source explains Berkeley's coffeehouse character: limits on alcohol sales within a mile of campus "insured that jazz and nightclubs stayed in San Francisco, leaving folk music and strong coffee".

**Berkeley Folk Music Festival (1958–70).** Barry Olivier pitched UC on a "Summer Folk Music Jubilee" and promised "the greatest folk music festival ever assembled anywhere". The first "Weekend of Folk Music" came in June 1958, **a year before Newport**, with San Diego oceanographer Sam Hinton as MC, Jean Ritchie, Frank Warner and Billy Faier ([Northwestern: early years](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/the-early-years/)). Pete Seeger and Alan Lomax came in 1959 and then sat on Newport's board. By 1962 it ran "thirty-five (count them, thirty-five) concerts, workshops, panels, round robins, campfires and coffee hours" (Robert Shelton, *NYT*) for some **20,000** people, with Bessie Jones, Roscoe Holcomb, Jean Redpath and the New Lost City Ramblers. There was also a one-off Winter Festival in Dec 1962 ([height of the revival](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/the-height-of-the-folk-revival-1962-1964/)). Local tradition bearers included Jesse Fuller and cowboy singer Slim Critchlow. In 1966 the Jefferson Airplane promoted the festival, and the late years mixed in Country Joe, Quicksilver and the Hackberry Ramblers. Olivier also ran events at Stern Grove (a 1964 Folk Song Jamboree with Doc Watson and Mississippi John Hurt), Asti and El Cerrito. He cancelled the 1969 **Wild West Festival** in Golden Gate Park after violent threats from protesters ([Northwestern](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/other-festivals-the-berkeley-folk-music-festival-collection-and-the-west-coast-folk-scene/)). The festival ended in 1970.

Laurie Lewis, then a teenager, remembered it this way: "Every night there were concerts, and during the day you'd be in a eucalyptus grove listening to someone making music with nothing between you and them" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurie_Lewis)).

**Arhoolie (1960).** Chris Strachwitz, a Silesian aristocrat's son, was teaching German at Los Gatos High when he went to Texas, missed Lightnin' Hopkins and recorded Mance Lipscomb instead ([Arhoolie Foundation](https://arhoolie.org/about-chris-strachwitz/)). He added the Old Timey reissue label in 1962 and hosted a KPFA show from 1965 to 1995. Country Joe and the Fish recorded "I-Feel-Like-I'm-Fixin'-to-Die Rag" in his home studio in 1965; McDonald gave him the publishing rights as a courtesy, and the Woodstock royalties later bought Arhoolie's San Pablo Avenue building in El Cerrito ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arhoolie_Records)). He opened Down Home Music in 1976 (still open Wed–Sun at 10341 San Pablo Ave; [site](https://www.downhomemusic.com/about-us/)). The Smithsonian acquired Arhoolie in May 2016, and Strachwitz died on May 5, 2023.

### 5. The string-band revival: bluegrass, old-time and Irish (1959–1990)

The best single source is the community-compiled **Bay Area bluegrass & old-time timeline** ([bgsignal](https://www.bgsignal.com/bay-area-bluegrass-history-1959-2001.html)):

- **1959.** Berkeley High friends Mayne Smith, Neil Rosenberg, Scott Hambly and Pete Berg form the **Redwood Canyon Ramblers**, the Bay Area's first bluegrass band (to 1963), named for a cabin east of Oakland. Smith went on to write the first scholarly study of bluegrass (1964) and led the Freight's 1983 conversion to a nonprofit. He died on Nov 12, 2025 ([Berkeleyside obituary](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2025/12/16/obituary-mayne-smith-richmond-bluegrass-musician)). The LoC holds a Redwood Canyon Ramblers collection ([finding aid](https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.afc/eadafc.af023001.3)).
- **Vern Williams & Ray Park** brought Ozark-style "hard" bluegrass, with a Starday EP in 1961.
- **1962–64: Jerry Garcia's acoustic years.** The Thunder Mountain Tub Thumpers, Hart Valley Drifters, Wildwood Boys, Black Mountain Boys (with Eric Thompson) and Asphalt Jungle Mountain Boys (with Jody Stecher). Garcia was based on the Peninsula, outside the MSA, but these bands played Berkeley constantly.
- **1968.** Butch Waller founds **High Country**. The **Freight & Salvage** holds its first shows (July 24/25) at **1827 San Pablo Ave**, in a failed used-furniture store whose lease and name founder **Nancy Owens** took over. (The brief's "Grove Street" and "Nicholas Seeger" are not supported.) Its 1970s bookings included Bill Monroe (Jan 1970), Kilby Snow and Ralph Stanley.
- **1968–70: the anti-contest.** The first old-time fiddlers' convention in Provo Park, between City Hall, the jail and Berkeley High, was billed as the "**35th Annual**". First prize was 3 lb of rutabagas and second prize 5 lb. Judges openly solicited bribes in their preferred brand of booze, and a 40-piece instant band called "The Family Cow" was "disqualified for unlawful assembly". The organizers promised Mike Seeger that when he finally came they would call it "The First Annual" ([Rita Weill's 1971 notes](https://www.berkeleyoldtimemusic.org/berkeley-farms-folkways-lp)). Seeger did come, recording the Folkways LP ***Berkeley Farms*** (FW 2436) with the New Tranquility String Band (Sue Draheim, Mac Benford, Jim Bamford, Will Spires), Mayne Smith's Frontier and others ([Sue Draheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sue_Draheim)). Mac Benford later co-founded the Highwoods String Band.
- **1969.** Paul Lampert opens **Paul's Saloon** in the Marina: bluegrass seven nights a week, with Mike Seeger, the New Lost City Ramblers and Hedy West in its first year. It closed in **1991**, "an event that many consider the death of bluegrass in San Francisco".
- **1970s Irish.** **Joe Cooley** (Tulla Céilí Band) and **Kevin Keegan** (Aughrim Slopes), Galway box players who both came via Chicago, adopted a crowd of long-haired Berkeley players. A banjo player found Cooley by looking up "Cooley, Joe" in the SF phone book, and Cooley told him, "It sounds as though you'd be great at it!" There were Tuesday nights at Cooley's house "after Patricia Kennelly's accordion lesson… a beer and some of Nancy Cooley's fine cake". The players formed the **Gráinneog Céilidh Band** (named for the hedgehog, "just the warmest, furriest little creature") and played the Freight, Dickens Fairs, Saturday nights at SF's Harrington Bar, the newly opened **Starry Plough** (1973) and the **Plough and Stars** (SF, opened Apr 30, 1975 per the SF Legacy Business registry). Cooley's advice was that what mattered was "the sunshine between the notes" ([Joe Cooley Tapes](https://joecooleytapes.org/)). Cooley went home ill and died on Dec 20, 1973; Keegan died in SF exactly five years later ([CCÉ Cooley-Keegan](https://sfcooleykeegancce.org/misc/cooley-keegan_story.html)). The SF Comhaltas branch, named for both, dates from the early 1970s. Mickie Zekley, founder of Lark in the Morning, credits the pair with his Irish-music start.
- **1974–79.** The Golden State Country Bluegrass Festival in San Rafael (1974). KPFA's "Pig in a Pen" (1974–) and "Panhandle Country" (1976–). The all-women **Good Ol' Persons** (1975, Kathy Kallick and Laurie Lewis), whose first show was at the Freight six weeks after their open-mic debut. The all-women **Any Old Time String Band** (1976), whose Arhoolie LP came out in 1977/78. The Red Vest Pizza Parlor in El Cerrito, a bluegrass venue from 1976 to 1986. The SFFMC's first **SF Free Folk Festival** (1977) and its **Plowshares Coffeehouse** at Fort Mason (1977–98). The David Grisman Quintet, with SF-born fiddler **Darol Anger** (1977). Lewis described the ethos: "There weren't a lot of cutting contests; it was all about making music together."
- **1980s.** The Freight became a nonprofit in 1983, the Berkeley Society for the Preservation of Traditional Music. Alasdair Fraser founded the **Valley of the Moon Scottish Fiddling School** (1984, Camp Campbell in the Santa Cruz Mountains, outside the MSA) and, with a newspaper ad, the **San Francisco Scottish Fiddlers** (1986, now 300+ members; [SFSF](https://sfscottishfiddlers.org/About-the-SFSF)). Fraser lives near Grass Valley, outside the MSA, and handed SFSF's direction to Caroline McCaskey in April 2025. The Freight moved to a larger room on Addison St in 1988.

### 6. Dance halls and diaspora institutions (1970s–1990s)

- **Ashkenaz** (1973) was founded by Westwind dancer **David Nadel** in a San Pablo Ave warehouse with a façade modeled on an Eastern European wooden synagogue. On **Dec 19, 1996** Nadel was shot by a patron he had ejected. The community formed a nonprofit, bought the building and reopened on **June 21, 1997**; a 2001–02 "fund the floor" campaign laid maple boards salvaged from a San Leandro pencil factory ([Ashkenaz history](https://www.ashkenaz.com/history); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenaz_(music_venue))). It is a Berkeley Landmark (1992 or 1993; sources differ).
- **Starry Plough** (1973) was founded by eight volunteers including Irish immigrant **Frances Hughes** (d. 2022) "as a safe space for political prisoners from Ireland, Chile and Honduras". Chilean exiles bought the building next door, which became **La Peña** (1975). It survived COVID on grants and a ~$67K GoFundMe, celebrated its 50th in 2024, and plans to become a nonprofit ([Berkeleyside](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2024/03/13/starry-plough-irish-pub-berkeley-50-years)). Shay Black, who started its Sunday sessions: "American bars are for people to get away from other people."
- **Kitka** (1979, Oakland; a Westwind offshoot), **SF Taiko Dojo** (1968, the first in North America), **Diamano Coura** (1975, Oakland) and **Kalilang Kulintang Arts** (SF).

### 8. Decline, consolidation and post-2020 (1990–2026)

- **Losses.** Paul's Saloon (1991); KCSM's "Bay Area Bluegrass" (dropped 1993/94 when the station went all-jazz); the Plowshares Coffeehouse (1998); Radio Valencia's Sunday bluegrass (closed June 2000). The **SF Bluegrass & Old-Time Festival** (2000–2011) last ran Feb 11–20, 2011; its website stayed frozen on that announcement through 2020 ([Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20200606042745/http://www.sfbluegrass.org:80/)), and the domain now serves gambling spam.
- **Gains.** "Bluegrass Signal" on KALW (1995). Warren Hellman's free **Strictly Bluegrass** (Oct 2001; "Hardly" added in 2004 after Emmylou Harris showed up with a non-bluegrass band), which drew an estimated 750,000 in 2011. Hellman died that year and his family still funds it without sponsors ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardly_Strictly_Bluegrass)); the 2026 edition was Oct 2–4 ([NCBS](https://ncbs.us/)). Suzy Thompson's **Berkeley Old Time Music Convention** relaunch (2004) started because the Ecology Center offered her $300 for a bluegrass festival, which "would barely cover a single bluegrass band, so we put on the string band contest instead". At the first contest the winning Squirrelly Stringband made Maria Muldaur dance so hard "her slip ended up around her ankles" ([Berkeleyside](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2023/09/20/berkeley-old-time-music-convention-2)). The Freight's purpose-built 490-seat hall at 2020 Addison (Aug 27, 2009). The SFFMC's El Cerrito Free Folk Festival (2009).
- **Deaths that closed the revival generation.** Archie Green (2009, SF), Faith Petric (2013), Sue Draheim (2013), Strachwitz (2023), Barry Olivier (2023), Barbara Dane (Oct 2024, Oakland, aged 97; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Dane)), Zakir Hussain (Dec 2024) and Mayne Smith (Nov 2025).
- **COVID.** The SFFMC went to Zoom in 2020 and was back in person by fall 2021 ([SFFMC](https://www.sffmc.org/history/)). The Freight livestreamed and modelled reopening at about 160 seats, a third of capacity ([Berkeleyside, May 2020](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2020/05/07/how-do-you-keep-live-music-playing-in-the-age-of-social-distancing)). Hardly Strictly went virtual and helped distribute $1M in relief. The Starry Plough closed for about a year.
- **The cost squeeze.** The Freight ran a deficit of about **$940K in FY2023**: revenue $4.9M against $5.9M in costs (up 28% year on year), ticket sales of $2.2M against a $2.8M target, 270 shows a year and a staff of 70. New managing director Clayton Shelvin, the Freight's first Black executive, asks: "Can we sustain doing 270 shows a year?" Co-chair Barbara Higbie: "The real majority of young people are not necessarily listening to folk music right now — not like the 60s." The Freight's answer has included a "Hip-Hop is Folk Music" initiative ([Berkeleyside, Mar 2024](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2024/03/25/berkeleys-freight-salvage-attempts-rebound-1-million-dollar-deficit)). I found no systematic study of musicians leaving the Bay Area over housing costs; the evidence above is about venues, not players.

### 9. Archives worth knowing

- **LoC "California Gold"**, plus the 2024 Story Map and Kerst's book.
- **Berkeley Folk Music Festival Archive at Northwestern.** About 33,500 items, bought from Olivier in 1973 through bookseller (and People's Park-era city councilman) John Swingle. It is fully digitized and also holds **Faith Petric's** folk-journal collection, "the Fort Knox of folk music" ([foreword](https://sites.northwestern.edu/bfmf/foreword/)).
- **Arhoolie Foundation** (El Cerrito; Strachwitz Frontera Collection) and the **Smithsonian Folkways** Arhoolie catalog. ***Berkeley Farms*** (FW 2436) is the essential snapshot of 1968–70 Berkeley old-time.
- **Joe Cooley Tapes** (free MP3s, about 1970–73), the **LoC Redwood Canyon Ramblers collection**, the bgsignal timeline, the Chicken on a Unicycle club histories, the Berkeley plaque project, the SFFMC's *folknik*, and KPFA tapes at the **Pacifica Radio Archives** (North Hollywood; folk holdings not itemized here).

### 10. Tunes and songs named for the place

The Session API has few hits ([search results](https://thesession.org/tunes/search?q=san+francisco)). The real "Bay Area tune" is **Cooley's**: tune #1 on The Session, in 6,800+ tunebooks, originally "The Tulla Reel" and renamed for a player who spent his last American years here ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/1)). With it go "The Wise Maid", "Kevin Keegan's Waltz" and Sean Leahy's "Farewell to Joe Cooley". Tunes actually named for the place:

- **The San Francisco** (reel), by Charlie Lennon; it follows his "The Windy City" ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/4387)).
- **Dr. Angus & Emily MacDonald's Trip to San Francisco** (jig), by Alasdair Fraser ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/18427)).
- **Golden Gate** (waltz), by Guillaume Sparrow-Pepin ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/19836)).
- **Trip to Berkeley** (jig), by Michigan's Marty Somberg ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/20111)).

The "**Frisco**" hornpipe is a false friend: it is the Sliabh Luachra tune *An Gleanntán*. As a Session commenter put it, no one in the Bay Area calls SF "Frisco" ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/11137)). Songs: "San Francisco Bay Blues" (Fuller), "Sweet Betsy from Pike" (Stone), and Barry Olivier's "I Remember Berkeley in the Forties". *Not found:* any tune for Oakland, Marin, Tamalpais, Alameda or Sausalito.

### Source disagreements and caveats

- **Freight & Salvage.** Founded 1968 by Nancy Owens at 1827 San Pablo Ave; it moved in 1988 to a larger Addison St room and on Aug 27, 2009 to the 490-seat hall at 2020 Addison (the Grammy site says "400-plus"). The brief's "Grove Street / Nicholas Seeger" is unsupported.
- **Joe Cooley's US arrival.** 1953 ([Tapes](https://joecooleytapes.org/)), 1954 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Cooley)) or after a 1957 farewell photo ([CCÉ](https://sfcooleykeegancce.org/misc/cooley-keegan_story.html)). CCÉ says he came to SF in the early 1970s.
- **Provo Park numbering.** The 1969 and 1970 editions were the "17th" and "22nd" according to the BOTMC site, but the reverse according to Weill's 1971 notes.
- **Ashkenaz landmark year.** 1992 or 1993.
- **Blind Lemon founding.** 1958 or the mid-1950s.
- **WPA project size.** 35 hours (Wikipedia) or 40 hours (AFC).
- **Colby Street house.** Placed in Berkeley (Tapes) or North Oakland (Wikipedia).
- **Plough and Stars.** The registry-based summary names Bob Heaney as founder and also as the Starry Plough's original owner, which conflicts with the Starry Plough's own account of eight volunteer founders. *Not resolved.*
- **Not verified for this file.** Kevin Carr, Erik Hoffman, Keith Terry, Paul Kotapish, Rushad Eggleston, Jeremy Kittel, Natalie Haas, and Lark in the Morning's SF store.
