# Minneapolis–St. Paul: the folk scene of the Twin Cities metro

*Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington, MN-WI MSA (CBSA 33460; 16 counties; 3.79 million people in 2025). Research as of October 2026. Companion data: `minneapolis.json`.*

## The scene in a nutshell

The Twin Cities run one of America's densest participatory folk calendars: about **200 recurring events a month** inside the 16-county MSA, almost all cheap or free. Two hubs carry it. In South Minneapolis, a single block of Minnehaha Avenue holds Tapestry Folkdance Center, which has run something nearly every night since it bought its building in 1999, and an American Legion hall across the street full of learner jams. In St. Paul's Midway, the Celtic Junction and the Dubliner Pub anchor Irish music. Irish sessions, old-time jams and bluegrass jams each run about 25–31 times a month; Sacred Harp is sung every Tuesday; a pub shanty sing runs four Mondays a month. The signature is Scandinavian-American: a Dalarna fiddle style that survived in Minneapolis, Hardanger and nyckelharpa groups, Nordic dance. Beside it thrives a younger heritage scene (Dakota and Ojibwe powwow, Hmong qeej, Somali dance) drawing crowds of 30,000–45,000. 2025–26 also brought real losses.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **The Minnehaha block.** Tapestry Folkdance Center (3748 Minnehaha Ave) and Giantvalley American Legion Post #234 directly across the street host **40+ participatory trad events a month** between them: contra, English, international, Israeli, Scottish, céilí, Nordic, balfolk, plus slow Irish, old-time and bluegrass jams. It is the densest trad block found anywhere in this research.
2. **A Swedish fiddle style that "should not exist."** Edwin Johnson left Rättvik in 1924 without his fiddle (his mother: "You are going to America to work, not to play"), built one, and passed a Dalarna repertoire to grandson **Paul Dahlin** (NEA Heritage Fellow 1996). Swedish fiddlers were "amazed" at a style "thought to have disappeared." It is now the 40+ member **ASI Spelmanslag**.
3. **Monday is singing night.** A free, unaccompanied shanty sing rotates through four venues on the 1st–4th Mondays (one outdoors at Minnehaha Falls, in a picnic shelter the singers rent themselves). It began around 2010, a decade before TikTok's shanty craze. Two community singalongs and the old-time square dance also fall on Mondays.
4. **Weekly Sacred Harp**, plus 2nd and 4th Sundays (~6.3 singings a month), a 35-year-old state convention and, oddest of all, an annual **Cooper Book** singing in St. Paul, an edition otherwise rooted in south Alabama, Florida and Texas.
5. **Two weekly céilís on the same Wednesday night** (the Dubliner Pub and Tapestry), and a 1979 old-time square dance that moved into the Irish arts center in 2026.
6. **One contra for 3.8 million people.** Tapestry's Saturday contra is the only weekly contra series in the metro; the scene concentrated in the building it owns rather than splitting into rival series.
7. **A St. Paul living room became world infrastructure.** Accordionist Paddy O'Brien recorded 1,500 Irish tunes at home on borrowed Library of Congress gear (1994–2013); in 2024 Dublin's ITMA made it the cornerstone of its PORT tune database.
8. **Church-run folk clubs.** The Coffeehouse Extempore was run by a coalition of nine campus ministries. In January 1970 its building burned at −14°F, froze into a block of ice, and the club reopened the same night in an abandoned bar.
9. **The state let its folklore go.** Minnesota's own folk-arts field archive (350+ artists) sits at UW-Madison because no Minnesota repository would take it; the dedicated folk-arts grant ended after FY2020; no NEA Heritage Fellow since 2000. Meanwhile Somali Week (45,000), West Side Cinco de Mayo (~30,000) and Hmong New Year (30,000+) dwarf the late Festival of Nations (1932–2019).
10. **The 2025–26 squeeze.** Homestead Pickin' Parlor (1979–2025, on a handshake lease; lessons fell from 20 teachers to 2), Palmer's Bar (1906–2025), MBOTMA's Fall Jam, a paused state fiddle championship, and a $185,000 emergency appeal for the Irish festival. New growth: the Twin Town Old Time Music Festival (2025), the MinneApple Fiddling Contest (2026) and Autumn Preserves (2026).

## Character: what makes the Twin Cities different

**Institution-heavy, nonprofit-run, and nearly free.** The scene's backbone is a set of 1970s–80s nonprofits that still own or anchor buildings: MBOTMA (1975), Tapestry (1983), the Cedar Cultural Center (1989), the Center for Irish Music (2004) and the Celtic Junction (2009). Song and singing events are almost all free; a contra is $15 ($10 for ages 18–30), old-time squares $10, céilí pay-what-you-can, and jams nearly always free. Listening-room tickets run $15–35. House concerts exist but are almost invisible online.

**Scandinavian-American at the core, Irish in the middle, new Minnesotans at the edge.** The fiddle signature is Scandinavian-American old-time: polkas, schottisches, waltzes, Reinlenders and Swedish polskas, carried by the spelmanslag, the Hardingfelelag (the first US-based group to compete at Norway's Landskappleiken), the Nyckelharpalag and the Minnesota State Fiddlers Association, whose contests require a waltz. Irish music is the busiest session tradition, with a deep oral history (Martin McHugh, Daithí Sproule, the O'Brien collection). The largest crowds belong to Dakota, Ojibwe, Hmong, Somali, Oromo, Karen and Mexican communities.

**Who shows up.** Tapestry reports "over 10,000" participations a year and growing attendance since COVID, with a youth discount aimed at 18–30-year-olds; its techno contra and the new Twin Cities Queer Contra pull younger dancers. The Monday Night Square Dance has run since 1979; the senior polka circuit has moved to senior centers and polka masses; the Somali Museum Dance Troupe credits TikTok with making regional dance "cool." No published demographic surveys were found.

**Geography.** Dance is almost entirely in South Minneapolis (Longfellow). Irish music splits between Longfellow (Merlin's Rest) and St. Paul's Midway (Celtic Junction, the Dubliner). Bluegrass and old-time spread outward into brewery taprooms, cafés, Legion halls, a senior community and a book warehouse in Anoka, Wright, Carver, Dakota and Washington counties. St. Paul has no contra; the Wisconsin counties of the MSA are nearly empty. Heritage life clusters on Franklin Avenue (Native), University Avenue and Frogtown (Hmong), Cedar-Riverside and Lake Street (Somali), and the Czech towns of Scott and Le Sueur counties.

## A visiting fiddler's week

- **Sunday** (the best day): Celtic Junction slow session at 4, main session at 6, then the Dubliner's fast session at 8. Or English country dance at Tapestry; Sacred Harp on 2nd and 4th Sunday afternoons; the Nordic dance at Tapestry on 2nd Sundays; old-time at the Hopkins bandshell from 1.
- **Monday:** sing (shanties at the Local, the Dubliner, Merlin's Rest or Minnehaha Falls by week; community singalongs) or dance (old-time squares on 1st/3rd Mondays at the Celtic Junction, set dancing 2nd/4th, Scottish country dance weekly, techno contra on the last Monday).
- **Tuesday:** Sacred Harp at University Baptist (5:30), then the Legion's slow Irish or slow old-time jam (6–8), Padraigs in Northeast (old-time 1st/3rd, Irish 2nd/4th), Edgcumbe old-time in St. Paul, or the Books for Africa warehouse bluegrass jam.
- **Wednesday:** free drum-and-dance at the Minneapolis American Indian Center (6 pm; listen, don't record without asking), a céilí at the Dubliner or Tapestry, or the Legion's bluegrass jams.
- **Thursday:** Israeli dance; balfolk or the Norway House Nordic pop-up on 3rd Thursdays.
- **Friday:** Merlin's Rest's fast session or the Dubliner's early session; international folk dance at Tapestry.
- **Saturday:** old-time at Mim's Café (9:30 am), Irish at Urban Forage (2nd/4th, 4 pm), then the Tapestry contra (7:30, $15).
- **Plan a trip around:** Winter Bluegrass Weekend (March), Twin Town Old Time (April), the Cooper Book singing (February), the Double All-Day (July), the SMSC Wacipi and Gammelgården spelmansstämma (August), the Sacred Harp convention (September), Autumn Preserves (November), Hmong New Year (late November).
- **Learn before you go:** "The Minnesota" (mazurka), the "Minnesota Waltz", "Saint Paul Waltz", a Dahlin polska ("Danielpojkens Polska"), and the Upper Midwest Folk Fiddlers tunebook.

## Part 1. Trad instrumental and dance scene

### The two hubs

#### Minnehaha Avenue: Tapestry and the Legion across the street

[Tapestry Folkdance Center](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/our-story-1) is the backbone of the dance side.

- **Founding:** it began in 1983 out of Ed Stern's 1970s dance hall, the Saltari Dance Emporium.
- **Early venues:** it spent its first years "nomadic", at the former Marshall-U High and then at Sabathani Community Center.
- **Own building:** in September 1999 it bought 3748 Minnehaha Ave S, "just short of 16 years after the organization's birth", with a sprung hardwood floor, a large hall and a small studio.

Tapestry now runs something almost every night:

| Day | Program | Time | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saturday | [Contra](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/contra) | 7:30 lesson, 8–11pm | $15 |
| Sunday | [English country dance](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/english-country-dance) | 6:30–9pm | $12 recorded music, $15 live |
| Monday | [RSCDS Scottish country dance class](https://rscds-twincities.org/classes/) | 7–9:30pm | $5, first six weeks free |
| Wednesday | [Twin Cities Ceili Club céilí](https://www.twincitiesceiliclub.org/weekly-ceili-dancing) | 7:30–9:30pm | pay what you can, $10 suggested |
| Thursday | [Israeli dance](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/israeli-dance) | 7–9:30pm | $12 |
| Friday | [International folk dance](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/international-folk-dance) | 7:30 lesson, request dancing to 10:30 | $12 |

The Friday request dancing leans Balkan: "the most-requested dances are from eastern European countries like Bulgaria, Yugoslavia, Greece."

Monthly programs at Tapestry:

- Techno contra (last Monday)
- Advanced international (second Monday)
- [Nordic dance](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/nordic-dance) (second Sunday)
- Mostly Waltz (third Sunday)
- Family dance (third Saturday, pay what you choose)
- [Balfolk](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/balfolk) with Bob Walser (third Thursday, new in September 2026). Walser "made his living as a shantyman (!) at Mystic Seaport" in the early 1980s.

The Tapestry [FY24-25 annual report](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/s/annual-report-2024_2025_FINAL.pdf) gives these figures:

- After a pre-COVID decline, attendance at regular programs "is on a consistent trajectory of growth".
- Total participation is "over 10,000" a year once special events and outreach are added.
- Its three weekends drew "close to 300 total participants": Call of the Loon (contra), Star of the North (English country dance) and the International Dance Weekend.
- The board approved a FY25-26 budget with a projected deficit of about −$50k, to pay staff and artists market rates.

Contra house bands ([roster](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/musicians-callers-instructors)) include Contratopia (since 1999), Northern Aire, the Flour City Doughboys (with Art Bjorngjeld, "a local Scandinavian legend"), Guiding Star (Danielle Enblom's Irish fiddle), Speed Dial and Tub o' Cookies (father and son Tim and Eli Hooker Reese). Pianist Mattie Rynkiewicz plays in four or five of them and also calls and writes dances.

On fifth Saturdays (about four times a year) the contra becomes a "Community Contradance" with the pick-up Patchwork Players.

Directly across the street at 3751 Minnehaha is the **Giantvalley American Legion Post #234**. There [Matt Jensen runs four learner jams](https://mattjensenmusic.com/slow-irish-session), each 6–8pm:

| Days | Jam |
|---|---|

| 2nd and 4th Tuesdays | Slow old-time jam |
| 1st and 3rd Wednesdays | Slow bluegrass jam |
| 2nd and 4th Wednesdays | Bluegrass fiddle-tune repertoire jam |

A Friday bluegrass jam (Dan Soneson) also meets at the Legion ([MBOTMA list](https://minnesotabluegrass.org/event-calendar/jams/)). Together the two buildings host **more than 40 participatory events a month** (about 31 at Tapestry and 12 at the Legion), which makes this the densest trad block I found.

Nearby Longfellow venues:

- **[Merlin's Rest](https://thesession.org/sessions/3340)**, 3601 E Lake St.
- **[Urban Forage Winery](https://thesession.org/sessions/8914)**, 3016 E Lake: an intermediate Irish and Scottish session on 2nd and 4th Saturdays, 4–6pm, organized by Julia Nerbonne.

#### Prior Avenue, St. Paul: the Celtic Junction

[The Celtic Junction](https://cjac.nfshost.com/cjac/mission-history/) at 836 Prior Ave N opened on May 2, 2009.

- **Founders:** it was bought by ex-Riverdancer Cormac Ó Sé and his Minnesota-born wife Natalie Nugent O'Shea, who met in 1997 when the Riverdance tour came to Minneapolis.
- **Tenants:** the [Center for Irish Music](https://www.centerforirishmusic.org/about-us/) (founded 2004 by Kate and Jode Dowling, a 501(c)(3) since 2008), O'Shea Irish Dance and the Irish Fest office.
- **Nonprofit arm:** the Celtic Junction Arts Center (CJAC) was formed in 2016.
- **Expansion:** its 2026 capital campaign plans to add an Irish pub inside the building.

Its Sunday schedule is the weekly anchor of Irish music in the metro:

1. **4–6pm: Keegan's Learners Session**, a volunteer-run slow session with its own Google group.
2. **6–8pm: intermediate/advanced session.**
3. **8pm: everyone moves to the Dubliner Pub's "fast" session**, about half a mile away on University Avenue ([thesession, July 2025](https://thesession.org/sessions/1577)).

A 2024 comment on that listing describes the 6pm session's repertoire as "lean[ing] solidly on the music of local legend Martin McHugh (RIP) which features a fair amount of Cuz Tehan's tunes." It adds that "in this and all sessions in Minnesota, there are no hired musicians to lead the proceedings."

The [Dubliner Pub](https://thedublinerpub.com/) also hosts:

- the long-running Friday 6–8pm session
- a weekly Wednesday céilí with live music and "Paul McCluskey calling"
- a monthly Great Irish Sing-Along

Its founding year is disputed ("since 1983" in listings, but [TC Daily Planet](https://www.tcdailyplanet.net/the-dubliner-pub-a-neighborhood-bar-at-heart/) says it changed from the Ace Bar to the Dubliner in 1997).

Other activity on or near Prior Avenue:

- **[Lóma Mór](https://www.loma-mor.org/classes)** teaches set dancing and sean-nós at the Celtic Junction on 2nd and 4th Mondays (17 dates, Oct 2026–May 2027).
- **[The Monday Night Square Dance](https://celticjunction.org/events/monday-night-square-dance/2026-11-02-1900/)** now meets there on 1st and 3rd Mondays.
- **[Books for Africa](https://jtradford.substack.com/p/making-music-at-books-for-africa)**, a block or two down Prior at number 717, hosts a bluegrass/eclectic jam "practically every Tuesday night in the warehouse" of the book-shipping charity. A blog describes "an aqua-green painted forklift stationed a few feet away" from the circle. The host, Roger Anderson, is the warehouse manager.

The metro's lone Scottish-music session is new. Merlin's Rest (est. 2007) deserves its own note as a one-building Celtic ecosystem. It hosts:

- the Friday session
- the monthly pipers' session
- a shanty sing
- an annual "Merlins Rest Day" morris gathering
- a resident highland pipe band ("occasionally one of them will play a set on the pipes. Be prepared.")
- a Friday kilt night, when "if you wear one, your second pint is free".

#### The McHugh lineage

The CJAC library's 2022 obituary of [Martin McHugh](https://cjac.nfshost.com/cjac/arts-review/issue-20-samhain-2022/martin-mchugh/) (born 1929 in Castlerea, Roscommon; arrived in St. Paul around 1949; died 2022) is the key document for the Irish scene's history.

- After the 1950s generation of immigrant players "died, moved away, or indulged in other pursuits," McHugh became "the lone survivor of an earlier era." The 1970s revival grew around him.
- Daithí Sproule (Altan's guitarist, in St. Paul since 1980) said: "Marty was always mainly a session warrior."
- Fiddler Mary MacEachron O'Driscoll learned his music in the 1970s and played with him in a St. Paul céilí band.
- Laura MacKenzie transcribed his settings and produced a 2013 CD of his playing.

The CJAC's [Eoin McKiernan Library](https://celticjunction.org/library/about-the-eml/) holds 5,200 books and 1,700 recordings, plus an oral history, *Irish Music and Musicians in the Twin Cities Community: 1950–1990*, made of 14 interviews by Sproule. One item is a recording of fiddler Tony Doherty playing "Devil's Dream", the tune with which he won a 1928 fiddle contest in Jordan, MN.

The library is directed by Brian Miller, whom Irish Arts Minnesota [honored on Oct 30, 2026](https://irishartsmn.org/events/):

- He is from Bemidji and toured for 14 years with Bua.
- He has taught at the Center for Irish Music since 2006.
- He received a Parsons Award from the American Folklife Center for research on Irish music in the Great Lakes region.

### Old-time and bluegrass

The **[MBOTMA jam list](https://minnesotabluegrass.org/event-calendar/jams/)** is the scene's real directory. MBOTMA (the Minnesota Bluegrass & Old-Time Music Association) was formed in 1975 and claims "close to 1000 members and 100 member bands"; a recent [Bluegrass Standard article](https://www.thebluegrassstandard.com/post/minnesota-bluegrass-and-old-time-music-association-mbotma-creating-community-through-bluegrass) says "over 700" members, so the sources disagree.

Within the MSA its list shows **about 25 old-time and 25 bluegrass jams a month**.

The suburbs are dominated by bluegrass jams in breweries: Torg in Spring Lake Park has two, and Hayes' in Buffalo has one. Other suburban jams:

- a Cajun jam at Showtime Studio in Roseville, on Saturday mornings

Festivals and the new energy:

- **Autumn Preserves:** MBOTMA's November Fall Jam is marked "CANCELLED" for 2026 and replaced by [Autumn Preserves](https://acousticmn.com/) ("a new flavor for the old jam"). It runs Nov 6–7 at the Celtic Junction: $45, with a band scramble, bluegrass karaoke and a Saturday square dance.
- **[Minnesota State Fiddlers Association](https://fiddlemn.com/about):** founded 1986 by old-time fiddlers led by Elmo Wick. It has about 150 members, dues of $15 a family, and "a special affinity for the music from fiddlers in the Upper Midwest". It holds a contest in Hastings (Sept 19, 2026).

### Squares, céilí and the other Wednesday

The **[Monday Night Square Dance](https://www.squaredancehistory.org/items/show/1191)** has run since October 1979. It began with a group of musicians, was the subject of the 1986 community-TV film *Monday Night Fever* with Pop Wagner, and celebrated its 40th anniversary at the Eagles club in 2019. In June 2026 it moved into the Irish arts center.

- **Schedule:** 1st and 3rd Mondays, 7–10pm.
- **Music:** a live old-time string band.
- **Cost:** $10, or $5 for children.
- **Callers:** include Shawn Glidden, Rina Rossi and Laura Idle ([North Country Dance](https://www.northcountrydance.org/region.html)).
- **Clogging:** the Wild Goose Chase Cloggers, also founded in 1979, dance at it and stage an annual Clog-O-Rama.

Modern Western club squares are a separate world: [Square Dance Minnesota's 2025 list](https://www.squaredancemn.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-01-01-SDM-Organizations.pdf) has seven clubs in the MSA, in Bloomington, Hopkins (two), St. Paul, Mound, Buffalo and Monticello.

**Céilí is the surprise.** The metro has two weekly céilís on the same night: Wednesdays at the Dubliner at 7pm and at Tapestry at 7:30pm. Both appear every week on Irish Arts Minnesota's calendar through December 2026. Lóma Mór adds set and sean-nós classes, the Ceili Club helps run quarterly family céilís at the Celtic Junction, and RSCDS Twin Cities (est. 1975) has held its Monday class for half a century. The Irish set-dance infrastructure used to be bigger still: a Third Saturday Céilí at Odd Fellows Hall, weekly set dances at the Vine Arts Center and a Second Sunday set dance at the Eagles club are all gone.

### Nordic: a lot of fiddlers, fewer dance nights

Scandinavian music is heavily institutionalized here:

- **[ASI Spelmanslag](https://www.asispelmanslag.org/):** founded 1985 by Paul Dahlin; plays Dalarna repertoire and rehearses on select Tuesdays at the American Swedish Institute. Visitors are welcome.
- **[Twin Cities Nyckelharpalag](http://tcnyckelharpalag.org/):** founded 1998; calls itself "one of the largest nyckelharpa groups outside of Sweden".
- **[Twin Cities Hardingfelelag](https://tchardingfelelag.org/about-us/):** founded in 1996 by the Valdres master Olav Jørgen Hegge. In 2006, after his death, it became the first US-based group to compete at Norway's Landskappleiken.
- **Twin Cities Nordic Ball:** has run since 1977 (or fall 1976 by one elder's account) and held its 47th ball in March 2026.
- **[Spelmansstämman](https://gammelgardenmuseum.org/event/spelmansstamman2026/)** at Gammelgården in Scandia: held its 36th edition in 2026, pairing Sweden with Japan, and drew a record 976 attendees.

Participatory Nordic social dance, though, comes to only about four nights a month:

- **Tapestry Nordic Dance:** 2nd Sundays.
- **Twin Cities Nordic Dancers:** Mondays from September to May at Charles Thompson Hall, St. Paul, with Char Bostrom and Art Bjorngjeld playing and Carol Sersland teaching. The schedule looks irregular.
- **[Norway House](https://www.norwayhouse.org/event-calendar/nordic-pop-up-music-and-dance-with-kari-tauring-and-art-bjorngjeld-1) happy-hour pop-up:** on the 3rd Thursday of each month Kari Tauring leads and button-accordion players supply the music.

The only weekly Nordic jam I found, **[Northern Roots](https://www.northernroots.org/)**, is in Northfield, outside the MSA. Players there bring hardingfele, nyckelharpa and harpeleik (the Norwegian chorded zither).

### Morris, sword and the rest of the world

**[Minnesota Traditional Morris](https://www.minnesotatraditionalmorris.org/about/history.html)** began with a 1974 notice for a meeting in Wesbrook Hall at the University of Minnesota: "Two clean, white handkerchiefs are required." Eleven men came. Its founder Ed Stern also started Saltari, the dance hall that Tapestry grew out of.

Morris highlights through the year:

- **Halloween:** Uptown dances at dusk in South Minneapolis, including the Manx turnip dance Hop-tu-Naa and the Abbots Bromley Horn Dance.

**International and diaspora dance:**

- **Balkan:** Tapestry's Friday request dancing leans Balkan. Ethnic Dance Theatre (1974) is the performing company, and the Bulgarian School's *Bulgari* dancers and bands such as Orkestar Bez Ime feed occasional dance parties.
- **Klezmer:** the [Longfellow Village Band](https://www.folkwillsaveus.org/klezmer), founded in 2020 as a secular community klezmer workshop, now jams about twice a month in the Cedar Cultural Center lobby. Admission is free, with $15–20 donations welcome.
- **Yiddish dance:** [Klezmer on Ice](https://www.klezmeronice.com/events) runs the *Red Sea Tantshoyz* series, a live klezmer band and Yiddish circle dancing inside the Red Sea Ethiopian restaurant in Cedar-Riverside, framed as a way to "support our neighboring immigrant communities".
- **Polka:** a parallel senior polka and old-time ballroom circuit includes Withrow Ballroom's Thursday-afternoon dances ($8) and the [Midwest Polka Association](https://www.midwestpolkaassociation.com/), founded 2003 for Polish-style polka with "over 800" members.

## Part 2. Folk song, listening rooms, traditional singing, radio, instruction and shops

*As of October 2026. Covers layers 2–3 (song and listening scene, traditional singing) plus media, instruction and
shops. Research used direct fetches of primary pages, because shared web search was heavily rate-limited; gaps are

### 1. Traditional singing: a city that sings in a hollow square and in pubs

#### Sacred Harp: more singings a month than most of the country

The single most striking fact in this thread is the shape-note schedule. University Baptist Church, next to the
University of Minnesota's East Bank, hosts Sacred Harp singing ["each Tuesday from 5:30–7:30 p.m. and the second and
fourth Sundays of every month from 4–6 p.m. in Room 303"](https://www.ubcmn.org/music-at-ubc), about **6.3 local
singings a month**. Weekly local singings are rare outside the Deep South. The local group, [Shape Note Singing in the
Twin Cities](https://mnfasola.org/), has no formal membership: "The 'group' is made up of the singers who attend on
any given session." It also runs Zoom singings and keeps loaner books.

On top of that come three annual singings ([shapenotesingings.com](https://shapenotesingings.com/)):

- **Minnesota State Sacred Harp Singing Convention.** Two days, the Saturday and Sunday of the fourth weekend in
  September. The Saturday session is at Olivet Congregational Church, 1850 Iglehart Ave, St. Paul; in 2025 the Sunday
  moved to The Landing in Shakopee, a living-history park. The [2025 minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=Minnesota&yr=2025&n=7139)
  call it "the 35th annual" convention, which puts the first one around **1991**. fasola.org has minutes back to
  1995. By my count from the minutes, about 146 lessons were led by about 83 different leaders, including visitors
  from across the Midwest. Chairs: Denise Kania, Angie Payne, Matt Wells.
- **Minnesota State Midwinter Singing, from the *Cooper Book*.** Held the Saturday before the second Sunday in
  February at Olivet. The Cooper revision of *The Sacred Harp* is mostly tied to south Alabama, Florida and Texas, so a
  standing Cooper singing in St. Paul is a real oddity. The 2025 minutes show about 71 lessons and about 51 leaders
  ([minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=Minnesota&yr=2025&n=6982)).
- **Minnesota Double All-Day Singing.** Held the second weekend of July at Unity Unitarian Church on Portland Ave,
  St. Paul, from the *Shenandoah Harmony* and the *Valley Pocket Harmonist*. The listing says it "generally includes
  singing school Friday evening and social gathering Saturday evening. Dinner on the grounds midday each singing day."

The fasola.org minutes search returns [453 singings mentioning "Minnesota"](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=Minnesota)
since 1995, a measure of how much Minnesotans travel to sing elsewhere. Duluth has its own group and an annual
singing (October 2025); it is outside the MSA. A cross-thread link: Steven Levine, who leads at the Sacred Harp
convention, is also a "songherder" at the shanty sings.

#### Sea shanties every Monday

The second surprise is the [Twin Cities shanty-sing rotation](https://www.fox9.com/news/songs-sea-popular-land-10):

| Monday of month | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | [The Local West End, St. Louis Park](https://westopolis.org/events/sea-shanty-sing-at-the-local/) | 7–9 pm, free; scheduled through Dec 2026 |
| 2nd | [The Dubliner Pub, Midway, St. Paul](https://seashanties4all.com/venues/dubliner-pub-sea-shanty-sing/) | the original; "No bands, no instruments" |
| 3rd | [Merlins Rest, Longfellow, Minneapolis](https://seashanties4all.com/venues/third-monday-shanty-sing-at-merlins-rest-pub/) | "less shanty-specific", more chorus songs |
| 4th | [Wabun Picnic Shelter D, Minnehaha Falls](https://irishartsmn.org/event/fourth-monday-wabun-outdoor-sing/) | outdoors; the singers rent the shelter themselves |
| 5th | ad hoc, e.g. [Shanties on the Shore, Harriet Island Great Staircase](https://happeningnext.com/event/shanties-on-the-shore-a-fifth-monday-sing-on-harriet-island-eid3a0cl59p49) | organized by Bonnie Jean MacKay, presented by the St. Paul Yacht Club |

Fox 9 (Sept 2025) traced it to "more than 15 years ago, when a group of folk singers, English folk dancers and
reenactors started holding regular get-togethers to perform traditional folk songs about water." That puts the start
around 2010, a decade before the 2021 TikTok shanty craze. Co-founder **Nat Case**: "We've gotten used to music being
a thing that you listen to on the radio... For most of human history, when we've been singing music, the whole point
is you get everyone to sing." Each sing has a **songherder** (Steven Levine, Robin Murie and Doug McNair are named)
"who takes names and makes sure everyone who wants to lead a song gets to do so." In October 2026 the Wabun sing held a
swap-meet fundraiser ("bring some folky, useful, handsome or intriguing item to donate") to pay its park-shelter
rental, which shows how informal and self-funded this is. Irish Arts Minnesota even gives these events their own
"Pub Singing" category on its calendar.

#### Community singalongs: Dan Chouinard's circuit

Accordionist and pianist **Dan Chouinard** runs a network of free community sings
([singalong page](https://danchouinard.com/community-singalongs/), [calendar updated Oct 30, 2026](https://danchouinard.com/calendar/)):

- **First Mondays, Danish American Center** (3030 W River Pkwy): "Always a Scandinavian tune or two and plenty of time
  for requests." The calendar credits hosts "with a longstanding tradition of community singing", a nod to the Danish
  *fællessang* (community singing) habit.
- **Loring Park, a Tuesday most months** (Loring Community Arts Center, 14th and Willow): "Now in our 19th year," so
  it started around 2008.
- **Third Mondays, St. Anthony Park** with singer-songwriter **Ann Reed**, at St. Matthew's Episcopal, live-streamed
  on her YouTube channel.
- **Quarterly at Northeast United Methodist**, with child care, "cookies and bars" and a community toy library.
- **December**: several holiday singalongs a year in the Dunsmore Room at **Crooners** in Fridley ("tickets do sell
  out"), plus holiday sings at the St. Anthony Park Library, Lakeshore Players in White Bear Lake and a Stillwater
  brewery. On Nov 1 there is an **All Souls Sing** at Lakewood Cemetery's mosaic chapel.

A lost piece: Forepaugh's restaurant in St. Paul "closed its doors in 2019, bringing to an end the lively Wednesday
night all-request sing-along piano bar (made possible by Post-It® notes)."

Note the clashes: the Danish sing and the St. Louis Park shanty sing both fall on first Mondays, and the St. Anthony
Park sing and Merlins Rest both on third Mondays. **Monday is the Twin Cities' singing night.**

### 2. Listening rooms, concert halls and house concerts

**The Cedar Cultural Center** (Cedar-Riverside) is the anchor. It opened on **Sept 16, 1989** in a 1948 movie theater,
started by Minnesota STAR (Society for Traditional Arts and Resources). It seats 400 or holds 645 standing, with no seat
"more than 30 feet away from the stage" ([history](https://www.thecedar.org/mission-history)). Wikipedia gives
450/625. The same building hosted Leo Kottke's midnight sets in 1969. At first "Social dance, international sounds,
folk artists and Celtic roots music thrive[d]." Its **Nordic Roots Festival** (1999, "a decade") brought new Swedish
and Norwegian trad bands, and it turned into the free **Global Roots Festival** (2009–). The Cedar describes itself
as "traditionally nontraditional." The fall 2026 calendar mixes singer-songwriters (Teddy Thompson, Bill Callahan)
with noise and film nights ([events](https://www.thecedar.org/events)). After Palmer's Bar closed on Sept 14, 2025,
**Spider John Koerner's 12-string Epiphone**, displayed at Palmer's since his 2023 retirement, was given to the Cedar
and now sits in its lobby ([Palmer's Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmer%27s_Bar)).

**Celtic Junction Arts Center** (836 Prior Ave N, St. Paul) is the other indispensable room. Its upcoming calendar
([events](https://celticjunction.org/events/)) includes the Monday Night Square Dance, a $15 Irish Arts Minnesota
tribute to **Brian Miller** (CIM teacher since 2006, Parsons Award winner for research on Great Lakes Irish music,
director of the in-house **Eoin McKiernan Library** of 5,200 books and 1,700 recordings), a talk on music in Joyce's
*Ulysses*, a $25 "Clog-O-Rama," and the new **Autumn Preserves Acoustic Music Gathering** (Nov 6–7, 2026, $45 pass). Autumn
Preserves "builds on the legacy of the Minnesota Bluegrass and Old-Time Music Association's Fall Jam," with two open
stages, a band scramble and "Bluegrass Karaoke." "Youth 18 & under receive free admission to most Celtic Junction
concerts." Its capital campaign's Phase 3 would add a three-floor lift and "a vibrant Irish pub" inside the building
([home](https://celticjunction.org/)).

Other rooms:

- **The Hook and Ladder** (Lake St), which advertises "intimate listening room vibes to full blown music festivals"
  across three stages and books Americana and bluegrass bills such as AMERIGRASS (Nov 2025)
  ([site](https://thehookmpls.com/), [listing](https://app.opendate.io/e/amerigrass-with-maygen-lacey-the-birdwatcher-turn-turn-turn-joseph-huber-penny-peaches-november-22-2025-603239)).
- **Crooners' Dunsmore Room** in Fridley, the supper club where Boiled in Lead, billed as "the longest-running
  Celtic-punk and world-folk-punk band in the United States," plays its final show before an "indefinite hiatus" on
  Halloween 2026 ($32.31) ([Irish Arts MN calendar](https://irishartsmn.org/events/list/)).
- **The Parkway Theater**, which hosted Womenfolk Radio's 20th anniversary in 2022 with Ann Reed, Molly Maher, Jonatha
  Brooke and others ($15 advance / $21 at the door) ([event](https://theparkwaytheater.com/all-events/womenfolk-radio-20-years)).
- **Padraigs Brewing** (Northeast Minneapolis) and the **Dubliner**, which host Irish bands and singers most weekends.

House concerts are almost invisible online. The one documented series is a private "Folk House" in Minneapolis, which
hosted Swedish singer Sofia Talvik in May 2025 with RSVP by email
([listing](https://www.upcomingevents.com/minneapolis/events/folk-house/sofia-talvik-live-at-folk-house-448405)). I found
no published Twin Cities house-concert price.

**History in brief.** The West Bank counterculture "congregated at coffeehouses, such as the Extemporé, The Scholar
and the Broken Drum" ([Cedar-Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar-Riverside,_Minneapolis)). **Coffeehouse
Extempore**, alcohol-free and with chess tables, "was at one time the nation's oldest continuously operated folk music
and arts center." It ran from the mid-1960s to **1987** and hosted Ramblin' Jack Elliott, Peggy and Mike Seeger and
local players such as Dakota Dave Hull. It was funded partly by General Mills, Pillsbury, Honeywell and Cargill,
which is a telling detail about Minnesota corporate philanthropy, and it still closed for lack of money.

### 5. Shops and makers

The defining story is **Homestead Pickin' Parlor**, 6625 Penn Ave S, Richfield. It opened in **November 1979** ("We
didn't keep track of the date... customers started walking in") and closed on **July 31, 2025**. Co-owner Marv
Menzel, 82, ran it with his wife Dawn on "a handshake agreement" with the landowner for 45+ years. A new landlord's
rent increase ended it. Before that, the landlord had taken "about 40% of our footprint," so the weekly jams (led for
years by Gene Walton, who died in 2022) never returned. "The business at best was break even... We haven't paid
ourselves for decades." On changing taste: "For a long time, Minneapolis in particular was a hotbed of bluegrass
banjo... Now everybody wants to play old-time banjo or clawhammer style"
([2024 profile](https://www.thecurrent.org/feature/2024/12/18/homestead-pickin-parlor-has-been-a-twin-cities-folkmusic-community-staple-for-45-years),
[closure](https://www.thecurrent.org/feature/2025/07/07/homestead-pickin-parlor-announces-its-closure)). Ellen Stanley,
Anthony Ihrig of The High 48s, Dakota Dave Hull and Peter Ostroushko all passed through it.

## Part 3. Heritage and diaspora traditions

### Hmong: the qeej as a working instrument

The Twin Cities have the largest urban Hmong population in the US. Figures vary with the geography: about 95,000 statewide per a [Hmong cultural organization's 2023 count](https://www.twincities.com/2023/10/02/how-many-hmong-people-live-in-minnesota-95094-according-to-st-paul-hmong-cultural-organization/); 33,000–36,000 in St. Paul proper per the ACS; and "over 66,000" in a [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/hmong-community-celebrates-its-new-year-and-50-years-as-americans/601534546) summary, which probably covers a wider area. 2025 marked 50 years since the first Hmong refugees arrived.

The **qeej** (a six-pipe bamboo free-reed mouth organ) is not a museum piece here. It still has a job: many Hmong families hold that a proper funeral cannot happen without it, and [traditional funerals last three days](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/07/12/future-of-us-forced-to-downsize-their-funerals-hmong-families-returned-to-their-roots) at St. Paul funeral homes. That funeral demand is the instrument's lifeline. The teaching hub is the **Hmong Cultural Center** on University Avenue (founded 1992). It runs [weekend qeej classes for ages 8–18](https://www.hmongcc.org/hcc-qeej-program.html) (Saturday and Sunday afternoons) covering the instrument, the funeral songs and the qeej dance. A state Legacy grant record calls HCC ["the only organization in Minnesota that offers Qeej instrument lessons"](https://www.legacy.mn.gov/projects/hmong-cultural-customs-arts-instructional-programs). The line of teachers is documented: co-founder Tougeu Leepalao taught qeej and wedding and funeral songs for decades; the late Ga Long Thao taught after-school qeej; his student Christopher Hang is now lead teacher; and master reciter Soua Sue Lee teaches the *MeejKoob* wedding songs and funeral procedures in three-hour call-and-response weekend classes ([Hmong Times](https://hmongtimes.com/the-culture-bearer-tradition-at-hmong-cultural-center/)).

HCC also created the **Minnesota Qeej and Hmong Arts Festival**, which it bills as the only one of its kind in the US: Landmark Center in 2022 and 2023, then Union Depot on April 27, 2025, with "more than 2,000" attending. **Kwv txhiaj**, Hmong sung poetry, is passed on through master–apprentice pairs. In FY2025 the State Arts Board paid for Gaosong Heu of Oakdale to keep studying with master Pang Her Vang ([MSAB FY2025 list](https://www.arts.state.mn.us/grants/2025/2025-awarded-ce.htm)), and song poet Bee Yang has been profiled by [TPT](https://www.tptoriginals.org/explore-the-mesmerizing-art-of-hmong-song-poetry/).

The two mass events are both run by United Hmong Family Inc. The **Minnesota Hmong New Year** at Saint Paul RiverCentre (45th annual, Nov 29–30, 2025; Nov 28–29, 2026) has singing and dance competitions ([RiverCentre](https://www.rivercentre.org/news/detail/minnesota-hmong-new-year-returns-to-saint-paul-rivercentre-november-29-30-2025)). The **Hmong International Freedom Festival ("J4")** at Como Park's McMurray Field (43rd in 2025) is mostly a sports tournament with music and family reunions ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/hmong-international-freedom-fest-2025-como-park-st-paul/)).

**Vitality: active.** There is a working teaching pipeline driven by real ritual need. The risk is that it rests on one small nonprofit and its grant cycle; the qeej festival says outright that it continues only "if sufficient funding support is received."

### Dakota and Ojibwe: powwow as a weekly practice

Minneapolis was a destination of federal relocation policy. The **Minneapolis American Indian Center** (opened 1975 on Franklin Avenue as the Minneapolis Regional Native American Center) says almost half of Minnesota's roughly 70,000 Native people now live in the Twin Cities ([MAIC history](https://www.maicnet.org/history/)). MAIC reopened in 2024 after a $32.5 million renovation and celebrated 50 years in 2025.

The best thing a visitor can do here is free, weekly and open to everyone. **CLAN Drum and Dance**, every Wednesday 6:00–7:30 pm in the Frances Fairbanks Memorial Gymnasium, offers drum instruction, dance instructors (hoop, women's fancy, jingle, grass) and practice for women back-up singers, and it is livestreamed ([CLAN page](https://www.maicnet.org/clan/)). The same program runs the **Tradish Project** (dance classes Wednesday and Thursday afternoons, plus an Elders' sewing circle that makes regalia for youth dancers), a third-Friday Family Fun Night, free online Dakota and Ojibwe language classes, and quarterly community powwows. Those include the Indigenous Peoples Day powwow (Oct 13, 2025, [MPR](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/05/22/memorial-day-powwows-what-to-know)) and the 20th Gathering for Our Children & Returning Adoptees Powwow (Nov 7, 2026, [The Circle](https://thecirclenews.org/events/)).

The annual cycle inside the MSA:

- the University of Minnesota AISCC spring powwow (27th in April 2025, "under 100 dancers registered"; 28th on April 18, 2026, [MN Daily](https://mndaily.com/campus/campus-activities/american-indian-student-cultural-center-hosts-27th-annual-spring-powwow/04/28/2025/));
- Little Earth's Mother's Day Powwow (25th on May 9–10, 2026, at Cedar Ave Field Park; Little Earth, founded 1973, is the only Native-preference project-based Section 8 community in the US, [Little Earth](https://www.littleearth.org/));
- the **SMSC Wacipi** in Shakopee in August, a three-day contest powwow with free admission and "thousands attending each day" ([Prior Lake News Compass](https://plnewscompass.org/4173/news/smscs-wacipi-celebration-draws-thousands-to-annual-event/)); SMSC also holds a winter wacipi;
- the **Mendota Mdewakanton** wacipi near Bdote, the confluence sacred to the Dakota (29th on Sept 11–13, 2026, [MMDTC](https://www.mendotadakota.org/powwow/));
- the **Mille Lacs Band Traditional Powwow** at Vineland, 59th in 2025 and so about 60 years old. Because Mille Lacs County is in the 2023 MSA definition, this lakeshore powwow about 90 miles from downtown technically counts as metro.

Twin Cities drums on the circuit include **Midnite Express** (mostly Ojibwe; it has played First Avenue), **TBZ – The Boyz** and the **Hoka-Hey Singers** of South Minneapolis ([First Avenue](https://first-avenue.com/performer/midnite-express/)). **Vitality: thriving.**

### Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Danish: the fiddle line that should not exist

The best fiddle story in the metro is Swedish. Edwin Johnson (born Ivares Edvin Jonsson) left Rättvik, Dalarna, in 1924. His mother forbade him to bring his fiddle ("You are going to America to work, not to play"), so he built one. He passed the music to his son Bruce and grandson **Paul Dahlin**, and as the American Swedish Spelmans Trio they debuted at the 1971 Snoose Boulevard Festival, played on *A Prairie Home Companion*, and gave a command performance for the King of Sweden in 1983. According to the [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/paul-dahlin), fiddlers from Sweden were "amazed at his faithfulness to a regional style and repertoire that was thought to have disappeared." Dahlin turned his American Swedish Institute class into the **ASI Spelmanslag** in 1985. It now has [more than 40 fiddlers "from teenagers to octogenarians"](https://www.asispelmanslag.org/about-1) and a youth feeder group, Lilla Spelmanslag (ages 8–18). Dahlin became an NEA National Heritage Fellow in 1996, and his "Danielpojkens Polska" won a polska-composition prize in Dalarna.

Around the ASI are about 20 affiliated groups ([ASI](https://asimn.org/about/affiliated-clubs-groups/)): the Twin Cities Nyckelharpalag, the Twin Cities Swedish Folk Dancers, the **Vasa Junior Folk Dancers (organized 1927**, ages 3–18, sponsored by four metro Vasa lodges), choirs, and regional clubs for Dalarna, Värmland and Västergötland. The Norwegian side centres on the **Twin Cities Hardingfelelag** (5–18 Hardanger fiddlers, "pre-teen to grandmother"). Its [calendar](https://tchardingfelelag.org/performances/) is a good map of the whole Nordic scene: the Second Sunday Nordic Dance at Tapestry, the **Twin Cities Nordic Ball** (more than 40 years old, held at the *Ukrainian* Event Center on NE Main), ASI's Midwinter Folk Festival and Julmarknad, Norway House "Hygge Happy Hours" with a pop-up dance, the Victoria Nordic Music Fest in Carver County, the Gammelgården spelmansstämma in Scandia, lutefisk dinners, and Lutheran "Nordic contemplative services."

The **Second Sunday Nordic Dance** at Tapestry Folkdance Center (6:30 lesson, 7–9 dance, $15, or $10 for ages 18–30) rotates live bands: ASI Spelmanslag, the Nyckelharpalag, Hardingfelelag, the Finnish group Lauluaika, Skandibears, Button Boxers. Teachers include Kari Tauring, Carol Sersland and Elise Peters ([Tapestry](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/nordic-dance)). Finnish kantele is niche but has a new group, Kantele kollektiivi, and author-player Diane Jarvi. The Danish American Center has 800+ member households.

The large outdoor days are the weak spot. **Svenskarnas Dag** (Minnehaha Park, since 1934) once drew about 20,000 and politicians and celebrities ([TC Daily Planet](https://www.tcdailyplanet.net/enduring-traditions-swedish-heritage-day-minnehaha-falls/)). It merged with Norway Day in 2014, and I could not confirm it still runs. Norway Day's successor, Nordic Midsummer Fest, has [announced it "will not be returning."](https://www.nordicmidsummerfest.com/) The indoor and community events (Syttende Mai banquet at Norway House, 45 years; ASI Midsommar; Mindekirken's Norwegian-language services) carry on. **Vitality: active** for fiddling and dance; fading for the mass public festivals.

### Polka, concertina and the Czech belt: alive in senior centers and polka masses

The southern edge of the MSA (Scott and Le Sueur counties) is Bohemian country. **Kolacky Days** in Montgomery, which calls itself the "Kolacky Capital of the World," held its "91st annual" in July 2025, with polka, the St. Paul Czech and Slovak Folk Dancers, prune spitting and a Bohemian tractor pull. The town also raises a Czech May Day pole ([Kolacky Days](https://www.montgomerymn.org/)). **Dožínky** in New Prague marked 41 years in September 2025. In St. Paul, Sokol Minnesota's **C.S.P.S. Hall** (1887) on West 7th is [described as the longest-serving Czech-Slovak hall in continuous use in the US](https://sokolmn.org/at-our-hall/).

German "Dutchman" concertina polka is the regional style. Its master craftsman, NEA Fellow **Christy Hengel** (1989), worked in New Ulm, just outside the MSA. He bought the Patek concertina factory's equipment and built 350+ instruments ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/christy-hengel)). Inside the metro, the **Concertina Bowl** at a Blaine bowling alley reached its 46th edition in January 2025. The **Midwest Polka Association** (800+ members) was founded in 2003 [precisely because](https://www.midwestpolkaassociation.com/about-mpa/) German, Czech and Slovak styles dominated the Twin Cities and Polish-style polka needed help. It lists the surviving ballrooms: Medina Entertainment Center (June Polka Party Festival), Lake Waconia, North Air/Fridley KC Hall, Park Ballroom in New Prague, and Withrow Ballroom. To see where polka actually lives now, read accordionist **Rod Cerar**'s [fall 2026 calendar](http://www.rodcerar.com/shows.html): Havenwood senior center, the Spero senior center in Lakeville, Minnesota Oktoberfest in Delano, a weekday afternoon dance at the Lennox Community Center, a Lutheran "polka service" in Rush City, and a **Polka Mass** at St. Boniface in Minneapolis. **Vitality: fading.** It still works as a circuit, but its audience is mostly retirees. Deutsche Tage at the Germanic-American Institute (67th in 2025) and Northeast Minneapolis's Polish parish fests keep the Central European calendar going.

### Somali, Oromo, Karen, Liberian: the newest traditions, the youngest dancers

The most energetic heritage dance ensemble in town may be the **Somali Museum Dance Troupe**. Mohamoud Osman Mohamed and his father Osman Ali founded it around 2015. It has about 20 members chosen by competitive audition, and it has performed at 1,000+ events and 15 regional dance styles ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/somali-museum-dance-troupe-minneapolis/)). Its favourite dance is the jaandheer from Sanaag. In the dhaanto, the camel dance, the troupe added an up-and-down hand move called the *jabis*, which "other dance groups have mimicked." Early on, conservative community members criticised men and women dancing together; now, the coach says, TikTok has made learning the dances "cool." In 2020 the troupe practiced at Tapestry Folk Dance Center, the same building as the Nordic dance. The museum runs open-enrollment classes, buraanbur (women's dance and song) and a kids' camp ([Somali Museum](https://www.somalimuseum.org/dance)). **Somali Week** (since 2017) fills four blocks of Lake Street and drew 45,000 in 2024. **Vitality: thriving.**

Other diaspora traditions, all **active**:

- **Oromo:** the Irreechaa thanksgiving is held twice a year, at the water (Powderhorn Park, September) and "on the mountain" (East Phillips Park, May), with Oromo Cultural Dancers ([City of Minneapolis](https://mplsartsandculture.org/events-2026/oromo-irreechaa-festival-at-the-lake)).
- **Karen:** St. Paul has the largest Karen refugee community in the US. Its Karen Traditional New Year (year 2764 = January 2025) is held at Washington Tech Magnet School, with *don* dance, and is co-run by four organizations ([KOM](https://mnkaren.org/series/karen-new-year-2764/)).
- **Liberian:** the first Minnesota Liberian Independence Day parade was held in Brooklyn Center in July 2025, 35 years after Liberians began arriving.
- **Tibetan:** multi-day Losar, the Tibetan New Year, is held at TAFM sites in St. Paul and Isanti.
- **Lao:** Lao New Year is held in Minnetonka. NEA Fellow and weaver Bounxou Chanthraphone (2000) settled here in 1982.
- **Vietnamese:** the Tết festival is held at Burnsville Center.

### Latin American: danza, Andes, bomba, and a festival after a crackdown

**Kalpulli KetzalCoatlicue (Danza Ketzal)**, called Minnesota's first Aztec dance group (30+ years), performs everywhere from Pride to the MayDay Parade. In August 2025 it performed at the *Swedish* spelmansstämma at Gammelgården ([COMPAS](https://compas.org/event/kalpulli-ketzalcoatlicue-aztec-and-nahuatl-dance-and-culture-performance/)). **Kalpulli Tlaloctecuhtli**, a queer-led group in Roseville, received $25,000 from the state for workshops in Mexica-Chichimeca tradition, *including mandolin playing*, the concheros string tradition. **Ecuador Manta** (St. Paul, since 1992, from the village of Atahualpa) has played every night of the Minnesota State Fair since the 1990s ([KFAI](https://kfai.org/minneculture-ecuador-manta-brings-music-from-the-andes-to-minnesota/)). Away Runakuna teaches Ecuadorian dance, and EcuaFiesta takes over the Northeast Arts District in August. BORIKEN Cultural Center and Tearra Oso keep **bomba and plena** going.

**West Side Cinco de Mayo** on Cesar Chavez Street drew about 30,000 in 2025. In 2026 it went ahead on a smaller footprint after the winter's federal "Operation Metro Surge" immigration crackdown. Organizers first had to decide "it was safe to hold it at all" ([Pioneer Press](https://www.twincities.com/2026/04/28/post-ice-crackdown-st-pauls-cinco-de-mayo-festival-returns-as-a-celebration-of-the-west-side/)). I could not verify a school mariachi program in the metro, which is unusual for a big US city.

### Irish, Scottish, Ukrainian, Greek, South Asian, Japanese, Black gospel

- **Irish:** Irish Arts Minnesota (1983) and Celtic Junction Arts Center are strong; the session scene belongs to the trad thread. The **Irish Fair of Minnesota** (1980) was renamed **Twin Cities Irish Fest** in February 2026, needed an emergency "I Love TCIF" fundraiser in May, and met its goal by August 19 ([TCIF](https://www.twincitiesirishfest.com/news)).
- **Scottish:** the Minnesota Scottish Fair and Highland Games is held at Macalester (July 18, 2026), with Midwest Pipe Band Association contests.
- **Ukrainian:** the Cheremosh Ukrainian Dance Ensemble takes dancers from age 4.
- **Greek:** St. Mary's Greek Orthodox youth dance program (2005) and The Greek Dancers of Minnesota (30 years).
- **South Asian:** the Gujarati Samaj's four-night garba in Woodbury, IndiaFest at the Capitol, and a FY2025 state grant to train people in **Tamil folk instrumental music**.
- **Japanese:** TaikoArts Midwest (founded as Mu Daiko in 1997).
- **Black gospel:** Sounds of Blackness, which grew out of the Macalester College Black Voices (1969), is the best-known product of the Black gospel and choral tradition.

### The infrastructure story

Minnesota's state folk-arts program had a strong run from 1983 to 2003. The NEA funded a Folk Arts Program Associate at the State Arts Board: John Berquist, then **Phil Nusbaum** from 1986 to 2003. Nusbaum ran the traditional-artist apprenticeship program and radio modules, and co-produced the five-LP **Minnesota Traditional Music Series** with MNHS: Norwegian-American (1989), Ojibway (1989/1997), *Minnesota Polka: Dance Music from Four Traditions* (1990), *African-American Music in Minnesota: from Spirituals to Rap* (1994) and *Música de la Raza* (1999). When he left in 2003, he gave the field collection (350+ artists) to UW-Madison "upon ... not finding an appropriate Minnesota repository" ([finding aid](https://digicoll.library.wisc.edu/cgi/f/findaid/findaid-idx?c=wiarchives;cc=wiarchives;rgn=main;view=text;didno=uw-ua-uac217)). The Arts Board's dedicated **Folk and Traditional Arts grant ran FY2010–2020 and is now ["discontinued"](https://www.arts.state.mn.us/grants/machf-fata.htm)**. Culture bearers now compete inside the general Cultural Expression program, which in FY2025 still funded a kwv txhiaj apprenticeship, Karen weaving, Somali dance and Mexica workshops.

**NEA National Heritage Fellows** listed for Minnesota on arts.gov total just five, all 1985–2000: Leif Melgaard (1985, Norwegian woodcarver, Minneapolis); Christy Hengel (1989, concertina maker, New Ulm, outside the MSA); Maude Kegg (1990, Ojibwe storyteller and beadworker, Mille Lacs); Paul Dahlin (1996, Swedish-American fiddler); and Bounxou Chanthraphone (2000, Lao weaver). No Wisconsin fellow comes from Pierce or St. Croix counties. Minnesota has had none since 2000, and none Hmong, Somali or Dakota.

## Part 4. History, people, festivals, archives and the fiddle angle

### Before the revival: Dakota, Ojibwe, Snoose Boulevard and the radio cowboys

The earliest documented music collecting in the region looks outward from the metro. [Frances Densmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Densmore), born in Red Wing in 1867 just past the MSA line, grew up listening to nearby Dakota singers. From 1907 she recorded thousands of Ojibwe, Sioux and other Native songs for the Smithsonian's Bureau of American Ethnology; most of those cylinders are now at the Library of Congress. Ojibwe tradition persists inside the official MSA in a way most visitors wouldn't expect. Mille Lacs County is part of the 16-county CBSA, so [Maude Kegg](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/maude-kegg), the Mille Lacs Ojibwe storyteller and beadworker who worked at the Historical Society's Mille Lacs museum, is technically a "Minneapolis metro" NEA Heritage Fellow. Today the Shakopee Mdewakanton Sioux Community's August [Wacipi](https://www.smscwacipi.org/about/) in Scott County draws "thousands" a day. I could not find its founding year, and Dakota-authored or MNHS sources on Dakota music at Bdote (the Mississippi–Minnesota confluence) are a gap in this thread.

The immigrant layer is better documented. By the 1860s Scandinavians were "pouring into Minneapolis". By 1880, five of the city's six Norwegian churches were near Cedar-Riverside, and from the mid-1880s Cedar Avenue was "noted for its abundance of saloons, theaters, and ethnic meeting halls" ([City of Minneapolis music-history context study](https://www2.minneapolismn.gov/media/content-assets/www2-documents/government/Minneapolis-Music-History,-1850--2000--A-Context.pdf)). The landmark was [Dania Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dania_Hall_(Minneapolis)) (1886), built by the Danish Society Dania, which burned in 2000 just as its renovation began. The star of this world was Hjalmar Peterson, known as [**Olle i Skratthult**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olle_i_Skratthult) ("Olle from Laughtersville"). He emigrated from Värmland in 1906, toured Scandinavian vaudeville with a large band (an evening often ended in a public dance), and died in Minneapolis in 1960. His 1915 recording "Nikolina" sold more than 100,000 copies and was reportedly still on the jukebox at the 5 Corners bar in the 1970s ([Twin Cities Music Highlights](https://twincitiesmusichighlights.net/venue/snooseblvd/)).

Radio carried this repertoire into the 1930s and 1940s. On WDGY, [Slim Jim and the Vagabond Kid](https://nafmp.csumc.wisc.edu/slim-jim-the-vagabond-kid/) (the North Dakota-born Iverson brothers) mixed Norwegian ballads, cowboy songs and "Scandihoovian" dialect comedy, and received up to a thousand fan letters a week. A little further out, New Ulm's Whoopee John Wilfahrt, first recorded in 1927, created the "Dutchman" concertina polka sound. NEA Fellow [Christy Hengel](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/christy-hengel) later bought the defunct Patek concertina factory's equipment and built more than 350 instruments.

Lumberjack song is part of the region's story through [Franz Rickaby](https://www.library.wisc.edu/music/collections/special-collections/franz-rickaby-collection/), who collected in Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan and North Dakota in 1919–25 (Ballads and Songs of the Shanty-Boy, 1926). I found no evidence that he collected inside the metro. The **Library of Congress** record is surprising. LOC's API lists 65 items from the 1937 Resettlement Administration trip by Sidney Robertson (Cowell): Finnish Kalevala runes and healing charms from Ely, Cloquet and Mountain Iron, and Hebridean Gaelic waulking songs from Duluth. None were recorded in the Twin Cities. The metro's one link is [Marjorie Edgar of Marine on St. Croix](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc9999005.6491/), who was recorded singing "Get Up and Bar the Door" at the 1937 National Folk Festival in Chicago and who, per the city study, collected Scots Gaelic, Serbian and Finnish material with Robertson. I found **no Lomax recordings made in the Twin Cities**.

The civic face of all this was St. Paul's **Festival of Nations**. It began in April 1932 as the International Institute's "Homelands Exhibit": 15 nationality groups and 3,500 visitors at the YWCA. A "Folk Festival" followed in 1934 with 28 groups, including Native Americans, and the name "Festival of Nations" was first used in 1936 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/festival-nations)). Hmong and Lao refugees first took part in 1980, and 100 groups took part by 1998. It was the Midwest's oldest multicultural festival. After COVID cancellations the Institute [ended it in August 2023](https://iimn.org/news/festival-of-nations-announces-conclusion/) to focus on refugee services. Svenskarnas Dag, Swedish Heritage Day in Minnehaha Park, dates to 1934 and still appears in recent listings, now sometimes as "Scandinavian Summer Fest" (medium confidence).

### The revival: Bluestein, the Scholar, the Extemp and the West Bank

The city study credits a pre-Dylan figure, **Gene Bluestein**, a banjo-playing Seeger disciple who came to the U of M in 1950. He sang labor songs and sea shanties on WDGY, brought Pete Seeger to the Unitarian Society in 1957, and ran a folk-song show on KTCA TV in 1958 that became the statewide "Minnesota School of the Air" radio lessons (1958–59). Theodore Blegen's Norwegian Emigrant Songs and Ballads (1936) had already put immigrant song into print.

The **Ten O'Clock Scholar** at 418 14th Ave SE in Dinkytown is the scene's founding myth. It first appears in the papers in December 1958, though one owner's obituary claims 1953 ([Twin Cities Music Highlights](https://twincitiesmusichighlights.net/venue/scholar/)). It seated maybe 50–90 people, with a four-inch stage in front of the plate-glass window, 30 kinds of tea, and chess by day. Spider John Koerner was a regular from 1958. Robert Zimmerman played there from about September 1959 to December 1960. Owner Dave Lee recalled that his wife would say, "They're beginning to leave. You'd better get rid of him." Dylan was eventually paid $2 a night, was refused a raise to $5, and left for the Bastille and then the Purple Onion in St. Paul ($6). The Scholar survived police harassment (in 1961 it was denied a food license amid reports of runaways and codeine cough syrup) and a string of owners. One, Red Nelson, bought it for "$5,000 and a motorcycle". In 1965 it moved to 247 Cedar Avenue, where Leo Kottke made his first public appearances and recorded his first album live. The original building was demolished on January 9, 1973.

Across the river, the **Coffeehouse Extempore** ([history](https://twincitiesmusichighlights.net/venue/extemp/)) opened in April 1965 above Smiley's Point at 2200 Riverside, founded by "laymen and clergymen". After hippies and a drug-traffic reputation closed it in 1967, it was revived by American Lutheran youth workers. From 1969 it was run by the West Bank Campus Ministry, a coalition of nine campus ministries from Catholic to Assemblies of God, and from 1971 by the nonprofit Young Adult Centers, Inc. On January 7, 1970 its building at 623 Cedar burned at −14°F and turned into a block of ice; the Extemp reopened the same night in an abandoned bar. Dakota Dave Hull, who arrived from Fargo at 19 in 1969, [wrote](https://dakotadavehull.com/coffeehouses/) that everyone got a monthly gig and an open stage every week, and that by the mid-1970s the club was "a stop on the national tour" for Dave Van Ronk, Martin Carthy, Utah Phillips and the Red Clay Ramblers. It closed in September 1987 with $176,000 of debt after five locations; Gordon Bok played the final show.

The West Bank ecosystem around it included the **Triangle Bar** (music from 1964, with Koerner the first paid act), the collectively run **New Riverside Cafe** (1970–97), the **Viking Bar** (closed in 2006, "a victim of the city's smoking ban"), the **400 Bar**, where Bill Hinkley and Judy Larson played weekly from 1980 to the mid-1990s, the **West Bank School of Music** (1970–2018, with fiddle, mandolin and banjo lessons), and **Palmer's Bar**, a 1906 dive that hosted Koerner so often that he had his newspaper delivered there. Pop Wagner and Bob Bovee's **June Apple Musician's Co-op** (1972) played between films at the Cedar Theater and ran its own label, Train on the Island. In Mary Dushane's words it was "a folk music underground". Koerner, Ray & Glover formed in 1962 and played Newport in 1964. The fanzine *The Little Sandy Review* (Paul Nelson and Jon Pankake, 1959–65; the city study misprints 1949) reviewed the national revival from Minneapolis. In spring 1978 the Minneapolis Folklore Society's first **West Bank Folk Festival** drew more than 2,000 people.

### Snoose Boulevard: the Scandinavian revival that got too big

The West Bank's strangest flowering was the **Snoose Boulevard Festival** ([source](https://twincitiesmusichighlights.net/venue/snooseblvd/)). In 1971 accordionist-folklorist Maury Bernstein asked singer Anne-Charlotte Harvey to fly back from San Diego for a few concerts of old Cedar Avenue vaudeville; all 1,500 seats at the Cedar Theater sold out. The 1972 festival drew 14,000, with accordion contests at the Viking Bar and a smorgasbord at a Japanese restaurant. "They came in big busloads from Northern Minnesota… Finns from the Iron Range who came down to dance." In 1973 it was broadcast live on 102 stations via NPR, and the Olle i Skratthult Project formed. By 1974 organizers claimed more than 100,000 visitors, with the old firehouse (later Mixed Blood Theatre) running old-time dance bands in shifts. The debts of that year took until 1977 to pay off. A former director of the Swedish Institute also objected to showing the "seamy side" of immigrant history. The festival matters for fiddlers because, [per the NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/paul-dahlin), Paul Dahlin's family trio made its first major public appearance there in 1971.

### The fiddle angle: Dalarna in Minneapolis, Hardanger in Valdres, polkas on a bridge

**Paul Dahlin** (b. 1954, Minneapolis; [NEA National Heritage Fellow, 1996](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/paul-dahlin)) is the metro's signature fiddler. His grandfather, Ivares Edvin Jonsson ("Edwin Johnson"), left Rojeråsen in Dalarna in 1924; his mother told him "You are going to America to work, not to play" and would not let him take his fiddle. As the American Swedish Spelmans Trio (grandfather, uncle Bruce and Paul), the family played Snoose Boulevard and A Prairie Home Companion and gave a command performance for the King of Sweden in 1983. Visiting Swedish fiddlers were "amazed at [Edwin's] faithfulness to a regional style and repertoire that was thought to have disappeared." In 1985 Dahlin turned his American Swedish Institute class into the **ASI Spelmanslag**, which played Musik vid Siljan in 1989. His "Danielpojkens Polska", named for his son, won a Dalarna composition prize against 40 entries. That is a Minneapolis-composed tune in the Swedish canon.

The Norwegian side runs through the **Twin Cities Hardingfelelag** ([about](https://tchardingfelelag.org/about-us/)), founded in the 1990s by Valdres master **Olav Jørgen Hegge**, who taught with his St. Paul wife, Mary. After his death in 2005 the group fulfilled his request and became the first US-based Hardanger group to compete at Norway's national **Landskappleiken** (2006). Most members studied with **Loretta Kelley**, and many with Andrea Een at St. Olaf. Behind them is a scholarly lineage. **LeRoy Larson** (1939–2020) wrote a 1975 U of M dissertation on Norwegian-American folk dance music and led the Minnesota Scandinavian Ensemble on APHC. **Phil Nusbaum**, the State Arts Board's folk-arts associate from 1986 to 2003, field-recorded aging Norwegian-American fiddlers across the state for the 1989 LP *Norwegian-American Music from Minnesota*. He still hosts *Bluegrass Saturday Morning* on KBEM and was advising MBOTMA workshops in 2026.

The most delightful recent story is **Mike Sawyer, "Clawhammer Mike"**. He is a banjo busker who [played the Stone Arch Bridge more than 100 times in 2022](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/04/15/chawhammer-mike-stone-arch-bridge-closure-minneapolis) and an amateur ethnomusicologist. Looking for old Minnesota fiddle tapes, he found that "the real treasure trove of recordings was no more than a half of mile from my house": a "dusty bowl" of Nusbaum's 1980s field cassettes on a closet shelf ([blog](https://minnesotafiddle.blogspot.com/2011/11/phil-nusbaum-and-his-research-of.html)). That led to the [Minnesota Fiddle Tunes Project](https://minnesotafiddle.blogspot.com/p/minnesota-fiddle-tunes-project-cd.html), a CD by 50+ local musicians with tracks such as "Selmer Ramsey's Waltz", "Ringnessen Reinlander", "Edwin's First Polska" (Adam Hurt) and "Saint Paul Waltz". It also produced *The Stoltman Tunebook* (60 tunes from northwestern Minnesota) and the 137-tune *Upper Midwest Folk Fiddlers Tunebook*, and an [Upper Midwest Folk Fiddlers](https://minnesotafiddle.blogspot.com/) jam in St. Paul since 2018, with chapters in Duluth and Moorhead. The **Minnesota State Fiddlers Association** ([1986, Elmo Wick](https://fiddlemn.com/about)) explicitly favours "heirloom" Upper Midwest tunes, and its 2026 workshops trace Swedish-immigrant music to the present.

**Local style:** contest and dance fiddling here mixes American hoedowns with the Scandinavian-American "old-time" dance set (waltz, schottische, polka, two-step, Reinlender, polska). Contest rules require a hoedown, a waltz and a tune of choice, which can be a rag, polka or jig. The 2025 state champion's set was "Our Waltz", "Dusty Miller" and "Allentown Polka" ([state championship](https://www.mesabisymphonyorchestra.com/fiddlecontest)).

**Tunes named for the place** are scarce: no Minneapolis reel is a session standard. The best are "**The Minnesota**", a mazurka played "from Norse Halls to Police Balls" ([The Session 3625](https://thesession.org/tunes/3625)); the "**Minnesota Waltz**", attributed to the Norwegian-American fiddler Al Sanderson, who took it to Washington State (tunearch, medium confidence); the "**Saint Paul Waltz**"; "**The Minnesota 6/8 Two Step**" (Bruce Bollerud repertoire); and Tommie Cunniffe's reel "**Return to Minnesota**". Seán O'Driscoll's jig "The Twin Cities" may not refer to Minneapolis–St. Paul at all, and "Red Wing" is a 1907 Kerry Mills song, not the town. The oddest entry: within three weeks of its January 2026 release, Bruce Springsteen's protest song "[Streets of Minneapolis](https://thesession.org/tunes/26357)" had been transcribed onto The Session as a "reel", to some grumbling from regulars.

**Contests:** the NOTFA-certified **Minnesota State Old Time Fiddle Championships** (43rd in 2025, so first held around 1983) is held on the Iron Range at Mountain Iron, outside the MSA. Its organizers [took 2026 off "due to lack of adequate funding"](https://www.findglocal.com/US/Unknown/795693740469610/Minnesota-State-Old-Time-Fiddle-Championships). In the metro, MSFA holds a September contest at the Le Duc Mansion in Hastings ([2026 list](https://fiddlemn.com/upcoming-fiddle-contests)), and a brand-new **MinneApple Fiddling Contest** ($2,000 in prizes, emceed by Joe Mailander of the Okee Dokee Brothers) ran at Gale Woods Farm in Minnetrista on Oct 3, 2026. Wisconsin's Laura Days in Pepin (Laura Ingalls Wilder's birthplace) runs "Pa's Fiddle Contest".

### Irish Minnesota: a collector's living room becomes world infrastructure

The Irish thread is quieter historically but has one world-class artifact. **Paddy O'Brien**, an accordionist from Daingean, Co. Offaly, moved to the US in the 1980s and settled in St. Paul. His repertoire runs to more than 3,000 tunes. In December 1994–January 1995, with an NEA grant of $6,500 and field equipment borrowed from the Library of Congress's American Folklife Center, the Minnesota Folklife Society and the Irish Music & Dance Association set up a temporary studio in his home and recorded 500 tunes ([history](https://paddyobrien.net/tune-collection/history/)). Two more volumes followed (2004 and 2013), for 1,500 tunes on 33 CDs with notes on where each tune came from. The project's backers called it the largest such collection since Captain Francis O'Neill's 1903 *Music of Ireland*. In 2024 Dublin's [ITMA made it the cornerstone of PORT](https://www.itma.ie/collections/paddy-obrien-tune-collection/), its new 12,000-tune interactive database, after Minnesota supporter Mark Bickford funded the work and personally transcribed almost all 1,500 tunes.

The institutions came later. The **Center for Irish Music** was founded by Kate and Jode Dowling in 2004, and the **Celtic Junction** (836 Prior Ave N) was bought in 2009 by ex-Riverdance dancer Cormac O'Sé and Natalie Nugent O'Shea. It now houses CIM, O'Shea Irish Dance, the Irish Fest office and the [Eoin McKiernan Library](https://celticjunction.org/library/about-the-eml/), with 5,200 books and 1,700 recordings, including deep Gael Linn holdings. In March 2026 a local real-estate co-op, Taproot, [sought investors for a loan](https://parkbugle.org/193917/top_stories/taproot-co-op-seeks-investors-for-celtic-junction/) to pay off an original investor's buyout. The **Irish Fair of Minnesota**, founded around 1981 and held on Harriet Island with 30,000+ visitors, became **Twin Cities Irish Fest** in 2026. It charged $30 a day and ran a [$185,000 emergency appeal](https://www.newsbreak.com/community-reporter-313943018/4679770394711-twin-cities-irish-fest-launches-emergency-fundraising-campaign).

### The infrastructure decades: APHC, MBOTMA, Red House, Tapestry, the Cedar

- **A Prairie Home Companion** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Prairie_Home_Companion)) began on July 6, 1974 at Macalester College in front of 12 people, mostly children. Keillor got the idea from researching the Grand Ole Opry. The show moved to the World (later Fitzgerald) Theater in 1978, went national in 1980 and at its 2015 peak reached 690 stations and 4 million listeners. For 40 years it was a paying stage for local old-time, Scandinavian and bluegrass players: Peter Ostroushko, the Dahlins, LeRoy Larson's ensemble, and musicians supplied through the Homestead Pickin' Parlor.
- **MBOTMA** ([history](https://minnesotabluegrass.org/History/)) was founded in September 1975 by bassist Tom O'Neill on the model of the San Diego Bluegrass Club newsletter. It had 40 members at the start, more than 400 within three years, and about 1,000 members plus 100–125 member bands later. Its 1980 festival began at Taylors Falls (inside the MSA). That site moved to Zimmerman, then had to leave in 2001 when Camp in the Woods was sold to a housing developer, and since 2002 the festival has been at El Rancho Mañana near St. Cloud (outside the MSA). Admission to the [2026 August festival](https://minnesotabluegrass.org/mn-bluegrass-festival/) was **free**: you pay only for parking or camping. The 1980 swap meet became the March **Winter Bluegrass Weekend** in Plymouth. MBOTMA turned 50 in 2025, but [Fall Jam 2026 was cancelled](https://minnesotabluegrass.org/fall-jam/).
- **Red House Records** (St. Paul, 1983) began with Greg Brown and recorded only Upper Midwest artists at first: Koerner, Ostroushko, Prudence Johnson.
- **Tapestry Folkdance Center** ([story](https://www.tapestryfolkdance.org/our-story-1)) grew in 1983 out of Ed Stern and Rolla Breitman's 1970s **Saltari Dance Emporium**. It moved from Marshall-U High to Sabathani Community Center before buying 3748 Minnehaha Ave S in 1999, "just short of 16 years after the organization's birth". Its History Project has filmed founders and longtime caller Ted Hodapp.
- **The Cedar Cultural Center**: the former Cedar movie theater (1948) where June Apple played between films and Snoose Boulevard began. It was donated in 1989 to Minnesota STAR (Society for Traditional Arts and Resources). It now holds 450 seated or 625 standing, has run a Global Roots Festival since 2009, and since November 2025 displays [Koerner's 12-string Epiphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Cultural_Center) in its lobby.

### COVID, the rebound, and the 2025–26 squeeze

The pandemic's direct toll is clearest at the **Homestead Pickin' Parlor** in Richfield, founded in November 1979. Its roster of 20 instructors fell to 2, and its jams, which once ran up to five nights a week, never came back after jam leader Gene Walton died in 2022 and the landlord took 40% of the space. A new landlord's rent increase closed it on July 31, 2025, after 45 years on a handshake lease. "We haven't paid ourselves for decades," said 82-year-old Marv Menzel ([The Current](https://www.thecurrent.org/feature/2025/07/07/homestead-pickin-parlor-announces-its-closure)). **Palmer's Bar** closed on September 14, 2025. Spider John Koerner died on May 18, 2024, Peter Ostroushko in 2021 and LeRoy Larson in 2020. **The Podium** (1959–2017), Dylan's string shop, was already gone.

Against that, there is real renewal. The **Twin Town Old Time Music Festival** ([2026](https://www.twintownoldtime.org/2026)) began in 2025 and runs four April days of concerts, workshops, square, honky-tonk, flatfoot and Cajun dances, crankie-making and jams, plus a monthly second-Tuesday jam. Its sponsor list includes MBOTMA, MSFA and the "Monday Night Square Dance". The MinneApple contest replaced a paused championship, and Clawhammer Mike's tunebooks put Scandinavian barn-dance tunes back into circulation.

## All surprises

1. One block of Minnehaha Avenue (Tapestry Folkdance Center plus Giantvalley American Legion Post #234 directly across the street) hosts 40+ participatory trad events a month.
2. A Dalarna (Rättvik) fiddle style that Swedish fiddlers thought had disappeared survived in Minneapolis through Edwin Johnson, who built a fiddle after his mother forbade him to bring one in 1924; grandson Paul Dahlin (NEA Heritage Fellow 1996) turned it into the 40+ member ASI Spelmanslag.
3. Sacred Harp is sung every Tuesday plus 2nd and 4th Sundays (about 6.3 local singings a month), and St. Paul hosts an annual Cooper Book singing, an edition otherwise tied to south Alabama, Florida and Texas.
4. A free unaccompanied shanty sing runs four Mondays a month (St. Louis Park, the Dubliner, Merlins Rest, outdoors at Minnehaha Falls); it began around 2010, a decade before the TikTok shanty craze. Monday is the Twin Cities' singing night.
5. Two weekly Irish céilís run on the same Wednesday night (the Dubliner Pub and Tapestry), and the Monday Night Square Dance (old-time squares with a live string band since 1979) moved into the Irish arts center in 2026.
6. Only one weekly contra dance (Tapestry, Saturdays) serves 3.8 million people; the scene concentrated in the one building it owns.
7. Paddy O'Brien recorded 1,500 Irish tunes in his St. Paul living room on borrowed Library of Congress equipment (1994-2013); in 2024 Dublin's Irish Traditional Music Archive made it the cornerstone of its PORT tune database.
8. Minnesota's own state folk-arts field archive (350+ artists, 1983-2003) is held at UW-Madison because no Minnesota repository would take it; the state's dedicated folk-arts grant ended after FY2020, and Minnesota has had no NEA Heritage Fellow since 2000 (none Hmong, Somali or Dakota).
9. The Festival of Nations (1932) was ended for good in 2023, while single-community festivals draw 30,000-45,000 (Somali Week, West Side Cinco de Mayo, Hmong New Year).
10. The founding folk club, Coffeehouse Extempore, was run by a coalition of nine campus ministries; in 1970 its building burned at -14°F, froze solid, and the club reopened the same night in an abandoned bar.
11. Homestead Pickin' Parlor closed July 31, 2025 after nearly 47 years on a handshake lease; its lesson roster had fallen from 20 teachers to 2 after the pandemic. Spider John Koerner's 12-string moved from closed Palmer's Bar to the Cedar's lobby.
12. A bluegrass jam meets most Tuesdays in the Books for Africa book-shipping warehouse beside a forklift, and a Yiddish dance series runs inside an Ethiopian restaurant in Cedar-Riverside.
13. The Twin Cities Nordic Ball is held at the Ukrainian Event Center, an Aztec dance group performed at the Swedish fiddlers' spelmansstämma in Scandia, and the Somali Museum Dance Troupe once practiced at the same folk-dance center as the Nordic dance.
14. A banjo busker ('Clawhammer Mike' Sawyer) found the state folklorist's 1980s fiddle field tapes in a closet half a mile from his house and turned them into the Minnesota Fiddle Tunes Project and a 137-tune Upper Midwest tunebook.
15. The 1970s Snoose Boulevard Festival, a revival of Swedish immigrant vaudeville, grew to a claimed 100,000 visitors and a live broadcast on 102 NPR stations before debt killed it.

## Data quality and method

Strong coverage for Tapestry, Celtic Junction, Sacred Harp, shanty sings, the MBOTMA jam list and the heritage festivals. Weaker: suburban bluegrass/old-time jams rely on the community-maintained MBOTMA jam list (medium/low confidence); Facebook-only events (gospel, some sessions) are undercounted; house concerts are essentially invisible; no Cape Breton or French-Canadian session found; morris practice schedules are not public (teams listed as organizations); the Wisconsin counties (Pierce, St. Croix) are nearly empty in all threads. web_search was heavily rate-limited for some threads, so many facts come from direct page fetches. Only five Minnesota NEA Heritage Fellows found on arts.gov (1985-2000). Lat/lng only where thesession.org supplied them (Nominatim refused requests). Svenskarnas Dag's current status is unresolved (sources conflict). Disputed founding dates: Irish Fair 1980 vs 1981; Ten O'Clock Scholar 1953 vs 1958; Dubliner Pub 1983 vs 1997. Dossier spot checks passed (Festival of Nations conclusion, Homestead closure, Scottish Fair date).

This dossier was assembled from four parallel research threads (trad/dance, song/singing, heritage, history), each checked against primary pages where possible, then merged and de-duplicated. The structured data is in `minneapolis.json` (93 recurring-event entries, 69 festivals, 71 organizations, 79 people, 129 timeline entries, 287 source URLs). Per-thread coverage notes are preserved in the JSON `data_quality_notes` field.
