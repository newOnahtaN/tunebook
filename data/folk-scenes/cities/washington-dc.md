# Washington, DC metro folk scene dossier (MSA 47900: Washington-Arlington-Alexandria, DC-VA-MD-WV)

*For Nate, a fiddler (old-time, Irish, Scottish/Cape Breton, contra). This merges four research threads run on Oct
30, 2026: trad dance and sessions; song, media and shops; heritage and diaspora; history and people. All sources are
public. Baltimore is a separate metro: shared institutions are mentioned, but only events inside the DC MSA are
counted, and anything outside is marked **(outside MSA)**. Clarke County VA and Jefferson County WV are counted
inside; Winchester, St. Mary's County and Anne Arundel County are not.*

## 1. Scene in a nutshell

Washington is the capital of the folklore bureaucracy. The Library of Congress's
[American Folklife Center](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2026/08/american-folklife-center-celebrates-its-50th-anniversary-with-a-dynamic-public-program-series/)
(50 in 2026), Smithsonian Folkways, the Folklife Festival and the [NCTA](https://www.ncta-usa.org/) all live here.
Its participatory scene is large, about 300 recurring events a month, but lopsided:

- **Plentiful:** Irish pub sessions, Israeli and Balkan folk dance, Scottish country dance and bluegrass jams.
- **Almost a one-venue affair:** contra. The weekly [Friday Night Dancers](https://www.fridaynightdance.com/history)
  contra has run every Friday since 1974 and is held at Glen Echo Park, a former amusement park in Maryland.

Dancing clusters in the Maryland inner suburbs. Irish sessions and brewery bluegrass jams fill a Northern Virginia
belt that has no regular contra. The volunteer [FSGW](https://www.fsgw.org/about-us), founded in 1964, holds it
together.

The deepest living traditions are homegrown:

- Piedmont blues, with a Saturday barbershop jam running since 1959.
- 1950s–70s bluegrass.
- Go-go, the District's official music.

## 2. Ten most interesting things

1. **One former amusement park hosts most weekly dancing.** NPS-owned Glen Echo Park holds the Friday contra,
   Wednesday international dance, Sunday waltz, squares and barn dances. The venues are a 1933 ballroom on the
   National Register ([NPS](https://home.nps.gov/places/spanish-ballroom.htm)) and a 1923 bumper-car ride turned
   dance floor ([Glen Echo](https://glenechopark.org/bumper-car-pavilion)).
2. **Northern Virginia is a contra desert.** The [contradances.net Virginia list](https://contradances.net/events/locate/us/va)
   for Oct 30–Nov 29, 2026 shows nothing near DC. The 1983-founded Reston contra and the Loudoun/Fauquier barn dances
   have left no 2024–26 trace.
3. **A barbershop blues jam has run every Saturday since 1959.** Archie Edwards' jam outlived him (he died in 1998)
   and two moves. It now runs in Hyattsville, with house guitars to borrow
   ([AEBHF](https://www.acousticblues.com/the-barbershop)).
4. **The first bluegrass festival may have been inside this metro.** "Bluegrass Day" was held at Watermelon Park,
   Berryville (Clarke County), in August 1960. Sources disagree on Aug 10 or 14
   ([Bill Clifton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clifton)).
5. **No Smithsonian Folklife Festival on the Mall in 2026.** The Mall went to the "Great American State Fair", and the
   festival toured other people's festivals for nine months instead
   ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Folklife_Festival)).
6. **The flagship roots station is becoming an archive.** Dick Spottswood co-started bluegrass on public radio in
   1967. He retired from live broadcasting in October 2026, and Ray Davis reruns began the same month
   ([Bluegrass Country](https://bluegrasscountry.org/shows/the-dick-spottswood-show/)).
7. **Israeli folk dance outnumbers contra about 8 to 1.** There are about eight weekly harkadot against one weekly
   contra ([directory](https://www.israelidancefestivaldc.com/local-israeli-dance)).
8. **Go-go is DC's official music (2020).** Its creator, Chuck Brown, was named an NEA National Heritage Fellow in 2005
   ([arts.gov](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/chuck-brown)). A Go-Go Museum opened in Anacostia in 2025.
9. **The only in-MSA fiddle contest is Scottish.** It is held at the Virginia Scottish Games in Fauquier County
   ([registration](https://vascottishgames.regfox.com/2026-fiddle-competition)). The nearest old-time contest, Deer
   Creek, is outside the MSA.
10. **The DC area hosted the National Folk Festival more than anywhere else.** It came to Washington in 1938–42 and
    1960–61, then Wolf Trap in 1971–82: 19 editions by the heritage thread's count
    ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Folk_Festival_(United_States))). The history thread's source
    ends the first run in 1941 ([NFF](https://www.nationalfolkfestival.com/full-history)).

## 4. A week in DC for a visiting fiddler

### Geography

The scene is suburban and splits by state:

- **Maryland inner suburbs** (Glen Echo, Silver Spring, Takoma Park, Greenbelt, Kensington): the dancing. Glen Echo
  Park alone hosts "350+ dances a year" ([GEPPAC](https://glenechopark.org/node/69)), although much of that is
  swing, blues and tango.
- **Northern Virginia:** pub sessions (Falls Church, Arlington, Old Town, Vienna, Fairfax, Warrenton) and bluegrass in
  breweries and farm parks. No dances.
- **DC proper:** a few transit-friendly spots: Kirwan's, The Saloon, the O Street jam, Capitol Hill shape-note, the
  Hill Center Cajun jam, the Columbia Heights squares and Busboys Takoma. DC is also home to the city's own heritage
  traditions (go-go in Anacostia, West African dance at Dance Place).
- **The western rim** (an hour or more out): the old-time triangle of Frederick, Harpers Ferry, Shepherdstown and
  Berryville.

Most dances need a car.

### Costs

- **Sessions and jams:** free; buy a drink. SMT learner jams $10.
- **Contra:** $15 (FND is a $5–30 sliding scale with $15 break-even).
- **ECD:** $17.
- **International dance:** $5–10. Squares $10–15. Scandia $10. GWCC céilí $25. Challenging Contras $20–25.
- **Free dances:** the Barn Dance and the Cajun dance.
- **Concerts:** house concerts about $20 suggested; listening rooms $25–30.
- **Discounts:** youth usually $5; FSGW membership discounts apply widely.

### Day by day

- **Monday:**
  - Irish at O'Connell's (advanced; ask first) or the Hawk & Griffin (2nd and 4th).
  - **4th Monday:** the DC Scottish Session in Merrifield, the month's Scottish-fiddle highlight. The same night has
    "Get Yer Lonesome On" bluegrass at the VFW.
  - **2nd Monday:** the free Capitol Hill Cajun jam.
  - **4th Monday:** the All Folks Waltz in Clarendon ($15).
- **Tuesday:**
  - Irish: **Ireland's Four Provinces**, Falls Church, 8 pm.
  - 1st and 3rd Tuesdays: the **old-time jam at The Saloon** on U St (Metro).
  - 1st Tuesday: the **Heartwood** contra-tune jam.
  - Singers: Takoma Spark or the Herndon open mic.
  - Dancers: Scottish country dance (Greenbelt or Frederick), Irish set classes or Israeli dance.
- **Wednesday:** the strongest dance night.
  - **ECD at Glen Echo Town Hall**, and international dance in the Bumper Car Pavilion.
  - Tunes: the Limerick Pub (1st and 3rd), McGinty's (4th), the Takoma old-time jam (3rd), Port City bluegrass (1st
    and 3rd) or Harpers Ferry (weekly).
  - Capitol Hill shape-note (3rd).

  - Celtic House, Arlington, or Magoo's, Frederick.
  - The Silver Spring contra (2nd).
  - The SMT slow jam (2nd and 4th).

  - Chanteys: the Auld Shebeen (1st), the Limerick folk sing (3rd) or the Tall Ship (4th).
  - O'Hurley's (Shepherdstown) or Barns of Rose Hill (3rd).

  - **NoVA Irish Session** at noon, Fairfax.

  - At night: the DC Square Dance Collective (about monthly), Scandia (3rd), Silver Spring ECD (2nd) or the

  - Bluegrass at O Street or Frying Pan (1st and 3rd) or CABOMA (2nd and 4th).

### Tuesday vs. Saturday

- **Tuesday is a musician's night.** About 53 events a month: sessions, jams, classes and two weekly open mics, but
  no contra or ECD.
- **Saturday is a daytime day.** About 31 a month. The most beginner-friendly Irish session and the unique blues jam
  both happen in daylight. After dark there are only occasional squares, Scandia, or the drive to Shepherdstown. The
  core metro has **no Saturday-night weekly contra**.

## 5. Venues

### Glen Echo Park

[Glen Echo Park](https://glenechopark.org/history) (7300 MacArthur Blvd) began as a Chautauqua in 1891. It closed as
an amusement park in 1968, and the NPS reopened it in 1971. The
[Glen Echo Park Partnership](https://glenechopark.org/about-partnership) manages it and reports 300,000+ visitors a
year. Its dance spaces:

- **The Spanish Ballroom** (1933; not climate-controlled): the Friday contra, Waltz Time, Contrastock and the
  Washington Folk Festival.
- **The Bumper Car Pavilion** (1923; heaters and roll-down curtains): international dance, squares, Cajun and Sunday
  contras.
- **The Ballroom Back Room:** the Barn Dance.
- **Glen Echo Town Hall:** Wednesday ECD.

The park also houses the Revels office, and the Irish Inn is steps away.

### Other dance halls

- **Silver Spring Civic Building**, Great Hall (sprung floor).
- **Greenbelt Community Center**, in a 1930s New Deal town.
- **Woman's Club of Arlington.**
- **St. Stephen & the Incarnation**, Columbia Heights.
- **War Memorial Building**, Shepherdstown.

## 11. Archives

- **[American Folklife Center / Archive of Folk Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Folklife_Center).**
  - **Founding and early staff:** the Archive was founded in 1928 with Robert Winslow Gordon at its head; he did field
    recording in Virginia, West Virginia and Kentucky in 1932. Alan Lomax was its first paid employee in 1936, at
    $620 a year, and Pete Seeger assisted him in 1939.
  - **Later heads:** Joe Hickerson (1963–98) and **Alan Jabbour**, an old-time fiddler who learned from Henry Reed and
    took over in 1969.
  - **The AFC:** created in 1976, it **turned 50 in 2026**. The *Alive in Many Hands* exhibition opened Sept 17, 2026.
    Its concert included old-time fiddler Bayla Davis
    ([Folklife Today](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2026/08/american-folklife-center-celebrates-its-50th-anniversary-with-a-dynamic-public-program-series/)).
    [Homegrown](https://www.loc.gov/concerts/folklife/index.html) concerts are free.
  - **Fiddle holdings:** Jabbour's Henry Reed recordings, the 1971 *American Fiddle Tunes* LP and the 1973 *Hammons
    Family* set.
  - **Recorded in DC:** Lead Belly (1937), Jelly Roll Morton (1938; the threads disagree on whether the sessions ran
    May–June or May–December) and Woody Guthrie (1940) ([finding aid](https://www.loc.gov/folklife/guides/Guthrie.html)).
- **[Ralph Rinzler Folklife Archives](https://folklife.si.edu/archives-and-resources/moses-and-frances-asch-collection/smithsonian):**

## 12. Heritage and diaspora traditions

Vitality ratings are the heritage thread's. "Niche" sometimes means the tradition couldn't be verified.

**African American traditions:**

- **Go-go (thriving).**
  - City Paper dates the beat to 1976, making 2026 its 50th year
    ([City Paper](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/776905/celebrating-50-years-of-go-go-in-2026/)).
  - In 2019, #DontMuteDC gathered 80,000+ signatures.
  - It became DC's official music in 2020 ([DC Code](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/laws/23-71)).
  - The [Go-Go Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-Go_Museum) opened on Feb 19, 2025. It cost about $2.5M, and
    its founders are Ronald Moten and Natalie Hopkinson.
  - The 2026 Go-Go Awards drew 1,000+ people and featured a youth all-star band.
- **Piedmont blues (active).** The AEBHF Saturday jam continues, and the lineage includes four NEA fellows plus
  Elizabeth Cotten.
- **Spirituals and shouts (active).** [Jubilee Voices](https://revelsdc.org/ensembles/jubilee-voices/) (2010)
  performs at historic sites.
- **Gospel go-go (active); gospel quartets (unknown).** The quartets are documented historically
  ([1987 Folklife essay](https://folklife-media.si.edu/docs/festival/program-book-articles/FESTBK1987_05.pdf)), but
  no current public program was verified.

**Latin American traditions:**

- **Salvadoran (active, disrupted).**
  - The metro has 315,279 people of Salvadoran ancestry, 5% of the population
    ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Americans)).
  - **Los Hermanos Lovo** of Leesburg play violin-led chanchona and recorded for Smithsonian Folkways
    ([Folkways](https://folkways.si.edu/video/los-hermanos-lovo-salvadoran-chanchona-group-from-leesburg-va-behind-the-scenes-documentary)).
  - Festival Salvadoreñísimo was cancelled in 2025 over fear of ICE, the first cancellation in 20 years
    ([Maryland Matters](https://marylandmatters.org/tag/salvadoran-festival/)).
  - [Fiesta DC](https://fiestadc.org/) went ahead.
- **Bolivian (thriving).**
  - The metro had 37,607 Bolivians in 2010, about four times New York's count
    ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_Americans)).
  - [Comité Pro-Bolivia](https://proboliviausa.org/) runs caporal, morenada and tinkus contests, and its 37th Festival
    Boliviano was Sept 6, 2026, in Manassas.
  - Many fraternidades are active.
- **Puerto Rican bomba and plena (active).** [Semilla Cultural](https://semillacultural.org/) holds a 4th-Sunday
  bombazo; there is also Cultura Plenera.
- **Afro-Brazilian (thriving).** [Batalá Washington](https://batalawashington.com/about-batala/) has 80+ women and is
  free to join. There is also Capoeira Angola (FICA, around 1994; its site was down) and a samba school.
- **Mariachi (active, commercial).** Hire bands only.
- **Peruvian (niche, unverified).**
- **Steelpan (active).** Panquility and World Steelpan Day, both from a search summary. UMD added steelbands in 2024.
  The Caribbean Carnival parade is unconfirmed.

- **West African (thriving).**
  - **KanKouran** (1983) held its 43rd conference in 2026

## 13. History

### Before 1928

- **1798–99:** George Washington attended Birthnight Balls in what became Gadsby's Tavern, Alexandria. Guests danced
  "in long lines called sets" below a musicians' gallery
  ([Alexandria](https://www.alexandriava.gov/museums/basic-page/tour-gadsbys-ballroom)).
- **Mount Vernon:** the estate's historians note that hiring a fiddler for a dance was a special occasion
  ([Mount Vernon](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/music/music-making-at-mount-vernon-beyond)). No named
  enslaved or free Black fiddlers of the area were found.
- **1861:** Julia Ward Howe, staying at the Willard Hotel, wrote "Battle Hymn of the Republic" to the "John Brown's
  Body" tune after hearing troops sing it at Bailey's Crossroads
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Hymn_of_the_Republic)).
- **1889:** Sousa's "The Washington Post" gave its name to the two-step.
- **1891:** Glen Echo began as a Chautauqua.

### The federal folklore era (1928–1950s)

- **The Library's archive.** It opened in 1928. The Seegers arrived in 1935. Lead Belly wrote "The Bourgeois Blues"
  after a 1937 visit ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois_Blues)). Jelly Roll Morton (1938) and Woody
  Guthrie (1940) recorded here.
- **The National Folk Festival** came to segregated Constitution Hall in 1938. W. C. Handy made his first appearance
  on a desegregated stage there ([NFF](https://www.nationalfolkfestival.com/full-history)).
- **1939 White House concert.** It featured Marian Anderson, the Coon Creek Girls and Lunsford's Soco Gap square
  dancers ([program](https://squaredancehistory.org/items/show/1087)).
- **Appalachian migrants built a country scene.**
  - The Stonemans arrived in 1932.
  - Connie B. Gay began broadcasting on WARL in 1946.
  - Roy Clark grew up here.
  - Buzz Busby came to work at the FBI in 1951.
  - Jimmy Dean's *Town and Country Time* followed (1954–57).
  - Elizabeth Cotten, working for the Seegers, was recorded by Mike Seeger.

### Bluegrass capital and the Folk Scare (1957–1980)

- **1957:** the Country Gentlemen formed after a car crash sidelined Busby's band, and made Georgetown's Shamrock their
  home ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Country_Gentlemen)).
- **1959:** Rebel Records, Fahey's Takoma Records and Archie Edwards' barbershop all began.
- **1960:** Bluegrass Day at Watermelon Park. A Howard-led campaign desegregated Glen Echo by 1961.
- **1963–66:** Mississippi John Hurt lived in DC.
- **1964:** *Hootenanny* called Dupont Circle "DC's own Washington Square", with the Cellar Door (from 1963), Ontario
  Place, the Unicorn and the Shadows. **FSGW was founded on Oct 23, 1964.**
- **1966:** the Birchmere and *Bluegrass Unlimited* began, and FSGW held its first Getaway and its first "Washington
  Folk Festival".
- **1967:** the Smithsonian Festival of American Folklife (Ralph Rinzler) and WAMU bluegrass began.
- **1968:** the amusement park closed, and FSGW held its first Sacred Harp sing.
- **1970:** "Take Me Home, Country Roads", written in Georgetown, was first performed at the Cellar Door
  ([Danoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Danoff)).
- **1971:** the Seldom Scene formed in Bethesda, NPS reopened Glen Echo, and the National Folk Festival began its Wolf
  Trap run.
- **1972:** HMT opened, and FSGW held its first dance.
- **1973:** Mary Cliff's *Traditions* and the Navy Band's Country Current began.
- **1974:** Friday square dances in the Sumner School basement became the Friday Night Dance
  ([FND](https://www.fridaynightdance.com/history)).
- **1975:** the Irish Tradition formed (Brendan Mulvihill, Billy McComiskey, Andy O'Brien), and the Seldom Scene
  began at the Birchmere.
- **1976:** the 12-week Bicentennial festival, and the AFC was created.
- **1977:** the Washington Folk Festival and the Washington Irish Folk Festival opened at Glen Echo.
- **1978:** the Takoma Park Folk Festival and CABOMA began.
- **Mid-1970s:** go-go rose with Chuck Brown.

### The Glen Echo dance era (1981–2019)

- **1981:** the Friday dance moved to Glen Echo (or 1983, by another FND source).
- **1982:** the Cellar Door closed, and the NEA Heritage Fellowships began.
- **1983:** Revels, KanKouran, the Glen Echo international dancers and the Reston contra began, and FSGW passed 2,000
  members.
- **1985:** FSGW's Sunday dance moved to Glen Echo.
- **1987–88:** Smithsonian Folkways.
- **1990:** the shape-note convention.
- **1993:** a Glen Echo fire cost FSGW about $34,000 of gear.
- **1994:** Joe Derrane's comeback at the Irish festival, by then at Wolf Trap
  ([Irish Echo](https://www.irishecho.com/2010/10/seventh-heaven-from-joe-derrane/)).
- **1995:** Waltz Time.
- **1996:** FSGW membership peaked, and John Duffey died.
- **1998:** Archie Edwards died; his foundation kept the jam going.
- **2000:** the last Irish Folk Festival, and the NoVA Irish Session began.
- **2001–10:** WAMU moved bluegrass off its main channel, Glen Echo was renovated, and DCBU was founded.
- **2008:** the blues jam left DC.
- **2016–17:** listeners saved Bluegrass Country, and SMT opened.
- **2018:** the DC Scottish Session began.
- **2019:** #DontMuteDC.

### COVID and rebound (2020–2026)

- **2020:** go-go became the official music, and festivals went virtual.
- **2021:** FSGW adopted a vaccination policy ([FSGW](https://fsgw.org/page-18515)).
- **2022:** the Folklife Festival and the Takoma Park festival returned in person. Zoom sings and Carpe Diem's daily
  online sing persisted.
- **2024:**
  - FSGW moved to a "community care" policy ([FSGW](https://fsgw.org/FSGW-covid19-response)).
  - FND turned 50 and took sole charge of the Friday dance.
  - The **Washington Folk Festival was cancelled** for lack of volunteers.
  - Mary Cliff's show ended.
  - Ben Eldridge, Phil Wiggins and Brendan Mulvihill died.
- **2025:** the Go-Go Museum opened, and the Washington Folk Festival returned. Salvadoreñísimo was cancelled.
- **2026:**
  - Go-go and the AFC both turned 50.
  - The Folklife Festival left the Mall, and Spottswood retired.
  - FSGW's Sunday contra is now listed as discontinued ([FSGW](https://fsgw.org/past-sponsored-events)).
  - New series: the Heartwood Jam, the All Folks Waltz, and the old-time jam's move to U Street.

Through all of this, sessions kept churning: about ten Irish sessions closed or moved between 2014 and 2026.

## 16. Character

**What makes DC distinct:**

- **It is the capital of presenting.** The archives, the Folklife Festival, Folkways, NEA folk arts and the NCTA are
  all here. Musicians often had federal jobs: Busby at the FBI, Gray at National Geographic, Emerson in the Navy Band,
  Jabbour at the Library.
- **It is a mixing bowl.** FND's history describes DC as "in the middle, geographically speaking ... a mixing bowl of
  people from everywhere" ([FND](https://www.fridaynightdance.com/history)). Appalachian migrants built 1950s
  bluegrass. Today's large Salvadoran, Bolivian, Ethiopian and West African communities sustain vital traditions that
  rarely appear on folk calendars.
- **It is suburban.** Montgomery and Prince George's dance; Fairfax, Arlington and Alexandria hold sessions and jams.
  DC proper has a few Metro-friendly events, plus its own Black vernacular traditions: blues, gospel and go-go.
- **It is racially layered.** A segregated Constitution Hall hosted an integrated National Folk Festival. Glen Echo
  was whites-only until 1961. The Piedmont blues lineage runs through the region, go-go is the official music, and a
  2026 DC Square Dance Collective dance grew from an African American caller project.

**Who shows up.** No demographics exist, but the clues are telling:

- **Older:** events gated at 55+, a seniors' Israeli dance, and an "I-70, I-270" jam for musicians turning 70.
- **Younger:** the Next Gen session, the DC Square Dance Collective, Hashdance's non-gendered calling, the All Folks
  Waltz, youth go-go bands and youth African dance academies.
- **Fewer volunteers:** FSGW fell from 2,969 memberships to 750+, and the Washington Folk Festival was cancelled in
  2024.

**Signature traditions:**

- Glen Echo dancing.
- The NoVA Irish pub session.
- Heritage bluegrass.
- Piedmont blues at Archie's.
- Go-go.
- Federal folklife.

For a fiddler, the distinctive mix is a weekly Glen Echo contra plus a real Scottish fiddle community, with Irish
sessions on the Virginia side and old-time at the edges.

1. **Weekly contras per million people.** DC has one for about 6.3 million. Does an anchor dance crowd out suburban
   series, as against Boston, Philadelphia or Seattle?
2. **Share at one venue.** Glen Echo probably hosts more than 40% of dancing. How do Glenside (Philadelphia) and
   Concord (Boston) compare?
3. **Session half-life.** About ten DC sessions died or moved in 2014–26. How common are closed or invite-only
   sessions elsewhere?
4. **State asymmetry.** Maryland suburbs dance; Virginia suburbs jam. Is it venues, organizations or demographics?
5. **International folk dance vs. contra.** Is strong international and Israeli dance with weak contra a Mid-Atlantic
   pattern?
6. **NEA fellows per capita.** Nine fellows have in-MSA residences. How do New York, Los Angeles and the Bay Area
   compare?
7. **The federal effect.** Do the AFC and the Smithsonian raise local participation, or face only the nation?
8. **Folk-society decline.** Is FSGW's fall from 2,969 to 750+ typical of folk societies founded in the 1960s?
9. **Folk radio hours.** How much terrestrial folk radio does each city have, compared with HD2 and streams?
10. **Shape-note per million.** DC has about four in-MSA singings a month, against Baltimore's two in a smaller
    metro.
11. **Chantey groups.** How many cities have a 30-year chantey group? Did shanty sings grow after 2021?
12. **Heritage blues jams.** Does any other city have a weekly jam with an unbroken lineage to the 1950s?
13. **Official city music.** Which other cities have one?
14. **Festivals under enforcement fears.** How did 2025 immigration enforcement affect diaspora festivals in LA,
    Houston and New York?
15. **Embassy ensembles.** How many embassy-run, community-open ensembles exist in other capitals?
16. **Transit and age.** How do Metro access and 55+ gating compare across cities?
17. **Brewery bluegrass.** Are breweries and farm parks the new suburban bluegrass venue elsewhere too?
18. **Lost festivals.** Which cities lost their flagship folk festival to volunteer shortages after COVID?

## 20. Data-quality notes

**Coverage:**

- Web search was rate-limited across all threads. Sources were direct fetches, DuckDuckGo snippets and FSGW
  newsletters (Dec 2025 to Nov 2026).
- Several heritage items rest on AI search summaries: steelpan, the Greek festival dates, Ethiopian music, mariachi,
  Chuck Levin's and the Ladino concert.
- Blocked or unreachable: tunearch (403), Slippery-Hill, Levine, Chuck Levin's (429), ficadc.org, and the arts.gov
  state filter (410), so the fellows list may be incomplete. Geocoding was throttled.

- FSGW's Sunday-contra page.
