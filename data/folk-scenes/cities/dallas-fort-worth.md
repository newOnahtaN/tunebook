# Dallas–Fort Worth: the folk scene of the Metroplex

*Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington, TX MSA (CBSA 19100; 8.48 million people in 2025): Collin, Dallas, Denton, Ellis, Hunt,
Johnson, Kaufman, Parker, Rockwall, Tarrant and Wise counties. As of October 2026. Built from five research threads
(sessions, dance, song, heritage, history) using web search and page fetches only; no audio or video was played.
Places just outside the 11 counties (Granbury, Glen Rose, Waco, the Lake Texoma camps) are flagged and kept out of
the totals. The structured data is in `dallas-fort-worth.json`.*

## Scene in a nutshell

DFW is where recorded Texas folk music was born, and today its scene lives mostly in suburbs, church halls and
once-a-year gatherings. Fort Worth gave the world the first radio barn dance (1923), western swing (1931–32) and the
1901 contest that founded Texas's first fiddlers' association; Benny Thomasson rebuilt the Texas contest style while
working in a Dallas body shop. Now bluegrass is the biggest jam culture (about 18 jams a month, mostly daytime, in
churches, senior centers, a library and town squares), Irish music is the best organized (about 15 open sessions a
month, largely coordinated by one nonprofit, TIMES), and Sacred Harp singers meet almost weekly in four rotating
towns. Old-time is nearly absent and contra has shrunk to one monthly dance, while modern Western square-dance clubs
outnumber it about ten to one. The largest living traditions are Mexican-American (school mariachi, folklórico,
matachines), Texas Czech polka in Ennis, intertribal powwows and Navratri garba.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **The first radio barn dance was in Fort Worth.** On January 4, 1923, WBAP put Capt. M. J. Bonner, a
   75-year-old Confederate veteran, on the air playing square-dance tunes, two years before the Grand Ole Opry
   ([TSHA: WBAP](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/wbap)).
2. **A Deep Ellum recording is in interstellar space.** Blind Willie Johnson cut "Dark Was the Night, Cold Was the
   Ground" in a temporary Columbia studio in Deep Ellum on December 3, 1927; it flies on the Voyager Golden Record
   ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Was_the_Night,_Cold_Was_the_Ground)).
3. **Texas's first folk club was in Dallas, not Austin.** Ron Shipman's Rubaiyat (January 1959) held 30 people, had
   the only espresso machine in Texas, borrowed chairs from Jack Ruby for its 900-person opening weekend, and its
   founder sang sea shanties standing on a barrel draped with fishing nets
   ([D Magazine, Apr 2025](https://www.dmagazine.com/publications/d-magazine/2025/april/rubaiyat-club-coffee-house-birth-of-texas-folk-music/)).
   The metro of 8.5 million has no shanty sing today.
4. **Texas contest fiddling was partly a Metroplex blue-collar art.** Benny Thomasson, father of the modern contest
   style, "spent most of his life working at an auto body shop in Dallas"
   ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Thomasson)), and later moved to Washington state. Hall of Famer
   Norman Solomon of Kaufman County put in 40.5 years at General Dynamics
   ([Fiddlers Frolics](http://fiddlersfrolics.org/new_page_22.htm)).
5. **Contra was born inside a dulcimer club, and squares now outnumber it ten to one.** The first public contra was
   at a Lone Star State Dulcimer Society meeting in an Irving church on Feb 12, 1983, for a $1 donation
   ([NTTDS history](https://www.nttds.org/history.html)). Today there is one adult community contra a month, in a
   strip-mall ballroom in Carrollton, while NORTEX lists 25 square and round dance clubs and Fort Worth's Swingtime
   Center won't rent to anyone but square-dance clubs through 2027 ([Swingtime](https://swingtimecenter.com/)).
6. **An invited "session players" institution, 24 years old.** Since Oct 13, 2002, TIMES has run a curated Sunday
   pub session of about four rotated top players with published rosters, first at Trinity Hall and since 2015 at The
   Celt in McKinney ([TIMES](https://irishtradmusic.org/activities/sessions-about/)).    It sits beside about 15 open
   Irish sessions a month.
7. **Bluegrass is a daytime scene.** Jams meet at 10:30am on a Thursday in a public library, in senior centers and
   church halls, and in a winery inside a 1945 post office. Meanwhile the lone Dallas old-time jam's welcome text says
   "we won't be playing bluegrass, Irish, or old country and western"
   ([group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/dallasoldtimejam/)).
8. **A Baptist seminary sings Sacred Harp.** Southwestern Baptist Theological Seminary in Fort Worth has hosted an
   annual singing since about 1985, founded by hymnologist William J. Reynolds and now led by his son Tim
   ([2025 minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=%22Southwestern%20Baptist%22&n=6970)). Dallas was also the
   capital of seven-shape gospel publishing and broadcast all-night singings from the Cotton Bowl.
9. **A Czech lodge hall is Fort Worth's home of western swing.** National Hall, built in 1938 by North Side
   meatpacking families (SPJST Lodge 92), now hosts the Cowtown Birthplace of Western Swing Festival and Tejano dances
   ([Texas Dance Hall Preservation](https://texasdancehall.org/national-hall-spjst-92/)). And the Czech-authenticity
   National Polka Festival in Ennis is headlined by Denton polka-rock band Brave Combo.
10. **Mariachi is probably the metro's biggest youth violin tradition.** The Fort Worth Stock Show runs a
    high-school mariachi championship ([FWSSR](https://www.fwssr.com/p/competitions--contests/mariachi-competition))
    alongside its $7,500 teenage Fiddle Showdown, and Grand Prairie ISD starts mariachi in 6th grade
    ([GPFAA](https://gpfaa.gpisd.org/fine-arts-academy/mariachi)). Nobody has counted the students.

## Trad instrumental and sessions

### Irish: one nonprofit, many pubs

Irish music is DFW's best-organized trad scene, and most of it runs through **TIMES**, the Traditional Irish Music
Education Society (Richardson). Ken Fleming, "a veteran traditional Irish musician who has promoted the learning and
playing of Irish music for nearly five decades", founded it in January 2005, inspired by the first O'Flaherty
retreat the previous October ([history](https://irishtradmusic.org/about/history/)). TIMES lists or hosts most of
the open sessions, runs the retreat, a youth camp and a five-day TradCamp, helped start the North Texas School of
Irish Music in 2012, and publishes unusually explicit session rules: popcorn format, the starter sets the tempo,
guitar and bouzouki players take turns or sit at opposite ends, bodhrán players don't sit together, no talking
during tunes ([rules](https://irishtradmusic.org/sessions/)).

The **Tuesday at J. Gilligan's** in downtown Arlington is the anchor. Its regular RickR wrote in July 2026 that it is
"the longest running open session in North Texas – established in 2005"; it is intermediate to advanced, regulars eat
dinner first, and "the ranks thin after 9pm" ([thesession 1062](https://thesession.org/sessions/1062)). A
[Shorthorn photo story (Sept 2024)](https://www.theshorthorn.com/multimedia/arlington-residents-band-together-with-irish-music/article_43d8f9ce-87fc-11ef-a9d9-17251694a319.html)
gives the backstory: the group played O'Dwyer's in Euless from 2003; when the bar was sold, Bob Moore talked
Gilligan's owner into hosting it. The players pictured were 63, 65 and 72. The session was suspended in September
2021 because the core group would not play in pubs that ignored CDC guidance, and was "limping along" by mid-2022.

The **Wednesday session at The Celt** in McKinney's historic downtown is the liveliest. It is held upstairs, each week
has a different host from the area's experienced players, the pace is "lively and dance-worthy", and sheet music is
allowed but rare ([thesession 3833](https://thesession.org/sessions/3833)); the pub's blurb says "typically 10+"
players ([Celt calendar](https://www.theceltmckinney.com/event/times-irish-music-session-wednesdays/2026-08-12/)).
It started as a Tuesday at Churchill's in 2014, which became The Celt in 2015.

TIMES has been expanding: a first-Monday session at **Gillespie's Tavern at the Shire** in Richardson (players get one
free drink), and a third-Thursday session at **Bearded Ax Brewery** in Midlothian, the newest outpost, in fast-growing
Ellis County (the brewery posted "The Irish musicians are gathering again tonight! 7pm!" on Aug 20, 2026;
[Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcRNDl1Rq2Q/)). Fort Worth proper has only one: Michael Gatch's
**Henry McCarty** second-Sunday session on West 7th, new in May 2026, "moderate to fast-paced", where musicians get
validated garage parking and a meal ([thesession 9740](https://thesession.org/sessions/9740)). Paying players in
kind (drinks, food, parking) is the local norm.

The **Denton Irish Music Session** is the scene's survivor. Thesession comments trace it from Banter (2002, "half
round-robin for beginners, half all out session warfare") through Sweetwater Grill (2015) and Denton County Brewing
(2018) to Steve's Wine Bar (2022) ([201](https://thesession.org/sessions/201),
[7315](https://thesession.org/sessions/7315)). Its founders, Giles and Karen Berry, moved away, but it continues.

The **session players** model is DFW's own invention, or at least its own institution. On Oct 13, 2002, Fleming
persuaded Trinity Hall's owner to host a Sunday 3–6pm session of invited top players, "rotated each time to limit the
number to five... Essentially, it's a performance" ([TIMES](https://irishtradmusic.org/activities/sessions-about/)).
It recorded a CD, *Live at Trinity Hall* (2004), moved to The Celt in 2015 and outlived Trinity Hall's 2021 closure.
The archived 2019 rosters read like a who's who (Gordon McLeod, Michelle Feldman, Linda Relph, Brink Trammell, Rick
Roberts, Andrew Simmons on pipes), with entries like "No Session: Sports Broadcast Event"
([schedule archive](https://irishtradmusic.org/activities/sessions-about/times-sessions-schedule/)). Today it is "a
rotating ensemble... typically 4 players in total" ([Celt](https://www.theceltmckinney.com/venue/the-celt/)); the
etiquette is to have a pint and talk to the players in the breaks.

The pattern across 24 years of thesession comments is that **venues die, musicians don't**. The Tipperary Inn,
Trinity Hall, the Cock and Bull, Ye Olde Bull and Bush, Allen Wickers, the Staggering Irishman (closed for unpaid
rent) and Malarkey's (where "BMI agents threatened to fine them since they did not have a license for live music";
[Jim Wells's history](https://thesession.org/sessions/7595)) all went, while the same names (Fleming, Roberts,
Feldman, Moore, Wells, the Berrys) recur across two decades. No Comhaltas branch was found.

### Bluegrass: the biggest jam culture

By volume, bluegrass wins: about 18 jam-dates a month inside the MSA, run by four volunteer bodies. The **Southwest
Bluegrass Club** (Grapevine; 50 in 2024) holds a monthly jam and meeting that fills several rooms of a Methodist
family life center, with lunch ([calendar](https://www.southwestbluegrassclubdfw.org/upcoming-bluegrass-events)); it
celebrated its 50th with a new free street festival, the **Grape Jam**
([Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/grapevine-colleyville-southlake/arts-entertainment/southwest-bluegrass-club-to-celebrate-50th-anniversary-with-festival-in-grapevine/)).
The **Garland Square Pickers**, a 501(c)(3) founded by the late Jim Paul Miller, play the downtown square on Saturday
nights in season and fund a young player's trip to Camp Bluegrass
([post](https://www.facebook.com/garlandsquarepickers/posts/the-jim-paul-miller-memorial-bluegrass-show-will-be-happening-on-january-17-2026/122284162490236679/)).
The **Saturday morning jam on the Denton courthouse square** once moved into the old courthouse lobby on a windy day,
with a tuba player and a security guard on bass ([group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/231568146896096/)).

The **Bluegrass Heritage Foundation** is a presenter rather than a jam host ([site](https://bluegrassheritage.org/)).
It runs the Farmers Branch Bluegrass Festival (with jam rooms), the Texas State Banjo, Guitar & Mandolin
Championships ($500 first prize; 2026 winners included Garland's Scott Owen on guitar), a free instrument-lending
library for players 8–21, a radio show, and since 2026 a monthly show-and-jam at Poor David's Pub, free with a tip jar
([BHF](https://bluegrassheritage.org/bluegrass-heritage-music-show-jam/)). In 2023 it took up the chance to acquire
Lester Flatt's 1942 Martin D-18 #81197, "the first guitar of bluegrass music"
([page](https://bluegrassheritage.org/lester-flatt-martin-d-18-81197/)).

Who shows up: the schedule says it all. A library jam at 10:30am on Thursday, a senior center at 6pm, church halls at
noon, gospel jams, potluck club meetings: a retiree and church-musician base, with a second, contest-oriented youth
stream from BHF's programs.

### Old-time: nearly missing

One listed jam, the **Dallas Old-Time Music Jam**, first and third Saturdays 2–4:30pm at the historic village in Old
City Park (bring an instrument and you get in free). Its contacts are Kirk Hunter, a Waxahachie chemistry professor
and the fiddler who co-founded DFW contra in 1983, and Dean Barber. But the only current evidence is standing listings
on the [NTTDS](https://www.nttds.org/sched.html) and [LSSDS](https://www.lssds.com/festivals.html) pages; the newest
dated event is a 2019 clip, and the park changed hands on May 27, 2024, when the Parks Department took over from the
Dallas County Heritage Society ([conservancy FAQ](https://oldcityparkconservancy.org/about/faq/)). Call before you go.
Fort Worth has only [r/FortWorthOldTime](https://www.reddit.com/r/FortWorthOldTime/), whose posts are from people
looking for someone to jam with. Old-time players drift instead to the **Lone Star State Dulcimer Society**'s
Arlington meetings ("play-around and jam", no dues) and to NTTDS's Fiddler's Dream camp on Lake Texoma.

### Texas contest fiddling, western swing and the rest

There is no public weekly Texas-style fiddle jam. The tradition lives in contests, teaching studios and families (see
the Fiddle angle). Western swing is a dance and performance tradition here, not a jam tradition; the nearest thing
to a pickers' circle is the **Cowtown Pickers Jam Session & Open Mic** in the Stockyards. No Scottish/Cape Breton,
Scandinavian, Balkan, klezmer, gypsy-jazz or French-Canadian session was found, despite a 1963 Scottish society,
two pipe bands and a Celtic harp circle revived by the [Dallas chapter of the American Harp
Society](https://www.dallasharpsociety.org/event-details-registration/celtic-harp-circle) in 2025. DFW jam directories
([fortworthlivemusic.com](https://fortworthlivemusic.com/jams/)) are dominated by blues and jazz.

## Dance

### Contra and English: one society, one dance a month each

The **North Texas Traditional Dance Society** (NTTDS; incorporated May 15, 1986; CDSS associate since February 1987)
runs everything in the CDSS world ([history](https://www.nttds.org/history.html)). Its **3rd Saturday contra** meets at
the 2155 Ballroom, a hall in a shopping strip at Marsh Lane and Keller Springs in Carrollton, with recorded waltzing
and teaching from 6:30 ([location](https://www.nttds.org/locations/2155.html)). Admission went back to $10 adult / $5
student / $25 family in August 2026 after three years of pay-what-you-can; snacks are potluck, with a $1
honor-system stand. 2026 lineups: Christina Weis with Sibling Revelry and Bell and Bonnet, Diego Brand with Laura
Brand and John Roby (a free dance, sponsored by a dancer as a birthday gift), Houston's Dorcas Hand with Paradoxical
Trio ([newsletter archive](http://us9.campaign-archive1.com/home/?u=0d0854214a49a0c1b99c45d3c&id=93b118c3fd)).

Before COVID, NTTDS ran 1st and 3rd Saturday contras plus a quarterly open-band, open-caller dance; earlier still
there were series in Irving, Fort Worth and Denton. The 2022 restart kept only the 3rd Saturday. Two newer things fill
in around it: **Contra Corners**, a free UT Dallas student club founded in February 2020 that kept dancing outdoors
during COVID, with caller Zooey and the band Bambi and Thumper
([UTD page](https://www.nttds.org/locations/utd.html)); and **African American Dance Caller Pathways**, launched in
September 2025 by FWAAMFest (a program of Decolonizing the Music Room) to train Black callers in technique,
traditional dances and the Black history of squares and contras. Its instructors are Dr. Clover Johnson, Becky Hill
and Paul Collins, it is free (including travel), and its free public dances at Kinfolk House are the only contras or
squares in Fort Worth proper since 2016 ([AADCP](https://www.fwaamfest.com/african-american-dance-caller-pathways)).
Ignore [contradancelinks.com](http://contradancelinks.com/schedule_TX.html) for Texas: it still lists a Fort Worth
dance that ended in October 2016.

**English country dance** meets 2nd Saturdays at Ridgewood Park UMC on East Lovers Lane (since spring 2026), always with
live music from the house band **Mad Robin**. Peter Lamborghini, named as a new caller in the mid-1980s, still calls
it in 2026 ([location](https://www.nttds.org/locations/rpumc.html)). Monthly ECD began at SMU in January 1992.
*Bridgerton*-style interest is spawning events outside the society: a free Liberty & Lace Ball at the Dallas Central
Library (June 2026) and a Regency masquerade ball in McKinney (November 2026), both with Mad Robin.

NTTDS also runs two weekends at the Sokol Athletic Center: **When In Doubt, Swing!** (since 1991, started with a $500
CDSS grant; "over 100 people from Texas, Oklahoma, and several other states"; 2027 is May 7–9 with Emily Rush and
Spintuition; [WIDS](https://www.nttds.org/wids/home.html)), and **Set for Spring**, a rotating regional ECD weekend
(Dallas, April 9–11, 2027, Gaye Fifer with Persons of Quality;
[Set for Spring](https://www.nttds.org/setspring/home.html)). Its New Year's **Fiddler's Dream** music and dance camp
(since 1999) is in Kingston, Oklahoma, outside the MSA, and has an all-camp band with a published tune list
([Fiddler's Dream](https://www.nttds.org/fdream/home.html)). The society's small budget shows in its North Texas
Giving Day 2026 total: 8 donations, $1,100 ([NTGD](https://www.nttds.org/ntgd.html)). Membership is not published.

### Squares: the big, ageing network

**NORTEX** (North Texas Square and Round Dance Association) says "Almost every evening, there is a dance somewhere!"
([nortex.org](https://new.nortex.org/trianglesquares/square-dancing/)), and its
[club list](https://new.nortex.org/nortex/club-events/clubs-alphabetically/) has 25 clubs: 20 square clubs in the
MSA, three round-dance clubs, a camping club and one in Tool (outside). The hub is the shareholder-owned **Swingtime
Center** on Fort Worth's SE Loop 820: a 6,200 sq ft floating floor seating 250, used by eight clubs (Circle Eight,
Double Plus, Fort Worth Squares, Roadrunners, T Square Gadabouts, Trailblazers, and two round-dance clubs)
([clubs](https://swingtimecenter.com/Clubs.html)). It hosts the 66th Harvest Holiday round-dance festival in
November 2026. **Rebel Rousers** dances 3rd Saturdays at the Richardson Senior Center with a Grand March; **Pegasus
Squares** (2014) is an IAGSDC club "providing outreach to the LGBTQ community", dancing at Grace UMC in East Dallas
([IAGSDC](https://iagsdc.org/pegasus-squares)). Eight fall 2026 lesson series were running
([lessons](https://new.nortex.org/nortex/lessons/square-dance-lessons/)). The eight clubs with published schedules
alone hold 11 square dances a month, so the whole network is likely 20–35 a month: ten to fifteen times the contra
frequency. No recurring public old-time square dance was found; squares turn up at AADCP dances and one-off barn
dances such as Grapevine's Nash Farm Barn Dance.

### Scottish, Irish, international and Israeli

Scottish country dancing in DFW is administered from Houston: the **RSCDS Houston & District** branch lists the two
North Texas classes ([classes](https://houstonrscds.org/classes/)). The **Denton Celtic Dancers** meet Sunday
evenings at the Green Space Arts Collective for $1, a price that has survived decades; the **Fort Worth Scottish
Country Dancers** (since Oct 2022, teacher Tiffany Howard, $5) meet 2nd and 4th Saturdays at Trinity Lutheran
([FW SCD](https://houstonrscds.org/fort-worth-scottish-country-dancing/)). There is no class in Dallas proper.
Highland dance (Dallas Highland Dancers, 1992) is competitive, and the US Inter-Regional Highland Dancing
Championships came to Dallas in July 2026. **No public céilí or set dance** was found; Irish dance here means
competitive step schools.

**Dallas International Folk Dancers** dance every Sunday 2–4pm at the Plaza Arts Center on the Carrollton square, the
converted theater NTTDS members gutted and floored in 2006. Admission is $5 cash; in late October 2026 "over 20
people came out", and teacher Susie was reviving the Russian dance "Katia", learned in the late 1970s
([difd.org](https://www.difd.org/)). DIFD descends from the Dallas Folkdance Co-op, which supplied many of the first
contra dancers in 1983. **RikuDallas** holds weekly Israeli dancing on Wednesdays (leader Linda Kahalnik; venue
disputed between Beth Torah in Richardson and the JCC; freshest dated event Sept 2025,
[Jewish Dallas](https://www.jewishdallas.org/event/rikudallas-mini-israeli-dance-workshop/)). Greek dance got a new
showcase in 2025: the **Rizes** Hellenic Dance Festival at St. Demetrios, Fort Worth, with 7 teams, dancers aged 5–65
and about 230 attendees ([National Herald](https://www.thenationalherald.com/vasiliki-malamatos-on-the-inaugural-rizes-showcase-hellenic-dance-festival-in-texas/)).
No Scandinavian, Hungarian, Balkan-specific, morris, sword or rapper group was found.

### Dance halls

The **Stagecoach Ballroom** (opened October 1961, 2516 E Belknap, Fort Worth) is where the 1987 film *Square Dance*
was shot; it still runs Friday lessons, Saturday bands and Sunday afternoons with lessons at 2:00 and 2:45 and the
house band The Coachmen at 4 ([site](http://www.stagecoachballroom.com/); a dated Sept 2026 listing:
[fortworthlivemusic.com](https://fortworthlivemusic.com/events/the-coachmen-2026-09-06-stagecoach-ballroom/)).
**Sons of Hermann Hall** (1910–11, Deep Ellum) still has its German lodges, a weekly swing night and a Thursday
acoustic campfire jam ([about](https://www.sonsofhermannhall.com/about-us)). Billy Bob's Texas is the big commercial
honky-tonk; the Longhorn Ballroom is listed by
[Texas Dance Hall Preservation](https://texasdancehall.org/longhorn-ballroom/). Garba during Navratri is probably the
biggest participatory folk dancing in the metro by headcount (clustered in Irving/Las Colinas and the Plano–Allen–
Frisco corridor), though the "20,000+" figure is an aggregator claim. **No recurring Cajun or zydeco dance series**
was found in a metro four hours from Louisiana.

## Folk song and listening

### Listening rooms

DFW's song scene has two halves that barely touch: a singer-songwriter world with real history, and traditional sacred
singing. The listening-room anchor is **Poor David's Pub**, opened by David Card on McKinney Avenue on March 15, 1977,
moved to the Cedars in 2004 (Guy Clark opened the new room) and handed to his daughter Amelia on June 1, 2026; Card
said the pandemic "took much of the fire out of him"
([Dallas Observer](https://www.dallasobserver.com/music/poor-davids-pub-keeps-the-music-playing-as-davids-daughter-inherits-the-legacy-venue-40692216/)).
It has hosted Lyle Lovett, Guy Clark, Nanci Griffith, Doc Watson, Townes Van Zandt and the "Dixie Chickens", and has
run the B. W. Stevenson Memorial Songwriting Contest since 1989 ($30 entry, $1,000 prize;
[contest](https://poordavidspub.com/songwriting-contest/)). One block away, **Opening Bell Coffee**, in the basement
of the old Sears building, calls itself "Dallas' best acoustic live music venue" (tickets about $20;
[events](https://www.openingbellcoffee.com/events)). Together they make the Cedars a two-room listening district.

The **Kessler Theater** in Oak Cliff (1942, once owned by Gene Autry, reopened 2010) books Americana and folk
([D Magazine](https://www.dmagazine.com/publications/d-magazine/2010/june/oak-cliffs-kessler-theater-is-back-now-what/)).
The **Granada Theater** will close in April 2027, its owner blaming COVID and Live Nation/AEG consolidation
([D Magazine, Oct 30, 2026](https://www.dmagazine.com/arts-entertainment/2026/10/granada-theater-owner-bathhouse-interview/)).

The church-hall coffeehouse is a DFW lineage. **Uncle Calvin's** (1982), started by Rev. Trey Hammond in NorthPark
Presbyterian's fellowship hall, alcohol- and smoke-free, billed itself "Texas' longest running folk music coffeehouse";
Chuck Pyle called it "the cosmic center of the folk universe in Texas"
([Dallas Observer, 2007](https://www.dallasobserver.com/music/after-25-years-uncle-calvin-s-coffee-house-still-spreading-the-word-7069203/)).
It went quiet after COVID ("No shows currently scheduled" in an
[April 2025 archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20250427223912/http://unclecalvins.org/); domain dead by Oct 2026).
The model continues at **Martin's Music Café**, a volunteer monthly Friday series in King of Glory Lutheran's
fellowship hall ([Undiscovered Music](https://undiscovered.music/venues/martins_music_cafe)).

Fort Worth has WineHaus's upstairs "Haus Concerts", Southside Preservation Hall (a historic church complex) and The
Post at River East. Waxahachie has **In The Music Room**, a listening room that has booked Muriel Anderson; smaller
rooms include Book Club Cafe (Rockwall), AllGood Cafe and The Wild Detectives
([Undiscovered Music](https://undiscovered.music/places/dallas_tx)). House concerts are word-of-mouth; **Concerts In
The Woods** (Fort Worth, since about 2016, roughly quarterly) is the one series visible in listings
([listing](https://undiscovered.music/venues/concerts_in_the_woods_TX)).

### Song circles and open mics

The **Dallas Songwriters Association** (formally 1987; independent nonprofit 1990) is the organizing hub: a monthly
Zoom critique meeting, songwriters-in-the-round at Guitars & Growlers in Richardson, an open mic at Cocktail Creamery
in McKinney, a song circle at Well Grounded Coffee on Garland Road, the Poor David's open mic, contests and a
Dallas Songwriters Hall of Fame whose 2026 inductees included Ray Wylie Hubbard, Jeannie C. Riley and Pat Boone
([events](https://dallassongwriters.blogspot.com/)). In Denton, **Dan's Silverleaf** runs a songwriter open mic every
Monday at 8, originals only, no cover ([Open Jam Events](https://openjamevents.com/songwriters-open-mic-dans-silverleaf)).
Fort Worth's newest institution is the **Fort Worth Songwriter Fest** (founded 2024 by Keegan McInroe): nearly 300
submissions and about 85 writers in its third year, with courtyard song swaps and late-night unamplified
after-parties ([Fort Worth Report](https://fortworthreport.org/2026/10/21/pull-up-a-chair-with-fort-worth-songwriter-fest-founder/)).
In this half of the scene, "folk" means Texas songwriter music. There is no folk club in the British sense.

### Cowboy song

The **Cowtown Opry** gives free Western music every Sunday at 2pm on the steps of the Livestock Exchange Building in
the Stockyards; second Sundays add the adult "Saddle Pals" and the under-17 "Buckaroos"
([Stockyards](https://fortworthstockyards.com/attractions/cowtown-opry/)). Founded Nov 2, 1992, from an idea by
Stockyards Hotel pianist Jean Marlowe Keim, it requires members to build "a personal western music repertoire" and
insists cowboy music is "distinctly separate from country/pop styles" ([history](https://www.cowtownopry.org/what-we-do)).
The **Red Steagall Cowboy Gathering** (since 1991) adds cowboy poetry, chuck wagons, ranch rodeo, cowboy church and a
youth fiddle contest every October ([our story](https://www.redsteagallcowboygathering.com/our-story/)).

## Traditional singing

### Sacred Harp

DFW is a real Sacred Harp town. **Sacred Harp North Texas** ([site](https://sacredharpnorthtexas.org/)) runs four
monthly singings that rotate around the Metroplex (table above), about 3.7 a month, with Arlington skipping
Thanksgiving and Christmas and Fort Worth skipping months when there's a singing in Waco or Henderson. All four have
adopted the new *Sacred Harp: 2025 Edition*; the two Saturday singings also use the Cooper book, the East Texas
tradition's book, which reflects the mix of revival singers and East Texas families (the Ross, Foreman, Thomas and
Reynolds names recur in the [minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=%22Dallas%20County%22)). The Fort Worth
singing meets in a retirement community; the Arlington one in a rooftop room. Denton's church says singings are free
and "no experience, talent, or commitment required"
([FUMC Denton](https://fumcdenton.com/main-events2/sacred-harp-singing-group-ar2fe)).

The **Dallas County Singing** (its "42nd Annual" on Oct 31, 2026) moved from Cochran Chapel in Dallas to Coppell after
a COVID pause; its 1999 minutes show about 37 leaders and 2022 about 25
([2022](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=%22Dallas%20County%22&n=6479)). The **William J. Reynolds Memorial
Singing** at Southwestern Baptist Theological Seminary uses the 1991 Denson book; its numbering doesn't add up (1995
was the "eleventh annual", 2025 the "39th"), probably because of skipped years
([1995](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=%22Southwestern%20Baptist%22&n=13)). Dallas people were at the center of
Texas Sacred Harp early: in 1930 George Pullen Jackson found W. H. Coston of Dallas presiding over the Interstate
Convention at Mineral Wells ([Jackson](https://fasola.org/essays/gp_texas.html)). Judge Donald R. Ross, who still
sings at the Dallas County singing, wrote the account of East Texas **Black Sacred Harp**: singings that ran to
midnight, a 25-cent "enrollment" fee to lead, and pitches keyed higher than whites would attempt; by 2005 he knew of
no surviving singers ([Texas Fasola](http://historic.texasfasola.org/blacksacredharp.html)). Austin has a weekly
singing and DFW does not; Houston has three monthlies. No Christian Harmony singing exists in DFW.

### Seven-shape gospel: the old capital

Dallas was the business center of seven-shape convention gospel. V. O. Stamps moved to Dallas in 1928 to run the
western half of **Stamps-Baxter**, which ran "normal" singing schools and published *Gospel Music News*; from 1936
Stamps quartets sang a daily noonday show on KRLD, and by 1938 the Dallas normal ended with an **All Night Singing in
the Cotton Bowl** broadcast internationally ([TSHA: Gospel music](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/gospel-music);
[TSHA: Stamps-Baxter](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/stamps-baxter-music-and-printing-company)). The
Chuck Wagon Gang began as the Carter Quartet on WBAP and first recorded "I'll Fly Away" (1948). Zondervan bought
Stamps-Baxter in 1974 and stopped publishing music in 1992. **No participatory seven-shape convention singing** was
found in the 11 counties today; what survives is staged, such as Rg Huff's "Heaven's Front Porch" hymn sing-alongs in
Frisco and Waxahachie ([site](https://texasgospelhymns.com/)), and KNON's daily early-morning gospel block.

### Shanties and ballads

None. No shanty sing, no ballad or unaccompanied-song group was found, and Sing Out!'s
[community sings directory](https://singout.org/communitysings/directory/) has no Texas entries. The irony: Texas's
first folk-club owner was a shanty singer, and Hermes Nye, a Dallas lawyer, recorded five Folkways albums of ballads
and Texas folk songs in the 1950s ([Dallas Observer](https://www.dallasobserver.com/news/a-nye-for-a-nye-7132447/)).

## Heritage and diaspora traditions

DFW's living heritage traditions show up less as weekly sessions than as youth pipelines and big annual gatherings.

**Mexican-American (thriving).** Mariachi lives in public schools. Dallas ISD's Mariachi Verde (Bryan Adams HS)
placed third at Mariachi Espectacular de Albuquerque in 2024 and plays Mavericks halftimes
([Dallas ISD](https://thehub.dallasisd.org/dallas-isd-news/dallas-isd-news-post-page/~board/dallas-isd-news/post/mariachi-program-blends-culture-and-competition));
Greiner Academy's Los Únicos is named for having been the district's only middle-school mariachi. Grand Prairie ISD
treats mariachi as a core fine art from 6th grade, and CBS reported every GPISD school offers it
([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/texas/news/grand-prairie-students-playing-mariachi-music/)); next door, Arlington ISD
has a single program ([Shorthorn](https://www.theshorthorn.com/el-nico-mariachi-arlington-isd-s-only-mariachi-band-grows-a-lasting-legacy/article_885747b2-da37-11ef-9988-23611c71d4ae.html)).
The circuit runs through UIL region contests, the FWISD Mariachi Extravaganza (full, with a waitlist), UTA's ¡Viva el
Mariachi! workshop day and the Stock Show's invitational. **Ballet folklórico** is old and deep: the Anita N.
Martinez Ballet Folklórico (1975, West Dallas; 40+ company dancers) was founded by Dallas's first Mexican-American
council member, who died in April 2026 aged 100 ([ANMBF](https://www.anmbf.org/aboutus)); Ballet Folklórico Azteca
de Fort Worth (1974 or 1975) turned 50. The **Guadalupe feast** at the downtown cathedral draws "tens of thousands",
with matachines dancing late into the night ([DMN](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/faith/2023/12/10/dallas-cathedral-guadalupe-to-celebrate-its-national-shrine-status/));
the cathedral has its own matlachines ministry. **Charreada and escaramuza** compete at the Stock Show
([FWSSR](https://www.fwssr.com/p/horse-shows/escaramuza-competition)). **Conjunto and Tejano** are club-based (the free
Cowtown Conjunto Festival reached its 13th edition in 2025), with no community accordion school like San Antonio's.
**Son jarocho** was planted in 2026: free City-funded workshop blocks with teachers from Mexico at Emanuel Lutheran
in East Dallas ([Emanuel](https://www.emanueldallas.org/content.cfm?id=102)). No standing fandango yet.

**Texas Czech (active).** The **National Polka Festival** in Ennis (Ellis County, inside the MSA) draws up to 50,000
on Memorial Day weekend to three fraternal halls (KJT, Sokol, SPJST), with 14 bands, a King and Queen contest in
*kroj*, and a rule that every parade entry and band must be Czech-themed
([history](https://www.nationalpolkafestival.com/history)). Sokol KHB Ennis (1908) adds a February Czech Music
Festival, monthly Monday jams, beseda lessons and a Czech film night. But the Ennis Czech Boys' 2026 calendar has only
two public dates in the MSA; the rest are Hallettsville, Shiner and Houston ([ECB](http://www.ennisczechboys.com/2026-schedule.php)).
Ennis is the northern outpost of the Central Texas polka belt. In Fort Worth, **National Hall** (SPJST Lodge 92,
organized 1910 by Czech packinghouse families who came after Armour and Swift opened in 1903) holds Czech Heritage Day
and old-tyme polka dances alongside Tejano and western-swing nights.

**Native American (active).** Dallas was a 1950s BIA relocation city; more than 20,000 Native Americans had been
moved to DFW by 1970 ([DMN 2013](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/2013/06/15/urban-inter-tribal-center-of-texas-moves-to-new-facility-in-dallas/)).
The result is an urban intertribal community with a powwow most months from March to December: UTA's student
powwow (30th in April 2026; 1,200+ attendees, about 90 competing dancers;
[Fort Worth Report](https://fortworthreport.org/2026/04/08/drums-that-hit-in-the-heart-uta-native-american-student-led-powwow-celebrates-30-years/)),
the North Texas Pow Wow (Oct 24, 2026, Esports Stadium Arlington, host drums Wild Band of Comanches and White Eagle;
[powwows.com](https://calendar.powwows.com/events/north-texas-pow-wow/)), a downtown Heritage Month powwow run by
AT&T's Native employee group, the IIAmericas celebration in Cleburne, and ICCT's children's Christmas powwow. Grand
Prairie's long-running September Traders Village powwow (54th in 2016) seems to have lapsed; and the "36th annual"
Traders Village powwow in November 2026 is in **Houston**, run by the DFW Inter-Tribal Association.

**Others.** Blues is history (Deep Ellum, Blind Lemon Jefferson) and a present bar-jam culture (11 weekly jams, not
counted). Zydeco is festival and line-dance based (Dallas Zydeco & Southern Soul Fest at City Hall Plaza, Nov 2026),
with KNON's Monday Cajun and zydeco hours. Juneteenth's Opal Lee walks in Fort Worth. South Asian traditions are
thriving: three Tyagaraja Aradhana group sings a year, eleven-night garba seasons, a new Big D Bhangra competition, and
a directory counting 27 classical dance and 15 vocal schools, most in Frisco ([Nāda](https://nada-dfw.org/)).
Japanese taiko since 1989 (Dallas Kiyari Daiko) and 1998 (Fort Worth's Dondoko Taiko); the Dallas Hua Yun Chinese
Orchestra (2013) teaches erhu, guzheng, pipa and yangqin. Greek festivals in Dallas and Fort Worth, Lebanese dabke in
Lewisville, Zorya Ukrainian dancers (45–50 years), Eritrea's national festival (Grand Prairie, 2026), West African drum
classes, and hula. Niche or unverified: Korean seniors' pungmul (last seen 2023), a Filipino rondalla, Sons of Norway,
klezmer.

## History timeline

- **1854–1901: frontier fiddling.** Moses J. Bonner's family settles near Dallas in 1854; he learns fiddle as a boy
  "from an old black man" ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/bonner-moses-j)). Old settlers' and
  Confederate reunions are the contest circuit: the 1881 Wise County reunion drew 5,000 and was "a success, only the
  music being a failure" ([Wise County Historical Society](https://wisehistory.com/site/wise-county-reunion/)). John
  Lomax teaches at Weatherford College around 1889–95.
- **1901:** 19 fiddlers meet at a Confederate veterans' camp in Fort Worth; Henry Gilliland wins and the Old Fiddlers'
  Association of Texas is formed, with Gilliland as secretary. TSHA notes he used the contests to spread "Lost Cause"
  ideology ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/gilliland-henry-clay)).
- **c. 1907–10:** Lomax collects "The Old Chisholm Trail" stanzas in Fort Worth's White Elephant Saloon for *Cowboy
  Songs*. **1909:** the Texas Folklore Society is organized in Dallas.
- **c. 1912:** Blind Lemon Jefferson plays daily at Elm and Central, partnering with Lead Belly
  ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/jefferson-blind-lemon)); "Dallas Blues" is published.
- **1922:** WBAP goes on air; Gilliland and Eck Robertson make the first commercial country recordings in New York.
  **1923:** WBAP's first radio barn dance.
- **1927–29:** Columbia and Victor record in Dallas: Blind Willie Johnson, Coley Jones's Dallas String Band ("Dallas
  Rag"), Eck Robertson's family band with fiddling Baptist editor J. B. Cranfill
  ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/robertson-alexander-eck)). V. O. Stamps moves to Dallas (1928).
- **1930–32: western swing.** The Wills Fiddle Band plays Fort Worth's Eagles' hall and Crystal Springs, becomes the
  Light Crust Doughboys (1931; sponsor W. Lee O'Daniel cancelled the show over its "hillbilly music", then
    reinstated it after listeners protested), and Milton Brown forms the Musical Brownies (1932), whose Cecil Brower brought Joe Venuti's bowing to
  Texas fiddlers ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/brown-william-milton)).
- **1935–39:** Don Law's sessions at 508 Park Avenue make 843 recordings, including Robert Johnson's last sides (1937)
  and early conjunto (1939) ([Encore Park](https://www.encoreparkdallas.org/1935-1960s)). **1938:** All Night Singing
  in the Cotton Bowl. **1940:** Eck Robertson records about 100 fiddle tunes at Jack Sellers' Dallas studio; lost.
- **1946–66:** the *Big D Jamboree* draws up to 5,000 a week to the Sportatorium
  ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/big-d-jamboree)); Johnny Gimble settles in Dallas (1949);
  Thomasson wins Crockett three years running (1955–57); Hermes Nye records for Folkways (1952–59).
- **1959–74: the folk revival.** The Rubaiyat opens (1959) with four copycat coffeehouses within a year; the Dallas
  Folk Music Society forms; the NTSU Folk Music Club starts in Denton (1963; Fromholz, Murphey, Hubbard;
  [North Texan](https://northtexan.unt.edu/issues/2014-winter/folk-music-club.html)); Jerry Jeff Walker writes "Mr.
  Bojangles" in a Rubaiyat bartender's basement after arriving in 1964. The Rubaiyat closes in 1974.
- **1961–79:** Stagecoach Ballroom (1961), Panther Hall (1963–78), the National Polka Festival (1966/67), TOTFA (1973),
  Southwest Bluegrass Club (1974), ANMBF (1975), Poor David's (1977), Brave Combo (1979).
- **1982–86: the dance revival.** Uncle Calvin's (1982); the first contra (Feb 12, 1983) and First Texas Céilí (Mar
  5, 1983); KNON (1983); contra moves to Winfrey Point on White Rock Lake (1984; 50–130 dancers); a rapper team (Dec
  1985) and a women's morris side (May 1986); NTTDS incorporated (1986).
- **1987–2019:** Alex Moore becomes DFW's first NEA Heritage Fellow (1987); WIDS (1991); Red Steagall gathering (1991);
  Cowtown Opry (1992); TIMES session players (2002); TIMES (2005); Plaza Theater dance hall (2006–16); Texas Shorty NEA
  Fellow (2010); Fort Worth contra (2012–16); Irving contra ends (2018).
- **2020–22: COVID.** NTTDS dances cancelled (Mar 2020); Gilligan's session suspended; Trinity Hall closes; Uncle
  Calvin's goes dark; NTIF virtual (2021); contra returns once a month (Mar 2022).
- **2024–27: rebound and reshuffle.** WIDS returns; Shipman dies and his archive goes to the Wittliff; Old City Park
  changes hands; the Grape Jam and Fort Worth Songwriter Fest start (2024); AADCP and Rizes (2025); Fiddler's Dream
  returns, the Henry McCarty session opens, Dirty Job closes, Poor David's passes to Amelia Card, son jarocho workshops
  begin, NTIF announces Denton (2026).

## Fiddle angle

**Local style.** DFW's defining fiddle contribution is the **Texas contest style** as rebuilt by Benny Thomasson. As
Texas Shorty told the NEA, Thomasson "took the old tunes that were simple melodies and he embellished them", adding
long-bow variations. Shorty took up fiddle at 13 after meeting him; Terry Morris was a disciple; Thomasson's move to
Washington state in the 1970s is a direct link to Nate's Pacific Northwest. The other lineage is **western swing**:
Brower and Ashlock created the twin-fiddle sound, and Gimble carried it on. The 1966 County LP *Texas Fiddle
Favorites* captured Major and Louis Franklin with Norman Solomon
([Texas Fiddle Archive](https://texasfiddle.blogspot.com/2018/03/texas-fiddle-favorites-major-franklin.html)).

**Tunes named for DFW places.** "Dallas Rag" (Dallas String Band, Columbia, Dec 6, 1927; TTA: country rag in F,
AABB; [wirz.de](https://www.wirz.de/music/jonescol.htm)); "Dallas Bound" (TTA: country rag in G, AA'BB'; source
recording not verified; [tunearch](https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Dallas_Bound)); "Dallas Blues" (1912); "Deep
Elm Blues" (first recorded by the Lone Star Cowboys in 1933, descended from 1923's "Georgia Black Bottom";
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Elem_Blues)). Recorded here, not named for here: "Texas Wagoner",
"Brilliancy Medley", "Amarillo Waltz" (Eck Robertson, Dallas 1929), and Bonner's "Ma Ferguson". The Session has no
Dallas, Fort Worth or Waxahachie tunes; its one "Denton" tune, "Upper Denton", is an English hornpipe, a false friend
([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/12554)). A ballad aside: Coley Jones's 1929 "Drunkard's Special" is Child
274, "Our Goodman", the same ballad as "Seven Drunken Nights".

**Contests.** See Festivals. The 1901 Fort Worth contest is the origin myth; today's in-MSA contest is Greenville's
Bob Wills festival, and the money is at the Stock Show.

## Character

**What's distinct.** DFW is the place where Texas folk music was first broadcast and recorded at scale, and the
place where contest fiddling and western swing were built by working men in Fort Worth dance halls and Dallas body
shops. Its present-day trad scene is suburban, daytime and volunteer-run, and organized around institutions more than
around neighborhoods: TIMES for Irish, four bluegrass bodies, NTTDS, NORTEX, Sacred Harp North Texas. It has one
striking local form (the invited session players) and one striking gap (old-time). Its biggest participatory folk
crowds are Mexican-American, South Asian, Czech-Texan and intertribal, at annual gatherings rather than weekly.

**Who shows up.** Irish sessions skew experienced and older (players in their 60s and 70s in the Shorthorn's photos).
Bluegrass jams are retirees and church musicians, with a separate youth contest stream. DIFD gets 20-odd dancers;
NTTDS's donor base is tiny. The youth are in school mariachis, folklórico academies, the Stock Show's fiddle and
mariachi contests, and the Cowtown Opry's Buckaroos.

**Geography.** Scattered and polycentric. Irish sessions ring the north and east (McKinney, Richardson, Denton) plus
Arlington and Midlothian, with a single monthly in Fort Worth. Carrollton is an unlikely dance hub (the contra
ballroom and DIFD's theater on the old square). Fort Worth owns western heritage (Stockyards, Swingtime, Stagecoach,
National Hall); Ennis owns polka; Irving, Plano, Allen and Frisco hold the South Asian scene; Garland the Vietnamese;
Carrollton the Korean. Sacred Harp deliberately rotates among Denton, Fort Worth, Coppell and Arlington. Expect to
drive 30–45 minutes between things.

**Costs.** Sessions and jams are free, and pubs often comp players (a drink, a meal, validated parking). Contra $10,
ECD $10, DIFD $5, Denton SCD $1, Sacred Harp and the Cowtown Opry free, listening rooms about $20, Stagecoach about
$8 (unverified), the Polka Festival $10–15 a day.

**A visiting fiddler on a Tuesday** has exactly one choice: J. Gilligan's in downtown Arlington at 7pm. Eat dinner
there with the regulars, expect intermediate-to-advanced players, and play before 9 when the ranks thin. Wednesday
gives you The Celt in McKinney (lively, upstairs) or the Cowtown Pickers in the Stockyards. On a 3rd Thursday, take
your pick of the Bearded Ax session in Midlothian or a bluegrass jam in Celina or Carrollton; on a 2nd or 4th
Thursday, sing Sacred Harp in Denton or on the Arlington rooftop.

**On a Saturday** you can fill the day. Start at 10am at the Denton courthouse-square bluegrass jam; on a 2nd
Saturday go on to the Southwest Bluegrass Club in Grapevine or the dulcimer society in Arlington; on a 1st or 3rd,
try (after calling ahead) the old-time jam at Old City Park at 2. Sacred Harp is at 2pm in Fort Worth (2nd) or Coppell
(3rd); the Denton Irish session is at 3 on 2nd (and maybe 4th) Saturdays. In the evening, the 3rd Saturday is the
NTTDS contra in Carrollton and the 2nd Saturday is English country dance in East Dallas; or two-step to a live band at
the Stagecoach. **On a Sunday**, DIFD at 2 in Carrollton, the Cowtown Opry at 2 on the Exchange steps, the Henry
McCarty session on 2nd Sundays, BHF's Poor David's jam or Lakeside Unplugged on 4th Sundays, and the Coachmen at the
Stagecoach at 4.

## Surprises

The ten above, plus:

1. Half of the first country record (1922) was Henry Gilliland, a former Parker County district clerk and the 1901
   Fort Worth contest winner; a former Prohibition Party vice-presidential nominee, J. B. Cranfill, fiddled duets on
   Eck Robertson's 1929 Dallas session.
2. Eck Robertson's 100 Dallas fiddle recordings of 1940 are lost, with no tune list.
3. A Denton session has survived five venues, and a Dallas session was ended by a BMI licensing threat.
4. Fort Worth is training Black square and contra callers, and their free dances are the city's only ones.
5. NTIF started in a bar and is leaving Fair Park for Denton; and the NEA's own Texas Shorty bio gives him
   Thomasson's 1955–57 three-peat, won when Shorty was 12.

## Curiosities

1. Contra density: DFW has about 0.12–0.24 contra dances a month per million. How does that compare across the 20
   metros, and why is the Sun Belt's largest metro so thin?
2. Did COVID permanently halve contra in Sun Belt metros (DFW from about 2.25 to 1 a month) while New England came
   back at full strength?
3. What is each metro's ratio of modern-Western square-dance nights to contra nights? Is DFW's 10:1 typical of the
   South and Midwest?
4. Why is old-time nearly absent here while bluegrass is strong? Is it the same in Houston, Atlanta and Phoenix, and
   does Texas contest fiddling fill the niche?
5. Does any other metro have an invited, rotated "session players" institution, and does it help or hurt open
   sessions?
6. How many students play in school mariachis in DFW, Houston, San Antonio, Phoenix and LA? Is mariachi now the
   largest youth fiddle tradition in Sun Belt metros?
7. Sacred Harp per capita: why does much smaller Austin support a weekly singing when DFW doesn't? Which metros have
   seminary- or university-hosted annual singings?
8. Why do some metros of 8 million have no shanty sing or ballad circle (DFW, apparently Houston) while small cities
   do? A Sun Belt, Texas or car-centered-metro pattern?
9. Among 1950s BIA relocation cities, which still have weekly or monthly drum or gourd-dance practice open to the
   community, and which have only annual powwows?
10. Are fraternal halls elsewhere (Houston SPJST lodges, Chicago and Cleveland Sokols) surviving by becoming
    western-swing, Tejano or general dance venues, as National Hall has?
11. Are legacy urban heritage festivals leaving historic fairgrounds for exurban ones, and what happens to attendance
    and session content?
12. Is a metro's folk memory usually archived inside it? DFW's sits 200 miles away in San Marcos.
13. How common are Black caller-training programs like AADCP (cf. Chicago's Southside Squares), and do they change who
    comes to dances?
14. Did COVID end more church-hall coffeehouses (Uncle Calvin's) than commercial rooms, nationally?

## Data quality and caveats

- **Method.** Five threads on Oct 30, 2026 (web search and fetch only), merged and deduplicated by hand, with a few
  merger spot-checks (Stagecoach Ballroom's Sept 2026 listing; Norman Solomon's Kaufman County and General Dynamics
  ties; the 2025 Decatur Celtic Festival). Search was rate-limited in places, and tunearch.org, loc.gov, TARO and UNT
  ArchivesSpace blocked automated fetches, so TTA tune details come from search snippets and no Lomax or WPA
  field recordings made in DFW could be confirmed.
- **Strong:** Irish sessions (thesession.org API and comments, TIMES, pub calendars), Sacred Harp (two 2026
  directories agree), NTTDS (2026 newsletters). **Medium:** bluegrass (an undated club list cross-checked event by
  event; aggregators garble days), NORTEX (8 of 20 square clubs have published schedules, so square dancing is
  undercounted), heritage (a verified floor; Facebook-only and temple-based activity is invisible).
- **Weak:** the Dallas Old-Time Music Jam (listings only; newest dated event 2019), Hooligan's (May 2024), Times Ten
  Cellars and Mid-Cities jams, Denton session frequency, RikuDallas's venue, Dallas Aztec Dancers, the Cowtown Opry's
  own site (stale), Uncle Calvin's status.
- **Exclusions from totals:** 11 bar blues jams (47.6/month), round dancing (5.3), Lindy swing, the invited TIMES
  Session Players (4.3), a concert series, spectator festivals, and two series without 2024–26 evidence. Annual
  participatory gatherings count 0.083/month. festivals_active (59) includes contests and some multi-genre festivals
  with folk stages.
- **Distinct traditions (39):** 16 in layers 1–3 and 23 heritage traditions with verified 2024–26 activity; the list
  is in the JSON. Traditions seen only before 2024 or only name-checked (Korean pungmul, rondalla, klezmer,
  Scandinavian, Vietnamese traditional music, morris, céilí, shanties, seven-shape singing) are not counted.
- **Contradictions** (details in the JSON): National Polka Festival founding 1966 (TSHA) vs 1967 (festival); Texas
  Scottish Festival 1986 vs 1987 and its 2025–26 status; the Reynolds singing's numbering; Cowtown Opry 1992 vs a 1995
  club date; the Bob Wills contest venue; the Cotton Bowl singing's start (1936 vs 1938); "Pancho and Lefty" written
  at the Rubaiyat (D Magazine's claim, disputed elsewhere); plus minor ones (street numbers, edition numbers, seat
  counts). The history thread's "no DFW tie" for Solomon and Burns was superseded.
- **Not found (not the same as absent):** Scottish/Cape Breton, Scandinavian, Balkan, klezmer, gypsy-jazz and
  French-Canadian sessions; céilí; morris or sword since the 1980s; shanty or ballad groups; seven-shape conventions;
  a Comhaltas branch; NTTDS and NORTEX membership counts; school mariachi student counts; gospel quartet anniversaries
  (not researched successfully).
