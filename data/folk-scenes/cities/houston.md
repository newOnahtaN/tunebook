# Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands: the folk scene, October 2026

*Metro slug `houston`. Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands MSA (CBSA 26420): Austin, Brazoria, Chambers, Fort Bend,
Galveston, Harris, Liberty, Montgomery and Waller counties, population about 7.9 million. Compiled Oct 30, 2026 from
five research threads (sessions, dance, song, heritage, history) on Nate's Windows workstation, using web search and
fetch only. Nothing was played. Structured data is in `houston.json`.*

## Scene in a nutshell

Houston's folk scene is big, sprawling and split into worlds that rarely meet. By volume its signature is **Creole
zydeco**. Jax Grill has had live zydeco every Friday and Saturday for more than 25 years, Black Catholic parishes still
hold church-hall dances and bazaars, and Black trail-riding clubs end their campouts with zydeco bands. One promoter
calendar alone lists 21 to 30 zydeco dance dates a month. Alongside it run 12 **modern Western square dance** clubs
(about 48 club nights a month, down from more than 100 clubs in 1949), a suburban **bluegrass** jam network (about 17 a
month), a compact **Irish session** cluster near Rice Village (about 16), and a small, 40-year-old revival scene of
contra, English, Scottish and international dance centered on one Midtown church. The song side is the **Texas
singer-songwriter** tradition of the Jester, Sand Mountain, the Old Quarter and Anderson Fair, tended since 1951 by a
volunteer folk society. West of town, **Czech and German halls** still hold monthly polka dances.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **Zydeco is Houston's biggest participatory folk form, by far.** A scrape of [ZydecoEvents.com](https://www.zydecoevents.com/zydeco/texas)
   found about 462 separate zydeco dance dates in the MSA in the 22 months from January 2025 to October 2026, a floor
   because pagination was capped. Jax Grill alone dances about 104 nights a year; Houston's whole contra series manages
   about 30.
2. **The word "zydeco" was made in Houston.** The first two records to use a variant of it for a music were cut here
   (Lightnin' Hopkins' "Zolo Go," about 1947, and Clarence Garlow's "Bon Ton Roula," 1949), and Houston folklorist Mack
   McCormick fixed the spelling in 1959/60 ([Handbook of Texas: Zydeco](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/zydeco)).
3. **A Black Catholic church-dance circuit still runs.** At least 7 of the 11 parishes that *Texas Monthly* named around
   1988 hosted zydeco in 2025–26, often through the Knights of Peter Claver
   ([Texas Monthly, "The Big Squeezy"](https://www.texasmonthly.com/arts-entertainment/zydeco-church-dances-houston/)).
   One parish ran a "Zydeco Breakfast Dance."
4. **One church carries the revival scene.** First Lutheran, 1311 Holman St in Midtown, hosts contra, English country
   dance, international folk dance, Scottish country dance and a Sacred Harp singing: about 15 events a month in five
   traditions ([HATDS](https://hatds.org/), [HIFD](http://www.folkdancers.org/), [RSCDS Houston](https://houstonrscds.org/classes/)).
5. **One Knights of Columbus hall serves three dance cultures.** Roemer Hall at 607 E Whitney St has a sprung floor
   "built specifically for dancing." It hosts the Bayou Bedlam contra weekend ([about](https://bayoubedlam.org/about)),
   a Black trail riders' zydeco kickoff dance and Czech polka dinner-dances ([KofC 2917](https://kofccouncil2917.org/events/summer-fling)).
6. **Square dancing shrank from stadium scale, yet it still dances the most nights.** There were 9,950 at the 1949 Sam
   Houston Coliseum festival and 20,164 dancers at the 1987 National Convention in the Astrodome
   ([Houston History](https://houstonhistorymagazine.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/square-dancing.pdf)). The 2026
   Houston Hoedown drew 230 ([recap](https://houstonsquaredance.com/2026/10/27/78th-houston-hoedown-what-a-weekend/)),
   but 12 clubs still list about 48 club nights a month.
7. **Sunday football keeps evicting the Irish session.** It was "booted from the third straight pub in favor of Sunday
   Football" before about 2005, then left The Harp, then Pimlico in 2019 ([TheSession #3672](https://thesession.org/sessions/3672)).
8. **No Texas-style fiddle contest is held inside the MSA.** The "World Champion Fiddlers Festival" is on Houston Avenue
   in Crockett, Houston County, about 120 miles north ([FB](https://www.facebook.com/FiddlersFestivalCrockett/)).
9. **The KKK bombed KPFT twice in 1970**, the second time while it was playing "Alice's Restaurant." Arlo Guthrie came to
   play it live when the station returned in January 1971 ([KPFT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPFT)).
10. **Lomax recording sites lie inside the metro.** The Sugar Land prison farm where Lead Belly served and where the
    Lomaxes recorded Iron Head Baker in 1933–34 is now a master-planned suburb, and four Brazoria County prison farms
    were recorded in 1933–39 ([LOC Folklife blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2024/06/caught-my-eye-iron-head-baker-and-the-mighty-blue-goose/)).

## Trad instrumental music and sessions

Houston's instrumental scene is **bluegrass first, Irish second, with old-time a recent arrival.** No Scottish, Cape
Breton, Cajun, Western swing, gypsy jazz, klezmer, Balkan or Scandinavian open session could be verified.

### Irish

TheSession's API shows only four live listings within 100 km of downtown. Three of the four (the Duck, Hugh O'Connor's
and the St. Thomas slow session) are within about 2.3 km of one another around Rice Village and Upper Kirby; the
fourth is out by NASA in El Lago.

- **McGonigel's Mucky Duck, Wednesdays 7:30, no cover.** This is the longest-documented session. Houston Public Media
  said in 2016 that it had run "more than 30 years," starting at the Red Lion on Main Street and moving to the Duck after
  the Red Lion burned ([HPM 2016](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/arts-culture/music/2016/03/17/215064/irish-session-plays-for-over-thirty-years-in-houston-pubs/)).
  It plays on the Duck's stage, "an onstage, in-the-lights, pseudo-performance," as a 2012 commenter put it
  ([TheSession #910](https://thesession.org/sessions/910)). Touring acts sometimes take the night. It restarted after
  COVID on February 9, 2022, and the November 2026 calendar lists it every Wednesday.
- **Hugh O'Connor's, Dincans St, Sundays 4–6 pm, "all skill levels welcome."** It appeared in February 2025, three
  months after the Penny Whistle Pub was sold. ceol.app has logged **38 dated sessions (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026) and 96
  distinct tunes** ([API logs](https://ceol.app/api/sessions/houston/hugh-oconnors-dincans/logs)). The most logged are
  The Diplodocus, Banish Misfortune and The Star of Munster (6 each), then The Mouse in the Mug and Old Joe's
  ([logged tunes](https://ceol.app/api/sessions/houston/hugh-oconnors-dincans/logged-tunes)). Several match the Houston
  School of Irish Music's semester lists (Castle Kelly, Farewell to Whalley Range, Old Man Dillon), so the school feeds
  the pub. The pub's own events page lists trivia and karaoke but not the session.
- **Chelsea Wine Bar, El Lago, Mondays about 7:15.** It descends from a Galveston Irish Session begun in **1997**. Later
  it was a Thursday session at Coffee Oasis/Soho Café on NASA Road 1, spent 2014 at Dunn Brothers in Friendswood, and
  was revived at the Chelsea in 2019. The June 2026 note says it is "a small session, usually 3–6 players… not held
  every week," announced on the private Facebook group "Irish Sessions Houston"
  ([TheSession #3190](https://thesession.org/sessions/3190)). A poster calls El Lago "one of the few remaining gems…
  largely as it was during the Apollo era."
- **Houston Slow Session, most Sundays 2:30–3:30**, in Strake Hall at the University of St. Thomas, run by the Houston
  School of Irish Music and open to anyone ([HSOIM](https://hsoim.org/)).

The infrastructure behind these is Catholic and academic. **HSOIM**, a branch of the Carrollton-based School of Irish
Music Inc., teaches Sunday group classes at UST and publishes its tune lists. Spring 2026 included Tralee Gaol, Lord
Mayo, Old Man Dillon and the Boy in the Boat ([class schedule](https://hsoim.org/class-schedule)). The **Gulf Coast
Cruinniú**, every early June at UST, gives nine progressive classes per student, nightly sessions, a céilí and a concert.
It is part-funded by the **Irish Government's Emigrant Support Programme**, its mascot is "Banjodilly," an armadillo with
a banjo, and it costs $325 before April 2027 ([GCC](https://gulfcoastirish.org/),
[registration](https://gulfcoastirish.org/registration-and-tickets/)). The 2027 faculty includes Mary Bergin and Eimear
Arkins.

The session's history is a story of eviction. The Sunday session of about 1998–2005 gave up after being "booted from
the third straight pub in favor of Sunday Football." Pimlico revived Sundays in 2014, with a beer discount and a
monthly slow session, until "bumped from our space… Perhaps we'll be back after (US) football season" in October 2019
([#3672](https://thesession.org/sessions/3672)). The Penny Whistle Pub ("very acoustically friendly") took the session
from 2019 until it was sold in November 2024 ([#6982](https://thesession.org/sessions/6982)). Dormant listings include
the **Kelvin Arms**, Houston's Scottish pub, where a 2007 session mixed "English, Scottish, and Irish music"
([#1722](https://thesession.org/sessions/1722)), and **Brew:30** in Cypress, whose 2019 session drew players "from
Clear Lake (NASA) to Houston to College Station" and has been "on hold" since the pandemic because of traffic
([#6811](https://thesession.org/sessions/6811)). Nothing verifiable runs in The Woodlands, Katy, Sugar Land or Galveston,
despite their many Irish-themed pubs.

### Old-time

In 2013 a local told a Banjo Hangout poster: "There are no, strictly Old Time jams of which I'm aware." The nearest was
in Montgomery, where "we play what we Texans know as 'Old Time' which is, mostly, road house music"
([Banjo Hangout](https://www.banjohangout.org/archive/267766)). That changed by 2026. **Greater Houston Old Time Music**
now jams on the 2nd and 4th Sundays, 7–9 pm, at Under the Volcano on Bissonnet, after a spell at a Pizza Inn on Mangum
Rd in April ([group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1274811384752990/), [ma.to listing](https://ma.to/event/old-time-jam-session-27-sep-2026)).
Its April 26, 2026 theme was "C tunes": Denver Belle, Cherry River Rag, East Tennessee Blues, Funs All Over, Texas
Fair, Pike's Peak, L&N Rag, Texas Gals and Rattlesnake Bit the Baby
([post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1274811384752990/permalink/1313885994178862/)). That is rags and Texan
repertoire, not Round Peak.

The oldest continuously running instrumental club in the metro is a dulcimer society. The **North Harris County
Dulcimer Society** was founded in 1985 with 10 people in a living room and grew to "over 100 members." It holds a slow
jam and free lessons on mountain dulcimer, autoharp and hammered dulcimer every 3rd Saturday, welcomes fiddles, and
runs a January retreat in Sugar Land ([about](https://nhcds.org/wp-nhcds/about-us/)).

### Bluegrass

The **Bay Area Bluegrass Association** (est. 1986) ran a 3rd-Saturday show at the Webster Civic Center for decades,
with jam rooms from 4 pm. On **October 5, 2026** president Randy Powell cancelled the rest of 2026: the schedule "is no
longer sustainable… This is not the end of our organization, rather a reconfiguration" ([BABA](https://bayareabluegrass.org/)).
The jams carry on. The **Houston Weekly Bluegrass Jam**, hosted by Doug Burtchaell and Chris Hirsch, continues "a
Houston tradition which began at the King Biscuit (in the Heights)," then Hickory Hollow and Buffalo Grille, and moved to
Hickory Pit BBQ in Bellaire on December 4, 2024 ([move post](https://www.facebook.com/100021212087211/posts/the-houston-weekly-bluegrass-jam-will-now-be-held-every-wednesday-at-the-hickory/1731977510852635/)).
**Cypress Bluegrass Rising** runs a Monday jam at a Cypress church, whose regulars formed four bands for a 2025 church
fun day, plus a 4th-Saturday jam at Kleb Woods Nature Center and a 2nd-Friday "Bluegrass Music Knight Out"
([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/BluegrassRising/)). BABA's own Tuesday jam is at Luka's BBQ near NASA. The
northwest suburbs (Cypress, Tomball) host three bluegrass events a month; Katy and Fort Bend host none.

Aggregators disagree with organizers. StartJamming puts League City on 1st Fridays (it is the last Friday), and both
StartJamming and Jamfinder still list the Buffalo Grill jam, gone since December 2024
([StartJamming](https://www.startjamming.com/jams?state=TX), [Jamfinder](https://www.jamfinder.org/jams/houston-buffalo-grill/)).
The memory lead "Houston Bluegrass Music Society" returned no results at all.

### Cajun, Scottish and the rest

There is **no Cajun jam** in the city that took in more Louisiana migrants than any other. A Cajun French Music
Association Houston Heritage Chapter was incorporated in 1991 and is now listed as "Forfeited Existence"
([Bizapedia](https://www.bizapedia.com/tx/cajun-french-music-association-of-louisiana-houston-heritage-chapter-inc.html)).
Scottish fiddle appears only as dance music: Judi Nicolson plays the RSCDS Ball. The **Houston Accordion
Association** (1990) is a 15-minutes-each showcase at a Pizza Inn ([site](https://houstonaccordion.org/)), undated.
Klezmer, Balkan and gypsy jazz exist only as gigging bands.

## Dance

### Contra, ECD and squares: HATDS

The **Houston Area Traditional Dance Society** is the metro's only contra series and main ECD series, a CDSS affiliate
with a five-person volunteer board ([about](https://hatds.org/about-hatds)). Contra runs on the 2nd and 4th Saturdays
at 7:30 in First Lutheran's Parish Hall, with some 5th-Saturday afternoon dances. An experimental **Second Thursday
contra** (August–November 2026) is subsidized by donors to $5 for everyone; "If attendance is good they may be extended
into 2027" ([home](https://hatds.org/)). ECD runs on 1st, 3rd and 5th Sunday afternoons, all to **recorded music**
([ECD](https://hatds.org/ecd)). The house band, *Contra Dan's*, is a rehearsed community band, "not a pick up or drop in
band," with ABC and MuseScore files online ([Contra Dan's](https://hatds.org/contradans)). Steve Bing and Erica McCready
called an occasional old-time square dance at Dauntless Dance and Movement in 2025 ("not to be confused as Western
Square Dance") ([contradances.net](https://contradances.net/events/locate/us/tx)).

Contra arrived from New England in the late 1970s or 1980 with Dorcas Hand and Barry Cooper, in "whatever space they
could find, including driveways!" ([history](https://hatds.org/history.php)). Dorcas Hand dates it to "1980 when a
couple of New Englanders (me) missed home too much" ([CDSS](https://www.cdss.org/centennial/community100/local-heroes/129-local-hero-mike-head)).
In 1986 the fiddler-architect **Mike Head** started the monthly series at the Heights Woman's Club: if it broke even
"he'd hold another dance the next month. It did and he did." He took summers off "when the air conditioning just
couldn't handle contra dancers," built a backyard dance hall ("Oxford Hall") and died in 2012
([memorial](https://hatds.org/MikeHeadMemorial)). HATDS was incorporated on September 14, 1990. Keith Holmes, one of the
1986 callers, still runs its website.

**Bayou Bedlam**, the February contra weekend, began in 1994 and reached its 32nd edition in 2026. It is held at Roemer
Hall's sprung floor and is known for home-cooked lunch and brunch. It costs $150, or **$60 for ages 18–30**
([fees](https://bayoubedlam.org/BB26/regInfo)). 2027 is Lindsey Dono with Root System. Houston also takes its turn
hosting **Set for Spring**, the rotating Texas–Oklahoma ECD weekend (2015, 2022 and a Joseph Pimentel tribute in March
2026; [history](https://hatds.org/sfs/about.php)).

### Scottish, Irish céilí, international and Israeli

The **RSCDS Houston & District Branch** (organized November 1978) is "the branch for… all of Texas. (Perhaps
Headquarters didn't realize how big Texas is!)" ([about](https://houstonrscds.org/about/)). Classes run Tuesdays at
First Lutheran and Mondays in The Woodlands, at $10 ($8 for members). The October 31, 2026 Ball has live fiddle and
keyboard and a program including "The Scottish Werewolf" ([Ball](https://houstonrscds.org/ball-2026/)).

There is **no regular céilí**. The big one is the Cruinniú's Houston Céilí ($10; Éamonn de Cógáin, "The Céilí King,"
called the 2026 dance on June 5) ([UST](https://www.stthom.edu/events/the-gulf-coast-cruinniu-and-william-j-flynn-center-for-irish-studies-present-houston-ceili-2026/)).

**Houston International Folk Dancers** (founded 1947, about 75 members, "more than 300 dances from 39 countries") holds
Friday request dancing at First Lutheran and Monday introductory dancing upstairs in the Heights Oddfellows Hall. It costs
$5 and is free for ages 25 and under ([folkdancers.org](http://www.folkdancers.org/)). Balkan dance in Houston happens
almost entirely here. The state umbrella, Texas International Folk Dancers, runs Texas Camp (founded 1949), outside the
MSA. Israeli dance has two weekly options: **Machol Houston** on Thursdays ([programs](https://www.macholhouston.org/programs))
and the **Evelyn Rubenstein JCC** on Tuesdays ([ERJCC](https://www.erjcchouston.org/course-session/intermediate-advanced-israeli-folk-dance-fall-2026/)).

There is **no morris team**. NAMDO lists "Men of Houston (Ceased)" ([NAMDO](https://morrisdance.org/south-southwest/)),
and the mixed side Shambles has left no trace.

The history is remarkable. In 1947 a dentist, **Dr. Carl Journell**, began teaching ("One class had 140 couples"), and
the Parks Department ran free 10-lesson classes that graduated straight into new clubs. The first Spring Festival
(April 1949, Sam Houston Coliseum) had 171 marked squares and 9,950 dancers and spectators; by 1953 it drew about
25,000. Records replaced live bands in 1967, the festival became the **Houston Hoedown** in 1985, contra was added in
1997, and the **1987 National Convention** put 20,164 dancers in the Astrodome, where they formed a silhouette of Texas
([Houston History](https://houstonhistorymagazine.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/square-dancing.pdf)). The industry's
biggest label, **Blue Star Records**, came out of the Merrbach studio at Rutland & 14th in the Heights in 1956, "the
largest and longest-running square dance music label in the world"
([Leader News](https://www.theleadernews.com/square-dancing-in-houston-a-tradition-of-community-fun-and-fitness/article_2eac7db6-e330-11ef-a45a-f780ca2aaf21.html)).
It is two blocks from where HIFD now dances on Mondays.

### Zydeco, polka and dance halls

These are the metro's biggest dance worlds; the Heritage section below covers them in depth. **Jax Grill** has live
zydeco every Friday and Saturday ("we've been doing it for 25+ years") ([FB](https://www.facebook.com/jaxgrillshepherd/videos/thats-right-every-friday-and-saturday-jax-grill-shepherd-host-zydeco-nights-come/1327115198073253/)).
The rotating bands include Keyun & the Zydeco Masters, Nooney & the Zydeco Floaters, Gerard Delafose and Curtis
Poullard. West of the city, **Needville SPJST Lodge 81** runs a $10 BYOB Friday community dance with South Texas'
Finest, a new band that began a monthly residency in 2025; the Sealy Lions Dance Club dances every first Saturday; and
Wallis's "Let's Dance" club runs about 1.5 dances a month ([Texas Polka News](http://www.texaspolkanews.com/live-music-calendar)).
Austin County keeps **7 of Texas's 18 known "round" dance halls**, including the 12-sided **Bellville Turnverein
Pavilion** (1897, built for $360 by Joachim Hintz), restored after Harvey with the air conditioning ducted under the
floor ([Preservation Texas](https://www.preservationtexas.org/honor-awards/bellville-turnverein)).

## Folk song and listening

Houston's "folk" leans hard toward **Texas singer-songwriters**. The listening rooms are old, volunteer-run and strict.

- **Anderson Fair**, 2007 Grant St in Montrose (1969 as a restaurant, music by 1970). It has about 80 seats and is run
  entirely by volunteers. If you talk during a set or let your phone ring, you get your cover back and are asked to
  leave ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/anderson-fair-retail-restaurant)). Alumni include Townes Van
  Zandt, Lucinda Williams, Lyle Lovett, Nanci Griffith (whose *One Fair Summer Evening* was recorded there in 1988) and
  Robert Earl Keen. In fall 2026 it runs Friday shows at $20–30 and a $15 Thursday songwriter showcase, cash bar, also
  streamed ([andersonfair.net](https://andersonfair.net/)).
- **McGonigel's Mucky Duck**, 2425 Norfolk, opened in 1990 (not 1987) in a building that had been a gambling den, "The
  Red Man's Lodge." The opening-night players (Shake Russell, Dana Cooper and Jack Saunders) left without collecting
  their pay so the club could get a good start ([Houston History](https://houstonhistorymagazine.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/Mucky-Duck.pdf)).
  It has touring acts most nights and a free **Monday open mic**: "comedians, poets, jugglers and mimes also welcome"
  ([open mic](https://www.mcgonigels.com/shows/open-mic-6-930-226/)).
- **Old Quarter Acoustic Cafe**, Galveston. Rex "Wrecks" Bell ran the original downtown Houston Old Quarter (1965–79),
  where Townes Van Zandt recorded *Live at the Old Quarter* in 1973, and reopened it on Galveston Island in 1996. Its
  Tuesday Night Song Circle allows up to 10 chairs, originals only, no PA, and "no percussion allowed in the circle or the
  room" ([event](https://www.prekindle.com/event/84339-tuesday-night-song-circle-galveston)). Blaze Foley's duct-taped
  wallet hung on its "Townes wall" until Hurricane Ike's surge took it in 2008 ([In Memory Of](https://oldquarteracousticcafe.com/in-memory-of)).
  The 30th Townes Van Zandt Wake is on January 1, 2027.
- **Millbend Coffeehouse**, The Woodlands (since December 1992), on second Saturdays at a Unitarian church. The audience
  votes at intermission on which charity gets the gate; about $45,000 has been given so far ([history](https://www.millbend.org/history.htm)).
- **Coffee House Houston** (Coffee House Live, 2013–2025) runs free, donation-only Thursday shows at Lambert Hall in the
  Heights ([about](https://coffeehousehouston.com/about)).
- **Main Street Crossing**, Tomball, is a 150-seat room that becomes a church on Sunday mornings ([about](https://mainstreetcrossing.com/about-us/)).
- **Fulshear and Champions House Concerts**: potluck, two 45-minute sets, $30 with 100% to the artist
  ([about](https://www.fulshearhouseconcerts.org/about-us)).

The **Houston Folklore and Music Society** (June 1951) runs the participatory core. It holds a first-Tuesday **song
circle** at Hickory Pit BBQ ("Bring an instrument, play along, sing along, tell a tall tale, or just listen") and
**Second Saturday concerts** at St. Andrew's Presbyterian ($20 suggested, cash or check), and plans a 75th-anniversary
show on December 12, 2026 ([HFMS](https://www.houstonfolkmusic.org/HFMS-Second-Saturday-Concerts)). Its newsletter, *The
Cotton Patch Rag*, is in Volume 61 and still prints chord sheets for jam tunes ("Cluck Old Hen," with a note on Fiddlin'
John Carson's 1923 record). The **Houston Songwriters Association** (founded 1989 in Richmond) holds monthly critique
meetings with a "Song of the Month" vote ([HSA](https://www.houstonsongwriters.net/about-us)).

## Traditional singing

**Sacred Harp is small but healthy.** There are three monthly singings: 2nd Monday at Epiphany Lutheran in Pearland,
3rd Monday at Covenant Church in the Museum District, and 4th Sunday at First Lutheran. All use the brand-new 2025
Revision and the Cooper book ([Texas Fasola](http://singings.texasfasola.org/monthlysingings.html)). Covenant lists the
singers on its own calendar and says the group "has collaborated in our worship"; the local group calls itself
"welcoming, democratic, and fiercely inclusive" ([houstonfasola.org](https://houstonfasola.org/)). The fasola.org
minutes show no Houston singing before 2004. The Houston All-Day Singing began at the Heights Church of Christ in 2004
and paused after 2019. **It returns on December 5, 2026** with a singing school, dinner on the grounds and a Sunday
**"composium"** of new compositions from the *Shenandoah Harmony* and the *Valley Pocket Harmonist*
([event](https://houstonfasola.org/events/houston-all-day-sacred-harp-singing/)). The Primm Birthday Singing is each
October. The deep rural conventions (East Texas, organized 1868; Southwest Texas, 1900) are outside the MSA. East Texas
also once had **Black Sacred Harp conventions**, where singers paid to "enroll" to lead and sang "at a T-Model pace"
until midnight. They were gone by the late 1970s ([Ross](http://historic.texasfasola.org/blacksacredharp.html)).

**There is no public shanty sing** in this port metro. The oddity is the **Shanty-Chor Houston**, formed in 1993 inside
the Houston Liederkranz (a German singing club founded in 1925). It sings shanties in High German, Plattdeutsch and
English, and its founder believed it "may be the first or only" such choir in the US
([history](https://www.oocities.org/houston_liederkranz/shanty_chor_history.html)). It is documented only through 2014.
No ballad circle, sean-nós group, Christian Harmony singing or cowboy-poetry gathering was found.

**Gospel quartets** are the strongest living community singing tradition, and the least documented. Don Robey's
Peacock Records (1949) made Houston a gospel quartet capital with the Dixie Hummingbirds, Sensational Nightingales and
Mighty Clouds of Joy; gospel sales "kept the label afloat" ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/duke-peacock-records)).
Today **Endurance of Houston** is a six-time Stellar nominee whose lead singer was converted to quartet singing in 1965 by
a group singing in a Fifth Ward storefront church ([Texas Highways](https://texashighways.com/culture/preserving-traditional-black-gospel-music/)).
The weekly anniversary programs could not be counted. On the white Southern-gospel side, the **Greater Houston Gospel
Songfest** holds three concerts a year in Spring ([Dignity Gospel Quartet](https://dignitygospelquartet.com/concerts.php)).

## Heritage and diaspora traditions

### Creole zydeco (thriving)

**Frenchtown**, four blocks on the northern edge of the Fifth Ward, was organized in 1922 by about 500 Creoles of color
from southwest Louisiana. They built Our Mother of Mercy church themselves, paying for it with **la-la dances** and gumbo
and boudin sales ([TSHA Frenchtown](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/frenchtown-houston)). On Christmas Eve
1949 accordionist **Willie Green** played an impromptu set at Irene's Café, and Creole accordion music moved into public
venues. Johnson's Lounge became Doris McClendon's **Continental Zydeco Ballroom**, the main room for half a century until
1997 ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/continental-zydeco-ballroom)). Roger Wood calls Louisiana "the
roots" and Houston "the urban hothouse" where la-la became electric zydeco
([Texas Standard](https://www.texasstandard.org/stories/texas-zydeco-origins-houston-gulf-coast-clifton-chenier/)).
Clifton Chenier played his first Houston gigs at the Silver Slipper ([Texas Highways](https://texashighways.com/culture/arts-entertainment/experience-one-of-the-best-live-music-scenes-in-texas-at-houstons-silver-slipper/))
and recorded "Zydeco Sont Pas Salé" at Gold Star in 1964. The Silver Slipper building is now for sale as a "Frenchtown
cultural landmark" ([Crexi](https://www.crexi.com/properties/2448847/texas-the-silver-slipper)).

Today zydeco runs on three networks:

- **Restaurants and clubs**: Jax Grill (Fri/Sat), Wing Quarter (free Saturday afternoons), The Big Easy (Sundays,
  [site](https://www.thebigeasyblues.com/)), Prospect Park, a Black-owned sports bar ("Zydeco Sundays"), Club Menai,
  A'dor Almeda, Carrington's and Blazin Kajun.
- **Black Catholic parishes.** Around 1988 *Texas Monthly* called Houston "the Los Angeles of Acadiana," with "a church
  dance nearly every Saturday night." In 2025–26 St. Francis of Assisi (Frenchtown), St. Francis Xavier, St. Philip Neri,
  St. Peter Claver, St. Monica, Our Lady Star of the Sea and St. Anne de Beaupré all held zydeco bazaars, matinees or
  dances, as did Barrett Station, Raywood and the Ames parish's Easter Rodeo ([St. Peter Claver](https://www.stpeterclaverhouston.com/event-details/st-peter-claver-fall-bazaar-october-10th-11th),
  [St. Francis Xavier](https://www.zydecoevents.com/event/st-francis-xaivier-presents-2026-parish-percussion)).
- **Trail rides.** "Most of the Black trail rides in Houston culminate with a zydeco dance" (Roger Wood). The Deep 6
  Riding Club held its 31st campout in 2025, the 290 Riders their 28th in Hempstead and the Acres Homes Riding & Roping
  Club its 34th in Conroe ([archive](https://www.zydecoevents.com/events/archive/2026/05)). The Prairie View Trail
  Riders, the rodeo's first African American ride, date from 1957 ([KHOU](https://www.khou.com/article/entertainment/events/rodeohouston/prairie-view-trail-riders-texas/285-34799a3a-37f2-48f6-b757-0cdac67b4abf)).

Houston bands include Step Rideau & the Zydeco Outlaws, Keyun & the Zydeco Masters, Curtis Poullard, J Paul Jr. and
Brian Jack. For Nate's purposes, the fiddle link is **Cedric Watson**, the Creole fiddler born in 1983 in **San Felipe,
Austin County**, who first played publicly at a Houston zydeco jam ([LoC](https://wwws.loc.gov/concerts/folklife/cedric-watson.html)),
and **Edward Poullard** of Beaumont, a Creole fiddler and 2025 NEA Heritage Fellow ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/edward-poullard)).

**Cajun**, as distinct from zydeco, is fading. Pe-Te's Cajun BBQ House, across from Ellington Field, held Saturday
dances for about 25 years until March 2005. Astronauts "played tapes of Lester 'Pe-Te' Johnson's Saturday-morning Cajun
radio program on KPFT while in outer space" ([Houston Press](https://www.houstonpress.com/news/end-of-a-cajun-era-6550289/)).
His *Cajun Bandstand* survives on KPFT.

### Mexican American (thriving, institution-backed)

**Mariachi** is driven by schools. HISD had mariachi at six or more high schools and two middle schools in 2023, and UH's
José Longoria saw his summer camp grow from 30 to 85 to 115 students in three years
([Houston Landing](https://www.houstonlanding.org/in-houston-schools-mariachi-programs-help-students-find-cultural-connections/)).
That may make school mariachi the metro's largest pipeline of young violinists playing a folk style. The **Houston
Mariachi Festival** fills the Wortham Center for three nights ([about](https://mariachifestival.com/about-us)).
**Ballet folklórico** companies date from 1977 (MECA), 1979 (Ambassadors) and 1992 (Mixteco). **Festival Chicano** at
Miller Outdoor Theatre (1977 or 1979; 46th in 2025) is the conjunto and Tejano flagship ([houstonse](https://houstonse.org/blog/46th-annual-festival-chicano-great-music-with-a-side-of-history/)).
A free **Houston Conjunto Fest** with instrument workshops started in 2025 ([CultureMap](https://houston.culturemap.com/eventdetail/houston-conjunto-fest/)),
and the Tejano Music Awards moved to a Katy dance hall. **Son jarocho could not be found** in Houston at all.

### Accordion: the shared folk instrument

**Accordion Kings & Queens**, Texas Folklife's free Miller Outdoor Theatre show (37th on October 10, 2026), puts zydeco,
Cajun, conjunto and polka on one stage ([Texas Folklife](https://texasfolklife.org/accordion-kings-and-queens/)). Its
youth contest, **The Big Squeeze**, held its 20th finals in Houston in May 2026.

### Czech and German (active, aging but renewing)

**SPJST Lodge 88** in the Heights (1910) has "one of the largest dance floors in Central Houston" and its own Czech
orchestra; you can bring one bottle with a $4 corkage fee ([dances](https://www.lodge88.org/dances)). Rural Austin and
Fort Bend county halls still hold monthly dances. The Cat Spring Agricultural Society (1856, Texas's oldest) holds a June
Fest in its 12-sided hall. The **Houston Saengerbund** (1883) bought a Heights church in 2022 ([about](https://houstonsaengerbund.org/about/)).
The **Slavic Heritage Festival** reached its 63rd year in 2026, with Polish (Wawel) and Croatian folk ensembles.

### Black community traditions

**Juneteenth was born inside the MSA**, at Galveston on June 19, 1865. Houston freedpeople bought ten acres for $800 in
1872 to celebrate it: Emancipation Park ([Juneteenth Houston](https://www.juneteenthhouston.org/history)). Blues lives on
as a community jam on Kirby Drive (Houston Blues Society, last Thursdays; [The Big Easy](https://www.thebigeasyblues.com/))
rather than in the Third Ward juke joints.

### Diaspora communities: festival-rich, practice-thin

The Greek festival (59th in 2025, 35,000-plus visitors), Lebanese festival with dabke, Caribbean Festival with mas bands
and "Pan in the Park," IgboFest, AfriFEST, the Tet Festival, Japan Festival, Korean Festival, Navratri garba with "tens
of thousands," and a Traders Village powwow (36th in 2026) are all active. Few have a verifiable weekly participatory
practice. Groups with an ongoing practice (not all with dated 2024–26 evidence) include Kaminari Taiko (1996), the Houston Chinese Traditional Music Group (2009), the Indian
classical societies (the Tyagaraja Aradhana's 48th edition was in 2025) and two Scottish pipe bands. The **Houston
Highland Games were cancelled in 2025** and have no 2026 date ([HHGA](https://houstonhighlandgames.com/)).

## History timeline

- **1831**: Industry, Austin County, the first permanent German settlement in Texas.
- **1835–37**: A 1,000-person ball for Stephen F. Austin at Brazoria; Sam Houston leads the grand march at the San
  Jacinto Victory Ball ([TSHA Folk Dance](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/folk-dance)).
- **1836**: By tradition Texans march into San Jacinto to "Will You Come to the Bower?"
- **1840**: Emil Heerbrugger gives violin recitals at the Capitol in Houston ([German Music](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/german-music)).
- **1851**: Czech emigration begins; Galveston is the port and Cat Spring the dispersal point ([Czechs](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/czechs)).
- **1865 / 1872**: Juneteenth at Galveston; Emancipation Park bought in Houston.
- **1883**: Houston Saengerbund. **1897**: Bellville's round hall.
- **1920–25**: Lead Belly at the Sugar Land prison farm.
- **1922**: Frenchtown organized. **1929**: Our Mother of Mercy, funded by la-la dances.
- **1933–39**: The Lomaxes record at Sugar Land and the Brazoria prison farms.
- **1934–36**: Western swing fiddle centers on Houston: Leon "Pappy" Selph's Blue Ridge Playboys, Cliff Bruner's Texas
  Wanderers, Shelly Lee Alley's Alley Cats.
- **1941**: Gold Star studio opens. **1946**: Harry Choates records "Jole Blon."
- **1947**: Square dance boom begins; HIFD founded; "Zolo Go" (about this year).
- **1949**: Peacock Records; Willie Green at Irene's Café; first square dance festival, 9,950 people.
- **1951**: Houston Folklore Society founded.
- **1952**: First rodeo trail ride (Salt Grass). **1956**: Blue Star Records. **1957**: Prairie View Trail Riders.
- **1959/60**: McCormick fixes the spelling "zydeco."
- **1961–66**: The Jester Lounge. **1962**: Silver Slipper opens (or 1952).
- **1964**: Chenier's "Zydeco Sont Pas Salé" at Gold Star.
- **1965**: Sand Mountain Coffee House, founded by two high-school seniors with $170; the Old Quarter opens downtown.
- **1968**: Folklore Society newsletter becomes *The Cotton Patch Rag*.
- **1969–70**: Anderson Fair. **1970**: KPFT signs on and is bombed twice.
- **1977**: Sand Mountain closes; MECA and Festival Chicano begin. **1978**: RSCDS branch; Men of Houston morris.
- **Late 1970s / 1980**: Contra arrives. **1979**: Pe-Te's Cajun BBQ House opens; the Old Quarter closes.
- **1982**: *Irish Aires*; Lydia Mendoza, the first Texan NEA Heritage Fellow; Lightnin' Hopkins dies.
- **Mid-1980s**: Irish session at the Red Lion. **1985**: NHCDS; the Hoedown is named.
- **1986**: Mike Head's contra series; BABA. **1987**: 20,164 square dancers in the Astrodome.
- **1988**: *Texas Monthly* documents the 11-parish zydeco church circuit.
- **1989–90**: HATDS; Accordion Kings & Queens; the Mucky Duck opens.
- **1992**: Millbend. **1993**: Shanty-Chor. **1994**: First contra weekend (Bayou Bedlam).

## Fiddle angle

**Tunes tied to the area:**

- **"Will You Come to the Bower?"**: by tradition the tune the Texans marched to at San Jacinto, inside Harris County
  ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/folk-music)).
- **"Houston"**: a D-major reel in DDad cross-tuning from Burl Hammons of West Virginia; it is not documented as being
  about the Texas city (medium confidence; [tunearch](https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Houston)).
- **"Midnight Special"**: the Lomaxes tied the train to Houston and the Sugar Land prison
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Special_(song))).
- **"Jole Blon," "Basile Waltz," "Lawtell Waltz," "Bayou Pon Pon"**: the Houston-recorded Cajun fiddle canon of Harry
  Choates, cut at Gold Star in 1946–47 ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/choates-harry-h)).
- **"Clemens Rag"** (Clemens State Farm, 1939) and **"Shorty George"** ([LOC](http://www.loc.gov/item/lomaxbib000547/)).
- TheSession has no tune titled Houston, Galveston, Brazos, Pasadena or Conroe.

**Styles:** Houston was a 1930s Gulf Coast **Western swing** fiddle center on KPRC and KXYZ radio. It sits on a
**Cajun/Creole fiddle** axis (Choates from Port Arthur, Frenchtown's la-la, Cedric Watson). Its **Irish** fiddle
centers on the Wednesday session and the Cruinniú. Its contest-fiddle culture lives elsewhere. A Texas-style jam was not
found anywhere in the metro.

**What the sessions play:** Hugh O'Connor's log (The Diplodocus, Banish Misfortune, The Star of Munster, The Mouse in
the Mug, Old Joe's, Castle Kelly) and HSOIM's semester sets (Tralee Gaol, Lord Mayo, the Boy in the Boat; slides like
the Peeler and the Goat). The old-time jam's lists run to Texas Gals, Texas Fair, L&N Rag and Cherry River Rag. The
Cotton Patch Rag prints "Cluck Old Hen." HATDS's Contra Dan's posts its tune files online.

## Character

**What is distinct:** Houston has no single folk scene. It has a Black and Creole dance world (zydeco), a white
suburban dance world (modern Western squares), a German and Czech rural dance world (polka halls), a small,
New England-rooted revival (contra, ECD, SCD, international), a bluegrass jam circuit, a Catholic-university Irish
cluster and a famous songwriter lineage. The most surprising crossings happen in buildings: First Lutheran's halls,
Roemer Hall at 607 E Whitney, and Miller Outdoor Theatre's accordion night.

**Who shows up:** The revival dances price aggressively for the young (contra $5 for ages 18–30, free under 18; HIFD
free to age 25; Bayou Bedlam $60 for ages 18–30), which suggests they are worried about age. Square dancing looks
older: a "Daytime Squares" club dances at 4:30 on Monday afternoons, which suggests a retired crowd, and the Hoedown fell
to 230. Zydeco crowds are
multigenerational and largely Black and Creole, organized through churches, riding clubs and fraternal orders. The
Irish scene skews to students and families of the Irish school. No attendance demographics are published anywhere.

**Geography:** Scattered, with three magnets. The inner loop (Midtown, Montrose, Rice Village, the Heights) has the
revival dances, three of the four Irish sessions within about 2.3 km, the listening rooms and the Sacred Harp. The NASA and
Clear Lake area has the Chelsea session, BABA, Saloon Door and an ECD group. Northwest Harris County (Cypress, Tomball,
Spring) has the bluegrass and square dance clubs. Zydeco centers on the Fifth Ward, northeast Houston, the Third Ward and
the south side. Polka is at the western rural edge. Katy, Sugar Land and Galveston are thin, except for Galveston's Old
Quarter.

**Costs:** Most participatory events cost $0–15. The dearest regular items are the $25 Millbend show and $15–20 Lodge
88 dances; weekends run $150.

**A visiting fiddler on a Tuesday:** BABA's bluegrass jam at Luka's BBQ near NASA (7–9 pm). Or, on the first Tuesday,
the folk society song circle at Hickory Pit in Bellaire, or the Old Quarter song circle in Galveston, if you have
original songs and no bodhrán. Tuesday also brings Scottish country dance at First Lutheran (7–9 pm) and Israeli dance at the ERJCC.

**On a Wednesday:** choose between the Irish session on the Mucky Duck's stage at 7:30 and the 6–8:30 pm bluegrass jam
at Hickory Pit in Bellaire.

**On a Saturday:** a 2nd- or 4th-Saturday HATDS contra at 7:30; otherwise zydeco at Jax Grill from 6:30 (a live band,
no published cover), then Club Menai late. In fall, add a parish bazaar. Head west for the first-Saturday Sealy dance.

**On a Sunday:** the HSOIM slow session at 2:30, Hugh O'Connor's at 4, and the old-time jam at Under the Volcano at 7
on 2nd and 4th Sundays, all within about 2.5 km. Alternatives are English country dance on 1st, 3rd and 5th Sunday
afternoons, Sacred Harp on the 4th Sunday at 2, or zydeco at Prospect Park or the Big Easy.

## Surprises

1. Zydeco has at least 21–30 listed dance dates a month, and Jax Grill's Friday and Saturday zydeco alone outnumbers the
   city's contra nights about three to one.
2. "Zydeco" was named and spelled in Houston.
3. At least 7 of 11 parishes on the 1988 church-dance circuit still hold zydeco events, organized by the Knights of
   Peter Claver.
4. One Knights of Columbus hall hosts contra, a trail-ride zydeco kickoff and Czech polka.
5. One Lutheran church hosts five traditions, about 15 events a month.
6. Square dancing fell from the Astrodome (20,164) to 230, yet still has the most club nights.
7. Sunday football evicted the Irish session from at least five pubs.
8. BABA stopped its shows after 40 years while its jams thrive.
9. A Houston session's tune log is public: 38 sessions, 96 tunes, and "The Diplodocus" on top.
10. Old-time arrived only around 2025, playing Texan rags.
11. No fiddle contest in the MSA; the "World Champion" festival is on Houston Avenue in another county.
12. "Jole Blon," the only Cajun Top 5 record, was cut in Houston and sold for $100 and a bottle of whiskey.
13. KPFT was bombed mid-"Alice's Restaurant," and Arlo Guthrie came to play it live.
14. The Sugar Land prison farm where the Lomaxes recorded is now a master-planned suburb.
15. Austin County has 7 of Texas's 18 round dance halls, one with its air conditioning under the floor.

Runners-up: a Plattdeutsch shanty choir in a city with no shanty sing; Sand Mountain founded by two high-school seniors
with $170; astronauts playing a Houston Cajun radio show in orbit; Millbend's audience voting on charities at
intermission; the Irish government part-funding a Houston workshop whose mascot is a banjo-playing armadillo.

## Data quality and caveats

- **Square dance (48.7/month)** is the listed-schedule ceiling. The HSRDC table (checked Oct 30, 2026) lists a weekly
  night for 11 clubs and one monthly club, but summer and holiday breaks are likely, and many nights are classes or
  workshops. A realistic figure is perhaps 40.
- **Heritage events (34.8/month)** were 66 in the naive merge, because zydeco and dance-hall records were
  double-counted across threads. They are now deduplicated. Zydeco figures rest on one promoter-fed calendar with capped
  pagination: the dance thread found at least 21 a month over 22 months, the heritage thread about 30 a month over 10
  months. Weekly rooms are under-listed there, so per-venue rates mix listings with venue claims (Big Easy Sundays rests
  on the venue's own site).
- **Not counted:** the Houston Accordion Association and Fort Bend Accordion Club (no dated evidence), Step N Class
  (undated), annual concerts (Kerrville showcase, Townes Van Zandt Wake, the Sängerfest, which are listed as festivals),
  BABA's cancelled show, and the announced but not yet started Club Menai Sunday.
- **Listening concerts** (HFMS, Millbend, Coffee House, house concerts, Gospel Songfest; about 4.6 a month) are in the
  total although they are not participatory.
- **Distinct traditions = 28**: 20 with a verified 2024–26 recurring participatory practice plus 8 with dated
  ensemble or festival activity. Cajun, danza azteca, Chinese orchestra, taiko, Brazilian, Vietnamese, Korean, Filipino,
  Garifuna, Hawaiian, klezmer, Scandinavian, son jarocho and shanty singing are excluded.
- **Contradictions noted:** Crockett festival month (the Handbook says June; 2026 is in November); Mucky Duck 1987 vs
  1990; Anderson Fair 1969 vs 1970; contra late 1970s vs 1980; Accordion Kings & Queens 1989 vs a 37th in 2026;
  Festival Chicano 1977 vs 1979; Greek Festival 1966 vs 1967; Silver Slipper 1952 vs 1962; the zydeco spelling 1959 vs
  1960; Chenier's move 1946/47 vs 1960s; Prairie View Trail Riders 1957/58 (and 1975); Club Menai on Homestead Ct vs
  Rd; Accordion Association 3rd vs 2nd Tuesday; contradancelinks' stale 1st/3rd Saturdays; and the Wikipedia claim that
  Houston hosted the 1951 National Folk Festival (the NCTA puts it in St. Louis).
- **Thin coverage:** private Facebook groups for the Irish, old-time and zydeco scenes; the Black gospel quartet
  anniversary circuit (real but uncounted); paywalled or blocked sources (Houston Chronicle, KHOU, UH Libraries,
  tunearch, Slippery-Hill, Smithsonian); rate-limited web search. Membership counts are unpublished except HIFD's (~75).
- **Not found** (probably real absences, but not proven): Scottish/Cape Breton, Cajun, Western swing, klezmer, Balkan or
  Scandinavian open sessions; Texas-style fiddle jams or contests in the MSA; a public shanty sing; a ballad group;
  Christian Harmony; cowboy poetry; son jarocho; a morris side.
