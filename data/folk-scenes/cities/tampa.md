# Tampa Bay: the folk scene of the Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro

*Folk-scene research for Nate, October 2026. The MSA (CBSA 45300, 3,418,895 people in 2025) is four counties: Hillsborough, Pinellas, Pasco and Hernando. Polk, Manatee, Sarasota, Citrus, Sumter, Orange and the White Springs country are outside it; anything there is flagged as outside. This dossier synthesizes four research threads (trad instrumental and dance; folk song and listening; heritage and diaspora; history, archives and the fiddle angle). Structured data is in `tampa.json`. No audio or video was played.*

## Scene in a nutshell

Tampa Bay's folk scene is broad, older-skewing and strongly seasonal, and its deepest roots are immigrant rather than Anglo. Tarpon Springs' Kalymnian Greeks have kept island violin, laouto and circle dancing alive since the 1905 sponge-diver migration. The first fiddler ever recorded inside the metro (WPA, 1939-40) was a Greek island violinist, and both of the MSA's NEA Heritage Fellows are Tarpon Greeks. Dunedin turned a 1957 gift of school bagpipes into a world-champion pipe band. Ybor City's 1880s cigar-worker clubs still stand, but their music has mostly gone; Puerto Rican bomba, Cuban dance classes and Caribbean carnival carry the Latin side now. For a fiddler, the weekly reality is about 26 bluegrass and 13 old-time jams a month, many held in daylight at senior centers, libraries and breweries; one host's Sunday circuit of four Irish sessions; and two contras. There is no folk society, but WMNF radio (1979), the Will McLean Foundation's festival ranch and house concerts hold the song scene together. Everything swells from November to April.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **The metro's first recorded fiddle is Greek.** In August 1939 and May 1940, WPA crews recorded Louis Peronis on violin, George Kaletizis on laouto and Harry Perris on santouri in Tarpon Springs, playing a [Halki sousta](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000334/) and a ["Kalymnos Island native dance"](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000336/). When the same program recorded Florida cracker fiddlers in 1940, it did so in [Sebring](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000381/), outside the metro. The violin line survives today in Michalis Kappas, who plays Epiphany-week taverna nights with laouto player Paddy League.
2. **A public school made a world-champion pipe band.** A gift set of bagpipes for the new Dunedin Highland Junior High in 1957, arranged through newspaper owner Roy Thomson, grew into the City of Dunedin Pipe Band. It won the [Grade 2 World Championship in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Dunedin_Pipe_Band_(United_States)) and the Grade 1 North American title at Maxville in 2019, after the 78th Fraser Highlanders had been wrongly announced as winners. Its pipe major also runs the bagpipe shop on Main Street.
3. **One person carries Irish music.** Jessica Conlan hosts every Irish session in the metro: Molly Malone's (1st and 3rd Sundays), the Irish Pub on 4th (2nd) and Flanagan's in Dunedin (4th), all at 5pm ([thesession 3444](https://thesession.org/sessions/3444)). Every session that died here was ended by a pub owner, including Four Green Fields, the thatched pub that cut its sessions in 2023 "after decades" ([thesession 6499](https://thesession.org/sessions/6499)).
4. **Jams by daylight.** Old-timers play Wednesdays at 11am under an oak on the Pinellas Trail sidewalk in Dunedin ([Tampa Bay Times](https://www.tampabay.com/photos/2025/02/23/trailside-jammers-pinellas-trail-dunedin-old-time-music/)). Bluegrass meets on Saturday mornings in a library gazebo and Saturday afternoons in a Lutheran church, and the Elfers Senior Center jam has run "more than 30 years" with a free lunch for players over 60 ([Tampa Bay Times, Oct 2026](https://www.tampabay.com/photos/2026/10/23/pasco-county-elfers-senior-center-music-jam/)).
5. **Florida's old-time championship was born in rural Pasco.** The Florida Old Time Music Championship ran 33 years (1982-2015) at the Pioneer Florida Museum in Dade City ([FOTMC](https://www.fotmcinc.com/our-story)). Co-founder Jim Strickland, "Mr. Ethnic," began programming old-time music on WMNF in 1980 and still runs the Barking Dogs jams at a railroad depot in San Antonio, Pasco.
6. **Jim Morrison's first stage was a folk coffeehouse in Pinellas Park.** At Tom Reese's Beaux Arts, a teenage Morrison read poetry with his back to the audience, Gamble Rogers and Will McLean played, and Reese threw Jack Kerouac out for drinking from a flask ([St. Pete Catalyst](https://stpetecatalyst.com/vintage-st-pete-tom-reese-and-the-beaux-arts-gallery/)).
7. **Sacred Harp at the State Fair.** A Cooper-book singing is held every February inside Cracker Country, the State Fair's 1898 living-history village ([listing](https://shapenotesingings.com/singings/florida-state-fair-singing)). The metro's other regular singing is at a rural Primitive Baptist church on the Polk County line.
8. **Sacred steel was "discovered" in St. Petersburg.** In 1992 state folklorist Bob Stone found House of God churches around St. Pete using steel guitars in worship, which led to Arhoolie's *Sacred Steel* series ([Arhoolie](https://archive.arhoolie.org/collections/robert-stone-sacred-steel-collection)). The style's pioneer, Willie Eason, lived in St. Pete from 1986 until his death in 2005.
9. **A folk foundation owns its own festival ground.** The 80-acre Sertoma Youth Ranch between Brooksville and Dade City, conceived in 1973 by a state policeman, an insurance agent and an auto-parts store owner, passed to the Will McLean Foundation in 2022 ([ranch history](https://floridasandmusicranch.com/about/)). It now hosts the McLean festival, Thanksgiving bluegrass and a monthly bluegrass campout.
10. **The snowbirds are named.** Session listings welcome "travelers, snowbirds, and other Floridia[n] session-ers"; Scottish country dancing and polka run only November to April; and every one of the 21 Tampa-area southern gospel concerts on a 2026-27 calendar falls between November and March ([gospel calendar](https://www.socalsoutherngospelconcerts.com/Florida.html)). Yet a PEI sister city, a Blue Jays spring camp and a 1,100-member Newfoundland society produce no Maritime fiddle session that anyone could find.

## Trad instrumental and dance

### Irish: a Sunday circuit and a graveyard

**Current (October 2026): three sessions, four a month, all on Sunday evenings, one host.**

| When | Where | Since |
|---|---|---|
| 1st and 3rd Sun, 5-8pm | Molly Malone's, Davis Islands, Tampa | 2013 |
| 2nd Sun, 5-8pm | The Irish Pub on Fourth, north St. Pete | 2020 |
| 4th Sun, 5-8pm | Flanagan's Irish Pub, Main St, Dunedin | 2024 |

Jessica Conlan ("celticmermaid" on thesession) plays flute, whistle, hammered dulcimer and fiddle, and hosts all three ([3444](https://thesession.org/sessions/3444), [7137](https://thesession.org/sessions/7137), [9004](https://thesession.org/sessions/9004)). The Molly Malone's listing describes "a solid core of experienced regulars and often a rotating cast of travelers, snowbirds, and other Floridia[n] session-ers"; the Flanagan's listing says "intermediate-advanced session, beginners more than welcome." The Irish Pub on 4th session lapsed when new owners Richie and Jill Hamm took over McArthur's after the 2024 hurricanes ([I Love the Burg](https://ilovetheburg.com/the-irish-pub-on-fourth-opens/)); its revival rests on the host's September 2026 comment, and the pub's own calendar does not list it. Molly Malone's also has a 1st-Tuesday bluegrass jam ("acoustic traditional bluegrass instruments only please," [bulletin](https://mollysbluegrass.otddi.com/)), so one Davis Islands pub hosts both traditions.

The **graveyard** on thesession.org explains why the circuit is so thin, and it has a single cause:

- **Bridie Gannon's, Tarpon Springs (2005).** A new owner "doesn't care much for sessions or anything Celtic": "Where we use to sit and play there are now electronic dart boards, jukeboxes" ([1055](https://thesession.org/sessions/1055)).
- **Four Green Fields, Platt Street (by 2006 to 2020).** The pub opened in 1992 as "the country's only authentic thatched roof Irish pub" ([Creative Loafing](https://www.cltampa.com/food-drink/tampa-irish-pub-four-green-fields-is-closing-its-curtis-hixon-park-location-20153732/)) and closed in December 2020 ([1249](https://thesession.org/sessions/1249)).
- **James Joyce Irish Pub, Tampa (2013).** "Never got off the ground" because of the owner ([3303](https://thesession.org/sessions/3303)).
- **Strandhill Public House, Brandon (2021).** "On hiatus until after football season, per the owner," and never back ([7177](https://thesession.org/sessions/7177)).
- **Four Green Fields at Curtis Hixon Park (2018-2023) and Channelside (2022-2023).** In August 2023 the company "decided to try a new approach to its business which included eliminating the traditional Irish music session" ([6499](https://thesession.org/sessions/6499), [7489](https://thesession.org/sessions/7489)).

**Around the edges.** No Comhaltas branch exists in the MSA; CCÉ North America places Florida in its Mid-Atlantic region and the known branch meets in Delray Beach ([CCÉ NA](https://www.ccenorthamerica.org/regions/)). No set-dance or céilí class turned up. Irish *step* dance is healthy: the Scariff School, led by former *Lord of the Dance* lead Stephen Scariff, had three Tampa Bay studios in 2024 ([Scariff](https://www.scariffschool.com/florida)), and St. Petersburg hosts two feiseanna, the Tampa Bay Feis (October) and Feis on the Gulf, the Florida State Championships (January) ([FeisMonitor](https://www.feismonitor.com/feis/tampa-bay-feis-2026/86ddfd48-3109-45e9-b1d4-b1a541ef3673/)). Dunedin-based **Tampa Bay Irish, Inc.** presents concerts in the 1888 Andrews Memorial Chapel and a January **Moonshine Music Fest** (3rd annual, Jan 13-16, 2027) ([tampabayirish.org](https://tampabayirish.org/)). The deepest Irish fiddle link is historical: Donegal fiddler **Edward J. Keeney** (1922-2009), from Gleann Domhain near Ardara, where John Doherty played in his family's kitchen, retired to the Tampa Bay area (New Port Richey per search summaries), taught state Irish-fiddle apprenticeships and won a [1999 Florida Folk Heritage Award](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/edward-j-keeney/) ([Donegal Fiddle Music](https://donegalfiddlemusic.ie/edward-keeney-1922-2009/)).

### Scottish and Dunedin: piping yes, fiddling no

Dunedin, a city of about 36,000 named by Scots J.O. Douglas and James Somerville after Edinburgh's Gaelic name ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin,_Florida)), is "the Scotland of Florida." Its sister cities are Stirling (1964) and **Summerside, Prince Edward Island** (2000) ([City of Dunedin](https://www.dunedin.gov/Community/Arts-Culture/Scottish-History-of-Dunedin)). The Dunedin Scottish Arts Foundation says it plainly: "A function in Dunedin is not complete without a Piper!" ([DSAF](https://www.dunedinsafoundation.com/history/)).

How the pipeline works:

- **Schools.** When Dunedin Highland Junior High opened in 1957, a reporter (later mayor) arranged gift bagpipes from Scotland through Roy Thomson, and newly arrived piper Matt Forsythe, "a piper extraordinaire who just moved to Dunedin from Scotland," taught the students. Today Dunedin Highland Middle and Dunedin High (the Scottish Highlander Band) each field pipe bands.
- **City band.** The City of Dunedin Pipe Band (1964) began "as a way for kids to get together and play pipes and drums they had learned in local schools." It runs Grade 1, 3, 4 and 5 units; in December 2018 it was ["the only Grade 1 band in the United States"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Dunedin_Pipe_Band_(United_States)), and its Grade 4 band were 2026 EUSPBA Southern Branch champions.
- **Leadership.** Sandy Keith (1937-2012), a Paisley-born pupil of Peter MacLeod Sr., led the program from 1982 to 2011 and "taught hundreds of pipers" ([Piping Press](https://www.pipesdrums.com/article/Sandy-Keith-1937-2012/)). Iain Donaldson, 23 years with Toronto's 78th Fraser Highlanders and then Shotts and Dykehead, has led since 2011; he is pipe major, the city's Director of Piping and director of both school bands, and with his wife Moira owns Lothian Kilt Rentals & Bagpipe Supplies on Main Street ([DSAF](https://www.dunedinsafoundation.com/about/)). Drumming director Eric MacNeill came from Oran Mor and Simon Fraser University.
- **Money.** The Dunedin Scottish Arts Foundation began as the Highland Games committee in 1967, incorporated in 1975 and renamed in 2015. Suncoast United Pipes & Drums (2022) is a separate adult band ([site](https://suncoastunitedpd.com/)).
- **Public moments.** City band rehearsals are ["open to the public"](https://www.cityofdunedinpipeband.com/join-us/) (email first). From October 10, 2026, a band piper plays "Piper on the Pier" at sunset every Friday and Saturday at the Dunedin Marina, ending with "Amazing Grace" ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/cityofdunedinpipeband/)).

The **Dunedin Highland Games & Festival** (April 3, 2027) opens with a Friday pipe band and clan march down Main Street and includes the Florida Open Highland Dancing Championships; past fields have run to "more than 120 solo piping, up to 500 members in pipe bands, over 100 Highland dancers" ([DSAF](https://www.dunedinsafoundation.com/highland-games/competitions/)). A Dunedin Celtic Festival follows on Nov 21, 2026.

**Scottish country dance.** The RSCDS-affiliated **Dunedin Scottish Country Dancers** began in 1980 within the Scottish American Society and organized formally in 1995 ([Strathspey](https://my.strathspey.org/groups/group/978/)). They dance Sundays 7-9pm at the Society's hall, 917 Louden Ave, **only from mid-November to April**; the 2025 season opened Nov 16 ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/dunedinscd/)). The nearest other groups are Sarasota and Orlando. Isle of Skye Highland Dancers teach competitive Highland dance in the same hall ([iosdance.com](https://www.iosdance.com/)).

**The gap that matters to a fiddler:** no Scottish fiddle club, no Scottish session, and no Cape Breton, Maritime or Québécois gathering anywhere in the MSA. That is despite the PEI sister city, the Toronto Blue Jays training in Dunedin since 1977, a Newfoundland & Labrador Society of Florida that claims 50+ years and about 1,100 members ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/groups/2009674299347582/photos/)), and large francophone snowbird groups. The only online traces are a Newfoundland-born pub singer called "Newfie Joe" at the Irish Pub on 4th ([events](https://www.irishpubstpete.com/events)) and a 2010 home recording on thesession by "Clan Tampa" whose sets include "Mouth of the Tobique / The Caribou" and "Cape Breton Jig #2" ([recording](https://thesession.org/recordings/3557)). If kitchen parties happen in Pinellas mobile-home parks, they are not online.

### Old-time, bluegrass and fiddle contests

**Old-time: Pasco roots, Pinellas sidewalks.** Florida's old-time revival has its roots north and east of Tampa. Jim Strickland started playing in 1976 after a banjo class from "Cousin" Ernie Williams, a Dade City banjoist from Alabama's Sand Mountain ([Banjoquest](https://banjoquest.blogspot.com/2009/02/ernie-williams-on-banjoquestbully-of.html)); Florida Memory photos show the pair teaching an old-time banjo workshop at the 1978 Florida Folk Festival. Strickland's bands have names worth keeping: the **Hyde Park Hysterical Society String Band**, **Mr. Ethnic's Wednesday Night Fiddle Support Group** and the **Barking Dogs** ([bio](http://fotmc.org/mrethnic/index.html)). In 1982 Williams came home from winning the banjo contest at the Tennessee Valley Old Time Fiddlers Convention in Athens, Alabama, and with Strickland, Dennis Devine and Luther Rozar started the **Florida Old Time Music Championship** at Dade City's Pioneer Florida Museum. It ran until 2015. Its two special awards are named for Dade City elders: Ed "Doc" Flemming, a Saint Leo professor, and "Cousin" Thelma Boltin, emcee of the Florida Folk Festival, who together created a Heart of Florida Folk Festival in Dade City whose years no one could find ([FOTMC awards](https://www.fotmcinc.com/special-awards)). FOTMC Inc. revived the contest in 2022, but at Stephen Foster State Park in White Springs, outside the metro. In 2020 the Florida State Fiddlers Association gave Williams and Strickland its Tom Staley Award together ([FSFA](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/tom-staley-award/)).

Current old-time jams in the MSA (about 13 a month, only one in the evening):

- **Dunedin Trailside Old-Time Jam**, Wednesdays 11am-1pm under the oak in front of the Celtic Shop at 354 Main St, beside the Pinellas Trail. Host Linda Li also fiddles in the contra band Jenny and the Stray Dogs. Founded about 2017 ([Tampa Bay Times, Feb 2025](https://www.tampabay.com/photos/2025/02/23/trailside-jammers-pinellas-trail-dunedin-old-time-music/); [FSFA list, Feb 2026](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/regular-jams/)).
- **Barking Dogs**, Thursday nights 7-10 at the historic San Antonio railroad depot, and Saturday mornings 10-1 in the San Antonio gazebo, east Pasco. Contact Jim Strickland ([Jamfinder](https://www.jamfinder.org/jams/san-antonio-barking-dogs-old-time/)).
- **Will McLean Festival "Old-Time Gathering,"** a tent of jams and workshops all three days in March ([schedule](https://www.willmclean.com/music-festival/schedule-performer-list)).
- Unverified: **Dunedin Dulcimers**, Tuesdays 1-3pm at the Hale Senior Activity Center (no dated evidence).
- Just outside: the **Tampa Bay Old Time Jam**, 2nd Thursdays at Cottonmouth Southern Soul Kitchen in **Bradenton** (Manatee County, since Dec 2025) ([Jamfinder](https://www.jamfinder.org/jams/bradenton-tampa-bay-old-time-jam/)), plus dulcimer clubs in Bradenton and Citrus County.

**Bluegrass: senior centers, breweries, Plant City.** At about 26 jams a month, bluegrass is the biggest participatory music in the metro, and it splits into two worlds. One is **brewery and bar jams**: the Pickin' Splinters jam at Woodwright Brewing, Dunedin, Thursdays 7-10, hosted by Hal Meyers of the Bluegrass Pirates ([schedule](https://www.bluegrasspirates.com/)); "Witchgrass Wednesday" at the Ale and the Witch in downtown St. Pete ("This weekly live bluegrass jam music series runs every Wednesday Night 7-9pm," host Fil Pate, [site](https://www.thealeandthewitch.com/about/)); and Molly Malone's on 1st Tuesdays. The other is **community-room jams**:

- **Elfers Senior Center**, west Pasco, Wednesdays, "more than 30 years."
- **Margaree's and Sonny Oden's Combined Jam**, Saturdays 1pm at King of Kings Lutheran, Port Richey: country, bluegrass and gospel, $4 at the door, musicians free ([Best of Pasco, Aug 2026](https://bestofpasco.com/weekend-guide/margarees-and-sonny-odens-combined-jam-brings-bluegrass-and-gospel-to-port-richey-this-saturday/)).
- **The Thonotosassa Jam**, 2nd and 4th Saturdays at 10am in the branch library. On Oct 31, 2026: "We will play in the gazebo in the park next door. Make sure you BRING A CHAIR." Its online songbook runs from "Angeline the Baker" through "Seminole Wind" to a house tune, "Thonotosassa Jam Blues" ([bulletin](https://jaminfo.otddi.com/)). One organizer, Martin Beattie, built bulletin sites for this jam and the Molly's and Tuesday Tampa jams, the closest thing the region has to a jam calendar.
- **The Tuesday Tampa Jam** at a north Tampa boba tea house (7pm), which may be moving to Wednesdays (low confidence).
- **Plant City**: the Krazy Kup coffeehouse loft on 1st Saturdays ([Krazy Kup](https://www.krazykup.com/monthly-events)), plus an unverified 2nd-Saturday jam at Snellgrove's, a family restaurant since 1954.
- Unverified: a monthly beginner slow jam at Leaven Brewing in Riverview, and the **Bluegrass Bunch at Soggy Bottom**, a camping club that meets the first weekend of each month at Florida Sand Music Ranch ([site](https://soggybottombluegrass.weebly.com/)).

The ranch is the region's festival ground. Its spring bluegrass festival, founded in 1977, was "now the oldest bluegrass festival in the southeast" when Ted Lehmann wrote in 2017 ([blog](https://tedlehmann.blogspot.com/2017/03/sertoma-bluegrass-festival-march-23-26.html)); no 2026 edition could be found, so it is marked as on hiatus. The **Thanksgiving Bluegrass Festival** runs Nov 26-28, 2026 ($21.50-$154, [EMS](https://evansmediasource.com/events/)). Bluegrass organizations are weak: a "Tampa Bay Bluegrass Association" Facebook page describes a performing group, and the Sunshine State Bluegrass Association (Plant City, 2015) last promoted an event in November 2024. There is no member-based bluegrass society.

**Fiddle contests.** None run inside the MSA today. The earliest local evidence comes from Hernando County newspapers in the Library of Congress: in April 1931 the Brooksville PTA asked "all the old-time fiddlers in the county" to "tune up their instruments and get the stiffness out of their fingers" ([Brooksville Journal](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn95047246/1931-04-16/ed-1/?sp=1)), and in August 1938 Joe Budowiski of the Nobleton/Stafford area won first prize at a Lake City contest ([Brooksville Sun](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn98026438/1938-08-19/ed-1/?sp=7)). The Pioneer Florida Museum's Labor Day fiddle contest has evidence only from [2012](https://pioneerfloridamuseum.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=135). At the official state contest, always held outside the metro, MSA hometowns appear from 1990 to 2011 ([FSFA winners](https://flafiddlers.wordpress.com/florida-fiddle-contest-preregistration/official-florida-state-fiddle-contest-winners-by-year/)): Michael Woolfe of New Port Richey (Junior champion 1992, Contemporary 1994), Aubrey Haney and Trent Watkins of Brandon, and Ian Malinowski of Tarpon Springs (Rustic champion 2002, after a decade of placings including twin-fiddle wins with Kathy Aagard of Tampa). No fiddle contest was found at the State Fair, the Strawberry Festival or the Pasco County Fair.

### Contra, English country, squares, clogging and international dance

**Contra.** **Tampa Friends of Old-Time Dance (TFOOTD)** was incorporated on March 22, 1992 and is a CDSS group affiliate ([tampabaycontra.com](https://tampabaycontra.com/)). It runs two dances a month, both $10 ($5 under 21, free under 12), each opening with a half-hour lesson:

- **2nd Saturdays, 7-10:30pm**, Polish American Society hall, St. Pete's Old Southeast. Nov 14, 2026: Rafter Ringers (Orlando) with caller Robin Russell.
- **4th Sundays, 3:30-7pm**, the Town Apartments North rec center in St. Pete. Until at least 2024 this was the Gulfport Casino Ballroom dance ([July 2024](https://tampabaycontra.com/event/gulfport-casino-contra-dance-28-july-2024/)).

The [band and caller list](https://tampabaycontra.com/home/bands_callers/) is a who's who of local trad. Bill Dudley "has been an integral part of Tampa Bay Contra Dancing since its inception." President Sarah Mitchell plays flute, whistle, hammered dulcimer and concertina "and feet." Kathie Aagaard plays 5-string violin. Bands include Jenny and the Stray Dogs ("an old-time barn-dance groove"), Coral Riff, Kumquats At Largo and the Wind That Shakes the Barley. Most callers drive in from Gainesville, Orlando and Tallahassee; Joey Norton of Largo is the main local one. House rules: bring your own water bottle and avoid fragrances.

Two weekends: the **Florida Snow Ball** in January (photo archive back to 2004; cancelled 2021), which for 2027 books Engine Room (Noah VanNorstrand, Amy Englesberg, Kelsey Wells), River Road, and callers Seth Tepfer and Alexandra Deis-Lauby in the Boca Ciega High School gym in Gulfport ([floridasnowball.com](https://floridasnowball.com/)); and the September **Fall Ball**. The nearest other contra series are Gainesville and Orlando, 1.5 to 2.5 hours away ([TFOOTD's list](https://tampabaycontra.com/home/other-florida-dances/)).

**English country dance** appears only at the Fall Ball: in 2025 Seth Tepfer called ten ECDs to Coral Riff ([playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLIPI84KAossk-3kOvzsdCKgPf85ZQYAZw)). No standing ECD series, Regency society, morris, sword or rapper team was found.

**Modern western squares and rounds** outnumber contra roughly eight to one. The DANCERgram hub (updated Oct 8, 2026, [link](http://www.dancergram.com/a-00-WestCoastClubsLink.html)) shows about eight clubs and halls and about 16 dances a month: Promenade Squares (St. Pete, almost every Friday), Temple Twirlers (Tampa, 2nd and 4th Mondays), Bo's & Bell's (Largo, 3rd Thursdays), Live Lively (St. Pete, Tuesdays), Suncoast Squares, the Stardust Dance Center for rounds, the Tampa Bay Square Dance Revival, and **Strawberry Square** in Plant City. Strawberry Square bills itself as "the square dance capital of the South": an air-conditioned hall with a floating hardwood floor inside a residential park whose website also handles lot sales, rentals and background checks ([site](https://www.strawberrysquaredancing.com/)). Its January **Winter Fiesta** (Jan 29-30, 2027, callers Tony Oxendine and Jack Pladdys) includes camping. The *West Coast Dancer* association newsletter ran from 1968 to 2018.

**Clogging.** The Florida Clogging Council lists seven MSA clubs: Buckshot (Tampa), Clogging Connection and Strawberry Express (Plant City), East Coast Connection (Brandon), TR Traveling Shoes (Dade City), Grapevine (Dunedin) and Club House Cloggers (Clearwater) ([FCC](https://www.floridacloggingcouncil.com/clubs-groups)). None was individually verified. In 1992 St. Petersburg's Southern Exposure won a third straight state clogging title ([TBT archive](https://www.tampabay.com/archive/1992/04/13/clogging-team-wins-state-championship/)).

**International and Israeli folk dance.** Tampa International Folk Dancers meet Thursdays at the Kate Jackson Rec Center in Hyde Park (free). Israeli dance meets Sundays at Menorah Life in St. Pete and Tuesdays in Tampa ([FFDC directory](http://www.floridafolkdancer.org/places.html)). Tampa's Scandinavian dance listing reads only "Check for current status," which is why Kathie Aagaard's Hardanger fiddle and polskas have no regular dance home.

**Polka.** The Polish American Society (1951) runs a November-April season of live polka and big-band dances, about two a month, plus an IPA Accordion Fest ([schedule](https://polishsociety.org/index.php/en-us/)). Its Facebook says Sunday 2-5pm; the heritage thread's mapping of the 2025-26 dates says Saturday afternoons. Ask before driving over.

## Folk song and listening

**No folk society, no folk club.** As far as the research could find, a metro of 3.4 million has never had a long-lived membership folk organization of the kind Boston, Philadelphia or Seattle take for granted. The song scene runs on four things instead.

**1. Community radio.** [WMNF 88.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMNF) signed on September 14, 1979, built with door-to-door fundraising, with "Folk and Acoustic music in the morning … and Bluegrass in afternoon drivetime." Its folk hosts have been there almost from the start (see Radio below).

**2. The Will McLean world.** Will McLean (1919-1990), the "Father of Florida Folk Music" and "Black Hat Troubadour," wrote [over 3,700 songs and stories](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-artists-hall-of-fame/will-mclean/), played Carnegie Hall with Pete Seeger in 1963, and was called by Seeger "the greatest living songwriter in America today." His first instrument was "[a gourd and cornstalk fiddle with a horsehair bow](https://www.willmclean.com/will-mclean-foundation/about-will-mclean)." The foundation his friends formed runs:

- **The Will McLean Music Festival**, every March (Mar 12-14, 2027): "80 folk music groups performing on 3 covered stages," workshops, jam areas, a young musicians' program, the Best New Florida Song finalists, a Saturday-night contra dance and the Old-Time Gathering ([festival](https://www.willmclean.com/festival)). Camping is $20-40 a night.
- **The Best New Florida Song contest**, with 80 entries in 2026 and a Young Songwriter category from 2027 ([news](https://www.willmclean.com/news)). Florida songwriting here is expected to be *about Florida*.
- **[Sunday Sampler Tampa](https://www.willmclean.com/sunday-sampler-tampa)** at New World Brewery, usually the second Sunday, 1:30-3:30pm, $15-20, November to May. The 2026-27 lineup includes the American Song Box Trio, Doug Spears and the Florida Cracker Collective, Patchouli, Moors and McCumber and Grant Peeples. Its Facebook page has [279 followers](https://www.facebook.com/SundaySamplerTampa/), a sign of how small this world is.

The foundation's own pages disagree about when the festival began (1990, 1991 or 1992) and whether it sits in Brooksville or Dade City; the ranch at 85 Myers Rd is inside the MSA either way.

**3. House concerts and listening rooms.**

- **[Sunshine City House Concerts](https://undiscovered.music/venues/sunshine_city_house_concerts)**, northeast St. Pete, about monthly from late September to April, none in summer, by invitation (2026-27: Dean Johanesen, Friction Farm, Billy Jonas and others).
- **[Coastal Lifestyle House Concerts](https://undiscovered.music/venues/coastal_lifestyle_house_concerts)**, Dunedin: "$25 dollar donation — 100% goes to the artists to provide a living wage," BYOB, October-April (Daniel Neihoff, Celtic Conundrum, The Whispering Tree).
- **[Wiley House Concerts at MUSIC4LIFE](https://undiscovered.music/venues/wiley_house_concerts)**, Clearwater, year-round touring Americana (Shawn Mullins, Liz Longley, Griffin House, Will Hoge).
- **[The Palladium's Side Door Cabaret](https://mypalladium.org/events/the-writers-room-showcase/)**, St. Petersburg College, "the perfect listening room." In 2025 it hosted the **Wednesday Writers Room**, eight local writers who since January 2025 have each written a new song every two weeks "based on a random prompt pulled from a hat."
- **[Safety Harbor Art and Music Center](https://www.safetyharborartandmusiccenter.com/)**, a volunteer nonprofit founded by artist-songwriters Kiaralinda and Todd Ramquist, mixing roots concerts with folk-craft workshops.
- **[Skipper's Smokehouse](https://skipperssmokehouse.com/history/)**, the open-air "Skipperdome" under live oaks since 1980 (blues, reggae, zydeco, bluegrass, folk), "Best Blues Club in America" in 2001. It closed during COVID, then reopened in July 2021 after the community "spoken, screamed, begged, and pleaded" ([LocalShops1](https://www.localshops1.com/blog/skippers-smokehouse-is-back-and-better-than-ever-in-tampa)). Free Sunday music 1-4pm.

**4. A huge bar open-mic circuit.** Thomas Wesolowsky's [Tampa Bay Jams list](https://www.surfboardrecords.com/tampabayjams/) (Sept 10, 2026) has about 60 open mics and 37 open jams a week, 19 of the open mics on Wednesday and 2 on Saturday. St. Petersburg has about 20 entries and Tampa proper about 7. Most are bar and blues-rock nights; the nearest to folk are Gulfport's Monday "Acoustic Jam" at the North End Tap House, Hank's Guitar Shop in Clearwater (Sunday noon-4), Steep Station tea house in St. Pete (Sunday 8pm) and Safety Harbor's little Main Street cluster. The one documented song circle, a capped-at-12 Monday sing-along at an Eagles Lodge in Pinellas Park, stopped after July 2024 ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/community-acoustic-jam/)). The [NSAI St. Petersburg chapter](https://www.nashvillesongwriters.com/chapter/st-petersburg-fl) meets first Tuesdays at Outcast Brewing.

**Other rooms worth knowing.** **[Seven C Music & Coffee](https://www.sevencmusic.com/)** in St. Pete is a guitar shop and coffee bar run by David Hosler, who designed Taylor Guitars' Expression System pickup with Rupert Neve. **[Good Folk Fest](https://www.creativeclay.org/upcoming-events/2025/11/16/good-folk-fest-30th-anniversary-celebration)** (since 2007) pairs folk art with roots music each November. **WMNF's Tropical Heatwave** has filled Ybor's Cuban Club since 1982 ([2026](https://www.wmnf.org/wmnf_events/tropical-heatwave-2026/)), eclectic rather than folk.

## Traditional singing

**Sacred Harp: the Cooper book in rural Hillsborough.** Florida's shape-note heartland is the Panhandle. The Tampa MSA nonetheless has two regular Cooper-book singings and no urban Denson practice group:

- **Bethlehem Primitive Baptist Church**, 17385 Bethlehem Road, "Lithia (Old Chicora)," sings from *The Sacred Harp, Revised Cooper Edition* every fifth Sunday, about four times a year (next: Nov 29, 2026 and Jan 31, 2027) ([listing](https://shapenotesingings.com/singings/bethlehem-pbc-5th-sunday-singing); [Central Florida Shape-Note Singing](https://sites.google.com/view/orlandofasola)). It also hosts the Sunday all-day singing, "with dinner on the grounds," of the [Central Florida Sacred Harp Weekend](https://shapenotesingings.com/singings/central-florida-singing) each January (4th annual in 2027). One listing says "Mulberry," a Polk County town, but ZIP 33547 is Lithia and OpenStreetMap puts that stretch of road in Hillsborough, so it is counted inside.
- **The Florida State Fair Singing**, a Cooper-book singing each February (10:30am-12:30pm) inside Cracker Country, the fairgrounds' 1898 village of 13 relocated buildings. The 2025 edition was on Feb 8, run by Orlando organizer Rachel Speer ([event](https://www.facebook.com/events/644048804745954/)).

At the first Central Florida all-day singing in 2024, David Ivey "recounted his experiences singing in Central Florida in the 1960s" ([minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=%22central+florida+sacred%22)).

**Gospel: a winter touring circuit, not a community sing.** A [regional calendar](https://www.socalsoutherngospelconcerts.com/Florida.html) shows 223 Florida southern gospel listings from October 2026 to December 2027, 107 of them in January and February. All 21 Tampa-MSA dates fall between November and March, centred on Pasco churches (Fellowship Bible in Zephyrhills, Word of Life in Hudson), and some are weekday-morning "Senior Events." No participatory all-day singing culture turned up.

**Sacred steel.** In the House of God churches of the St. Petersburg area, steel guitar is a liturgical instrument (see Heritage). Visitors should ask first and treat any service as worship, not a concert.

**Cracker song and sea shanties.** Cracker and cow-hunter song lives through statewide performers such as [Benjamin Dehart, "The Cracker Tenor"](https://myfloridahistory.org/frontiers/article/83) (Sumter County, outside), rather than a local community repertoire. No shanty sing exists in this maritime metro; the shanty band booked for Tampa's 813 Day in 2026 cancelled, the promoter said, because it had "lost their compass and are lost at sea" ([Creative Loafing](https://www.cltampa.com/arts/tampas-813-day-celebrations-include-a-concert-from-giant-sea-shanty-ensemble/)).

## Heritage and diaspora traditions

### Tarpon Springs: the Kalymnian heart

Tarpon Springs has the highest share of Greek-Americans of any US city, ["more than one in ten residents"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarpon_Springs,_Florida). Bahamian and Key West sponge hookers came first, in the 1880s. In 1905 John Cocoris brought Greek helmet divers; men from Aegina and Hydra were soon outnumbered by islanders from Kalymnos, Symi and Halki, and by 1907 there was a parish. Panayotis League's ethnography explains why the culture stayed so intact: Kalymnians ["dominated the social, political, and economic life of Tarpon Springs"](https://open.bu.edu/items/d439c8ba-a303-4cae-ae3a-26c47e61a998) and could meet American society "from a position of relative power, without the immediate need to compromise linguistic, social, or occupational identity."

**The instruments.** For a fiddler, the key fact is that Dodecanese dance music is violin music. The 1939-40 WPA discs ([60 Tarpon items](https://www.loc.gov/collections/florida-folklife-from-the-works-progress-administration/?fa=location:tarpon+springs)) include "[Amaxas](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000253/)" with "fiddle, santouri (cymbal), and mandola," and the 1940 Philoptochos Society program, whose Kalymnian dance is "a large group of men and women dancing in a great circle, arms crossed, hands clasped and with slow, short steps." Today:

- **Violin.** Michalis Kappas, ["a violin player from the Island of Kalymnos"](https://greektampabay.com/greek-events/attend-music-of-the-dodecanese-islands-event-on-february-17th/), plays with Paddy League on laouto in "the style established by Giorgos Makrigiannis ('Nirsirios') and passed on to the current generation by Mikes and Manolis Tsounias" ([Levendia](https://levendiatarponspri.wixsite.com/wdc2017)). In January 2025 they played the Epiphany-week [Taverna Night at St. Nicholas Community Center](https://www.facebook.com/epiphanytarpon/posts/epiphany-celebration-schedule-2025-join-us-for-one-of-tarpon-springs-most-cheris/997835289056700/) with singer Manolis Houlis; two nights later the Agios Panteleimon Kalymnian Society held its own at 42 W Morgan St. In March 2025 the library brought Greek violinist [Kyriakos Gouventas](https://exploretarponsprings.com/event/sounds-of-greece/) to "illuminate the complexity and power of the Greek violin."
- **Paddy League** is the bridge: a Tallahassee-based musicologist and laouto, violin and tsambouna player who taught a 2019 state apprenticeship in "Greek Kalymnian Music & Oral Poetry" to two Tarpon apprentices ([list](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/apprenticeship-program/apprenticeship-participants/)) and toured Florida libraries in 2025 with ["Golden Harvest: The Music and Traditions of the Greek Spongers of Tarpon Springs"](https://floridahumanities.org/speaker-directory/golden-harvest-the-music-and-traditions-of-the-greek-spongers-of-tarpon-springs/).
- **Tsambouna.** Nikitas Tsimouris (born 1927 on Kalymnos, in Tarpon from 1968) learned the island bagpipe from his father "while they watched their sheep," worked sponge boats and stucco, and was custodian of St. Nicholas. His double chanter used "two bamboo reeds" tuned with "blobs of beeswax," pacing "complex seven- and 13-beat rhythms" with the dancers; in Florida his music ["is considered antiquated by many"](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/nikitas-tsimouris). He became the MSA's first NEA National Heritage Fellow in 1991. No resident tsambouna player was found performing in 2024-26.
- **Bouzouki, outi, lyra.** State apprenticeships ran in bouzouki (Spiros Skordilis 1987, George Soffos 2011, Leonidas Zafiris 2014), outi (Zafiris 2017-18), laouto (Nicholas Mastras 2005) and Cretan lyra (Konstantinos Maris of New Port Richey, 2007, who also builds lyras). Working bands include Ellada and John Demas & the Omega Project.

**Dance is the healthiest strand.** **Levendia**, founded in 1979 by John Lulias, still teaches lessons at the street dinner [A Night in the Islands](https://downtowntarpon.com/events/a-night-in-the-islands/) (Nov 15, 2025). The **St. Nicholas Dance Ministry** fields age-group teams; in January 2026 its "Asteria" team won the folk-expression and costume awards for the Kalymnian "Afousis" at the Hellenic Dance Festival in New Orleans ([Plato Academy](https://platoacademy.net/tarpon-springs/hellenic-dance-festival-2026/)).

**The ritual calendar.** Epiphany on January 6 is the big one: the 120th cross dive in 2026 had 74 divers in Spring Bayou ([Fox 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/tarpon-springs-annual-epiphany-day)), and Athos Karistinos caught the cross, as his father had in 1991 ([Greek Reporter](https://greekreporter.com/2026/01/06/tarpon-springs-celebrate-milestone-epiphany-historic-cross-dive/)). If 2026 was the 120th and 2006 the 100th, the series began around 1907, not the oft-quoted 1903. Epiphany week, with its taverna nights, ball and glendi, is the best time to hear island violin. Add the Greek Independence parade (4th Sunday of March) and five parish festivals: St. Stefanos in St. Pete (February), Clearwater GreekFest, St. George in New Port Richey (43rd, November 2026), the Tampa Greek Festival (since 1981; cancelled after Hurricane Milton in 2024, [Osprey Observer](https://www.ospreyobserver.com/2025/10/opa-tampa-greek-festival-returns-strong-after-hurricane-recovery/)) and Night in the Islands.

**The crafts.** Tarpon's second NEA Fellow is copper diving-helmet maker [Nicholas Toth](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/nicholas-toth) (2003). George Saroukos is described as the ["only remaining builder of traditional Greek sponge diving boats in the Western Hemisphere"](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/george-saroukos/).

### Ybor City and West Tampa: the clubs outlived their music

Ybor City was founded in 1886 by cigar makers leaving Key West, and it grew around [mutual-aid clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ybor_City) that were hospital, insurance plan, library and ballroom in one: Centro Español (1891), L'Unione Italiana (1894), the Cuban club (1899, renamed Círculo Cubano in 1902), La Unión Martí-Maceo for Afro-Cubans barred from it under Jim Crow (1900), and [Centro Asturiano](https://www.centroasturianotampa.org/history) (1902), whose 1914 theater staged zarzuela so well that a Spanish theater magazine kept a correspondent in Ybor. On June 21, 1939, Herbert Halpert recorded the Cuban Club's band: a [punto guajiro](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000046/) sung by Estela Echezabal, and drummer Ramón Bermúdez's ["Five Cuban Drum Rhythms"](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000054/) (son, rumba, bembé, conga and maní).

All five clubs survive, mostly as historic buildings and rental halls. In December 2025 they held a joint ["grand Piquiniqui"](https://centroespanoltampa.org/2025/11/28/november-2025-update/) for Ybor's 140th. The telling detail is the Asturian bagpipe: the Centro Asturiano [remembers its late gaitero](https://www.facebook.com/CentroAsturianodeTampa/posts/1237390344671150/), and for Día de Asturias 2024 it hired a Highland-trained piper to play the anthem on gaita. **Fiesta Day**, born as a cigar workers' "verbena or day of rest," reaches its 80th edition on Feb 20, 2027 as a food-and-crafts street fair ([Ybor Chamber](https://members.ybor.org/events/details/80th-fiesta-day-2027-5981)). Cuban tradition is participatory in dance class rather than in the clubs: [Yusniel Garcia](https://www.yusnielgarcia.com/) teaches casino and rueda Wednesdays at 8pm ($20), and Casino Revo and SalsaSon teach rumba and son. No open community rumba with drummers was found.

### The newer Latin and Caribbean Tampa

- **Puerto Rican bomba and plena** is the liveliest Afro-Latin participatory tradition. The free [Festival de Bomba y Plena en Tampa](https://boricuacom.blogspot.com/2025/07/festival-de-bomba-y-plena-en-tampa.html) reached its 10th edition on Oct 19, 2025, where "everyone participates in playing instruments (panderos, barriles, guiros…) and singing along" ([Floricua](https://floricuanews.com/local/bomba-for-beginners-where-to-learn-how-to-drum-or-dance-along/)). Bomba Body Dance & Drumming Co. (CuKiAra, 2007) teaches Sunday classes; Pasco hosts Parrandón Fest at Christmas.
- **Mexican folklórico.** [Grupo Folklórico Mahetzi](https://www.wfla.com/hidden-history/hispanic-heritage-month/a-mission-to-preserve-mexican-folkloric-dance-in-tampa-bay/) in Clearwater (about 2010) had about 60 children rehearsing twice a week; founder Mere Pioquinto lays concrete floors by day. No son jarocho fandango or folklórico group was found in the farmworker towns of Wimauma, Ruskin or Plant City.
- **Caribbean.** The [Tampa Bay Caribbean Carnival](https://tampabaycaribbeancarnival.com/) (20th in April 2025) parades to Perry Harvey Sr. Park on the old Central Avenue corridor; the Tampa Bay Steel Orchestra (St. Pete, 2003) plays calypso and soca. [Tampa Haitian Flag Day](https://haitiantimes.com/2025/05/19/haitians-in-tampa-bay/) is new ("only the second edition" in 2025). Bahamians mark independence with imported junkanoo groups.
- **Others active:** [PhilFest](https://pcfitampa.org/philfest-2025-entertainment-schedule/) with the Musikong Kawayan Bamboo Ensemble; **Sangeetaalaya** (2013), which teaches Carnatic vocal and violin; the Festival Colombiano; a Serbian folklore dance group at St. Basil of Ostrog.

### African American traditions

**Sacred steel** is the surprise. Robert Stone found steel guitars in ["several predominantly African American House of God churches… in the St. Petersburg area"](https://www.floridamemory.com/items/show/241286) in 1992; his archive (about 6,500 photos and 88 interviews) is being digitized with a 2024-25 NEA grant ([Arhoolie](https://archive.arhoolie.org/collections/robert-stone-sacred-steel-collection)). Willie Eason ("Little Willie and His Talking Guitar") settled in St. Pete in 1986; his Folk Heritage citation says Florida has ["more than 40 House of God churches, more than any other state"](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/willie-claude-eason/). Which St. Pete congregation plays steel today was not verified. **Blues** is mostly memory: Tampa's [Central Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Avenue_(Tampa)) was razed for the interstate, and the Cotton Club closed in 1974. **Street cries** survived in St. Pete's Tommy Walton (1930-1997), whose rhymed hot-dog cries at Cardinals spring training ["blended blues and gospel lyrics"](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/folk-heritage-awards/list-of-past-recipients/tommy-e-walton/).

### European clubs and older colonies

The **Polish American Society** (1951) has Thursday nights where guests may "try their hand at playing the accordion" ([I Love the Burg](https://ilovetheburg.com/st-pete-polish-american-society-reopening/)) and its winter polka season. The **German-American Society of Pinellas County** (also 1951) holds Saturday band dances and has the Gulf Coast Enzianer Schuhplattler Verein ([site](https://germantampabay.com/)). Hernando's Slovak colony of **Masaryktown** (1924-26) was recorded by the WPA in 1939 ([9 items](https://www.loc.gov/collections/florida-folklife-from-the-works-progress-administration/?fa=location:masaryktown)), but its festival is gone ([NatureCoaster](https://naturecoaster.com/masaryktown/)). Seminole medicine man **Bobby Henry** led stomp dances at the Florida Folk Festival; in 2014 the Rays brought him to Tropicana Field as a ["rainmaker"](https://www.tampabay.com/archive/2014/06/10/medicine-man/). No public Tampa Bay powwow was verified.

## Instruction

- **Dunedin public-school piping and drumming** (since 1957): middle school band, Dunedin High's Scottish Highlander Band, then the city band, with a full-time drumming director and DSAF scholarships.

- **State apprenticeships:** the Florida Folklife Apprenticeship Program has funded about 30 Tampa Bay pairs since 1984, from tsambouna and diving helmets to Scottish bagpipe (Sandy Keith, 1987), Highland dance (2004), old-time banjo (Dale Webber of Odessa, 1990) and Irish fiddle (Edward Keeney, 1997-98 and 2006-07) ([list](https://dos.fl.gov/cultural/programs/florida-folklife-program/apprenticeship-program/apprenticeship-participants/)).

There is no folk music school, no Irish music class series and no old-time fiddle class.

**[Radio St. Pete](https://radiostpete.com/)** (internet streams plus Sunshine 96.7 FM downtown) courts "'snowbirds' living here or up north." Its flagship, the ***Florida Folk Show – Live*** (Saturdays 10am-noon, founded by Pete Gallagher), broadcasts before a live audience from the Craftsman House Gallery and marked "Twenty-five years on public radio" in 2026; *High Country Celtic* and *Get to the Roots* round it out. In print, Charlie Souza wrote *Tampa Bay Music Roots*, and Jeff Klinkenberg's "Real Florida" columns in the *Tampa Bay Times* earned a [2018 Florida Folk Heritage Award](https://www.pressreleasepoint.com/secretary-state-announces-recipients-2018-florida-folk-heritage-awards).

- **1944** Charlie Brantley forms the Original Honey Dippers in West Tampa; Ray Charles plays with them around 1947.

- **1953** First Florida Folk Festival, White Springs (outside).
- **1957** Gift bagpipes start Dunedin's school pipe band.
- **c.1961** Jim Morrison reads poetry at Reese's coffeehouse.
- **1964** City of Dunedin Pipe Band founded; Stirling becomes a sister city.
- **1967** Dunedin Highland Games committee formed.
- **1968** Tsambouna player Nikitas Tsimouris arrives from Kalymnos.
- **1974** The Cotton Club, last business on Central Avenue, closes.

- **1979** WMNF signs on (Sept 14); Florida Folklife Program created; Levendia founded.

## The fiddle angle

**Local styles.** Three fiddle lines meet here, and none of them is a distinctive "Tampa style":

1. **Dodecanese Greek island violin**, the oldest documented (Louis Peronis, 1939-40) and still alive in Michalis Kappas' "Nirsirios" style. It plays syrtos, sousta and Kalymnian circle dances with laouto, and its island repertoire makes it the most distinctive fiddling in the metro.
2. **Old-time and contest fiddling**, centred on east Pasco and Hernando: the 1930s Brooksville contests, the Dade City championship (1982-2015), the Barking Dogs and Trailside jams, and 1990s state champions from New Port Richey, Brandon and Tarpon Springs. The best Florida cracker fiddling, such as G. G. Albritton with C. W. O'Berry beating "straws" ("foot-long fiddle sticks whittled out of fat pine") in [Sebring, 1940](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000381/), was recorded just outside the metro, as were Goose Culbreath of Cortez and Richard Seaman, Vassar Clements and Chubby Wise.
3. **Celtic and contra fiddling**: Kathie Aagaard (who also plays Hardanger fiddle and Swedish polskas), Linda Li, Jessica Conlan, the memory of Edward Keeney's Donegal fiddle, and whatever the snowbirds bring.

**Tunes and songs named for or made in the area.** No fiddle tune named for Tampa, Ybor, Pinellas, Tarpon or Suwannee was found on [The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/search?q=Tampa&format=json); TuneArch blocked automated searches. The list instead:

- **Dodecanese dance tunes recorded in Tarpon, 1939-40:** "Halkiticos, Sousta," "Kalymnos Island native dance," "Pentozali," "Kalamatianos," "Syrtos Politicos," "Maroulli" and "Amaxas" ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000334/)).
- **Cuban songs and rhythms recorded in Ybor, 1939:** punto guajiro, guabina and the "Five Cuban Drum Rhythms."
- **"Big Sponge Boy"** (John K. Gianaros), a Greek-American song about the Tarpon divers; no recording located.
- **"Helleno-Americanike Nea Genia"** ("Greek-American New Generation"), recorded in Tarpon in 1939 ([LOC](http://www.loc.gov/item/flwpa000236/)).
- **"Thonotosassa Jam Blues,"** the house tune in a local bluegrass jam's songbook ([bulletin](https://jaminfo.otddi.com/)).
- **"St. Pete Florida Blues,"** credited to Ray Charles, and Hank Ballard's "The Twist," said to be inspired by dancers on Central Avenue (both per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Avenue_(Tampa)) only).
- **"The More We Get Together,"** anthem of the Tampa-founded Tin Can Tourists.
- **Florida-named tunes from outside the metro:** "Collier in the Scrub," "High Puppies" and "Hug 'em in Sebring" (Sebring, 1940); Fiddlin' Arthur Smith's ["Florida Blues"](https://www.slippery-hill.com/content/florida-blues) (1937); "Orange Blossom Special" (1938); "Florida Rag."
- **Near-misses:** "[Dunedin Valley](https://thesession.org/tunes/12637)" is named for Dunedin, *Ontario*; "[The Clearwater](https://thesession.org/tunes/8703)" has no Florida link; "Hillsborough Castle" is in County Down; "[The Gulf of Mexico](https://thesession.org/tunes/11178)" is a version of "The Eighth of January."

## Character

**What makes it distinct.** Three things set Tampa Bay apart from most big metros:

- Its deepest folk traditions are **diasporas that held power**: Kalymnian Greeks who ran their town, and Ybor's club-builders.
- **Public folklore did much of the documenting.** The 1939-40 WPA trips, the state folklorist who found sacred steel in 1992, decades of apprenticeships, and a city-employed folklorist in Tarpon.
- Its Anglo-American trad scene is **retiree-powered and seasonal**: daytime jams, a dozen snowbird-season calendars, and squares that outnumber contras.

The signature traditions are Greek island music and dance, Dunedin piping, bluegrass and old-time jamming, and Florida-about-Florida songwriting.

**Seasonality.** These all run roughly November to April: Scottish country dance, polka season, house concerts (Sunshine City, Coastal Lifestyle), the Sunday Sampler, the gospel tour, Piper on the Pier, the Snow Ball, Epiphany, the McLean festival and the Highland Games. Wiley House Concerts, Skipper's and most jams run year-round. The southwest Florida square dance newsletter publishes "Weekly In Season (Oct-Apr)." No headcounts exist to show how much the jams swell in winter.

**Who shows up.** No published demographics exist. The signs point older: jams in senior centers at 11am, a free lunch for players over 60, weekday-morning "Senior Events," and Radio St. Pete courting snowbirds. A Beaux Arts regular's verdict still fits: its crowd was "retirees. Or young people who appreciated being there, but just didn't have the money." Contra pushes against this, with a youth price, a lesson at every dance and young callers. The open mics and the Latin, Caribbean and Greek youth dance groups are much younger.

**Geography.** The scene is scattered, and Molly Malone's to the San Antonio depot is about 40 miles.

- **Pinellas** has most of the dance, Celtic and song life: Dunedin (pipes, Scottish dance, Flanagan's, the Trailside jam, Woodwright), St. Pete and Gulfport (contra, polka, house concerts, open mics, Witchgrass), Tarpon Springs (Greek) and Safety Harbor.
- **Tampa proper** has surprisingly little trad: one Irish session, a couple of bluegrass jams, squares, international dancers, WMNF, Ybor and the State Fair.
- **East Hillsborough and Pasco** carry bluegrass and old-time (Plant City, Thonotosassa, Elfers, Port Richey, San Antonio, Dade City).
- **Hernando** has the festival ranch, and **rural southeast Hillsborough** the Sacred Harp church.

**Costs.** Contra $10; house concerts about $25 (donation); Sunday Sampler $15-20; Cuban class $20; the Port Richey jam $4. Sessions, jams, open mics, singings and most heritage festivals are free.

**A visiting fiddler's week:**

- **Tuesday (thin):** Dunedin Dulcimers at 1pm (unverified), the Tuesday Tampa Jam at 7pm (may have moved), or on 1st Tuesdays bluegrass at Molly Malone's and the NSAI songwriters at Outcast Brewing. There is no Tuesday Irish session. The honest alternative is an open mic, where a fiddle tune is a novelty.
- **Wednesday (the best weekday):** old-time under the oak on Main Street, Dunedin at 11am; the Elfers Senior Center jam; Witchgrass Wednesday bluegrass at the Ale and the Witch, 7-9pm; or Cuban casino class at 8pm.
- **Thursday:** Pickin' Splinters bluegrass at Woodwright Brewing (Dunedin) or the Barking Dogs old-time jam at the San Antonio depot, 7-10pm. In Dunedin you can also watch the Grade 1 pipe band rehearse (email first).
- **Saturday:** 10am Barking Dogs in the San Antonio gazebo or the Thonotosassa jam (2nd and 4th, bring a chair), or the live *Florida Folk Show* broadcast in St. Pete; 1pm the Port Richey church jam ($4); evening, the 2nd-Saturday contra at the Polish American Society ($10, 7pm) or a Wiley house concert in Clearwater, with Piper on the Pier at sunset in Dunedin in season.
- **Sunday (the Irish day):** a 5pm session somewhere every Sunday except a fifth. On the 4th Sunday, contra at 3:30 in St. Pete, then Flanagan's in Dunedin at 5. In season, Scottish country dancing in Dunedin at 7pm, polka at the Polish club and the Sunday Sampler at 1:30. On a fifth Sunday, drive out to Bethlehem Primitive Baptist Church and sing the Cooper book.
- **Best weeks to visit:** Epiphany week (Jan 2-6, for Kalymnian violin), the Snow Ball (late January), the State Fair singing (February), the Will McLean festival (mid-March), the Highland Games (early April) and Thanksgiving bluegrass at the ranch.

## Defunct and lost

- **Beaux Arts Gallery and Coffeehouse**, Pinellas Park (1950s-1994): fires, code and tax fights; razed. Its owner "would brag openly that he had the oldest coffeehouse in the south."
- **Six Irish sessions** (2005-2023), all ended by pub owners (see Irish above).
- **Contests and festivals:** the Dade City old-time championship (1982-2015, now at White Springs), the Heart of Florida Folk Festival, the Pioneer Florida Day fiddle contest (last evidence 2012), Guavaween (1985-c.2016), the Masaryktown Slovak festival, the Tarpon Springs International Glendi.
- **Rooms:** Sacred Grounds near USF (about 1996-2016); the [Hideaway Cafe](https://ilovetheburg.com/the-hideaway-cafe-st-pete/) (2009-2022, lost its lease) and the Neon Lunchbox in the same room (2024-2026); [Ella's Americana Folk Art Cafe](https://www.cltampa.com/food-drink/tampas-ellas-americana-folk-art-cafe-will-close-on-september-1-18405995/) (2009-2024); the Eagles Lodge song circle (2023-24); the Tampa Scandinavian dance group.
- **Black Tampa:** Central Avenue and the Cotton Club (to 1974); Charlie Brantley's Honey Dippers (1944-c.1949). Also the Ybor club ballrooms as working dance halls.
- **Online:** tampabaymusichistory.com (now spam) and the Tarpon folklife-center page (gone in 2026).

The pattern: venues die of rent, retirement and owner decisions, while the music moves into breweries, living rooms, senior centers and roving series.

## Data quality notes

- **Sources.** All four threads ran under heavy rate limits, so much rests on single pages, thesession.org, Jamfinder, federation directories and search snippets. Facebook, Florida Memory, TuneArch and some venue sites blocked fetches.
- **Counting.** Of 65 in-MSA events, 58 are counted. Excluded but kept: pipe band rehearsals, Piper on the Pier and restaurant Greek music (not participatory), and four jams with no dated 2024-26 evidence.
- **Seasonality.** `per_month` is the in-season rate; seasonal events also carry `seasonal` and `per_month_annual_avg` (about 139 a month over 12 months vs 143 in season). Annual events count at 0.083.
- **Open mics** (about 39 a month counted) are a curated subset of one Pinellas-centric list; without them the total is about 104.
- **Distinct traditions:** 24 by hand count, replacing an inflated free-text count of 76 (criteria in the JSON).
- **Not found** (not proof of absence): Comhaltas, céilí, Cape Breton or Québécois sessions, a Scottish fiddle club, morris, an ECD series, a shanty sing, a folk society, son jarocho, joropo, a powwow, a current tsambouna player, tunes named for the area.
- **Conflicts:** polka day (Sunday or Saturday); the McLean festival's founding year (1990-92) and town; Epiphany's start (c.1907, 1903 or 1906); Highland Games 1966 vs 1967; Bethlehem PBC's town (counted inside); Toth's NEA year (2003 or 2004).
- **Unverified:** the Irish Pub on 4th revival, the Tuesday Tampa Jam's day, Mr. Ethnic's show after 2017, the 2026 Sertoma spring festival (marked hiatus), the clogging clubs. Several founding years are inferred from edition numbers, and lat/lng values are sparse.
