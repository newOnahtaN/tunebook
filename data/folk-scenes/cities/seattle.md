# Seattle–Tacoma–Bellevue, WA: the folk scene (as of October 2026)

*MSA 42660: King, Pierce and Snohomish counties, 4,161,883 people (Census Vintage 2025). Synthesized from four research
threads (trad instrumental/dance, song/singing/media, heritage/diaspora, history/people/fiddle) plus spot-checks made
while editing. Items outside the three counties are marked **(outside MSA)**. Structured data: `seattle.json`.*

## 1. Scene in a nutshell

Greater Seattle's folk scene is dance-led, weeknight-heavy and run by long-lived volunteer institutions. The Seattle
Folklore Society (1966) and Skandia Folkdance Society (1949) anchor it, and Northwest Folklife (1972) still draws about
250,000 people, free, every Memorial Day weekend. Two weekly contras carry the dance world: Lake City on Thursday (born
as a 1974 tavern square dance) and Emerald City on Friday at the Phinney Center. There is no Saturday contra. Nordic
dance is a first-tier tradition here: twice-monthly Skandia dances with live spelmanslag, Hardanger teachers and a
Midsommarfest held since 1959. Irish sessions are few (about 23 slots a month), old-time jams meet on weekday
afternoons and in suburban WOTFA halls, and bluegrass was rebuilt by a club founded in 2024. Sacred Harp runs about five
singings a month plus a 100-singer February convention. Heritage life is calendar-driven: 25 free Festál festivals,
Coast Salish canoe journeys and powwows, and Bon Odori since 1932.

## 2. Ten most interesting things

1. **"Hootenanny" is a Seattle word.** In summer 1941 the New Deal Washington Commonwealth Federation voted on a name
   for its monthly fund-raising parties; "Hootenanny won out by a nose over 'wingding'". Seeger and Guthrie took it
   back to New York ([PNWFS, quoting Seeger](http://pnwfolklore.org/Hootenannies.html)).
2. **The contra scene began as old-time squares in a Pioneer Square tavern** (Sandy Bradley and the Gypsy Gyppo String
   Band, Nov 1974). Contras arrived in 1979, and the dance has moved four times since (G-Note Tavern, Ballard Eagles,
   Lake City, Latvian Center) ([Footprints](https://www.seattledance.org/contra/lakecity/footprints/)).
3. **"Spotted Pony" was named in Seattle by mistake.** Per Stuart Williams, the tune was "Snowshoes" until an editing
   error on Phil and Vivian Williams's first Voyager LP, *Fiddle Jam Sessions* (1967), swapped the titles
   ([thesession #9927](https://thesession.org/tunes/9927)). It is now a contra and jam standard.
4. **Nordic dance is a first-tier tradition here.** Skandia (Jan 9 1949) runs Friday dances with a live spelmanslag,
   a free Monday class and Norwegian hardingfele teachers at Springdans. Its founder left **500+ reel-to-reel Nordic
   field recordings** ([Skandia history](https://www.skandia-folkdance.org/history.html)).
5. **Folklife drew 123,000 people in 1972 on a $6,000 budget, with no auditions and no paid performers.** It is still
   admission-free 54 years on, with FY2025 net assets of **−$253,551**
   ([HistoryLink 20470](https://www.historylink.org/File/20470);
   [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/911311548)).
6. **Old-time happens at 2pm on weekdays.** The best-documented city jams are Phinney (1st/3rd/5th Thursdays 2–4) and
   Columbia City (Fridays 2–4, in an outdoor alley alcove). WOTFA's Pierce district jams Mondays at 1:30 in a senior
   centre ([Phinney jam](https://sites.google.com/view/1st-3rd-thursday-old-time-jam);
   [WOTFA D8](https://wotfa.org/district-8-2/)).
7. **A bookstore-café is the most multi-traditional session room in town.** The Couth Buzzard (Greenwood) hosts Irish,
   Québécois, klezmer, Cajun and choro, and its Irish session is hybrid (2–4 players online). When it nearly closed in
   2023, a GoFundMe reportedly raised about $60,000 from 600 donors in two weeks
   ([Phinneywood](https://phinneywood.com/2023/04/breaking-fundraiser-created-to-save-the-couth-buzzard/);
   [PNA blog PDF](https://media.journoportfolio.com/users/287888/uploads/3e4655ff-a0e3-43d8-a771-73dd9856e003.pdf)).
8. **A Deep-South Sacred Harp book is sung near Mt. Rainier.** The August Mt. Rainier Singing uses the Cooper Book
   (2012), and the next day the Willard Birthday Singing (15th in 2025) uses a hymnary by Tacoma composer Karen Willard
   ([2025 Minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/OnlineMinutesPDFFiles/SacredHarpMinutesOnline2025.pdf)).
9. **An Appalachian logging town supplied the revival.** North Carolina "Tar Heel" families in Darrington (Snohomish
   County) fed the Williamses' recordings and, in 1977, a bluegrass festival on a flatbed at the rodeo grounds. Its
   49th edition was July 17–19 2026 ([festival history](https://www.darringtonbluegrass.com/history);
   [AFS](https://americanfolkloresociety.org/vivian-williams-1938-2023/)).
10. **Native practice is big and current.** The Muckleshoot Skopabsh Pow-Wow pays **$10,000** to the winning drum
    ([powwows.com](https://calendar.powwows.com/events/muckleshoot-skopabsh-pow-wow/)). Seafair Indian Days drew a record
    20,000+ in 2026 ([Tulalip News](https://tulalipnews.com/2026/07/22/37-years-of-tradition/)). The modern Canoe
    Journey began as the 1989 "Paddle to Seattle".

## 4. Trad instrumental and dance scene

The shape of it: dances are strong and weeknight-based, instrumental sessions are thin, and Saturday night is the weak
spot. The core is a north-Seattle arc about five miles across: Ballard, Fremont, Wallingford, Phinney/Greenwood, Green
Lake, the U District and Lake City. Lynnwood's Cedar Valley Grange is the Nordic and international-dance hub. WOTFA
old-time is suburban (Des Moines, Puyallup, Arlington), and Tacoma runs a small parallel scene.

### Old-time

Three overlapping cultures:

**1. Urban daylight jams.**
- **[Phinney old-time jam](https://sites.google.com/view/1st-3rd-thursday-old-time-jam):** **1st, 3rd and 5th Thursdays
  2–4pm**, Phinney Center Blue Building Room 5; in summer, at Green Lake by the Bathhouse Theater.
  - Steve Johnston runs it and keeps a tune-history list. It says plainly it is "not a slow or beginner jam".
  - It skips Fiddle Tunes week and the Portland Old-Time Gathering.
- **Columbia City:** [Tony Mates](https://tonymates.com/) runs a jam **every Friday 2–4pm** in an outdoor alcove across
  from Lottie's Lounge, 4900 Rainier Ave S ([WOTFA](https://wotfa.org/event/columbia-city-weekly-friday-jam-2/2025-03-07/)).
- **[Slower Than Dirt](https://slowerthandirt.org/):** a monthly beginner jam with a core-tune list and play-along
  tracks; new leadership in 2026.
- **Announcements:** the Seattle Old-Time Music Facebook group and [groups.io](https://groups.io/g/oldtimeseattle). Old-time
  is "music you might expect to hear at a square dance".

**2. Squares and gatherings.**
- **[Tractor Tavern Square Dance](https://www.meetup.com/tractor-tavern-square-dance/events/314881626/):** **4th Mondays**,
  Ballard, 7–10, **$10, 21+**. Alex MacLeod organizes and bands rotate (the Mississippi Hotdogs with caller Dave
  Thielk, Oct 2026).
- **[Old Growth Oldtime](https://oldgrowtholdtime.org/)** (2023): a pay-what-you-want weekend at the Latvian Center
  (Oct 9–11 2026) with concerts, a slow jam and a 2-band square dance; SFS-sponsored.
- **[Haller Lake Community Dance](https://www.seattledance.org/hallerlake/):** **3rd Saturdays Oct–Apr, 6–8pm**. An

### Contra, squares and family dances

- **[Lake City Contra](https://www.seattledance.org/contra/lakecity/), every Thursday:**
  - Seattle Latvian Center, 11710 3rd Ave NE. Workshop 7:15, dance 7:30–10.
  - **$10–20 sliding; kids and first-timers free.** Coordinator Sherry Nevins.
  - Lineage: 1974 Pioneer Square tavern → 1977 G-Note Tavern → 1979 contras → 1985 Ballard Eagles Hall → 1998 Lake City
    Community Center → Jan 2018 Latvian Center. A Lake City Virtual Contra launched in mid-May 2020
    ([Footprints](https://www.seattledance.org/contra/lakecity/footprints/)).
  - It adopted Larks & Ravens by open letter in Oct 2018 and now uses Larks & Robins.
  - Specials: a Thanksgiving-eve contra with an Irish open band (Doug Plummer, Mara Kim, Joe Micheals), a klezmer contra
    (Beregovski's All-Stars), a live techno contra, and the non-stop **Contra Marathon** benefiting Folklife (19th on
    Mar 11 2027).
- **[Emerald City Contra](https://www.seattledance.org/contra/emeraldcity/), nearly every Friday:**
  - Phinney Center Community Hall; workshop 7, dance 7:30–10. Sponsored by SFS.
  - **$10–20 sliding, students $5, under-16 free; capacity 140.** First-timers get a free-return coupon, but only five
    are redeemed per night.
  - Dark during Folklife and on the 1st Friday of December.
  - Fall 2026 bands: Raven & Goose; the Bazooka Band; the Ice Cream Truckers with George Marshall; **Raccoon Carnival**
    (Sarah Comer's 12–15-piece orchestra with "5 or 6 fiddles"); **Clickbait** (Ryan McKasson, Anita Anderson, Dave

  **$15–30 sliding**, all gender-neutral; organizer Matt Temmel.
- **The touring circuit:** touring bands play Lake City Thursday, Emerald City Friday and Tacoma Sunday. That

- **Summer:** an open-band contra on the Green Lake lawn on Monday evenings (Jul 27 and Aug 31 2026). Callers sign up

- **No Saturday-night contra in the MSA,** and none on the Eastside or in Everett. Dancers drive to Olympia (South Bay

- **Weekends** ([seattledance festivals](https://www.seattledance.org/festivals/)):

### Scandinavian: the signature

  - **Dances:** **1st and 3rd Fridays** at the Cedar Valley Grange, Lynnwood. Teaching 7:30–8:30, dance 8:30–10:30, also

  - The **Seattle Skandia Spelmanslag** (Frank Brown, all levels). It "traces its roots to the days when Skandia dancers
    first traded 45-rpm records for the lift and energy of live musicians."

- **Outreach singings:**
  - Mountlake Terrace Library, Oct 25 2026 ([Shoreline Area News](https://www.shorelineareanews.com/2026/10/sacred-harp-shape-note-singing-october.html)).

- **The Pacific Northwest Sacred Harp Convention (Washington session)** meets the third weekend of February at the
  Sunset Hill Community Club, Ballard: Saturday singing school, Sunday all-day.
  - **2025 was the 32nd:** 104 singers from 2 countries, 8 states and 2 provinces sang 203 tunes. It alternates with an
    Oregon session.
  - **Founding year:** the threads disagreed (c.1993–94 vs c.1991–92). fasola.org's first minutes are for Feb 26 1995,

    Hauff teaching about 80 students ([fasola search](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=Pacific+Northwest+Sacred+Harp+Convention)).

  Cooper Book (2012 revision)**, the south Alabama–Florida–Texas book, rare in the West.

  Karen Willard.
- **Outside MSA:** Olympia All-Day (March; 32 leaders, 85 songs in 2025), Bainbridge Island (April), Quilcene, Morton,
  Trout Lake, and the American Christmas Harp singing (December).

## 7. Heritage and diaspora traditions

Heritage music life here is **institutionalized and calendar-driven**. Its spine is
**[Seattle Center Festál](https://www.seattlecenter.com/events/featured-events/festal)**: 25 free festivals a year
(Feb–Nov 2026) produced with 25 community organizations, founded in 1997.

- **Attendance:** the official figures conflict: 150,000+ (2023), "over 300,000" (2024 Community Impact Report,
  [Center Spotlight](https://centerspotlight.seattle.gov/2025/04/16/seattle-center-releases-2024-community-impact-report-showing-continued-growth-and-community-engagement/))
  and "approximately 400,000 visits" (2026 boilerplate). The jump probably reflects a change in counting.
- **Origin:** also disputed: "eleven annual cultural events" vs "one of five ethnic festivals" in 1997.

The best index of hand-to-hand transmission is the state's
**[Heritage Arts Apprenticeship Program](https://wacultures.org/2026-heritage-arts-apprenticeship-program-teams-announced/)**
(WACultures, run by ArtsWA and Humanities Washington; 9th year in 2026).

- Masters and apprentices work 100 hours together, then give a public showing, often at Folklife.
- Metro masters in 2025–27 teach klezmer violin (Sandra Layman teaching Shulamit Kleinerman), Carnatic violin (Ganesh
  Rajagopalan), taiko (Ringtaro Tateishi), capoeira angola, Lao classical dance, Purépecha stickball, flamenco,
  Kosraean canoe-building, Sm'algyax, and "songs documenting PNW history" (Hobe Kytr teaching Joe Seamons).

## 13. History

### Narrative

**Before 1900.**
- The first documented fiddle in Puget Sound country played at a **1841** Fort Nisqually dance, where Sequalitchew
  people thought a "devel… must be in the little box" ([HistoryLink 7565](https://www.historylink.org/File/7565)).
- Boas (1888) described the **Puget Sound hop fields** as a Chinook Jargon song marketplace
  ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/jstor-534240)).
- **"Old Settler's Song" ("Acres of Clams", also "Puget Sound")**, c.1874, is set to "Old Rosin the Beau", the tune of
  Sacred Harp 338.

**1900s–30s.**
- At the **Everett Massacre** (Nov 5 1916), Wobblies were singing "Hold the Fort" as shooting began
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everett_massacre)).
- Seattle-born Earl Robinson wrote "Joe Hill" (1936).
- WPA writer Paul Ashford and Ivar Haglund collected songs "with a couple of bottles of beer"
  ([PNWFS](http://pnwfolklore.org/PaulAshford.html)).
- Tar Heel families reached Darrington from 1937 ([HistoryLink 8798](https://www.historylink.org/File/8798)).

**First revival (1941–65).**
- **1941:** Guthrie wrote his BPA songs, and the WCF hootenanny gave the revival its word.
- **1949:** Skandia was founded.
- **1953–54:** the PNW Folklore Society was founded, then silenced by the Red Scare.
- **1955:** Tracie opened the Folklore Center. Its 1957 festival brought out the local North Carolina old-time players.
- **1958–60:** coffeehouses (Café Encore, Pamir House and others) opened
  ([Firth](http://pnwfolklore.org/wp-nwhoot/index.php/2017/01/24/seattle-coffeehouses-during-the-folk-revival-of-the-1960s-by-don-firth/)).
  The British Invasion emptied them by the mid-1960s.
- **1962:** KRAB-FM and UW Ethnomusicology began.

**Folklife era (1965–80s).**
- **1965:** WOTFA was incorporated.
- **1966:** Vivian Williams won the first state contest; SFS was founded.
- **1967:** Voyager Recordings.
- **1972:** Folklife drew 123,000. With 225,000 in 1973 it became the most successful of the four NFFA regional
  festivals ([HistoryLink 20470](https://www.historylink.org/File/20470)).
- **1974:** the tavern square dance began.
- **1977:** Darrington's first festival.
- **1979:** contras arrived; square dancing became the state dance.
- **1980:** Mossyback Morris.
- **1980s:** *Potluck* went national.

**1984–2019.**
- **1984:** KRAB was sold.
- **1992:** Folklife artists beat an admission fee.
- **c.1992:** the Sacred Harp convention began.
- **1993–2005:** Martin Hayes lived in Seattle.
- **1994:** Wintergrass began in Tacoma.
- **1997:** Seeger played all four days of Folklife.
- **1998:** Folklife's debt crisis; Lake City moved to its community center.
- **2017:** "Free is the f-word": Folklife was saved by raising its gate target.

**2020–26.**
- **2020:** COVID cancelled Folklife and sent Lake City online.
- **2021:** PNWFS ended.
- **2023:** Vivian Williams died, Old Growth Oldtime and SeaWitch Morris began, and the Couth Buzzard was rescued. Pubs
  closed and took sessions with them.
- **2024:** Outlanders, the Conor Byrne co-op and the Ladino circle began.
- **2026:** SFS turns 60.

**Local style.** Phil Williams argued that dance bands assembled from mixed backgrounds produced "an amalgamation of
fiddle styles and a range of tunes played unique to this region… An identifiable traditional Northwest style emerged…
which has mostly died out today with the absence of the community dances"
([Voyager NWFFR](http://web.archive.org/web/20220123115004/https://voyagerrecords.com/nwffr.htm)).

- Voyager's field lists for King and Pierce counties run to **waltzes, schottisches, two-steps, rags and "hi-ball" dance
  tunes**, not Appalachian breakdowns.
- Vivian Williams added Scandinavian and Celtic inputs to "a unique Northwest style".
- Texas contest style arrived with Thomasson and spread nationally through O'Connor. The older Canadian and
  Scandinavian-tinged dance style ran through Pancerzewski and Sanderson.
- **Today's living styles are imports done well.** New England contra fiddling (Raccoon Carnival's 5–6 fiddles), Irish
  (Russ, Bays), Scottish and Cape Breton, and Swedish and Norwegian spel (Skandia, Hardanger teachers at Springdans).

## 16. Character

**What makes it distinct:**
1. **The revival was imported from Portland.** Reed alumni (Ullman, the Williamses) founded SFS because Seattle
   presented "virtually none" of the roots acts Reed booked ([SFS](https://www.seafolklore.org/about-sfs/history-of-sfs/)).
2. **A free, unauditioned mega-festival** (Folklife) has been the public face for 54 years.
3. **Nordic dance is first-tier:** Skandia (1949), the Tracie archive and live spelmanslag.
4. **Dances before sessions, squares before contras, born in taverns.** About 10.6 contras and 17 Nordic events a month
   against about 23 Irish sessions.
5. **An Appalachian logging-town source** (Darrington) within an hour of the city.
6. **A labor-song lineage:** "hootenanny", Everett 1916, Earl Robinson, the 1997 Labor Chorus.
7. **Municipal and state heritage infrastructure:** Festál and HAAP.

**Who shows up.** No surveys exist. Proxies:
- **Older players:** WOTFA membership fell from 1,500 to 628 (2007–2022); its jams meet in senior centers; old-time and
  RSCDS classes meet in weekday daylight.
- **Small circles:** song circles draw "9-plus".
- **Inclusive dance culture:** contra and ECD dances are labeled **gender-neutral** and **fragrance-free**
  ([ECD](https://www.seattledance.org/english/)), run a queer contra camp, and let first-timers and kids in free.
- **Youth:** visible in mariachi, Fiddlesticks, the femme and non-binary bluegrass jam, and the "strikingly
  intergenerational" Ladino circle.

**A visiting fiddler's Tuesday vs Saturday.**
- **Tuesday is thin.** The **Little Red Hen bluegrass jam** (Green Lake; beginners ~7, projam ~8:15) is the only sure
  weekly fiddle jam in Seattle proper. On 2nd and 4th Tuesdays, ECD at the Swedish Club. There is no reliable Tuesday
  Irish or old-time session.
- **Saturday is a daytime day.**
  - Irish at the Couth Buzzard (noon, 1st/3rd), the Dusty Strings beginner session (2nd) or Québécois (1:30, 4th).
  - WOTFA in Des Moines.
  - Evening: the Haller Lake open band (3rd, Oct–Apr), Tacoma Nordic (4th), or the drive to Olympia for contra.
- **Prime time is Thursday and Friday:** afternoon old-time jams, then contras.

**Costs:**
- Contra $10–20 sliding (`costs` = $15); students $5, kids and first-timers free; Tacoma $15–30.
- ECD $10–20; Skandia $15 ($10 members); Balkan $10–15; Sno-King $8; Tractor square dance $10.
- Old Growth Oldtime is PWYW; Folklife is free ($10/day suggested).
- Weekends: Supersonic $150–270; Queer Contra Camp $125–475.
- Jams free, or a $5 donation at Dusty Strings.
- House concerts by donation or lottery.

**Geography.**
- **North-Seattle core,** within about 5 miles: Phinney Center (contra, Skandia class, old-time, RSCDS, morris, SFS

## 18. Surprises

1. **"Hootenanny" was coined in Seattle (1941)**, beating "wingding" "by a nose".
2. **"Spotted Pony" got its name from a 1967 Voyager editing error**; it was "Snowshoes".
3. **No Saturday-night contra in a metro of 4.2 million.** The flagships are Thursday and Friday.
4. **The best old-time jams meet at 2pm on weekdays**, one of them in a Columbia City alley alcove.
5. **Folklife began with 123,000 people on $6,000**, no auditions and no pay. It is still free, with −$253,551 in net
   assets.
6. **A bluegrass club founded in 2024 runs five weekly jams** on about $7k a year.
7. **A bookstore-café hosts Irish, Québécois, klezmer, Cajun and choro.** About 600 donors gave about $60k to save it
   in 2023.
8. **The Muckleshoot powwow pays $10,000 to the winning drum**; Seafair Indian Days drew a record 20,000+ in 2026.
9. **A Deep-South Sacred Harp book (Cooper) is sung near Mt. Rainier**, and a Tacoma composer has her own birthday
   singing (15th in 2025).
10. **A 1954 Pete Seeger concert killed Seattle's first folklore society** over one man's Boeing security-clearance
    fears.
11. **"Acres of Clams" = "Rosin the Beau" = Sacred Harp 338**, and one of its titles is "Puget Sound".
12. **Squares survive as a $10, 21+ dance at the Tractor Tavern**, the bar that was once the New Melody Tavern's
    open-band contra room.
13. **The Skandia founder's 500+ Nordic field reels** sit in Ballard; Midsommarfest's maypole shrank from 45 to 35 feet.
14. **Puyallup hosted the 2024 Canoe Journey on about four months' notice.**
15. **Tacoma's Bon Odori dances to live min'yo singers**; Seattle taiko began on car tires.
16. **Tacoma's whole 2004–14 Irish session network died**; a whiskey bar revived one session in 2025.
17. **A trad house concert drew 81 requests for 43 seats**, seated by lottery, with sourdough.
18. **No Washington fiddler has been an NEA Heritage Fellow.**

## 19. Curiosities (questions for phase 2)

1. **Saturday contra:** why do Seattle's flagship contras avoid Saturday? Is it halls, demographics or the touring
   circuit? How do Portland, Boston, Minneapolis and Denver schedule theirs?
2. **Daytime jams:** how common are weekday-afternoon old-time jams in other metros, and do they signal an aging scene?
3. **Irish density:** Seattle has about 5.6 Irish session slots per million residents a month. How do Boston, Chicago,
   Philadelphia, Denver and Minneapolis compare?
4. **Nordic:** is Seattle's the largest Nordic dance scene in the US, ahead of Minneapolis? Which metros have
   Scandinavian dance societies founded before 1950?
5. **Sacred Harp:** is greater Seattle the densest metro west of the Mississippi? Are there other Cooper Book singings
   outside the South?
6. **Free festivals:** Folklife had a 17% donor rate in 2017. Did the other NFFA regionals die, and is "free with
   suggested donation" sustainable anywhere?
7. **Municipal heritage series:** which metros have one comparable to Festál, and how do they count attendance?
8. **Apprenticeships:** do other states' programs fund as many urban diaspora teams as Washington's HAAP?
9. **Community ownership:** are community-owned rooms (a rescued bookstore, a pub co-op) a survival model in other
   cities?
10. **Fiddlers' associations:** how does WOTFA's 59% membership drop compare with other states', and does any big metro
    still host an in-county fiddle contest?
11. **Contra's arrival:** did contra reach other West Coast cities through taverns, around 1979?
12. **Appalachian enclaves:** are there Darrington-like Appalachian enclaves feeding revivals near other metros?
13. **Pungmul and Ethiopian music:** why are both invisible in Seattle when they are visible in the Bay Area, New York
    and Washington DC?
