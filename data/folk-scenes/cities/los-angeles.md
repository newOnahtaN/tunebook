# Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim (LA + Orange counties): folk scene dossier

*As of October 2026. Metro population 12.84M (2025). Built from five research threads (sessions and jams; dance; song and singing; heritage; history) that were then deduplicated and merged. Structured data: `los-angeles.json`. The Inland Empire (Riverside and San Bernardino counties) is a separate metro with its own dossier. Claremont, Pomona and San Dimas are in LA County and are counted here.*

## Scene in a nutshell

Los Angeles has no single folk scene. It is a sprawling federation of small ones, spread across a hundred suburbs, and most of its folk energy is in dance and heritage traditions rather than Anglo-Celtic tunes. By event count, the scene is dancers, not fiddlers. About 300 verified recurring meetings a month are led by international and Israeli folk dance (~95), Scottish country dance and céilí classes (~30), weekly heritage practice such as fandango, Danza Azteca, rumba and the Leimert Park drum circle (~50), and Songmakers' living-room hoots. Fiddlers get about 23 Irish sessions a month (anchored by a 36-year-old Monday seisiún in a North Hollywood social club), about 35 bluegrass jams (many on Saturday mornings in parks), only ~3 old-time jams and four Saturday contras. LA's real distinction is history and institution-building: Lummis's 1904 Californio cylinders, the naming of Western swing, the Ash Grove and McCabe's, KPFK's FolkScene (since 1970), the Topanga Banjo-Fiddle Contest (1961), Folk Alliance (born in Malibu in 1989), and 18 NEA Heritage Fellows.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **It's a dance town.** International and Israeli folk dance runs about 95 sessions a month and Scottish country dance about 27 class or dance nights, against only four contras, all on Saturdays. There is no weeknight contra anywhere in a metro of 12.8 million ([Federation clubs](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/clubs.htm); see Part 2).
2. **The square-dance/folk-dance split was political.** In 1948 Federation leaders proposed loyalty oaths for member clubs. The motion failed, but "all of the square dance clubs" had left by 1950 to form their own association, and the two worlds still barely overlap ([Federation history](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/history.htm)).
3. **About 640 former Southern California folk dance groups** are listed on the Federation's memorial page, including Hollywood-era dance cafés where stars danced kolos. 22 member clubs remain, many in senior centers and a gated retirement village ([Lest We Forget](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/lest_we_forget.htm)).
4. **One hootenanny club seeded three institutions.** Songmakers splinters, directly or indirectly, became KPFK's FolkScene (1970, after the Larmans quit over a donation to a North Vietnamese orphanage), the California Traditional Music Society (1980) and Folk Alliance International (Malibu, 1989) ([Songmakers](https://songmakers.org/roz-larman/); [FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/milestone/rip-clark-weissman/)).
5. **The NEA Heritage Fellowships were designed by an LA guitar teacher.** Bess Lomax Hawes taught mass guitar classes in LA from 1951, taught Ed Pearl before he opened the Ash Grove, and MC'd the 1963 Topanga contest where teenage Richard Greene, David Lindley and Ry Cooder competed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bess_Lomax_Hawes); [FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/60-years-later-the-first-topanga-banjo-fiddle-contestants/)).
6. **The Irish anchor is a social club, not a pub.** The Celtic Arts Center, founded in 1985 by James Connolly's grandson Brian Heron, runs a free Monday learners' session, céilí class and session at the Mayflower Club. About 15 pub sessions have died around it, one because "Irish trad music has no place in an Irish pub" ([PRINT](https://www.printmag.com/daily-heller/brian-o-h-eachtuigheim-nee-heron/); [thesession 2811](https://thesession.org/sessions/2811)).
7. **"Western swing" was named here**, in 1942, by Spade Cooley's promoter, the year Cooley "beat" Bob Wills at the Venice Pier Ballroom, where several thousand dancers turned out on Saturday nights ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spade_Cooley)).
8. **Obon is the biggest open folk-dance circuit.** About 16 free bon odori run at Buddhist temples from late June to late August. Kinnara Taiko, the first temple taiko group in North America, began in an impromptu post-Obon jam at Senshin Buddhist Temple in 1969 ([Senshin](https://senshintemple.org/groups/kinnara/)).
9. **Eighteen NEA National Heritage Fellows** have lived in LA or Orange County: mariachi, jarocho, Afro-Cuban batá (twice), Brazilian pandeiro, Persian santur, kunqu opera, Cambodian dance, koto and more. None of them are Anglo, Celtic, old-time or bluegrass ([arts.gov, California](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/list?state=CA)).
10. **LA's folk sound archive lives in Chapel Hill.** UNC bought the John Edwards Memorial Foundation in 1983, and McCabe's live masters and FolkScene's tapes followed. The oldest layer stayed home: Lummis's 1904 Californio cylinders, for which Manuela García sang about 150 songs from memory into a $44.25 Edison machine ([Southern Folklife Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Folklife_Collection); [Autry](https://theautry.org/explore/blog/spanish-songs-old-california)).

## Part 1. Sessions and jams: Irish, bluegrass, old-time, Scottish, plus teachers and shops

### What a visiting fiddler can do, night by night

**Monday is the best night in the metro.**
- **Celtic Arts Center at the Mayflower Club, North Hollywood.** Free; bar on site. A learners' slow session runs 7–8pm, a céilí dance class 8–9pm, and the main seisiún 9–11pm. On first Mondays a "Grand Seisiún" runs 7–11pm instead. The center says it has hosted a session "for over 36 years," and a thesession visitor in July 2026 called it "a great group, very welcoming" ([CAC](http://celticartscenter.com/Main.htm), [thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/476)).
- **Orange County Celtic Jam, St. Paul's Episcopal, Tustin.** 7–9pm, $3 room donation, tunes from the group's shared Dropbox tunebooks, so readers are welcome ([FolkWorks listing](https://folkworks.org/event/orange-county-celtic-jam/2026-11-02/)).
- 1st and 3rd Mondays: **bluegrass jam at Patsy's Irish Pub, Mission Viejo**, from 6pm ([CBA](https://californiabluegrass.org/event/mission-viejo-bluegrass-jam/2026-10-05/)).

**Tuesday**
- **Tuesday Soup Jam, Signal Hill**, 7pm weekly, "open to everyone," "held in an industrial building" ([Southwest Bluegrass list, July 2026](https://www.southwestbluegrass.com/2026/01/19/jams/)).
- 1st Tuesdays: **South Bay Bluegrass Jam at Project Barley, Lomita** (pizza, beer, open mic). The list says it "is back"; check the brewery calendar ([labluegrass.com](https://labluegrass.com/jams)).
- I found **no Irish session on Tuesdays.** Timmy Nolan's (Toluca Lake) is still on FolkWorks' 2022 list, but thesession treats it as inactive.

**Wednesday**
- **Lawless Brewing, North Hollywood**: weekly bluegrass jam, 6–10pm, "great players and new players alike" ([labluegrass.com](https://labluegrass.com/jams)).
- **Downey acoustic and dulcimer jam**, usually 4th Wednesdays, Barbara J. Riley Senior Center, 5:30–7:45pm, "no drums" ([SCDH calendar](https://www.scdh.org/SCDH/Calendar.html)).
- **COLLAGE, San Pedro**: "Acoustic Roots Session" on 4th Wednesdays ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/events/)).

**Thursday is the second-best night.**
- **The Back Abbey, Claremont**: a new (Feb 2026) weekly Irish session, 6–9pm, all levels ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/9611)).
- **Lamppost Pizza, Orange**: bluegrass, 6–8pm.
- 2nd Thursdays: bluegrass at the **Oracle Tavern** at the edge of Chinatown (8pm) and the **OC Bluegrass Jam** at Fountain Valley Community Center (5–8pm) ([Southwest Bluegrass](https://www.southwestbluegrass.com/2026/01/19/jams/)).
- The long-running **1642 old-time jam** in a tiny Echo Park bar ("flatfooters welcome") has traditionally met on 1st Thursdays, 8–11pm ([FolkWorks list](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/old-time-jams/)). A 2026 listing instead says "held on the first of the month" and shows Sunday Nov 1 ([Mato](https://ma.to/event/old-time-jam-2026-11-01)). Confirm before you go.

**Friday is the dead night.** The only regular option is the **2nd-Friday Irish session at Mt. Lowe Brewing, Arcadia**, 6–9pm ([AllEvents, Nov 13 2026 listing](https://allevents.in/arcadia/irish-session-mt-lowe-brewery-2nd-friday/200030834515311)).

**Saturday is for outdoor mornings and afternoon sessions.**
- 10am every week: **Long Beach Dog Park Jam** ("close your case, there are dogs around") and **Mile Square Park "Bluegrass for Beginners"**, Fountain Valley.
- 10am on 2nd and 4th Saturdays: **Oso Creek Jam**, Mission Viejo ([CBA](https://californiabluegrass.org/event/oso-creek-jam-2024-03-09/2026-10-10/), [Southwest Bluegrass](https://www.southwestbluegrass.com/2026/01/19/jams/)).
- Afternoons: 2nd Saturday, the **Fret House bluegrass jam** in the store, Covina (1–3pm); 3rd Saturday, the **Patsy's Irish session**, Mission Viejo (1–4pm, [thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/9756)); 4th Saturday, the **Debs Park old-time jam** at the Audubon Center (1–4pm), hosted by Joe Wack "for the last 20 years" ([Ojai Rotary bio, Nov 2025](https://ojairotary.org/CalendarItem/Details?CalendarItemId=bf51591b-0529-45e3-aec5-edb0ad9f1078&Start=2025-11-21&End=2025-11-21)).

**Sunday is the richest daytime.**
- **Every Sunday:** **Burbank's George Izay Park bluegrass jam** from noon; a Friday email confirms each week ([CBA](https://californiabluegrass.org/event/burbank-bluegrass-jam-at-george-izay-park/2026-10-04/)). The **Auld Dubliner session in downtown Long Beach**, 4–7pm: "Still happening every Sunday," July 2025 ([thesession](https://thesession.org/sessions/1740)).
- **1st Sundays:** **CSOTFA District 4 old-time fiddlers jam**, Fullerton, 1–4pm ([CSOTFA](https://www.csotfa.org/districts.htm)); **El Camino College outdoor bluegrass jam**, Torrance, 1pm.
- **2nd Sundays:** **Pasadena Conservatory "Second Sundays"**, a free concert followed by a guided family jam. Some months are "Old Time Americana," with handouts including *Boil 'Em Cabbage*, *Cluck Old Hen* and *Pasadena Girls* ([Pasadena Conservatory](https://pasadenaconservatory.org/event/old-time-americana/)).
- **Once a month:** the **Scottish Fiddlers of LA** jam (summer 2026 at the Orange Grove Friends Meeting in Pasadena, 2pm, slow first hour then faster; [SFLA](https://scottishfiddlers.org/?cpt_news=scottish-fiddlers-summer-jams-pasadena)), and a free ukulele jam at COLLAGE, San Pedro.

**Tuesday vs. Saturday, in short:** on a Tuesday you are choosing among bluegrass jams in the harbor area. On a Saturday you can play bluegrass in a park at 10am, then a session or old-time jam in the afternoon, and still drive to nothing in the evening: LA's weekend trad scene mostly ends by dinner.

### The character of the scene

#### 1. The Irish scene: a cultural center holds it up, pubs come and go

LA's anchor is not a pub. The **Celtic Arts Center (An Claidheamh Soluis)** was founded in 1985 by **Brian Heron**, grandson of the 1916 Easter Rising leader James Connolly and founder of New York's Irish Arts Center ([PRINT](https://www.printmag.com/daily-heller/brian-o-h-eachtuigheim-nee-heron/)). Besides the free Monday program, it holds Samhain, Burns Night, a St. Patrick's Grand Session and a Lughnasadh market ([CAC](http://celticartscenter.com/Main.htm)).

Around that anchor, **pub sessions have proven fragile.** Thesession.org's LA listings, with comments back to 2002, show sessions at the Buchanan Arms (Burbank) and the Irish Times (Culver City) dropped for plugged-in bands to attract "a younger, rowdier, money-paying" crowd ([thesession 1006](https://thesession.org/sessions/1006)); Ireland's 32 in Van Nuys ending in 2011 because "management decided that Irish trad music has no place in an Irish pub" ([thesession 2811](https://thesession.org/sessions/2811)); and Griffins of Kinsale (South Pasadena) holding its "final session in late February 2023" ([thesession 7005](https://thesession.org/sessions/7005)). One session has moved four times in four years as breweries opened and closed: **O'Brien's → Wingwalker → Over Town → Mt. Lowe** ([thesession 9303](https://thesession.org/sessions/9303), [8701](https://thesession.org/sessions/8701)).

The **Westside**, where McCabe's is, has **no active Irish session left**. The Westchester session moved in 2025 to Steve O'Loughlin's hand-painted art studio and went on hold in June 2025 because of his health ([thesession 9213](https://thesession.org/sessions/9213), [7358](https://thesession.org/sessions/7358)).

Sessions here tend to welcome readers. Westchester played from the free King Street Sessions tunebook and the Celtic Arts Center slow list; its "always gets critical mass" list was Drowsy Maggie, Harvest Home, Morrison's, Cooley's, the Teetotaller, Banish Misfortune, Musical Priest, Wind that Shakes the Barley, Merry Blacksmith, Fisher's and the Kesh.

The LA **Comhaltas** branch website ([comhaltasla.com](https://www.comhaltasla.com/)) is unedited template text, and I found **no dedicated Irish traditional music school** in LA or OC. Learners use the Celtic Arts Center, private teachers such as Michael Kelly in Long Beach ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/music-instruction/)), and online academies.

#### 2. Bluegrass: the largest, sunniest and most beginner-friendly part

Bluegrass jams outnumber Irish sessions about two to one by meetings. Their defining feature is **Saturday-morning outdoor park jams**, many explicitly "Bluegrass for Beginners." The machine-readable **California Bluegrass Association** calendar is the best source ([CBA](https://californiabluegrass.org/)), with **labluegrass.com** and the **Southwest Bluegrass** list filling in. The **Bluegrass Association of Southern California**'s website no longer exists, and **Viva Cantina** in Burbank, LA's "musician's fort" for Americana from 1996, has closed ([tribute site](https://codysvivacantina.com/)). Orange County's densest cluster is **Mission Viejo**, where Patsy's Irish Pub hosts both a bluegrass jam and an Irish session.

#### 3. Old-time: small, Eastside, artistic, and historically deep

Old-time has few regular public jams (Debs Park, 1642, CSOTFA in Fullerton) but outsized cultural depth. Much of it centers on **David Bragger's Old-Time Tiki Parlour**, a house venue, record label and film project that records touring players and hosts potluck jams ([Tiki Parlour](https://oldtimetikiparlour.com/category/calendar/)). Its biggest yearly event, the **LA Old Time Social**, now in its 16th year, ran May 16, 2026 at the Velaslavasay Panorama: workshops, jams, a cakewalk ("musical cakes") and a square dance, $20–45 ([tickets](https://www.tickettailor.com/events/panorama/2115784)). The scene is threaded with **animation-industry artists**: Joe Wack, host of the Debs Park jam, is a retired animator who worked on *The Simpsons*.

Its **lineage stories** are remarkable:
- Fiddler **Mel Durham** (1914–2008) brought tunes from **Alonzo Janes** (1850–1937), a once-enslaved fiddler, from Illinois to Southern California after WWII. Bragger traced Janes' descendants, and the **Santa Barbara Old-Time Fiddlers' Festival** (outside the MSA; Bragger is artistic director) renamed its main stage for Janes ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/the-socal-old-time-music-community-3/)).
- Bragger first learned banjo from **Mike Seeger** in UCLA's ethnomusicology string-band ensemble and later directed it as a 21-person old-time ensemble in 2017 ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/the-ucla-old-time-string-band-ensemble/)).
- The late **Pete Tinker** ran the West Valley "pizza jam" at Jerry's Pizzeria, recorded every session from 2011 to 2019, and published a **tune popularity index** with *Soldier's Joy* at the top ([igotinker](https://www.igotinker.com/jam/)). The site is now a memorial, and I found no evidence the jam continues.

The **Topanga Banjo•Fiddle Contest** (1961–present; next on May 16, 2027) is the scene's origin story (see Part 5).

#### 4. Scottish, Cape Breton and the missing traditions

The **Scottish Fiddlers of Los Angeles** are about 45 years old and "nearly 60 players." They grew out of a group started by Colin Gordon and have been directed for 37 years by **Jan Tappan**, who compares them to Scotland's strathspey and reel societies. Their repertoire now takes in **Cape Breton, Scandinavian, Welsh and English** music, and they have worked with Alasdair Fraser, Natalie Haas, Ashley MacIsaac and Kevin Henderson ([FolkWorks interview](https://folkworks.org/interview/jan-tappan-and-the-scottish-fiddlers-of-los-angeles-at-45/)). They hold monthly rehearsals and jams and play concerts, including Pasadena on Oct 24, 2026, $10 ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/events/)).

Searches found **no** Scandinavian spelmanslag or Hardanger group, **no** open Balkan, Greek, Armenian, French-Canadian or Cajun jam, and **no** fiddle camp inside the MSA (the nearest is the CBA's Julian Family Fiddle Camp in San Diego County). Klezmer exists as **KAILA's free weekly Zoom workshop** (Wednesdays, 5pm), run by Gustavo Bulgach of Klezmer Juice ([KlezCalifornia](https://klezcalifornia.org/events/kaila-workshops-wednesdays/)), and occasional in-person jams, such as a sold-out July 2025 jam at Holocaust Museum LA ([HMLA](https://www.holocaustmuseumla.org/event-details/yiddish-klezmer-music-jam)).

#### 5. Dulcimers and senior centers

**Southern California Dulcimer Heritage (SCDH)**, a volunteer nonprofit, keeps a regional calendar of senior-center jams (Downey; Fountain Valley on 1st and 3rd Wednesday afternoons), Claremont park jams, Zoom hammered-dulcimer classes ($200 for 8) and a "dulcimer rescue" consignment program ([SCDH](https://www.scdh.org/SCDH/Calendar.html)). Anaheim's **Living Tradition** monthly folk jam is now an occasional special event ([TLT](https://www.thelivingtradition.org/)).

### Geography: six clusters, no folk neighborhood

1. **East San Fernando Valley / Burbank–North Hollywood:** the Celtic Arts Center (Mon), Lawless Brewing (Wed), Izay Park (Sun). The likeliest place for a visitor to base.
2. **Northeast LA to the San Gabriel Valley:** Echo Park's 1642, Debs Park, Pasadena (Conservatory, Scottish Fiddlers, Orange Grove Friends), Arcadia (Mt. Lowe). The old-time and Scottish heartland.
3. **Harbor / Long Beach–South Bay:** Auld Dubliner (Sun), Dog Park (Sat), Signal Hill (Tue), Project Barley Lomita, El Camino College, COLLAGE San Pedro, Downey.
4. **Central and north Orange County:** Tustin (OC Celtic Jam), Fullerton (CSOTFA), Orange (Lamppost), Fountain Valley.
5. **South Orange County:** Mission Viejo (Patsy's ×2, Oso Creek).
6. **Eastern edge:** Covina (Fret House) and Claremont (Back Abbey, Folk Music Center, Shelton Park).

The **Westside** (Santa Monica, Culver City, Westchester) has the famous shops, McCabe's and Boulevard Music, but **no active sessions**. Downtown has only the monthly Oracle Tavern jam. Claremont to Santa Monica is about 50 miles, so a visitor should pick a cluster.

### Costs

Almost everything is **free**. Exceptions:
- OC Celtic Jam: $3 room donation.
- LA Old Time Social: $20–45 per day ($20 student).
- Scottish Fiddlers concert: $10.
- Living Tradition concerts: $25 suggested; its contras $15.
- Paid instruction runs about $55 a week for McCabe's group guitar ($220 for 4 weeks).

### Instruction

There is no Old Town School-style folk school. McCabe's offers private, kids' and group lessons ([McCabe's](https://www.mccabes.com)), as do the Folk Music Center, the Fret House and Boulevard Music (private only). Traditional specialists include Jan Tappan (Scottish fiddle), David Bragger (old-time fiddle and banjo; students have placed at Topanga and Galax), Michael Kelly (Irish fiddle) and Natalie Brejcha at the Pasadena Conservatory ([FolkWorks instruction](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/music-instruction/)).

### Shops and makers

- **McCabe's Guitar Shop**, Santa Monica (1958): stocks dulcimers, fiddles, bouzoukis and ouds; its back-room concerts are covered in Parts 3 and 5.
- **Folk Music Center**, Claremont (August 1958): founded by Charles and Dorothy Chase, Ben Harper's grandparents, in the back room of a real-estate office (rent $35 a month). It moved to 220 Yale Ave in 1970 and added a non-profit museum in 1976. It is now run by their daughter Ellen Harper; **Ben Harper bought the store to keep it in the family** ([history](https://www.folkmusiccenter.com/history)). The Chases also ran the **Golden Ring** cafe (1965–c.1970), which brought Rev. Gary Davis, Doc Watson, John Fahey and the New Lost City Ramblers to Claremont. A rival dealer once sneered that Claremont was "an island of culture in a sea of slobs" ([FMC](https://www.folkmusiccenter.com/), [museum](https://www.folkmusiccenter.com/museum)).
- **The Fret House**, Covina (1969): Elizabeth Cotten and Merle Travis played there; one of LA County's longest-running open mics ([Fret House](https://frethouse.com/pages/about-us)).
- **Violin shops:** **Hans Weisshaar** (1947), founded by a Sacconi pupil for Hollywood's studio-orchestra era, now in San Marino ([Weisshaar](https://hweisshaar.com/)); plus Metzler (Glendale) and Callier-Scollard (Pasadena).

## Part 2. Dance: international, Israeli, Scottish, English, contra, squares, morris

### History: how LA became an international-folk-dance town

**Origins (1941–46).** The Pasadena Folk Dance Co-op has danced "since 1941" ([Co-op](https://www.pasadenafolkdancecoop.org/)). Records were scarce, so early club members recorded piano or accordion tracks onto homemade discs and traded copies ([history](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/history.htm)). On **March 10, 1946**, eleven groups, including East LA and Whittier clubs, met at the first Southern California festival in Griffith Park. On **May 11, 1946** the first Statewide Festival closed Ojai's main street: 500 dancers and 3,000 spectators.

**The loyalty-oath split (1948–50).** After the Soviet-backed takeover of Czechoslovakia, a motion was made to require every member club to swear a loyalty oath. It was defeated, but "many clubs had already dropped out, including all of the square dance clubs, who formed their own association in 1950." Some leaders then branded European dances "foreign" ([history](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/history.htm)). Modern western square dance still runs through separate associations ([Associated Square Dancers club list](https://asquared.org/associated-clubs/)) and barely overlaps with the folk world.

**The coffeehouse era (1964–2007).** LA's distinctive contribution was the paid-admission folk dance coffeehouse:

- **The Intersection** (1964–1984, Alvarado St, then W. Temple St) drew Marlon Brando, Ginger Rogers and Omar Sharif, and spawned many other coffeehouses ([Intersection archive](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/coffeehouses/intersection.htm)).
- **Café Danssa** opened in December 1965 on West Pico, Israeli on some nights and Greek, Balkan or Brazilian on others ([Jewish Journal, 2007](https://jewishjournal.com/culture/arts/14358/)). Founder Dani Dassa sold it in the early 1970s. From Dec 31, 1975 until February 2007 it was owned by jazz composer **Dave Blume** and folk singer **Carolyn Hester**, the Greenwich Village singer who hired Bob Dylan to play harmonica. Neither danced. The family memoir calls Danssa "the first stop for Israeli immigrants when they land at LAX."
- Others included **Hadarim** (1969–75, Fairfax), **Veselo** in Anaheim (1970; its successor club closed on Dec 31, 2023) and about two dozen more ([coffeehouse index](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/coffeehouses.htm), [Veselo Selo](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/coffeehouses/veselo_selo.htm)).

**Why so big in LA?** Not settled, but the sources point to a federation that standardized dances, a large Jewish community and Israeli teachers, Hollywood glamour and the coffeehouse model, and cheap public recreation space. The Federation blames the 1980s decline on inflation and the loss of free public halls, and admits the repertoire never absorbed the dances of LA's own Mexican, Central American and Asian immigrants.

**Contra and ECD arrive later.** The Living Tradition began in **1982** as patio contras at Carolyn Russell's home in Garden Grove and moved to the Downtown Anaheim Community Center in 1999 ([TLT history](https://www.thelivingtradition.org/tltbodylhistory.html)). Culver City ECD started in January 2006 and took over the regional ball (first held in 2001) as "Playford-to-the-Present" ([CCECD history](https://culvercityecd.com/a-brief-history-of-ccecd/)). The **Southern California Community Dance Coalition** (SCCDC), long the umbrella for LA contra and ECD, began calling all its contras "California Twirl" in 2025 ([SCCDC, archived March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250313223404/https://www.sccdc.org/)).

**COVID and after.** Federation member clubs fell to 22. The Westwood Co-op (active by 1946) closed in 2024, and in February 2024 the Federation formed an Outreach Committee "to slow and stop the decline of international folk dancing" ([history](https://www.socalfolkdance.org/history.htm)). In July 2026 Narodni in Bellflower went "dark until further notice" ([Narodni](http://www.callicomp.info/narodniorg/calendar.htm)).

### What exists now, strand by strand

#### Contra (≈4.2 dances/month, Saturday only)
- **SCCDC "California Twirl"** contras: 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 5th Saturdays, with a lesson around 6:30, dancing until 10 (11 on 5th Saturdays), live bands, gender-neutral "Larks and Robins" calling, and a sliding scale with $20 suggested ($30 on 5th Saturdays).
  - The **2nd Saturday** is at **St. James Episcopal, South Pasadena** (Nov 14: caller Lindsay Verbil).
  - The venue for the **1st and 3rd Saturdays** is disputed: 2026 search listings say the **Culver City Masonic Lodge**, while a 2025 scrape and FolkWorks say the **Woman's Club of South Pasadena** ([contradances.net](https://contradances.net/events/locate/us/ca), [FolkWorks list](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/contra-dancing/)).
  - **5th Saturdays** are a four-hour experienced dance, usually at **Skandia Hall, Pasadena**.
  - The SCCDC Meetup says "typically we get about 50–60 people per dance night" ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/contra-dance-los-angeles-area-sccdc-org/)).
- **The Living Tradition**, Anaheim: 4th Saturdays most months. A potluck is part of the format, then a 6:10 lesson and dancing 6:30–9:20, for $15. Nov 28 features Whirled Peas; there is no dance in October or December ([TLT dances](https://www.thelivingtradition.org/tltbodydance.html)). It is the only contra in Orange County.
- **No weeknight contra exists anywhere in the metro.** San Diego Folk Heritage's FolkWorks listing (undated) shows four dances a month in a metro a quarter LA's size.
- Weekends: **Fiddling Frog** at the Pasadena Masonic Temple, Feb 26–28, 2027; $205 for adults, sliding $100–180 for ages 18–30, youth free ([Fiddling Frog](https://fiddlingfrog.com/info-2/)). **Camp Sturtevant** on Memorial Day weekend is a 4-mile hike into the Angeles National Forest, with donkeys for gear; the 2020 Bobcat Fire and 2025 Eaton Fire closed access to it.

#### English country dance and historical balls (≈9/month)
- **Culver City ECD**: 2nd and 4th Sundays 2:30–5:30 at the Lindberg Park Stone House, always live music, $20 sliding ([upcoming](https://culvercityecd.com/upcoming-ccecd-dances/)).
- **Monrovia ECD**: 2nd and 4th Fridays, $5 cash, St. Luke's Guildhall ([Monrovia](https://monroviaecd.org/)).
- **Historical Tea & Dance Society**: 2nd, 4th and 5th Tuesdays at Skandia Lodge, Pasadena, $10 ([CDSS listing](https://cdss.org/event/english-country-dance-classes-in-pasadena/2026-10-13/)).
- **Orange County ECD**: odd Saturdays at the Downtown Anaheim Community Center, $15, pre-registration through the city's parks site ([OCECD](http://www.ocecd.sdecd.org/)).
- Balls: **Playford-to-the-Present**, Oct 3, 2026, Pasadena Scottish Rite, $50/60 ([CDSS](https://cdss.org/event/2026-playford-to-the-present-ball-weekend/2026-10-02/)); **Jane Austen Evening**, Jan 23, 2027, Pasadena Masonic ([JAE](https://www.janeaustenevening.org/)); **Admiral Nelson Ball**, June 5, 2027, Fullerton.

#### International folk dance (≈76/month), including Israeli, Balkan and Greek
- **Evening anchors:** the **Pasadena Folk Dance Co-op**, every Friday at St. James, South Pasadena, $8, with a beginners' hour on 1st and 3rd Fridays ([events](https://www.pasadenafolkdancecoop.org/events/)); **Foothill International Folk Dancers**, Thursdays, La Cañada ([foothill.dance](https://foothill.dance/)); **Friday Night L.I.F.E. - **Greek:** the Kypseli Greek Dance Center holds periodic Sunday parties at The Tango Room, Sherman Oaks, with live bands ($25 including appetizers).

#### Scottish country dance (≈27/month) and Irish céilí (≈3/month)
- **RSCDS Los Angeles** ([classes](http://www.rscds-losangeles.org/Classes.html), [events](http://www.rscds-losangeles.org/Events.html)): Van Nuys on Mondays (at Valley College since March 1974), Santa Monica on Tuesdays (experienced), Torrance on Fridays (35+ years; hybrid), a monthly dance on the 4th Saturday, the St. Andrew's Ball, and a **60th-anniversary ball** on Nov 28, 2026 in Long Beach.
- **RSCDS Orange County**: Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday classes in Tustin ([OC classes](http://www.rscds-oc.org/Classes.html)). Four SoCal branches hold a September Interbranch Dance and a dance after the Costa Mesa Highland Games.
- **Irish céilí:** the Celtic Arts Center's free Monday class (see Part 1). I found no regular public **Irish set-dance** class or live-band céilí in LA or OC.

#### Squares: two different worlds
- **Modern western** (called, recorded music, graded levels): about 19 sessions a month across 15 clubs. Ribbons and Souls in Culver City advertises that dancers "are free to choose their dancing gender," and **Golden State Squares** in Irvine is an LGBTQ+ club ([GSS](https://www.iagsdc.com/goldenstate/)).
- **Traditional old-time squares** with live string bands are occasional only: the LA Old Time Social's evening dance (Part 1), with a square-calling workshop by Jeremy Horton ([Panorama](https://panoramaonview.org/events/15th-ever-la-old-time-social)), and an "L.A. Squares" dance in September 2025 ([ma.to](https://ma.to/venue/socalsquaredance)).

  **For Nate:** there is no monthly old-time square dance series. That is a real gap for an old-time fiddler.

#### Morris
**Rising Phoenix Morris** practices Mondays in Santa Monica and dances the sun up at **6am on May Day at Venice Pier** ([RPM](https://www.risingphoenixmorris.org/calendar.htm)).

#### Cajun/zydeco and Scandinavian: the two near-absences
- **Cajun/zydeco.** In Oct 2026 the arnb.org Pacific calendar shows **no dances in LA or Orange County** through February 2027 ([arnb](https://www.arnb.org/pacific.php?genre=cz)). What remains: occasional zydeco bar nights and the commercial **Crawfish Festival** in Fountain Valley with zydeco lessons ([Crawfish Fest](https://www.crawfishfestival.com/)). The Creole background is in Part 4.
- **Scandinavian.** Skandia South (Anaheim) and the Culver City Skandia dances still appear in FolkWorks lists, but I found no 2024–26 evidence. They are not counted.

### Geography

Activity clusters in three places: **the Pasadena / South Pasadena / Monrovia / La Cañada foothills** (contra, ECD, the Folk Dance Co-op, balls, Fiddling Frog); **the Westside and Culver City** (contra, Culver City ECD, Balkan, RSCDS Santa Monica, morris); and **Anaheim / Tustin / Laguna Woods** in Orange County (contra, ECD, RSCDS classes, international clubs). Israeli dance lives in the **San Fernando Valley**, and the daytime international clubs follow senior centers in the **San Gabriel Valley and Cerritos**. Downtown, South and East LA are almost empty of these forms today.

### Who shows up

The crowd is aging. Jan Rayman wrote in 2017 that when she and Marc arrived in 1986 "we heard many expressions of concern about how folk dancing here was getting smaller and grayer"; thirty years later they were still "some of the younger folk dancers in the area" ([Rayman](https://socalfolkdance.org/articles/foothill_folk_dancers_rayman.htm)). Contra's gender-neutral calling and youth pricing point to a younger target audience, but no age data was found.

## Part 3. Song, traditional singing, festivals and radio

### 1. Song circles and hoots: the living-room scene

#### Songmakers
Songmakers is the backbone. Its [WordPress event calendar](https://songmakers.org/events/month/) (Oct 2026) shows these LA County circles, among others:

| Circle | When | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [West LA Hoot & Potluck](https://www.meetup.com/songmakers/) | 4th Sun 4–8pm | private home, West LA | Meetup RSVP, capped at 20, potluck at 6. "Everyone gets one or more turns": sing, lead, request, or "Pass." |
| [Topanga Sages](https://songmakers.org/event/sages-topanga-song-circle-3/) | 4th Thu 1–3:30pm | Topanga Community House | daytime; in Nov/Dec it moves outdoors "under the oaks in Red Rock Canyon" |
| [Culver City Song Circle](https://songmakers.org/event/live-culver-city-song-circle-11/) | monthly Sun 2pm | Culver City | max 15, reserve by email |
| San Pedro Song Circle at Collage | semi-regular (Jan 11, Mar 8 2026) | Collage, San Pedro | for "our geographically Southern members" |

Two things stand out. First, **the format is private and controlled**: addresses go only to people who RSVP, attendance is capped (15–20), and a potluck is built in, a hootenanny tradition adapted to a sprawling, car-dependent city. Second, **the club's centre of gravity has drifted northwest out of the MSA**: its only weekly in-person circle is at [Pitts Ranch Park in Camarillo](https://songmakers.org/event/pitts-ranch-park-song-circle-4/).

The [president's 2026 letter](https://songmakers.org/) announces a members' jam cruise through the Panama Canal departing San Pedro in March 2027. The yearly cycle includes the February **Roundup**, camping weekends, summer picnics, a workshop tent at Topanga, and the November "Music in the Mountains" ($212; San Bernardino County). Dues are $10 per household per year. The site says only that it [incorporated in the '60s](https://songmakers.org/bylaws-and-incorporation/).

#### Santa Monica Traditional Folk Music Club and Beach Cities
The [SMTFMC](https://santamonicafolkmusicclub.org/) meets on the **first Saturday, 6–9pm, in the Kaizuka Room of the Culver City Memorial Building**. Admission is "$5 at the door, if you can afford it"; "2026 is our 48th year." It is emphatically not a concert presenter: "if you are contacting us in the hopes that we will put on a concert, please don't" ([about](https://santamonicafolkmusicclub.org/about/)). Its former president is the folksinger and FolkWorks columnist Ross Altman. The [Beach Cities Folk Music Club](https://beachcitiesfolkclub.org/) meets on the **third Tuesday, 6–9pm**, around "a big red rug in the Wayland's living room" in Manhattan Beach.

#### Collage, San Pedro: a new hub
[Collage: A Place for Art and Culture](https://www.collageartculture.org/) opened in July 2021 at 731 S. Pacific Ave and has become the harbor's participatory-music centre:
- **Sea Shanty Sing**, 2nd Wednesday, 7pm, free, led by Steve Saunders and Chris Halsted.
- **Acoustic Roots Session** with Rebecca Lynn, 4th Wednesday, 7pm: a singing circle for "Appalachian, old-time, country blues, and acoustic folk" that ends "with a jam on a classic". It is the song event closest to Nate's world.
- **Sunday Ukulele Jam**, 3rd Sunday, 2–4pm, and Songmakers' new San Pedro circle.
FolkWorks shows the shanty and Acoustic Roots series as monthly since May 2025 ([FolkWorks events API](https://folkworks.org/wp-json/tribe/events/v1/events)).

### 2. Open mics and singer-songwriter rooms

- **[Kulak's Woodshed](https://kulakswoodshed.com/)**, North Hollywood (1999; volunteer-run, no alcohol). At the [Monday open mic](https://kulakswoodshed.com/open-mic-and-booking-guide/) you sign up 7–7:30pm, and **35 performers are drawn by lottery "from a basket on the stage," one song each**; every performance is webcast and archived.
- **[Grand Annex Open Mic](https://www.grandvision.org/)**, San Pedro, 2nd Thursday: sign-up 6:30pm, first 12 guaranteed, two songs in under 8 minutes, $5–8.
- **[Folk Music Center Open Mic](https://www.folkmusiccenter.com/events)**, Claremont, last Sunday at 6pm, $3: "Two songs or five minutes … All styles of music welcome!"

The historical antecedent is the Troubadour's Monday hoot (see Part 5).

### 3. Listening rooms and presenters

**[McCabe's Guitar Shop](https://www.mccabes.com/concerts/)** (Santa Monica; history in Part 5) remains the flagship, a 150-seat room. Its ticket catalog shows about 40 shows from late summer 2026 into early 2027 at **$24–48**, including Holly Near, Les Frères Savoy (Cajun), Chris Smither and **Nora Brown & Stephanie Coleman** (old-time banjo and fiddle, Jan 7, 2027).

**[Pasadena Folk Music Society](https://pasadenafolkmusicsociety.org/)** began as the [Caltech Folk Music Society, founded in 1983 by grad students Brian Toby and Simon Davies](https://pasadenafolkmusicsociety.org/about/). It presents roughly monthly at Knox Presbyterian Church and Caltech's Beckman Auditorium: April Verch & Joe Newberry (Oct 31, 2026), the Tannahill Weavers (Nov 14), and in 2025–26 Andy Irvine and Kevin Burke. Adult tickets are $39.19 online or $40 at the door. Its ["Hear Here"](https://pasadenafolkmusicsociety.org/hear-here/) page is a useful regional roots-concert list.

**Shops as venues.** Much of LA's acoustic presenting happens in instrument stores: [Boulevard Music](https://www.boulevardmusic.com/product-category/concert-tickets/) (Culver City), [The Fret House](https://www.frethouse.com/) (Covina), whose basement stage "The Underground" was "in full swing in 2024", and the Folk Music Center (Claremont; see Part 1), which presents $10 concerts.

**San Pedro's cluster.** Besides Collage, the [Grand Annex](https://www.grandvision.org/) books folk-rock and blues. Per capita, San Pedro is now the most active folk-song neighborhood in the metro.

**Orange County is thin.** I found no OC song circle, shape-note singing or shanty sing; OC's participatory traditional music is instrumental (Part 1).

**House concerts.** The best-known series, [Russ & Julie's House Concerts](http://www.houseconcerts.us/) (since 1997; invitation-only; suggested $25 donation), is in **Oak Park, Ventura County**, just outside the MSA, but the LA community treats it as its own.

### 4. Traditional singing

#### Sacred Harp and shape-note
[Los Angeles Sacred Harp](https://lasacredharp.org/) lists three monthly singings, all using the 2025 Denson *Sacred Harp*:
- **1st Sundays, 3:30–6pm, Eagle Rock**: in the O'Leary home.
- **2nd Saturdays, 2–4:30pm, Mar Vista**: "Westside Shapenote."
- **4th Saturdays, 2–4:30pm, Pasadena**: at the **Orange Grove Friends (Quaker) Meetinghouse** (also *Christian Harmony*, with loaner books).

The **All-California Sacred Harp Convention** "rotates every year between the San Francisco Bay Area and Los Angeles"; the next is in the Bay Area, Jan 16–17, 2027. The [fasola.org minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/) show a **Southern California / Los Angeles Regional Singing each May from 1995 to 2017**, then nothing. The regional all-day singing died, but the monthly singings carry on, two of them in homes, mirroring the Songmakers model.

#### Sea shanties
The original **San Pedro Shanty Sing** was founded by volunteers of the Los Angeles Maritime Institute, who chose a pub because "we'd like a pint of beer to go with the songs," and met upstairs at the Whale & Ale ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/article/san-pedro-shanty-sing/)), which [closed abruptly in June 2023](https://www.randomlengthsnews.com/archives/2023/06/08/whale-ale/45073). Collage revived the sing in May 2025: "A port town deserves nautical music, and we're bringing it back." A search for other shanty sings in LA, Long Beach and Orange County found **nothing verifiable**.

#### Balkan, Eastern European and other ensemble singing
**[Nevenka Folk Ensemble](https://www.nevenka.org/about)**, an LA women's chorus founded in **1976**, sings Albanian, Armenian, Bulgarian, Croatian, Georgian, Greek, Hungarian, Macedonian, Russian, Ukrainian, Ladino and Romani songs. It is the clearest survivor of the 1970s international-folk revival in the song layer. Searches for a Georgian polyphony choir, a Yiddish song circle and Black gospel quartet "anniversary" programs found nothing verifiable online.

For Scots-language song, the [Celtic Arts Center](https://folkworks.org/event/the-celtic-arts-center-presents-iona-fyfe-in-a-robert-burns-song-workshop/) hosted Iona Fyfe's Robert Burns song workshop in July 2026 ($30).

### 6. Radio and media

| Station | Show | Host(s) | When | Since |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KPFK 90.7 | [FolkScene](https://www.kpfk.org/on-air/folkscene/) | Allen Larman & Kat Griffin; engineer Peter Cutler (since late '70s) | Sun 6–8pm | 1970 |
| KPFK | [Roots Music & Beyond](https://www.kpfk.org/on-air/roots-music-and-beyond/) | Tom Nixon / Patrick Milligan / Art Podell / Mark Humphrey / Mary Katherine Aldin (by week of the month) | Sat 7–9am | ? |
| KXLU 88.9 | [Wildwood Flower Radio Hour](https://www.wildwoodflowerradiohour.com/) | Mike Bone; resident fiddler DJ Meeks | Tue 7–10pm (own site; FolkWorks says 7–9) | ? |

**FolkScene's origin story** is the best in the dossier. Howard and Roz Larman were 1960s Songmakers members; Howard pitched KPFK a weekly broadcast hoot. Before KPFK replied, "one of the legendary Songmakers blowups occurred": the board voted to donate to an orphans' home in North Vietnam. Howard worked for a military contractor and feared losing his clearance, so the Larmans quit, and when KPFK came back they offered a folk program instead. The punchline: "the U.S. Government itself was contributing to the orphans' home" ([Songmakers memorial page](https://songmakers.org/roz-larman/)). Howard died in 2007 and Roz in October 2016, after 46 years on air. Their son Allen co-hosts today.

Losses: KCSN's *Bluegrass, Etc.* with Frank Hoppe ended in September 2017 after nearly 30 years ([Bluegrass Today](https://bluegrasstoday.com/bluegrass-etc-gone-from-kcsn/)).

**FolkWorks** is the scene's connective tissue. Leda and Steve Shapiro [founded it in January 2001](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/brd-steve-shapiro/); volunteers distributed **15,000 newspapers bi-monthly** across Southern California until 2007, when it went online ([25th anniversary page](https://folkworks.org/event/folkworks-25th-anniversary-benefit-concert/)). Its event calendar remains the single best machine-readable source for SoCal folk events.

## Part 4. Heritage and diaspora traditions

### Mexican and Central American traditions

#### Mariachi: thriving

Mariachis have gathered at "La Boyle" (First Street and Boyle Avenue) since the 1930s, waiting outside a donut shop for hire ([MPFF history](https://www.mariachiplazafestivalfoundation.org/about-us/history); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariachi_Plaza)). In 1989 a group including **Nati Cano** (Los Camperos) launched the **Mariachi Festival y Fiesta de Santa Cecilia**, which now advertises its 35th edition. Cano (NEA Heritage Fellow 1990, [Monterey Park](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/natividad-cano)) is the pivotal figure. **Mariachi de Uclatlán** (UCLA, 1961) calls itself the first mariachi at an academic university ([UCLA](https://schoolofmusic.ucla.edu/ensembles/mariachi-de-uclatlan/)), and **Mariachi USA** has filled the Hollywood Bowl since 1990 ([FAQ](https://mariachiusa.com/faq/)). **Garfield High School** in East LA has about 50 students in three mariachi ensembles ([Garfield](https://www.garfieldhs.org/apps/pages/index.jsp?uREC_ID=183910&type=u&pREC_ID=1660090)).

#### Son jarocho and the fandango: thriving

LA's jarocho story has two layers: the immigrant singer **José Gutiérrez**, a 1989 NEA Heritage Fellow listed in Norwalk ([arts.gov](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/jose-gutierrez)), and the Chicano fandango revival. In 1992 Quetzal Flores "transplanted the Fandango to East Los Angeles as a local sustainable community practice" ([quetzalflores.com](http://www.quetzalflores.com/)). Today **Son del Sereno** meets every Friday 7–9pm at the **Eastside Cafe** in El Sereno, where Las Cafeteras started as students in 2005 ([collectives](https://www.eastsidecafela.com/collectives)); **Son del Centro** in Santa Ana still runs a Thursday taller; and the **Encuentro de Jaraneros de California** reached its 24th edition, set for November 1, 2026 at LA Plaza and postponed for forecast rain ([LA Plaza](https://lapca.org/event/24th-annual-encuentro-de-jaraneros/)).

#### Oaxacan bandas and the Guelaguetza: active, under pressure

The **Banda Filarmónica Santa María Xochixtepec**, a village brass band re-formed in Koreatown nearly 50 years ago, is "the origin and epicenter" of LA's Oaxacan bands ([Impulso, March 2026](https://impulsonewspaper.com/la-banda-filarmonica-santa-maria-xochixtepec-son-pioneros-de-la-musica-oaxaquena-en-los-angeles/)). "Growing up, you either played with the band or danced for the band," one director says. **ORO's Guelaguetza** (1988) was cancelled in 2025 "because of the immigration raids that terrorized migrant communities," and returned on August 23, 2026 in Inglewood to a noticeably smaller crowd ([Impulso](https://impulsonewspaper.com/la-guelaguetza-de-los-angeles-brindo-un-dia-de-conexion-y-cultura/)). The same enforcement surge cancelled more than 20 LA-area July 4th events in 2025 ([CALÓ News](https://www.calonews.com/communities/la-county-cities-cancel-or-postpone-summer-events-due-to-immigration-raids/article_ce6d4d51-983b-4aed-8edb-a22b1ede7580.html)).

#### Other Mexican-rooted practices

- **Danza Azteca** (active): Ritual Azteca holds free open practices with the huehuetl drum every Tuesday and Thursday.
- **Día de los Muertos** (thriving): Self Help Graphics held LA's first large procession in 1973 ([Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/day-of-the-dead-in-los-angeles-self-help-graphics-art/TgUhcMFIjQzwQg?hl=en)).

#### Central America and the Caribbean

- **Garifuna** (active): the Garifuna American Heritage Foundation United's drumming ensemble played MacArthur Park in July 2026 ([MAC·LA](https://mac-la.org/artist/gahfu-garifuna-drumming-ensemble/)).
- **Afro-Cuban** (active): Kati Hernández's weekly rumba with live drums, and the legacy of two batá drummers who were NEA Fellows.
- **Brazilian** (active): Samba Los Angeles bateria school and Capoeira Angola.

On top of these come the **Nisei Week** closing ondo (the festival dates from 1934, [Nisei Week](https://www.niseiweek.org/about/)). LA Kansai Club's **Team Kawachi Ondo** hands out 1,000 free uchiwa fans at Obons each summer to pull bystanders into the circle ([Cultural News](https://www.culturalnews.com/?p=49942)). The dances are free, the steps repeat, and newcomers are welcomed: in practical terms this is LA's closest analogue to a contra dance.

#### Taiko, minyo, koto and Okinawan arts

Kinnara began as Rev. Masao Kodani's gatha-chanting group; taiko followed after the 1969 Obon, and membership is by *dana* (giving time, talent or money) ([Senshin](https://senshintemple.org/groups/kinnara/)). The kaDON Taiko Block Party runs on Labor Day ([kaDON](https://kadon.com/taiko-block-party-little-tokyo/)). Koto player **June Kuramoto** (Alhambra; founding member of Hiroshima) became an NEA Fellow in 2024 ([arts.gov](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/june-kuramoto)). The **Okinawa Association of America** runs its center in Gardena ([OAA](https://www.oaamensore.org/)).

### Korean, Filipino, Southeast Asian, Chinese and South Asian traditions

- **Korean (active).** The Korean Resource Center (1983) still runs summer poongmul camps that pair drumming with political education ([KRC](https://www.krcla.org/intro)).
- **Filipino (active).** Rondalla master **Tagumpay Mendoza De Leon** (Burbank) was a 2021 NEA Fellow ([arts.gov](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/tagumpay-mendoza-de-leon)); **Kayamanan Ng Lahi** has taught Philippine folk dance since 1990.
- **Cambodian (active, weakened).** Long Beach has about 20,000 Cambodians, described as the largest concentration outside Cambodia ([Long Beach Post](https://lbpost.com/esd/hi-lo/photos-cambodia-town-parade-long-beach-2025/)). But the **Khmer Arts Academy**, co-founded in 2002 by NEA Fellow **Sophiline Cheam Shapiro**, closed in 2022 ([KhmerConnection](https://khmerconnection.com/wiki/Khmer_Arts_Academy)).
- **Vietnamese (niche for music).** The UVSA Tết Festival draws 30,000+ ([tetfestival.org](https://tetfestival.org/)), but no living đờn ca tài tử or cải lương club could be verified online.
- **Indonesian gamelan (active).** I Nyoman and Nanik Wenten began teaching at CalArts in 1975 and were honored by the first **LA Gamelan Festival** (June 14–15, 2025) ([nwenten](https://nwenten.com/lagf-about/)).
- **Chinese (active).** The **LA Classical Chinese Orchestra** (1989, 100+ members) claims to be the largest Chinese classical orchestra in America ([chinamusic.org](https://www.chinamusic.org/)).

### Middle Eastern, Armenian, Jewish and European traditions

- **Persian (active).** Santur virtuoso **Manoochehr Sadeghi** (NEA 2003) lives in LA. In January 2026 Farhang Foundation cancelled its 16th Nowruz festival at UCLA amid loss of life in Iran ([Farhang](https://www.farhang.org/2026/farhang-cancels-2026-nowruz-festival-at-ucla)).
- **Armenian (active)** in Glendale, **Arab (active)** in Anaheim's Little Arabia ([AACC](https://aaciviccouncil.org/2025/08/30/layali-little-arabia-a-day-of-culture-resilience-and-community/)), and **Greek (thriving):** LA Greek Fest at St. Sophia draws 15,000+ ([site](https://lagreekfest.com/)).
- **Jewish (thriving and active).** Israeli dance is in Part 2 and klezmer in Part 1.
- **European.** The German Phoenix Club, founded 1960, moved from Anaheim to Brea in 2024 ([Anaheim](https://www.anaheim.gov/168/The-Phoenix-Club)), and Alpine Village (Oktoberfest since 1967) closed in 2023 ([Roadside America](https://www.roadsideamerica.com/story/21723)). Scottish Highland games run at the OC Fair & Event Center, Costa Mesa, every Memorial Day ([USS](https://www.unitedscottishsociety.com/)).

### African diaspora traditions

The **Leimert Park drum circle** began in the summer of 1989 with drummer Al "Tootie" Heath's "devotional" rhythms ([LA Times 1990](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-03-27-me-69-story.html)) and is led today by Pape Diouf ([American Community Media](https://americancommunitymedia.org/environment/papes-little-village-drum-circle-the-heart-of-leimert-park/)). The **Watts Towers Day of the Drum** reached its 43rd edition in September 2025 ([LA City](https://lacity.gov/news/watts-towers-day-drum-festival-jazz-festival)).

**Louisiana Creole (fading).** LA once held what Eater calls the largest Creole community outside Louisiana, about 15,000 people ([Eater](https://la.eater.com/2022/3/1/22956648/creole-food-cuisine-los-angeles-history-harold-belles-darrows)). In 1996 the LA Times called the culture's standard-bearers "perhaps the last in Los Angeles" ([LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-04-26-ls-62983-story.html)). FolkWorks wrote in 2015 that California's Cajun/zydeco scene survived through "church dances" ([FolkWorks 2015](https://folkworks.org/article/hot-dancing-from-southwest-louisiana/)). No recurring LA zydeco dance appeared in 2026 listings. For a fiddler this is a lost thread: la la was house-party fiddle and accordion music.

### Native Californian, powwow and Pacific traditions

- **Hawaiian (thriving).** 37 hālau are listed (18 in LA County, 19 in Orange County) ([Heleloa](https://heleloa.com/halau/)).

## Part 5. History, people, archives and the fiddle angle

### 1. Before the revival: Californios, Tongva and a $44 phonograph

The Tongva lived in as many as 100 villages across the Los Angeles Basin before colonization; the Spanish called them Gabrieleño and Fernandeño after Missions San Gabriel and San Fernando ([Wikipedia: Tongva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongva)).

The first recorded LA folk music is Californio song. Charles Fletcher Lummis, the *LA Times*' first city editor ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Fletcher_Lummis)), founded the Southwest Society in 1903. In early 1904 the society bought "an Edison recorder, horn, and cylinders for $44.25 from H. C. Fiske, Jr. & Co. of Los Angeles" and began recording Californio folksongs ([Autry Museum](https://theautry.org/explore/blog/spanish-songs-old-california)). Lummis called the project "catching archaeology alive," and in 1923 wrote that he had recorded "more than 450 unpublished Spanish Songs."

The star of the collection is **Manuela C. García**, a Los Angeles-born teacher and bookkeeper. Lummis wrote in 1905 that she "has sung the records of no less than 150 songs, with the full words! Few can do that in any language, from sheer memory." Musicologist John Koegel calls her recordings "the earliest group of recordings by one singer in L.A. history" ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/manuela-c-garcia-the-voice-behind-a-treasure-trove-of-old-mexican-songs)). Some of her cylinders, such as "Laura," are online ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/WC.B.36)).

### 2. Hillbilly radio and the Okie migration (1930–1941)

LA's first famous "hillbilly" band was invented by a radio station. In **1930**, KMPC manager Glen Rice "discovered" an enclave of rural folk in the hills above Beverly Hills and paraded them out on wagons and mules ([IWMA](https://iwesternmusic.org/performer-detail.php?id=2224)); among the hired hands driving the Beverly Hill Billies' costumed ad wagons were Jack and Woody Guthrie, "and the rural garb was not a costume." By 1933 Bob Nolan, Leonard Slye (later Roy Rogers) and Tim Spencer were the Pioneer Trio, soon the Sons of the Pioneers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_the_Pioneers)).

Woody Guthrie reached LA in May 1937 and that July began on KFVD; with Maxine Crissman ("Lefty Lou") he drew a flood of listener mail ([collection guide](https://woodyguthriecenter.org/archives/collections/maxine-crissman-woody-and-lefty-lou-radio-show-collection/)). KFVD's owners fired him in 1939 after the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie)). His LA years left **"Los Angeles New Year's Flood,"** about the New Year's 1934 Crescenta Valley flood, which he recorded for Alan Lomax in 1940 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescenta_Valley_flood_(1933_and_1934))).

### 3. The ballroom fiddle era (1940–1961)

For a fiddler, this is LA's most distinctive chapter. **Spade Cooley**, an Oklahoma-born, part-Cherokee fiddler, took over Jimmy Wakely's band at the **Venice Pier Ballroom**, where "several thousand dancers would turn out on Saturday nights" during a record 18-month run. In July 1942 Cooley fought a "battle of the bands" there against Bob Wills and claimed he won. That same year his promoter, Foreman Phillips, made **the first documented use of the term "Western swing"** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spade_Cooley)). Cooley's "Shame on You" topped the country chart for two months in 1945, and from 1948 his KTLA variety show broadcast from the **La Monica Ballroom** on Santa Monica Pier ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Pier)). In 1961 he was convicted of murdering his wife, Ella Mae.

The postwar barn-dance circuit had three anchors: **Hometown Jamboree** (1949–1959), hosted by Cliffie Stone, which moved from El Monte to the **Harmony Park Ballroom in Anaheim** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hometown_Jamboree)); **Town Hall Party** (1951–1961) at the Compton Town Hall, whose house band had **Fiddlin' Kate (Margie Warren) and Billy Hill on fiddles**, with Joe Maphis and Merle Travis on guitars ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Hall_Party)); and **the Palomino** in North Hollywood, co-founded about 1949 by Western swing bandleader Hank Penny, closed 1995 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palomino_Club_(North_Hollywood))). Capitol's *Folk Songs of the Hills* (1947) by Merle Travis gave the world "Sixteen Tons" and "Dark as a Dungeon" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merle_Travis)).

### 4. The folk revival: hootenannies, coffeehouses and the Ash Grove (1951–1973)

The revival's teacher was **Bess Lomax Hawes**. She moved to LA in 1951 and taught guitar, banjo, mandolin and folksong to large groups through UCLA Extension, and from 1963 taught at San Fernando Valley State College (now CSUN). In 1977 she became the first director of the NEA's Folk and Traditional Arts Program and created the National Heritage Fellowships ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bess_Lomax_Hawes)). One of her UCLA guitar students was **Ed Pearl** ([CSU Dominguez Hills finding aid](https://csudharchives.libraryhost.com/repositories/6/resources/511)).

Pearl, from Boyle Heights ([LA Times obituary via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/ed-pearl-whose-ash-grove-001439867.html)), raised $10,000 from family and friends and in 1958 opened the **Ash Grove** at 8162 Melrose in a converted furniture factory, with a 250-seat hall, a record store and a school teaching banjo and guitar. Ross Altman called it the "West Coast University of Folk Music" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Grove_(music_club))). It is where Clarence White first saw Doc Watson and rethought bluegrass guitar, Linda Ronstadt met Kenny Edwards and started the Stone Poneys, and Ry Cooder, 16, first played in public. It closed in 1973 after the third fire in four years; patrons blamed anti-Castro exiles angry at its Cuba events. Pearl died of COVID-19 complications on February 7, 2021, at 88.

Around the Ash Grove grew a ring of rooms:

- **The Troubadour**, opened by Doug Weston in 1957 as a 65-seat coffeehouse and moved to Santa Monica Boulevard in 1961 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Weston)). Its Monday "Hoot" auditions in the early 1960s were where labels scouted the West Coast folk revival ([Troubadour](https://www.troubadour.com/history/)); Jim McGuinn, Gene Clark and David Crosby met at its hoots in 1964 and became the Byrds ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Byrds)).
- **The Ice House** in Pasadena (1960), co-run by Bob Stane from 1961 to 1978 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Stane)).
- **The Golden Bear** in Huntington Beach, a folk club from 1963, closed in 1986; a revival is planned for Anaheim in 2028 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Bear_(nightclub))).

The most durable of all opened in 1958 as a shop: **McCabe's Guitar Shop** on Pico Boulevard, Santa Monica. Its back-room concerts began with a benefit, organized by Mike Seeger, to send the stranded Elizabeth Cotten home. McCabe's own site dates that first show to 1968; Wikipedia says 1969 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCabe%27s_Guitar_Shop)). In the mid-1970s Takoma Records ran cables from the 150-seat room to its studio two doors east, producing live albums by Norman Blake (1976), Country Gazette (1974–75) and Byron Berline (*Live at McCabe's*, 1978). The owners retired in 2020, citing COVID; concerts returned in 2022 under Nora and Walt McGraw ([McCabe's](https://www.mccabes.com/concerts)).

Topanga Canyon was the revival's rural annex: the blacklisted Will Geer moved his family to Topanga land, staged Shakespeare with other blacklisted artists, and hosted Pete Seeger and Woody Guthrie ([LAist](https://laist.com/news/arts-and-entertainment/theatricum-botanicum-topanga-canyon-historic-shakespeare-outdoor-theater-turns-50)). The Theatricum Botanicum survived the January 2025 Palisades Fire.

### 5. The scholars: UCLA, JEMF and why the archives left

**Mantle Hood** built the first US university ethnomusicology program at UCLA ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_Hood)). In 1962 **D.K. Wilgus** came from Western Kentucky to teach Anglo-American folksong ([UNC finding aid](https://finding-aids.lib.unc.edu/catalog/20003)), and collectors including Wilgus, Archie Green and Norm Cohen formed the **John Edwards Memorial Foundation** around the record collection of Australian collector John Edwards, housed at UCLA ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/jemfquarterlyser1971john)). Wilgus also ran the **UCLA Folk Festivals** of 1963 and 1964, with the Kentucky Colonels, the Dillards, the New Lost City Ramblers and Pete Seeger ([1963 Sunday concert](https://archive.org/details/calauem_000574)).

Then the archives left town. UNC Chapel Hill bought JEMF in 1983, and it became the core of the **Southern Folklife Collection** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Folklife_Collection)), which also holds McCabe's Takoma-era live masters, Wilgus's papers, and the **FolkScene** tapes from 1970 to 1997 ([finding aid](https://finding-aids.lib.unc.edu/catalog/20517)). What stayed local: the **Lummis cylinders** at the Autry's Braun Research Library, the UCLA Ethnomusicology Archive, and the **Ed Pearl / Ash Grove Collection** at CSU Dominguez Hills ([finding aid](https://csudharchives.libraryhost.com/repositories/6/resources/511)).

### 6. Fiddlers who made LA a bluegrass and newgrass town

- **The Kentucky Colonels** began as the Country Boys in **Burbank** in 1954 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Colonels_(band))). In 1964 they added the wild five-time national fiddle champion **Scotty Stoneman**, whom Jerry Garcia called "the bluegrass Charlie Parker" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotty_Stoneman)).
- **Richard Greene**, born in Beverly Hills and raised in LA, is credited with popularizing the fiddle "chop," which he said came from wrist pain and "laziness" while playing for Bill Monroe ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Greene_(musician))).
- **Byron Berline** came to Southern California after Monroe and the Army. In 1969 he played the fiddle on the Rolling Stones' "Country Honk," then worked with the Flying Burrito Brothers, Country Gazette, the L.A. Fiddle Band and California. He moved to Guthrie, Oklahoma, in 1995 and died in 2021 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Berline)).
- **Gabe Witcher** took Berline's chair in Herb Pedersen's **Laurel Canyon Ramblers** in 1995 and later co-founded the Punch Brothers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabe_Witcher)).

### 7. The Topanga Banjo-Fiddle Contest

LA's signature fiddle institution started in 1961 as the "Banjo Pickers and Open Fiddling Contest," organized by Margot Slocum and Peg Benepe in Topanga Canyon. About 500 fans came. The official history counts 30 contestants and 4 judges; Wikipedia gives 26 banjo pickers and 5 fiddlers ([official history](https://www.topangabanjofiddle.org/2020-performers); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topanga_Banjo_and_Fiddle_Contest)). In 1962 it moved to Camp Wildwood and added folk singing, old-time and bluegrass. Bess Lomax Hawes was MC in 1963, when the winners list, saved among her papers, included teenage **Richard Greene, David Lindley and Ry Cooder**; Cooder later sold his headache-loud Mastertone for a 1947 Packard ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/60-years-later-the-first-topanga-banjo-fiddle-contestants/)).

Then came Altamont. After the December 1969 stabbing, LA County supervisors banned "dangerous outdoor activities," including the contest. In 1970 Peter Feldmann moved it to UC Santa Barbara, and the demand it created there led him to found the **Santa Barbara Old Time Fiddlers' Convention** in 1972. The contest then wandered:

| Years | Site |
|---|---|
| 1971–72 | UCLA |
| 1973–77 | Santa Monica College |
| 1977–85 | UCLA |
| 1986–89 | El Camino College |
| 1990–c.2018 | Paramount Ranch, Agoura Hills |
| By 2025–26 | King Gillette Ranch, Calabasas |

The Woolsey Fire burned Paramount Ranch in November 2018. The 2026 festival was on Sunday, May 17, and the site already advertises **May 16, 2027**, with contests at 9 a.m. and performances at noon ([site](https://www.topangabanjofiddle.org/)). The rules are amateurs only (no more than 50% of income from performing), a 100-point scale, a three-minute limit, and "when competing in the Fiddle category, no other fiddle may be played on the stage at the same time" ([contest info](https://www.topangabanjofiddle.org/music-competition-info)). Alumni include Jackson Browne, David Lindley, Taj Mahal, John Hartford, Berline, Dan Crary, Stuart Duncan and Steve Martin.

Topanga also seeded a Scottish scene. Colin Gordon won the first Scottish fiddle competition in Southern California, held at Topanga, and in 1981 founded the **Scottish Fiddlers of Los Angeles** (Part 1), which calls itself "the oldest Strathspey and Reel Society in the United States" ([Scottish Fiddlers](https://scottishfiddlers.org/?page_id=66)).

### 8. The 1970s–2000s: teaching festivals, dance cafes and Celtic Mondays

- **FolkScene** (1970), **Café Danssa** (1965–2007) and the **Celtic Arts Center**'s Monday sessions are covered in Parts 3, 2 and 1.
- **The California Traditional Music Society** began in 1978 as an amateur club and was incorporated in 1983 by **Clark and Elaine Weissman** ([ACTA](https://actaonline.org/profile/california-traditional-music-society/)). The couple ran the **Summer Solstice Festival**, a teaching festival that began as "The Dulcimer Festival" at Greystone Mansion in 1980 (or 1981), moved to CSUN for 1986–90 and to Soka University in Calabasas from 1991. In 1989 the Weissmans convened the Malibu meeting that became **Folk Alliance International** ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/milestone/rip-clark-weissman/)). Clark died in 2021, and CTMS's website domain no longer resolves.
- **Coffee Gallery Backstage**, Bob Stane's 49-seat Altadena listening room, ran from 1998 until April 2023 ([Pasadena Now](https://pasadenanow.com/main/local-music-promoter-bob-stane-hospitalized-coffee-gallery-backstage-permanently-closed)).

### 9. COVID and after

McCabe's concerts stopped in 2020 and resumed in 2022; Topanga went virtual in 2020. Ed Pearl, Clark Weissman and Byron Berline died in 2021. The January 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires hit Altadena and the Topanga and Palisades area. By 2026 Topanga is booking 2027, the Celtic Arts Center is back to 11 p.m. closings, and Sacred Harp singers meet three times a month ([LA Sacred Harp](https://lasacredharp.org/)).

### 10. The fiddle angle: tunes named for LA

The honest finding is that **LA almost never shows up in fiddle-tune titles.** The Session's tune search (October 2026) returned nothing for Topanga, Malibu, Pasadena, Anaheim, Burbank, Glendale, San Pedro, Santa Monica or Long Beach. It does list a "Catalina" waltz (116 tunebooks, attributed in comments to Maurice Lennon/John Hurley, with no evidence of a Catalina Island link) and a "Hollywood" jig of unknown origin ([2886](https://thesession.org/tunes/2886), [15493](https://thesession.org/tunes/15493)). The Traditional Tune Archive blocked automated queries, so some TTA-only titles may have been missed.

The pieces that do carry LA:

- Woody Guthrie's **"Los Angeles New Year's Flood."**
- **"Shame on You"** and the Venice and Santa Monica ballroom repertoire.
- Manuela García's **"Laura," "La Ausencia," "Las Horas Amargas"** and **"La Pobre Flor,"** LA's oldest recorded traditional tunes.
- **"Huckleberry Hornpipe,"** released as a 1973 single by the LA band Country Gazette.
- **"Snowflake Breakdown,"** a contest and jam standard (its B-part chord is "the bane of unwary guitarists"), composed and first recorded in 1957 by Ontario fiddler **Wally Traugott** ([Ontario Traditional Music](https://www.ontariotraditionalmusic.ca/song1.php?id=Mjgw)). Traugott later became a senior mastering engineer in Capitol's Hollywood tower, cutting records by the Beatles, Queen and Pink Floyd ([Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/engineers-producers/wally-traugott/)).

LA's local fiddle styles are best described as three layers:

1. Californio violin-and-guitar song and dance, known mostly through the Lummis cylinders.
2. Ballroom Western swing: Cooley, Fiddlin' Kate and the Town Hall Party band.
3. A studio-polished bluegrass and newgrass line: Stoneman with the Colonels, Greene, Berline, Witcher.

Topanga and the Scottish Fiddlers bridge that line to amateurs.

## Surprises

- By event count, LA's folk scene is dancers, not fiddlers. International and Israeli folk dance alone runs about 100 meetings a month, Scottish country dance about 27, and contra only 4 (all on Saturdays). There is no weeknight contra anywhere in a metro of 12.8 million.
- The square-dance/folk-dance split was political. In 1948 Federation leaders moved to require loyalty oaths of member clubs. The motion failed, but by 1950 'all of the square dance clubs' had left to form their own association.
- The Folk Dance Federation's memorial page lists about 640 former Southern California folk dance groups. 22 remain, many now meeting in senior centers in Chinese- and Taiwanese-American suburbs (Monterey Park, Alhambra, Cerritos) and inside the gated Laguna Woods retirement village.
- One Songmakers hootenanny club, directly or indirectly, spawned KPFK's FolkScene (1970, after Howard Larman quit over a donation to a North Vietnamese orphanage), the California Traditional Music Society (1980, after the board voted out Clark Weissman) and, through the Weissmans, Folk Alliance International (Malibu, 1989).
- Bess Lomax Hawes, who created the NEA National Heritage Fellowships, spent 1951-75 teaching Angelenos guitar in mass classes. She was Ed Pearl's guitar teacher before he opened the Ash Grove, and she MC'd the 1963 Topanga contest where teenage Richard Greene, David Lindley and Ry Cooder competed.
- LA's longest-running Irish session (36+ years) is not in a pub. It meets Monday nights in a North Hollywood British social club, run by the Celtic Arts Center, which was founded in 1985 by James Connolly's grandson. Meanwhile about 15 pub sessions have died since 2002, one because 'Irish trad music has no place in an Irish pub'.
- The Westside, home of McCabe's, now has no active Irish session. Trad has migrated to the Valley, the San Gabriel Valley, Long Beach and Orange County.
- Bluegrass jams outnumber Irish sessions about 3:2 by meetings, and many are Saturday-morning park jams. The Long Beach one meets beside a dog park ('close your case, there are dogs around').
- The phrase 'Western swing' was coined in LA (1942) by Spade Cooley's promoter, the year Cooley 'beat' Bob Wills in a battle of the bands at the Venice Pier Ballroom. LA's first famous hillbilly band, KMPC's Beverly Hill Billies (1930), was a radio publicity stunt.
- Summer Obon is LA's biggest participatory folk-dance circuit: about 16 free bon odori from June to August 2026, open to anyone. Kinnara Taiko (1969) was born in an impromptu post-Obon jam at Senshin Buddhist Temple, on drums made from wine barrels. FandangObon puts son jarocho, bon odori and dabke in one circle.
- Eighteen NEA National Heritage Fellows have lived in LA or Orange County, in mariachi, jarocho, Afro-Cuban batá (twice), Brazilian pandeiro, Persian santur, kunqu opera, Cambodian dance, kathak, Filipino rondalla, koto and more. Almost none are Anglo-Celtic.
- LA once had perhaps the largest Louisiana Creole community outside Louisiana (~15,000), with church-hall zydeco dances, but no regular LA zydeco dance appears in 2026 listings.
- LA's folk sound archive mostly lives in Chapel Hill, NC (JEMF, sold to UNC in 1983; McCabe's live masters; FolkScene tapes; D.K. Wilgus's papers). Lummis's 1904 Californio cylinders (Manuela García sang ~150 songs into a $44.25 Edison machine) stayed home at the Autry.
- Altamont nearly killed the Topanga Banjo-Fiddle Contest. LA County's 1970 ban on large outdoor events sent it to UC Santa Barbara, spawning the Santa Barbara Old Time Fiddlers' Convention (1972). Topanga ran again on May 17, 2026 at King Gillette Ranch.
- The composer of the contest standard 'Snowflake Breakdown', Wally Traugott, became a Capitol Records mastering engineer in the Hollywood tower, cutting Beatles and Pink Floyd lacquers.
- A metro with the busiest US port complex has exactly one verified public sea-shanty sing (2nd Wednesdays, San Pedro), revived in 2025 by an arts space after its pub closed.
- LA's song circles live in private living rooms: RSVP-only, capped at 15-20, potluck at 6 pm. Songmakers' center of gravity has drifted into Ventura County, outside the MSA.
- The best listening rooms are inside instrument shops: McCabe's (1958; its first concert was a benefit to get a stranded Elizabeth Cotten home), the Fret House basement (open mic since 1969), Boulevard Music, and Claremont's Folk Music Center (bought by Ben Harper from his grandparents).
- LA's old-time scene is entwined with animation: the Debs Park jam host is a retired Simpsons animator. An Illinois fiddler's tunes from Alonzo Janes, a once-enslaved Tennessee fiddler, came back to life via David Bragger and the LA Old Time Social.
- AI search summaries were repeatedly, confidently wrong: they placed Madison's spelemannslag and Cincinnati's Riley School in LA and read 'Pasadena OR Long Beach' as Oregon.

## Data quality and caveats

Web search was heavily rate-limited across all threads (shared quota), and Facebook, Instagram and Meetup pages were mostly unreadable, so the census undercounts groups that organize only on social media or email lists, especially old-time jams, house concerts and heritage practice groups. The main contra organizer's website (California Twirl / SCCDC) was down all session; sources disagree whether the 1st and 3rd Saturday contras meet in Culver City or South Pasadena. Some dance listings show future dates (Nov-Dec 2026); these are recorded as last_evidence 2026-10 with next_listed_date. Modern square-dance club schedules come mostly from a Jan 2025 association listing (medium confidence). RSCDS Orange County class evidence is from May 2025. Scandinavian dance groups (Skandia South etc.) had no 2024-26 evidence and are not counted. Bon odori practice-night counts (~4.7 a month averaged) are estimates. Several heritage founding years were derived from edition numbers. Unverified: whether CTMS still exists (website gone), when its Summer Solstice festival last ran, current folk shows on KUCI/KCSN/KSPC, Songmakers' founding year, and Topanga attendance. Not found: Yiddish song circles, Georgian choirs, Black gospel quartet programs, a dedicated Irish music school, and an active LA zydeco dance series; these are 'not found', not 'absent'. The Traditional Tune Archive blocked automated access, so the tunes-named-for-LA result is medium confidence. Overlaps with Riverside: Claremont items (Back Abbey session, Folk Music Center, Israeli dance) and San Dimas's Huck Finn Jubilee are in LA County and counted here; the Riverside file lists them as outside_msa.
