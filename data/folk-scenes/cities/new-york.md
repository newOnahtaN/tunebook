# New York-Newark-Jersey City: the folk scene (dossier, October 2026)

*Synthesis of five research threads (Irish/Celtic, old-time and dance, song and listening, heritage and diaspora,
history and archives) for the 23-county New York-Newark-Jersey City MSA (pop. 20.1 million). Structured data is in
`new-york.json`. Research only: web fetches and APIs, no audio or video played, nothing committed or pushed.*

## Scene in a nutshell

New York is less a folk scene than the place where folk music was recorded, named, published and argued over, and
it still runs on that history. In October 2026 the metro had roughly 410 participatory event-nights a month (about
325 if you keep only medium- and high-confidence listings). Irish sessions lead, at 78 to 141 a month, the legacy
of 1920s Sligo fiddlers whose Manhattan 78s standardized Irish music worldwide. Next comes a bar-and-brewery
bluegrass circuit (about 70 jams a month) that dwarfs old-time (about 15). English country dance has run here since
Cecil Sharp's 1915 visit, but contra is small for 20 million people (about 9 dances a month). Song lives in
volunteer institutions (FMSNY, the Peoples' Voice Cafe, New Jersey's Folk Project), a weekly Sacred Harp singing and
harbor shanty sings, while Brooklyn's Jalopy anchors the roots revival. And the heritage layer is vast: bomba,
rumba, steelpan, rara, tassa, klezmer, Balkan brass, mariachi and powwows, much of it nurtured by CTMD since 1968.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **New York set the standard for Irish music in Ireland.** Most 1920s Irish 78s were cut in the New York area, and
   their repertoire became, in ITMA's words, ["a de facto national standard that persists in Ireland to this
   day"](https://newyork.itma.ie/recording-age/).    Coleman, Killoran, Morrison and McGann are buried near one another in
      St. Raymond's Cemetery in the Bronx.
2. **The first country fiddle records were made in Manhattan.** Eck Robertson recorded "Arkansaw Traveler" and
   "Sallie Gooden" for Victor in New York on June 30-July 1, 1922; Gid Tanner, Uncle Dave Macon, Ernest Stoneman and
   Charlie Poole's band all made their first records here in 1924-25 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eck_Robertson)).
3. **The string band was born on the Bowery.** The Virginia Minstrels (Dan Emmett on fiddle, banjo, bones,
   tambourine) debuted on Feb 6, 1843 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Minstrels)); a year earlier
   Dickens watched a Five Points fiddler "go at it tooth and nail"
   ([*American Notes*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/675/pg675.txt)).
4. **Long Island's great painter was a dance fiddler who patented a violin.** William Sidney Mount copied out
   hundreds of tunes, wrote "In the Cars on the Long Island Railroad" (1850), and patented a hollow-backed "Cradle of
   Harmony" fiddle in 1852; the Crystal Palace judges declared every violin "unfit for competition"
   ([Bard Graduate Center](https://www.bgc.bard.edu/about/news/618/01-jun-2017-a-cradle-of-harmony-at-the-crystal-palace)).
5. **North America's first Comhaltas branch was in Mineola, Long Island** (1972-73), and the first US qualifying
   fleadh was held in the same hall ([Irish American Society](https://www.irishamericansociety.org/cce-mulligan-quinn-comhaltas)).
6. **Bluegrass, not old-time, is the city's big string-band practice.** About 71 bluegrass jam-nights a month vs 15
   old-time across the metro, many in Irish pubs, breweries, a canoe club and a yacht club
   ([nybluegrass.com](https://nybluegrass.com/)).
7. **The only square-dance club left in New York City is LGBTQ+.** Times Squares (1984) calls itself "New York City's
   only square dance club" and the country's largest LGBT-and-friends club ([Times Squares](https://timessquares.nyc/)).
8. **A Manhattan folk-arts nonprofit's recordings are in interstellar space.** Bulgarian singing recorded by CTMD's
   founders is on the Voyager Golden Record ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/ethel-raim)), and the same
   center later spun off the Mariachi Academy and hatched Cherish the Ladies.
9. **The Cabaret Law made social dancing illegal in unlicensed rooms until 2017.** It killed the Eagle Tavern's
   Friday céilís in the 1980s and capped such rooms at three musicians, no wind instruments
   ([ITMA / Don Meade](https://newyork.itma.ie/revival-years/eagle-tavern-blarney-star/)).
10. **The word "hootenanny" came to New York from Seattle.** Seeger and Guthrie picked it up at 1941 Washington
    Commonwealth Federation fund-raisers and used it for their Almanac House rent parties
    ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hootenanny)).

## Trad instrumental and dance

### Irish and "Celtic"

**Scale and shape.** The metro has 43 Irish session listings: about 141 session-nights a month if you count everything,
78 with medium or high confidence. They range from mic'd house gigs in Manhattan to Brooklyn learners' jams and
suburban Comhaltas outposts. There are six Comhaltas branches (Úll Mór; Killoran-Clancy-Whelan; Coleman-O'Grady;
Mulligan-Quinn; Martin Mulvihill; Rafferty-Madden), the [Irish Arts Center](https://irishartscenter.org/capital-campaign/the-building)
(founded 1972; its $60 million building opened Dec 8, 2021, inside the façade of a century-old tire shop), and the
[New York Irish Center](https://www.newyorkirishcenter.org/events) in Long Island City (2004).

**The weekly circuit.** Monday is the surprise peak night:
- [Landmark Tavern](https://blarneystar.com/work-1) (Hell's Kitchen, 8pm), Don Meade with a named weekly guest, in a
  bar dating from 1868.
- [Hartley's](https://www.hartleysnyc.com/) in Clinton Hill, led by Matt Stapleton.
- [Iona](https://ionascottishsession.com/) in Williamsburg (9pm), the only regular Scottish-repertoire session in the
  metro: West Highland pipe tunes on fiddle and small pipes, weekly since 2013.

Tuesday brings the classic open round-robin at [Sláinte](https://thesession.org/sessions/17) on the Bowery and the
mic'd [Swift](https://www.instagram.com/p/DdCbDHJGysa/) house session a block away. Wednesday has
[Grace's](https://www.gracesnyc.com/music/) on 14th Street, the every-other-week queer Irish trad session at Mary's
Bar in Greenpoint, and the first-Wednesday [Brooklyn Traditional Slow Jam](https://www.meetup.com/brooklyn-traditional-slow-jam/events/dxdkftyhcfbhb/)
at the Old Stone House. Thursday is [Mary O's](http://www.maryossession.com/) on Avenue A, founded in 2014 by
Caitlin Warbelow and the most beginner-friendly room in Manhattan ($15 suggested for leaders).

Sunday is the big day:
- [An Béal Bocht](https://www.anbealbochtcafe.com/events-programs) in Riverdale (3:30pm), "the Bronx's longest
  running Irish trad session".
- Rullo's in Park Slope (2nd and last Sundays).
- [The Dead Rabbit](https://www.thedeadrabbit.com/whats-on/sunday-seisiun) (a polished closed house band since 2013).
- [The Laurels](https://thelaurelsnyc.com/), and late, Tony DeMarco's session at the [11th Street Bar](https://11thstbar.com/)
  from 9:30pm: open in principle, upper-intermediate to advanced in practice.
- Suburbs: Moriarty's on McLean Avenue in Yonkers, the Cat and Fiddle session at The Hutton in Jersey City, the
  Cold Spring session (2nd and 4th Sundays).

Saturday is thin in the city: the [New York Irish Center's learners' slow session](https://thesession.org/sessions/2895)
(3pm), monthly afternoon sessions at Farrell's (Windsor Terrace) and Whiskey Red's (East Meadow), and the Irish
American Association of Northwest Jersey's third-Saturday slow-plus-regular double bill.

**Geography.** ITMA's present-day essay notes that the Irish bars that "once lined Broadway in Inwood and Bainbridge
Avenue in the Bronx are gone". The strongholds now are Katonah Avenue in Woodlawn, McLean Avenue in Yonkers, the
Rockaways and "especially, in and around Pearl River" ([ITMA](https://newyork.itma.ie/present-day/)), which is
where the teaching lives (the [Pearl River School of Irish Music](https://www.prsoim.com/), Rose Conway Flanagan's
school, the Erin Loughran school). Sessions cluster instead where young players drink: the East Village and NoHo,
Hell's Kitchen, and brownstone Brooklyn (at least eight Brooklyn sessions listed in 2025-26). Long Island runs on a
rotating collective, the [Grafton Street Open Irish Session](https://thesession.org/sessions/7990). New Jersey's
sessions are spread thin, from Jersey City and Montvale to Hillsborough, Highland Park, Lambertville and Spring Lake.
No current Staten Island session was found.

**Dance.** Set and céilí dancing survives in Irish clubhouses: weekly lessons at the Irish American Society in
Mineola (Wednesdays, $15) and the Bergen Irish Association in Dumont (Mondays, $10), a term course at the Irish
Arts Center ($204 for 8 weeks), and monthly céilís by Mulligan-Quinn CCÉ ($20) and at the NYIC. The IAC says its
course teaches "the popular dances done at céilís every weekend in the metropolitan area", but the main céilí
calendar (Maureen Donachie's Ceol Agus Rince, emailed to "nearly 2,000") could not be read by script, so céilís are
undercounted. Step dance is huge and competitive (Inishfree, McManus, O'Donnell, Niall O'Leary, Donny Golden and
more), and was not counted school by school. The New York Sean Nós Dance Club keeps the freer old style going.

**Scottish.** Scottish New York organized early: the [Saint Andrew's Society](https://standrewsny.org/page/HistoryTimeline)
(Nov 19, 1756) and the [New York Caledonian Club](https://nycaledonian.org/about/) (1856). Scottish country dance has
a distinguished pedigree: a Scottish Nationality Weekend at Michael Herman's Folk Dance House in 1954 inspired the
group that in 1959 became the RSCDS's second official US branch ([RSCDS NY](https://www.rscdsnewyork.org/a-history-of-the-ny-branch)).
Today there are three weekly classes (Manhattan on Thursdays at [Metro Baptist Church](https://www.rscdsnewyork.org/classes),
Elmsford on Wednesdays, Ho-Ho-Kus on Tuesdays, $12-15) plus Brooklyn socials. Scottish *music* is just Iona and
occasional Caledonian Club workshops. **Cape Breton** barely registers: Natalie MacMaster's first US concert at the
Blarney Star, and two tunes ("The New York" jig and the "Manhattan" hornpipe).

### Old-time and bluegrass

**Old-time: small, Monday-centered, pedagogical.** In the five boroughs it comes down to:
- [Idaho Bar](https://www.idahobarnyc.com/open-old-time-jam-session-idaho-bar-nyc) (East Village), 1st and 3rd
  Mondays at 7pm, hosted by Mark Piro, who has led Manhattan jams for over twenty years.
- The [Old Time Music Learning Jam](https://www.meetup.com/old-time-slow-jam-nyc/) in a Park Slope church, 2nd and
  4th Mondays, $10 that "goes to pay our jam leader", with 688 Meetup members and a shared tune list.
- [Grimm Artisanal Ales](https://ma.to/event/old-time-jam-09-july-2026) in East Williamsburg, 2nd Thursdays, billed
  as "participatory, trance-like folk music".

The steadier old-time jams are suburban: the [Eastern Long Island Old Time Music Jam](https://lonedoggy.wixsite.com/eliotmj)
at Hallockville farm in Riverhead (2nd and 4th Thursdays), a first-Saturday jam at
[Hilltop Hanover Farm](https://parksevents.westchestercountyny.gov/event/old-time-music-jam-5/2026-11-07/) in
Yorktown Heights, and a New Jersey cluster in Hunterdon, Somerset and Monmouth counties: Oakley Farm in Freehold,
Conclave Brewing in Flemington, Flounder Brewing in Hillsborough, Ringoes Grange and Milford
([All Jam No Spam, Oct 2026](https://newjerseybluegrass.org/all_jam_no_spam.pdf)). The craft brewery has largely
replaced the pub as the place to jam.

**Bluegrass: a bar culture, well documented.** A volunteer Google Calendar on [nybluegrass.com](https://nybluegrass.com/)
(116 entries) maps it. The anchors:
- **Tone's Bluegrass Jam at [Sunny's](https://www.sunnysredhook.com/calendar2/)**, Red Hook, Saturdays from 8pm in
  fall to spring, Sunday afternoons outdoors in summer. Sunny's opened in 1890 as John's Bar for stevedores, was
  gutted by Hurricane Sandy and reopened in 2013 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunny%27s_Bar)).
- **[Mona's](https://www.jamfinder.org/jams/nyc-ny-monas-bluegrass-session/)** Monday Night Bluegrass on Avenue B
  (since 2011), where Billy Strings first met banjoist Billy Failing in 2014. Mona's Monday used to be an Irish
  session.
- **[Paddy Reilly's](https://ma.to/event/bluegrass-jam-2026-10-04)** Sunday bluegrass at 5pm, one-mic, in an Irish
  pub; its mirror image is Irish Haven in Sunset Park, an Irish bar with a Monday bluegrass jam.
- Suburbs: the [Bluegrass Club of Long Island](https://bcliorg.wixsite.com/bcli) (1986), the
  [Westchester Bluegrass Club](https://www.westchesterbluegrassclub.com/schedule) shows in Purdys, and
  [BOTMA](https://newjerseybluegrass.org/lineup_text.htm) (1978), whose 3rd-Sunday $8 "festival" in Little Silver
  bundles a stage show, a jamming workshop and separate bluegrass and old-time jams.

**Albert Music Hall, Waretown.** In the Pine Barrens corner of Ocean County, Joe and George Albert's 1950s
hunting-cabin gatherings (George on fiddle, Joe on washtub bass) became a public hall in November 1974. Six bands
still play every Saturday night, with a Pickin' Shed out back; Pete Seeger played there on May 21, 1976
([Albert Hall](https://www.alberthall.org/timeline)).

**Fiddle contests:** none found inside the MSA. The nearest is Howell Living History Farm's "Fiddlin' on the Farm"
(36th year in 2026), "the largest and longest-running traditional fiddle contest in New Jersey", which ends with 30
fiddlers playing together, just over the line in Mercer County ([Howell Farm](https://howellfarm.org/fiddlin-day)).

### Contra, English country, squares, morris and international dance

**The CDSS lineage.** English Folk Dance Society branches formed in 1915 (Boston, then New York), and in 1933 the
national society was established "with a national headquarters established in New York City", May Gadd as director;
CDSS's office stayed in the city until 1987 ([CDSS](https://cdss.org/about/history/)).
[Country Dance New York](https://cdny.org/about-us) says it and its predecessors "have been putting on dances in NYC
for 100 years". Today CDNY runs **English country dance every Tuesday** and **contra on most Saturdays** at the
Church of the Village on West 13th Street ($20-25, $10 ages 16-18), with live chamber trios for ECD and callers'
terms listed as "positional" or "gendered" ([calendar](https://www.cdny.org/calendar-events)).

**Contra elsewhere.**
- [Brooklyn Contra](https://www.brooklyncontra.org/about-bkc) (2010) at Camp Friendship in Park Slope on 1st and
  3rd weekends ($15), with an explicitly political mission ("participatory folk culture" as an alternative to "the
  profit-driven culture fabrication of modern capitalism") and a stated chronic problem: "the constant quest for
  affordable space in NYC". It runs the Five Borough Fling weekend (Nov 6-8, 2026) and in fall 2026 held free barn
  dances on Manhattan West Plaza and in Battery Park City.
- [Village Contra](https://www.villagecontra.org/), "30+ years of gender-free contra", relaunched in fall 2026 with
  "welcome back techno dances" and alternating mask-required nights, sliding $15-30.
- [Swingin' Tern](https://folkproject.org/swingin-tern/) (the Folk Project) in Chatham NJ, 1st and 3rd Saturdays
  since January 7, 1984, two blocks from the NJ Transit station.
- LITMA's Sunday contras at the Brush Barn in Smithtown ($15).
- Gaps: no regular contra in Queens, the Bronx, Staten Island or Westchester; Rockland's monthly dance could not be
  confirmed for 2024-26; the region's only weekly contra (Princeton) is outside the MSA.

**English country dance** is the steadier form: besides CDNY, [North Jersey English Country Dancers](https://www.northjerseyenglishcountrydancers.org/)
in Ridgewood (2nd and 4th Sundays, $20) and [Country Dancers of Westchester](https://sites.google.com/site/countrydancersofwestchester/)
(1974) in White Plains, which is marking the centenary of co-founder Fried de Metz Herman's birth in November 2026.

**Squares and flatfoot.** [Times Squares](https://timessquares.nyc/) (Modern Western, LGBTQ+, 1984) is the city's
only square-dance club. Megan Downes's [City Stompers](http://www.nycitystompers.com/home/City_Stompers/About.html)
teach flatfooting and clogging on Fridays in the East Village and call themselves "New York City's only clogging
school".

**Morris and sword.** [Ring O'Bells](https://legacy.neffa.org/cgi-bin/public/showperf.pl?P=1695) (1974) is "the
oldest women's Morris side in North America"; the [Bouwerie Boys](https://www.bouwerieboys.com/) dance Sherborne;
[Half Moon Sword](https://sites.google.com/view/half-moon-sword/festival) (1981) hosts the New York Sword Dance
Festival, 40th edition in February 2026 with ten troupes, "the oldest and first event in North America to focus on
English-style sword dancing". NAMDO lists four other area sides as ceased ([NAMDO](https://morrisdance.org/ny-pa-nj/)).

**International folk dance** descends from Michael and Mary Ann Herman, who opened a folk dance center in October
1940 that became Folk Dance House on 16th Street; when the Moiseyev company first came to New York, its dancers went
there to learn American dances ([SFDH](https://sfdh.us/encyclopedia/folk_dance_house.html)). Today:
- [Balkan Wednesdays](http://dancemanhattan.com/calendar/event/89) at Hungarian House on East 82nd Street, weekly
  with a live band ($20); the same building hosts the fiddle-led [NYC Táncház](https://www.meetup.com/nyc-tanchaz-hungarian-folk-dance-music/events/317106536/).
- [Scandia New York](https://nordstjernan.com/news/east/2772/), alternate Wednesdays ($15) to the live New York
  Spelmanslag on fiddle, nyckelharpa and hardingfele.
- [Brooklyn Heights International Folk Dancers](https://brooklynheightsfolkdancers.org/) (Thursdays, $10).

## Folk song and listening

**The institutions that outlived the clubs.** Most of the commercial Village rooms that made the city famous are
gone, but four volunteer institutions keep going:
- The [Folk Music Society of New York](https://folkmusicny.org/about/) began in 1965 as the New York Pinewoods Folk
  Music Club, a CDSS division. It is all-volunteer with about 150 members. Its hand-assembled monthly newsletter
  (vol. 61 in 2026) is the best single calendar for the region's song scene
  ([Oct 2026 issue](https://folkmusicny.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/FMS2029-10.pdf)). It runs song circles,
  a concert series at the Brooklyn Society for Ethical Culture ($35), workshop-concerts in members' homes, a Fall
  Weekend (in the city for the first time in 2025, with Elizabeth LaPrelle teaching Appalachian ballads) and the
  TradMaD camp at Pinewoods.
- The [Peoples' Voice Cafe](https://www.peoplesvoicecafe.org/about.html) (1979) presents most Saturday nights at
  Judson Memorial Church: alcohol-free, $20 suggested, homemade snacks, often streamed. In October 2026 it
  presented Klezmatics fiddler-singer Lisa Gutkin unamplified and in the round.
- Columbia's student-run [Postcrypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcrypt_Coffeehouse) (1964), free on Friday
  and Saturday nights. Its Instagram now says it is "only accessible to CUID holders and their guests for the time
  being".
- New Jersey's [Folk Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Folk_Project) (organized 1973, incorporated 1976),
  the healthiest suburban society in the metro (below).

**The Folk Project and the Troubadour.** Mike Agranoff remembers the first show on July 25, 1975, in the basement of
a French restaurant in Chester: $1, about 25 seats, cable reels for tables
([Morristown Green](https://morristowngreen.com/2025/07/24/still-singing-after-all-these-years-the-troubadour-turns-50/)).
That series is now the [Troubadour](https://folkproject.org/troubadour/), every Friday at 7:30 in a new 300-seat,
alcohol-free hall at the Morristown Unitarian Fellowship. COVID made it bigger: paid membership rose 66% to 500,
attendance passed pre-COVID levels, and the Project raised $140,000 for performers. In 2025 it retired its donation
"creel" for a flat $20 ($15 members) with occasional $55 headliners; organizer and fiddler Mark Schaffer says
"twenty percent of the big shows pay to support the lower tickets". It also runs a monthly Open Stage, free
Streaming Tuesdays, a Zoom songwriting workshop, the Swingin' Tern contras, a spring
[Acoustic Getaway](https://folkproject.org/acoustic-getaway-spring-2026/) in Stony Point ($338 with lodging), and
Alan Rowoth's 1994 artist-support community [Big Orange Tarp](https://folkproject.org/bigorangetarp/).

**Shrinking suburban societies.** The [Folk Music Society of Huntington](https://fmsh.org/) (incorporated 1970)
announced that from Oct 1, 2026 it "will no longer present or have involvement in the presentation of the Hard Luck
Cafe or any other concerts or events open to the public". The Hard Luck Cafe (since 1992) continues under the
Cinema Arts Centre, and its library jam becomes the Pidgeon Hill Acoustic Folk Jam. Fair Lawn's
[Hurdy Gurdy Folk Music Club](https://hurdygurdyfolk.blogspot.com/) (1981; Doc Watson, Tommy Makem and Pete Seeger
on its stage) has no dated evidence after May 2024, and Chatham's 450-seat Sanctuary Concerts appears inactive.

**Listening rooms and coffeehouses today.**
- Manhattan: [The Bitter End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bitter_End) (1961), still open, with John Platt's
  *On Your Radar* songwriter showcase on 2nd Tuesdays; Cowgirl Seahorse near the Seaport for folk and bluegrass
  bills; [Joe's Pub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe%27s_Pub) for world-folk bookings.
- Brooklyn: the [Jalopy](https://jalopytheatre.org/aboutjalopy) (2006), venue, school, label and festival producer,
  which became a nonprofit by joining Living Traditions, the organization behind KlezKamp. Its Wednesday
  [open mic](https://jalopytheatre.org/open-mic) draws names from a hat at 6:30, one song each.
  [Pete's Candy Store](https://www.petescandystore.com/history) (1999) is a free back-room stage;
  [Folkus-NYC](https://www.folkus-nyc.org/) runs unplugged concerts in Williamsburg; the Lincoln Road Center runs
  Sunday 4pm house concerts with a "cat & dog in residence"; Sunny's back room hosts Edith's
  [Agnostic Gospel Jam](https://tockify.com/sunnysmusic/detail/279/1791131400000).
- Long Island: [Our Times Coffeehouse](https://ourtimescoffeehouse.org/) at the Ethical Humanist Society in Garden
  City (First Fridays, $20 cash); the Hard Luck Cafe and the NorthShore Original Open Mic in Huntington.
- New Jersey: the Troubadour; [Outpost in the Burbs](https://www.outpostintheburbs.org/about) in Montclair (1987),
  which pairs concerts with volunteering.

## Traditional singing

**Sacred Harp.** [NYC Sacred Harp](https://www.nycsacredharp.com/nycsingings) holds:
- a **weekly Wednesday** singing (6:00-8:15pm) at St. John's Lutheran on Christopher Street, with loaner books;
- the monthly **Joe Beasley Singing** (2nd Sundays) in Carroll Gardens, named for an Alabama singer; in 1996 a
  Brooklyn singer was vice-chair of the Beasley memorial singing in Winfield, AL
  ([minutes](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?n=362));
- a monthly **seven-shape** singing from the *Christian Harmony* and *Mull's*;
- pop-up singings "on boats, in graveyards, on rooftops".

The [NYC All-Day Singing](https://www.nycsacredharp.com/nycads) (about 2005-) drew 177 registered singers on Sept 19,
2026, with 100 leaders leading 95 songs. New Jersey's roots go deeper, per [Linda Griggs's history](http://lowereastsidesing.vocis.com/A%20Deep%20Rooted%20Tradition%20Returns%20Shape%20Note%20Music%20and%20Sacred%20Harp%20Singing%20in%20NJ%20+%20coronation.pdf):
*The Easy Instructor* (1801), the first shape-note book, was co-compiled by a Hopewell man; Great Migration Black
singers held Sacred Harp singings in Newark and Vauxhall with the Cooper book and the *Colored Sacred Harp*, a
tradition that "passed on with the aging singers"; and Lowell Mason, who mocked "dunce notes", is buried three miles
from the Montclair meeting house where the Garden State Convention met from 1993 (last minutes 2018,
[fasola](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=Garden+State)). Today's in-MSA New Jersey singing is the Sacred Harp
of Central New Jersey (1st Tuesdays, New Brunswick; low confidence).

**Sea shanties.** The [South Street Seaport Museum's](https://southstreetseaportmuseum.org/in-person-chantey-sing/)
first-Sunday chantey sing "began over 5 decades ago"; from May to November it meets on the deck of the 1885 iron
ship *Wavertree*, hosted by members of [The New York Packet](https://seashanties4all.com/groups-artists/new-york-packet-the/).
On third Sundays, the William Main Doerflinger Memorial Sea Shanty Sessions meet at the
[Noble Maritime Collection](https://noblemaritime.org/public) inside Sailors' Snug Harbor on Staten Island, the old
sailors' home where Doerflinger collected shanties from the residents. In Spring Lake, NJ, the Kerry O'Keigh
singing session (1st Wednesdays) mixes Irish, Scottish and maritime song.

**Singers' clubs and circles.** The [Sunnyside Singers Club](https://folkmusicny.org/sunnyside-singers-club/), every
Tuesday since 2014 (now at the Woodside Cafe), is the nearest thing to a British folk club in the city:
unaccompanied ballads, stories and recitations, "traditional songs are especially welcome". FMSNY runs
[song circles](https://folkmusicny.org/community-song-circle/) in Park Slope (1st Wednesdays) and at the St. George
Library on Staten Island (4th Saturdays); LITMA has a second-Saturday *Rise Up Singing* house sing with potluck.

## Heritage and diaspora traditions

The metro has dozens of heritage scenes side by side, often on the same subway line. Much of it rests on the
[Center for Traditional Music and Dance](https://ctmd.org/about/mission-impact/) (founded 1968 as the Balkan Arts
Center), which says it has worked with 20 NEA Heritage Fellows and founded seven organizations, among them the
[Mariachi Academy of New York](https://www.mariachiacademyny.org/english.html) (2002), [Pachamama Peruvian Arts](https://www.pachamamaperuvianarts.org/)
and FolkCOLOMBIA. CTMD started after Bronx-raised Ethel Raim asked Ralph Rinzler at Newport in 1968 why no performer
"had accents like the European immigrants remembered from her childhood in the Bronx"
([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/ethel-raim)). Around it sit [City Lore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Lore)
(1986), [Long Island Traditions](https://www.longislandtraditions.org/about) (1991) and the Bronx Music Heritage
Center's [Bronx Music Hall](https://whedco.org/arts-and-culture/bronx-music-heritage-center/). Brooklyn Arts
Council's NYSCA-funded folk arts program ended in 2020.

**Thriving, with weekly practice:**
- **Puerto Rican bomba and plena.** [Los Pleneros de la 21](https://www.losplenerosdela21.org/bpaw) (1983, NEA
  Fellow Juan Gutiérrez) teaches drumming on Mondays and dance on Tuesdays in East Harlem; children's workshops are in
  their 36th cycle. [Casita Rincón Criollo](https://citylore.org/places/rincon-criollo-casita/) in the South Bronx
  (1976; National Register 2024) started a monthly summer BomPlenazo in 2026; Bombazo Dance Co. gives free Saturday
  dance, drum and skirt-sewing classes in Hunts Point.
- **Trinidadian steelpan.** Nine Brooklyn panyards, mostly borrowed lots used for weeks before Labor Day, one with a
  petition to "PROTECT this Panyard" ([When Steel Talks](https://www.panonthenet.com/world/newyork/panyard_locations.htm)).
  Pan Evolution (2015) won Panorama in 2024, 2025 and 2026 ([results](https://www.panonthenet.com/results/NewYork/2026/ny-panorama-2026-results.htm)).
- **Haitian rara and Vodou drumming.** [Plezi Rara](https://haitiantimes.com/2025/09/03/haitian-plezi-rara-brooklyn-jouvert/)
  leads a midnight procession into Prospect Park before J'Ouvert ("It could have been 1793," said one first-timer);
  weekly classes at [Cumbe](https://www.cumbedance.org/) and [Kriyol Dance! Collective](https://www.kriyolcollective.com/classes);
  NEA Fellows Frisner Augustin (1999) and Peniel Guerrier (2025).
- **Indo-Caribbean tassa** in Richmond Hill, at every Phagwah parade, Diwali motorcade and Hindu wedding.
- **Klezmer and Yiddish.** CTMD's 1970s work with senior musicians "helped to spark an international revival of
  klezmer". KlezKamp (1985 to 2014 or 2015) gave way to [Yiddish New York](https://yiddishnewyork.com/) (Dec 26-31).
  For a fiddler, the open door is the [New York Klezmer Series Community Klezmer Band](https://nyklezmer.com/series-overview/community-klezmer-band/),
  every other Tuesday at Hudson Yards Synagogue: "Everyone is welcome, please bring your own instrument." Hasidic
  Williamsburg, Borough Park and Monsey keep a much larger, mostly private wedding-music world with the rhyming
  badchen.
- **Balkan.** The [Golden Festival](https://goldenfest.org/our-story/), born in a Manhattan loft in 1986, held its
  40th edition in January 2026 in Astoria with 40-60 bands; Slavic Soul Party! plays Barbès "Every Tuesday (for
  ever..)"; NEA Fellow Yuri Yunakov (Bloomfield NJ, 2011) shaped Bulgarian Romani wedding saxophone.
- **Mariachi.** The Mariachi Academy, the idea of Queens mariachis Ramón Ponce Sr. and Jr., has about 120 students
  on Monday-Wednesday evenings in East Harlem.
- **Tibetan and Himalayan.** The Richmond Folk Festival's listing for CTMD's New York Tibetan Ensemble calls Queens "home to the largest Tibetan
  population outside of Asia" ([Richmond Folk Festival](https://www.richmondfolkfestival.org/2026-performers/2026/8/10/new-york-tibetan-ensemble)); Lhakar
  gorshey circle dances fill Queens parks on summer Wednesdays.

**Active and seasonal:**
- **Cuban rumba** in Central Park on summer Sundays, a practice traced to the 1960s; in the 1990s police
  confiscated the drums and rumberos played on their bodies, benches and beer coolers
  ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/central-park-rumba-dancing-with-memory.htm);
  [El País](https://english.elpais.com/usa/2024-08-11/its-sunday-in-new-york-and-central-park-moves-to-cuban-rumba.html)).
- **Greek regional music** in Astoria: the Pontian Society Komninoi (1966), Cretan lyra nights at Omonoia, the
  Society of Epirotes' museum, and the [Greek American Folklore Society](http://www.greekamericanfolkloresociety.org/)'s
  Monday classes.
- **Son jarocho** (Radio Jarocho since 2005, Jarana Beat), **Colombian** (FolkCOLOMBIA's Saturday classes in Jackson
  Heights), **Venezuelan joropo** from recent arrivals, **Garifuna** (the Bronx has proclaimed a Garifuna-American
  Heritage Month since 2009), **West African** griot culture in Harlem's Little Senegal
  ([Jalikunda](https://www.remix-culture.org/jalikunda), 2019), **Georgian polyphony**, **Ukrainian bandura**,
  **Polish górale** in Clifton NJ, **Arab maqam and dabke** in Bay Ridge and Paterson, **Korean pungmul**, **taiko**
  ([Soh Daiko](https://www.sohdaiko.org/), 1979, "first taiko ensemble on the East Coast").
- **Native nations.** The [Shinnecock Pow Wow](https://www.shinnecock-nsn.gov/annual-powwow) held its 80th edition in
  2026 ($20). The Thunderbird American Indian Dancers (1963), "the oldest resident Native American dance company in
  New York", run a [Queens County Farm powwow](https://qns.com/2026/07/queens-farm-annual-thunderbird-american-indian-powwow/)
  (47th in 2026) and a winter powwow concert. Prospect Park hosted intertribal powwows from 1916 to 1972; a
  Lenape-led powwow returned in 2025 and 2026 ([source](https://www.javiersoriano.com/2026/09/27/3-lenape-pow-wow/)).

**Fading:** Cantonese opera clubs (the 1931 Chinese Musical and Theatrical Association now lives mainly as a
[MOCA archive](https://www.cmtaarchive.com/about)), Toisan muk'yu song, the Bronx Cambodian community, village-style
Calabrian music like the DeFrancos' of Belleville NJ, and street-corner doo-wop. **Growing:** Tibetan, Venezuelan,
West African, Garifuna, Albanian (Staten Island's first Albanian Festival was in September 2026), and the Lenape
cultural return.

**The fiddle crossover.** Bowed strings are central in táncház, klezmer, Pontic and Cretan lyra, Bulgarian gadulka,
mariachi and joropo. Andy Statman (NEA 2012), a Queens kid taught mandolin by David Grisman who became Dave Tarras's
protégé and inherited four of his clarinets ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/andy-statman)), is the
bridge between bluegrass and klezmer. And in September 2026 Ghanaian drummers from Ekome Femo played an "Irish
Ghanaian Fusion" set with fiddle, banjo and bodhrán at the Van Cortlandt House Museum in the Bronx
([listing](https://www.blackevents.us/event/bronx-concert-irish-ghanaian-fusion-2026-09-19)).

## Venues

City Island Yacht Club, the deck of the 1885 *Wavertree*, Sailors' Snug Harbor, and four farms.

## Radio and media

New York's folk radio has a big past and a thin present.

**Still on air:**
- **WFUV 90.7 (Fordham):** Irish music seven hours every Sunday: Kathleen Biggins's [*A Thousand Welcomes*](https://wfuv.org/content/thousand-welcomes)
  (8-11am, since 1986) and the student-run [*Ceol na nGael*](https://wfuv.org/content/ceol-na-ngael) (noon-4pm,
  since 1974).
- **WFDU 89.1 (Teaneck):** Ron Olesko's [*Traditions*](https://wfdu.fm/hd1/shows/traditions) (Sun 10pm-1am, since
  1980) plus his 24/7 internet channel *Folk Music Notebook* (2019).
- **WKCR 89.9 (Columbia):** [*The Moonshine Show*](https://spinitron.com/WKCR/show/124851/The-Moonshine-Show)
  (bluegrass and old-time, Sun 10am-noon; bluegrass on WKCR since Pete Wernick in 1963), flanked by the gospel show
  *Amazing Grace* and the *Tennessee Border Show*.
- **WBAI 99.5:** [*Folk Radio*](https://wbai.org/program.php?program=367) (Thu 10pm-midnight), rotating hosts
  including Eli Smith.

**Gone:** Oscar Brand's *Folksong Festival* (WNYC, Dec 10, 1945-Sept 2016, a Guinness record, where Dylan made his
solo New York radio debut; [WNYC](https://www.wnyc.org/story/remembering-oscar-brand/)), Bob Fass's *Radio
Unnameable* (WBAI, 1963-2021), Bob Sherman's *Woody's Children* (WFUV, 54 years to 2023), and John Platt's *Sunday
Supper* (WFUV, ended Feb 15, 2026: "After almost 30 years, the on-air chapter of 'Sunday Supper' is coming to a
close", [WFUV](https://wfuv.org/content/sunday-supper-february-15)). WFUV now has no weekly singer-songwriter folk
show.

**Labels and print.** New York was the record capital of the first revival: Folkways (Moses Asch, 1948; 2,168
titles; to the Smithsonian in 1987), Elektra and Vanguard (both 1950), *Sing Out!* (1950; last print issue 2014),
*Broadside* (1962), Fast Folk (1982-97; first recordings of Suzanne Vega, Shawn Colvin, Lyle Lovett). Irish labels:
the Shannon label (piper Tom Ennis), Dublin Records (Paddy Killoran, 1956) and Shanachie (1975). Today: Jalopy
Records and FMSNY member Don Wade's Minstrel Records (Almeda Riddle, Frank Warner, Jeff Warner). The
[Irish Echo](https://www.irishecho.com/events-from-the-tri-state-area) (1928) carries Dan Neely's trad column.

## History: a condensed timeline

The JSON holds about 240 dated entries. The 40 that matter most:

- **1700s:** colonial NY and NJ runaway-slave advertisements describe ["nearly one hundred fiddlers"](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv7xbrzd),
  the region's earliest body of evidence for fiddling.
- **Mid-1700s:** [Pinkster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinkster), the Dutch Pentecost, becomes an African
  American festival of dancing and drumming in New York and Brooklyn.
- **1801:** *The Easy Instructor*, the first shape-note tunebook, co-compiled by a Hopewell NJ man.
- **c.1820s-40s:** Black Long Islanders and New Jerseyans [dance for eels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_for_eels)
  at Catharine Market.
- **1842:** Dickens describes the fiddler at Almack's in Five Points.
- **1843 (Feb 6):** the Virginia Minstrels debut at the Bowery Amphitheatre; **1859:** Emmett's "Dixie" premieres
  at Mechanics' Hall.
- **1900:** Ellen O'Byrne DeWitt opens her Irish music shop; RTÉ calls her ["the founding mother of the Irish record industry"](https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2023/1124/1418174-ellen-obyrne-dewitt-irish-music-recordings-new-york/).
- **1915:** Cecil Sharp's visit; English Folk Dance Society branch in New York.
- **1916 (Sept 15):** Columbia's first Irish dance-music session, after DeWitt guaranteed to buy the records
  ([NY Irish History](https://nyirishhistory.us/article/obyrne-dewitt-a-window-on-irish-music-recording-in-america/)).
  Prospect Park's intertribal powwows begin (to 1972).
- **1921-36:** Michael Coleman cuts about 80 sides; **1922:** Eck Robertson records for Victor in New York;
  **1925:** Ukrainian fiddler and violin maker Pawlo Humeniuk begins recording for OKeh.
- **1935:** Lead Belly arrives; **1940:** Guthrie writes "This Land Is Your Land" here and meets Pete Seeger;
  the Hermans open their folk dance center.
- **1941:** the Almanac Singers and their Sunday "hootenannies".
- **1945:** Coleman dies in the Bronx; Oscar Brand's *Folksong Festival* starts on WNYC; People's Songs founded.
- **1961:** Friends of Old Time Music's first concert (Feb 11); the Washington Square "beatnik riot" (April 9);
  the Bitter End opens.
- **1965:** the Immigration Act ends mass Irish arrival and the Tuxedo Ballroom closes; McGann, Burke and Dolan
  record *A Tribute to Michael Coleman*; FMSNY founded.
- **1966:** Kathleen Collins becomes the first American-born senior All-Ireland fiddle champion.
- **1968:** Martin Koenig founds the Balkan Arts Center (CTMD).
- **1972-75:** Irish Arts Center (1972); Mulligan-Quinn CCÉ (1972-73); WFUV's *Ceol na nGael* (1974); Albert Music
  Hall (1974); the Eagle Tavern Monday session (June 10, 1975); the Troubadour (July 25, 1975); NJ Folk Festival
  (1975).
- **1979-86:** Peoples' Voice Cafe (1979); LITMA (1980); Fast Folk (1982); Los Pleneros de la 21 (1983); Swingin'
  Tern and Times Squares (1984); Cherish the Ladies concert (1985); Brian Conway's senior All-Ireland fiddle title,
  City Lore, the Golden Festival and the Sword Dance Festival (all 1986).
- **1987:** Folkways goes to the Smithsonian, Folk City loses its lease, CDSS moves its office to Massachusetts.
- **1993:** the Eagle Tavern closes; concerts move to the Blarney Star in Tribeca.
- **2004-06:** New York Irish Center opens (2004); first NYC Sacred Harp singing (about 2005); Jalopy opens (2006).
- **2009-14:** Brooklyn Folk Festival (2009); Brooklyn Contra (2010); Mona's bluegrass and the Washington Square Park
  Folk Festival (2011); Hurricane Sandy floods Sunny's and burns a Rockaway session pub (2012); Dead Rabbit and Iona
  sessions (2013); Mary O's, Porch Stomp and the Sunnyside Singers Club (2014).
- **2020:** COVID ends the 45-year Eagle/Blarney Star concert run at NYU, the NY Klezmer Series' weekly seasons and
  the Clearwater Revival's traditional form.
- **2021:** the new Irish Arts Center opens (Dec 8); Joanie Madden named an NEA Heritage Fellow.
- **2022:** Mick Moloney dies; the Blarney Star concerts revive at the New York Irish Center.
- **2024-25:** Rockwood Music Hall closes abruptly; Casita Rincón Criollo joins the National Register; Rutgers ends
  its NJ Folk Festival run; the Lenape powwow returns to Prospect Park; Peniel Guerrier named an NEA Fellow.
- **2026:** WFUV's *Sunday Supper* ends (Feb); the Golden Festival's 40th (Jan); the Jalopy is burgled and the
  community covers the losses (June); Cherish the Ladies win the Gradam Ceoil; FMSH stops presenting (Oct 1); the
  80th Shinnecock Pow Wow.

## The fiddle angle

**"New York Sligo."** The house Irish style is south Sligo fiddling as filtered through Coleman's records and refined
by Andy McGann, whose "elegant and urbane interpretation of the classic Sligo repertoire" passed to Brian Conway
(taught by Mulvihill and Martin Wynne, who taught him "the intricacies of the old Sligo bowing style") and on to Pat
Mangan and others ([ITMA](https://newyork.itma.ie/post-world-war-ii/andy-mcgann/)). Rose Conway Flanagan calls her own
playing "New York Sligo style". The style was often adopted, not inherited: Longford's Paddy Reynolds "re-made himself
in New York as a Sligo stylist" after hearing Lad O'Beirne, and Brooklyn's Italian-Irish Tony DeMarco titled an album
*The Sligo Indians* ([ITMA](https://newyork.itma.ie/post-world-war-ii/paddy-reynolds/)). Other strands: East Galway
(the Coens, Mike Rafferty, Willie Kelly), Sliabh Luachra through Denis Murphy and Johnny Cronin, and Wexford's Tom
Dunne playing Paddy Cronin's Kerry style. Larry Redican's "rock-solid tempo" made him the step dancers' favorite after
the McNiffs slowed dance tempos in the 1950s.

**Recordings to know:** Coleman's 78s (DAHR has the sessions); McGann, Burke and Dolan's *A Tribute to Michael
Coleman* (1965), recorded in one unedited session and "widely regarded as one of the finest Irish traditional music
discs of all time"; Brian Conway's *First Through the Gate* (Smithsonian Folkways, 2002, with McGann's last studio
session); Humeniuk's *King of the Ukrainian Fiddlers: New York 1925-1927*; FOTM's *The Folk Arrival 1961-1965*;
Gilbert Ross's *The Cradle of Harmony* (Mount's tunes on Mount's violin, 1976); Eck Robertson's 1922 Victor sides;
the Down Hill Strugglers on the *Inside Llewyn Davis* soundtrack. Beyond Irish and old-time, the city's fiddle worlds
include Scandia's spelmanslag, táncház, klezmer, Pontic lyra and mariachi (see heritage, above).

## Character

**What makes it distinct.** Three things set New York apart from other big American folk scenes:
- **It is a gatekeeper and amplifier more than a source.** Folk forms came from Sligo, Texas, Kentucky, Bulgaria and
  San Juan and were recorded, published, booked, broadcast and politicized here, then sold back. The 78s that
  standardized Irish music, the first country records, Folkways, *Sing Out!*, FOTM and the Village clubs all sit on
  that pattern. Ethel Raim's 1968 question about accents turned the lens back on the city's own immigrants, and CTMD
  has been doing that work ever since.
- **Its institutions are fragile; its lineages are not.** Rent ended the Eagle Tavern, Folk City, Cornelia Street
  (where rent went from $450 to $33,000 a month, [Gothamist](https://cms.prod.nypr.digital/food/after-41-years-cornelia-street-cafe-will-close-on-january-2nd/)),
  the Living Room and Umanov's retail floor; a fire code shrank the Eagle; the Cabaret Law banned dancing; a pandemic
  ended the Blarney Star at NYU. Yet the lines run unbroken: Coleman to McGann to Conway's pupils; Raim and Koenig to
  CTMD; Cohen and Rinzler to Eli Smith and the Brooklyn Folk Festival; Sharp's 1915 visit to CDNY's Tuesday ECD.
  Church halls, Ethical Society buildings, libraries and nonprofits outlast bars.
- **It is many scenes that rarely meet.** The Irish scene lives in Comhaltas halls, the Bronx-Yonkers-Pearl River
  corridor and East Village pubs; old-time and contra in South Brooklyn and suburban church halls; bluegrass in bars
  and breweries; heritage traditions in their own neighborhoods. Crossovers are notable when they happen: a Paddy
  Reilly's bluegrass jam in an Irish pub, Andy Statman between bluegrass and klezmer, an Irish-Ghanaian set on a
  Bronx lawn, Jake Shulman-Ment in a Balkan band.

**Its signature tradition** is Irish traditional music, by count (the most event-nights), history (the 78s, the first
North American Comhaltas branch, eight NEA Heritage Fellows counting sean-nós singer Joe Heaney) and style ("New York Sligo"). A case can be made for the heritage
mosaic as a whole, and for English country dance as the oldest continuous participatory dance form.

**Who shows up.** No survey exists for any scene. What the sources say:
- Manhattan Irish sessions skew young and professional, with upper-intermediate players at 11th Street; the
  suburban céilí and set-dance world is older and Irish-born; RSCDS New Jersey has members "in their teens and in
  their 90s".
- City contra leans young, queer-friendly and political (Larks and Robins, positional calling, mask-required
  nights still scheduled in 2026). CDNY describes "old-timers who have been dancing for decades to young people who
  have just discovered" the dances, "straight and gay, people from a variety of ethnic backgrounds".
- The Folk Project's audience is described as mostly musicians: "We really are amongst our people here... I'll bet
  most of them are musicians themselves" (Buddy Woodward of the Kingston Trio).

**Geography.** By event-nights, Manhattan (about 154 a month) and Brooklyn (about 89) dominate, then the New Jersey
counties (about 61). Within the city the action is concentrated: the East Village and NoHo (Irish sessions, Idaho,
Mona's), Greenwich Village (CDNY, Peoples' Voice, Sacred Harp, Bitter End), Hell's Kitchen (Landmark, IAC, RSCDS) and
a few blocks of South Brooklyn (Red Hook, Gowanus, Park Slope, Carroll Gardens). Queens and the Bronx have almost no
old-time or contra but dense heritage practice (Astoria Greek, Jackson Heights Tibetan and Colombian, Richmond Hill
tassa, South Bronx bomba). Staten Island has only its song circle and shanty session. The suburbs run their own
institutions with their own halls: Smithtown's Brush Barn, Chatham, Morristown, Ridgewood, White Plains, Little Silver,
Waretown, Pearl River and Mineola. Hunterdon and Monmouth are New Jersey's old-time heartland.

**Costs.**
- Pub sessions and jams: free (Mary O's suggests $15 for leaders; the Park Slope learning jam is $10).
- Contra: $15 is the commonest posted price (Brooklyn Contra, LITMA, Swingin' Tern's low end); CDNY is $20-25.
- English country dance: $20. Balkan Wednesdays $20; Scandia $15; Brooklyn Heights folk dance $10.
- Céilí $20; set-dance class $10-15; Scottish country dance class $12-15; Blarney Star concert $25.
- Coffeehouse or house concert: about $20 (Peoples' Voice, Troubadour, Our Times); FMSNY concerts $35.
- Free: Sacred Harp, shanty sings, song circles, Postcrypt, Central Park rumba, Washington Square Park Folk Festival,
  Porch Stomp. Brooklyn Folk Festival $75 a day.

### What a visiting fiddler should do

**On a Tuesday night** (the city's most varied night):
1. Go to the open round-robin [Sláinte](https://thesession.org/sessions/17) session on the Bowery at 8pm (or hear the
   mic'd Swift house session a block away).
2. Or, if you'd rather dance to a fiddle, CDNY's English country dance at the Church of the Village (7-10pm, $20-25),
   with a live chamber trio.
3. On alternate Tuesdays, bring the fiddle to the [New York Klezmer Series Community Band](https://nyklezmer.com/series-overview/community-klezmer-band/)
   at Hudson Yards Synagogue (6:30pm), the closest thing to an open heritage session for a melody player.
4. For singing, the [Sunnyside Singers Club](https://folkmusicny.org/sunnyside-singers-club/) at the Woodside Cafe
   (7pm, free): bring a ballad.
5. Late: Slavic Soul Party! at Barbès in Park Slope (9pm), or Lucinda's bluegrass on Avenue A.
6. Suburbs: the Oakley Farm old-time jam in Freehold, the Flounder Brewing old-time jam in Hillsborough, the weekly
   session at St. Stephen's Green in Spring Lake, the third-Tuesday Grafton Street session at Jack Dolan's in
   Smithtown.

**On a Saturday:**
1. Midday on 1st Saturdays: the old-time jam at Hilltop Hanover Farm in Yorktown Heights ($5).
2. Afternoon: the New York Irish Center's slow session in Long Island City (3pm), or on 3rd Saturdays Farrell's in
   Windsor Terrace; on 4th Saturdays, the ferry to the St. George Library song circle.
3. Evening, option A (the Manhattan-Brooklyn double): CDNY's contra in the Village at 7pm, then
   [Tone's bluegrass jam at Sunny's](https://www.sunnysredhook.com/calendar2/) in Red Hook from 8pm.
4. Option B (1st or 3rd Saturdays): NJ Transit to [Swingin' Tern](https://folkproject.org/swingin-tern/) in Chatham.
5. Option C (something only this metro has): drive to [Albert Music Hall](https://www.alberthall.org/timeline) in the
   Pine Barrens for the Saturday show and the Pickin' Shed.
6. Option D: a monthly táncház at Hungarian House (fiddle-led, with a teaching hour), or a Peoples' Voice Cafe concert.

**Monday** is the best night overall and **Sunday** the biggest day: see the Irish and old-time sections above for
the Landmark, Hartley's, Iona, Idaho and Mona's on Mondays, and An Béal Bocht, the Dead Rabbit, 11th Street Bar,
Paddy Reilly's bluegrass and the Sunday sings on Sundays.

**For a history-minded fiddler:** the Long Island Museum in Stony Brook (Mount's violins and manuscripts; check
what's on view), Michael Coleman's grave at St. Raymond's in the Bronx, the MacDougal-Bleecker-West 4th triangle
(Folklore Center, Gaslight, Folk City, Cafe Wha?, Bitter End, Allan Block's sandal shop at 171 West 4th), and the
Washington Square fountain on a Sunday. Bring a set: "In the Cars on the Long Island Railroad", "The Flowers of
Brooklyn", "The Pride of the Bronx", "Bonkers in Yonkers", "Staten Island".

## Defunct and lost

The JSON lists 100 defunct items. The losses that shaped the scene:

- **Dance halls:** Donovan's (c.1906), the county halls of 125th Street, Brooklyn's Pride of Erin (1931-by 1945),
  the Star of Munster in the Bronx, the Tuxedo Ballroom (closed 1965 "almost in direct response" to the Immigration
  Act), City Center and Jaeger House (gone by 1970), the Tower View in Woodside (the last), Rockaway's summer
  "Irishtown" ([Ridge](https://nyirishhistory.us/article/dance-halls-of-irish-new-york/)).
- **Folk Dance House** (1940-early 1970s; building demolished) and the **Village Barn** (1930-1960s), a square-dance
  nightclub that hosted NBC's *Village Barn* (1948-50), the first network country TV show, and later became Jimi
  Hendrix's Electric Lady Studios ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Barn)).
- **The Village rooms:** Café Society (1938-48, red-baited), the Village Vanguard as a folk room (to 1957), the
  Gaslight (1958-71), Gerde's Folk City (1960-87), the Village Gate (1958-94), the Folklore Center (1957-73; Izzy
  Young moved to Stockholm), Caffè Vivaldi (2018), Cornelia Street Cafe and the SideWalk Cafe (both 2019), the Living
  Room (2015), Rockwood Music Hall's main stage (2024), the Owl Music Parlor (2025).
- **Concert series and societies:** Friends of Old Time Music (1961-65, 14 concerts), the Eagle Tavern/Blarney Star
  concerts (1975-2020 in three homes), Clearwater's Great Hudson River Revival (1978-2020), the Folk Music Society of
  Huntington's public events (to Oct 2026), the Garden State Sacred Harp Convention (last minutes 2018), Newark and
  Vauxhall's Black Sacred Harp singings, KlezKamp (1985-2014/15), CTMD's Queens Ethnic Music and Dance Festival
  (1976-91), the Middletown (NJ) Folk Festival (1968-84), the Brooklyn Arts Council folk arts program (1988-2020).
- **Sessions:** about 90 dead NY-area sessions on thesession, including Lillie's Union Square (last session Oct 7,
  2017), the Harbor Light Pub (burned in Hurricane Sandy), the Wild Goose on Staten Island, the Staten Island Arts
  seisiún (dormant since about 2022), Mona's Monday Irish session (now bluegrass), and in 2024-26 Willie McBride's,
  Napper Tandy's, Westhampton Beach Brewing and Uncle Tommy's. Unconfirmed since 2024: the Lowlands old-time jam,
  Rockwood's First Monday bluegrass jam, Sunny's 3rd-Sunday old-time jam, Dave Harvey's NYC Barn Dance, Rockland
  Contra.

Why things ended, in order of frequency: rent and real estate, owners and leases, the Cabaret Law and fire codes,
the 1965 Immigration Act (for the Irish halls), the death of a founder, and COVID.

## Data quality notes

- **Sources and method.** Five threads researched in October 2026 under heavy search rate limits, mostly from
  primary pages (venue and society sites, the FMSNY October 2026 newsletter, the nybluegrass.com calendar, NJ's All
  Jam No Spam PDF, CTMD's events archive, NEA pages, ITMA) plus listings and social-post snippets. The merge
  deduplicated 74 records by name, and I removed further cross-thread duplicates by hand.
- **Dropped from the census** for lacking dated 2024-26 evidence: the Lowlands old-time jam, Rockland Contra,
  Shmulik Gov-Ari's Israeli sessions, Monmouth Folk Dancers, Rumberos United and Buckley's (last evidence 2022).
  Also dropped as one-offs or schools: the BKCM square dance, Ukrainian Village Voices rehearsals, Cumbe's ~30
  classes a month and the Mariachi Academy's youth classes (both still under instruction).
- **Confidence.** Totals include low-confidence listings: about 410 event-nights a month in all, about 324 at medium
  or high confidence. The Irish figure (140.6) is the least certain: only 6 of 43 listings have current official
  evidence and 17 rest on MurphGuide (June 2024), a single thesession post or word of mouth, so 77.8 is the safer
  number, and it still includes listen-only house sessions. Bluegrass (71.3) leans on a volunteer calendar whose
  open-ended entries may outlive real jams. Seasonal series are counted at in-season rates. About 29 a month are
  listening series and dance parties, not sessions. Heritage events (34 a month) are badly undercounted because
  many traditions run on annual feasts and private gatherings.
- **Distinct traditions (69)** was counted by hand, replacing the merge's inflated free-text figure (140): 24
  Anglo-Celtic-American instrumental, dance and song forms from the census, plus 45 heritage traditions rated
  thriving, active or niche, excluding fading, historic-only, classical and museum-recreation forms. About half of the
  heritage ratings rest on one or two sources.
- **Unreadable:** Facebook and most Instagram pages, litma.org, tunearch.org, the Ceol Agus Rince céilí calendar (so
  monthly céilís are undercounted), citylore.org and the NYT.
- **Source conflicts** (dates, editions, venues) are itemized in the JSON's `data_quality_notes`.
- **Not found:** attendance or demographic surveys for any scene. **Thin:** Staten Island, the Bronx, Westchester
  beyond Yonkers and White Plains, Irish step-dance schools and pipe bands, and Jamaican, Ethiopian, Nigerian,
  Bangladeshi and Punjabi traditions. The borough split above comes from a place-name heuristic and is approximate.
