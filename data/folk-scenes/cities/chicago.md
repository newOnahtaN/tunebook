# Chicago-Naperville-Elgin, IL-IN: the folk scene (as of October 2026)

*Metro #3 (9.43M people). Research for Nate's tune book project, compiled from four parallel research threads (trad and dance; song, singing and media; heritage traditions; history, festivals and the fiddle angle). Machine-readable data: `chicago.json`.*

## The scene in a nutshell

Chicago is America's Irish-session capital and its biggest folk institution town. About 89 public Irish sessions a month run in roughly 30 pubs, from Lincoln Park to Oak Lawn and the McHenry County exurbs. They are led by a near-professional core (Jimmy Keane, John Williams, Pauline Conneely, Larry Nugent) with a lineage back to police chief Francis O'Neill's 1900s tune-collecting network and the 1956 Irish Musicians Association. The [Old Town School of Folk Music](https://www.oldtownschool.org/aboutus/) (1957) taught 33,845 students in 2025 on a $17.6M budget, and it still runs free half-hour singalongs. Around these sit a weekly Monday contra in the Irish American Heritage Center's ballroom, four Sacred Harp singings, 50-year-old suburban sing-arounds in brewpubs and church basements, and a deep heritage layer: 16 NEA Heritage Fellows in the metro, sold-out bomba schools, górale fiddlers, a 73rd-year urban powwow, tamburitza and blues jams. Old-time is the thin spot: there is one weekly open jam, in a pizza parlour.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **A police chief wrote down the tunes.** Francis O'Neill (Chicago police chief 1901-05) compiled *O'Neill's Music of Ireland* (1903) and *Dance Music of Ireland* (1907) from a network of musicians, several of whom he put on the force. The reel "Sergeant Early's Dream" is named for a Chicago cop. "The Chicago Reel" is older than its name: it appears in an 1883 Leitrim manuscript and only became "Chicago" in O'Neill's 1907 book ([The Session](https://thesession.org/tunes/840)).
2. **Session density is extraordinary.** Roughly 89 Irish sessions a month. One pub, the [Galway Arms](https://galwayarms.com/chicago-galway-arms-live-music) in Lincoln Park, runs four free sessions a week (Sun, Tue, Wed, Thu), and accordionist Jimmy Keane leads or co-leads five regular sessions and keeps the city's de facto session list.
3. **Nashville copied Chicago.** WLS's National Barn Dance (1924) came first, and George D. Hay announced it before he went south to found the Grand Ole Opry. The first house-band leader, fiddler Tommy Dandurand, was a retired Kankakee streetcar operator.
4. **The Old Town School is a giant.** It began as $2 guitar lessons in Dawn Greening's Oak Park living room in 1957. In 2025 it had 33,845 students (24,542 in group classes), 276 teaching artists and $17.6M in revenue ([annual report](https://www.oldtownschool.org/media/doc/2026/OTSFM2025Annual%20Report.pdf)). Toddlers (Wiggleworms) out-register string-band students about 3 to 1. In 1981 it was down to about 300 students a week and survived partly because teachers worked unpaid.
5. **Contra lives in an Irish building.** Chicago's busiest contra, the Chicago Barn Dance Company's weekly Monday dance, is held in the 4th-floor ballroom of the Irish American Heritage Center. The same building hosts a Thursday Irish session, Friday céilís and the Comhaltas branch. Even so, modern western square dance clubs still schedule about six dances for every contra.
6. **Old-time is the thin spot.** The only weekly open old-time jam is "Banjo After Dark" at Borelli's pizza parlour in Lincoln Square, and no fiddle contest operates inside a metro of 9.4 million. The old-time players who are here lean toward Midwestern (Illinois/Indiana) repertoire rather than Appalachian.
7. **Heritage Fellows with day jobs.** Sixteen NEA National Heritage Fellows are in the metro. Liz Carroll (All-Ireland senior fiddle champion at 18 in 1975) [never played full-time and worked as a nurse](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/liz-carroll). Piper Joe Shannon was a Chicago firefighter. Milan Opacich built tamburitzas in the basement of a Gary firehouse. Michael Flatley was a Fellow in 1988, six years before Riverdance.
8. **Stubborn suburban singing.** The Fox Valley Folklore Society has held a weekly Wednesday sing-around since 1975, now in a brewpub in Aurora. Two Way Street Coffee House is a 60-seat church basement that pays union scale. Two monthly sea-shanty sings run a thousand miles from salt water.
9. **Sacred Harp came in through the folk scene.** Chicago's shape-note revival came through folkies rather than church choirs. Ted Johnson was at the Old Town School's opening night in 1957, co-founded the Chicago Sacred Harp Singers, wrote "New Agatite" for the 1991 *Sacred Harp*, and was still taking Irish fiddle classes in his 90s. He died on Oct 10, 2026, at 98.
10. **Polka fading, górale thriving.** The national polka convention was invented in Chicago (1963) and moves to Erie, PA in 2027. Meanwhile the Polish Highlander (górale) fiddle bands of a newer immigrant wave fill Dom Podhalan's calendar. Puerto Rican bomba is booming too: every La Escuelita Bombera class for the coming semester was sold out in Oct 2026.

## Character

Chicago's signature is **institution-building and documentation**. A police chief's collecting network, a radio barn dance and a community music school each turned a living practice into something written down, broadcast or taught at scale. The Irish scene is unusually **professional-led and pub-anchored**, and it is spread over three zones: the North Side corridor (Lincoln Park, Lincoln Square, Avondale, Mayfair/IAHC, Evanston), the South Side Irish belt (Beverly, Mount Greenwood, Oak Lawn, Gaelic Park in Oak Forest) and the far suburbs (Naperville, St. Charles, Joliet, Crystal Lake, Woodstock, Harvard). The song scene is the reverse: one huge city institution ringed by small, volunteer-run suburban clubs in church basements, village halls and brewpubs (Downers Grove, Aurora, Batavia, Grayslake, Hinsdale). The heritage layer is the deepest of any layer, rooted in neighbourhoods (Humboldt Park bomba, Pilsen/Little Village son jarocho, Archer Avenue górale, Lemont Lithuanian, the Calumet tamburitza belt, South and West Side blues). Festivals cluster in summer, but the oldest, the University of Chicago Folk Festival (1961), is a February tradition.

## What a visiting fiddler should do

- **Monday:** Chicago Barn Dance Company contra and squares at the Irish American Heritage Center (Mayfair), $7-10.
- **Tuesday:** Irish sessions at the Galway Arms (Pat Quinn), Galway Bay's "Tunesday" (Jimmy Keane) or the Wolfhound (Brendan Byrne, Avondale). Or Irish set dancing at Chicago Gaelic Park ($2), or the Logan Square Sacred Harp singing (2nd and 4th Tuesdays, Comfort Station).
- **Wednesday:** Galway Arms or Mrs. Murphy & Sons (Larry Nugent); Fox Valley sing-around in Aurora; the Old Town School Americana jam.
- **Thursday:** the city's one weekly open old-time jam, "Banjo After Dark" at Borelli's. Or Irish at the Fifth Province (IAHC) or the Harp and Fiddle (Park Ridge).
- **Saturday:** daytime pickers' jam on the Naperville Riverwalk. On the 2nd Saturday, the Chicagoland Scottish Fiddle and Accordion Club at Seman Violins (Skokie) and the Valparaiso contra; on the 3rd, Queer Contra Chicago. Evening concerts at the Old Town School or Two Way Street.
- **Sunday:** the deepest Irish day: Galway Arms, Chief O'Neill's, the Kerryman, Celtic Knot (Evanston), Clancy's (Oak Lawn). Monthly ScandiJam at the Swedish American Museum; 3rd Sunday North Side Sacred Harp.

## Part I. Trad sessions, dance and jams

### Character

Chicago's trad scene is **Irish at the core and dance-hall-centred at the edges**. On a typical week there are about 18 weekly Irish sessions plus a dozen monthly ones, about 89 session-nights a month, which puts Chicago in the top tier of American Irish-music cities. The sessions are unusually **professional-led**: a handful of well-known players (piano accordionist [Jimmy Keane](https://jimmykeane.com/sessions), John Williams, banjo player Pauline Conneely, flute player Larry Nugent) host most of the best-known ones. The Irish heritage is deep. Police chief [Francis O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_O%27Neill) collected his famous books from Chicago players, and the [Irish Musicians Association of America](https://www.irishmusiciansassociation.org/history), founded here in 1956, is described by its president as [the oldest Comhaltas branch in the United States](https://thesession.org/members/39391).

The single most important building is the **Irish American Heritage Center (IAHC)** on the Northwest Side. Its pub hosts the busiest beginner-friendly Thursday session. Room 111 hosts the Francis O'Neill Club's céilí and set dancing. Its 4th-floor ballroom hosts **the Chicago Barn Dance, a weekly Monday-night contra and square dance with live old-time bands**, plus the Thanksgiving contra weekend. American contra, Irish set dancing and Irish sessions therefore share one address, and that cross-traffic is the most distinctive thing about the city.

Outside that core, the scene is spread across the suburbs rather than concentrated in a neighbourhood: English country and international dancing in Glen Ellyn and Glenview, Nordic music and barn dances in a Downers Grove coffeehouse, bluegrass jams in Tinley Park and Naperville, new Irish sessions in McHenry County breweries, and a contra in Valparaiso, Indiana. The one thing Chicago lacks for a fiddler like Nate is a deep **open old-time jam culture**. There is exactly one weekly open old-time jam (in a pizza parlour), and the city's old-time players mostly appear as **contra dance bands**, about 45-50 of them in the past year.

### Irish sessions

#### The North Side and city core

The busiest single venue is the **[Galway Arms](https://galwayarms.com/chicago-galway-arms-live-music)** (2442 N Clark, Lincoln Park). From September 2026 it advertises four free weekly sessions, each 8-10pm with rotating guests: Sunday (John Williams and Katie Grennan), Tuesday (Pat Quinn), Wednesday (Katie Grennan) and Thursday (Tim Fleming and Lexi Kennedy). The session has been on The Session [since 2004](https://thesession.org/sessions/556), when fiddler Chris Bain and box player Sheila Doorley of the band Gan Bua ran it. Its reputation has swung between "prima donna fiddlers" (a sour 2012 comment) and a 2023 visitor who found Tim Fleming's Thursday crowd "really friendly and welcoming". Regulars say not every night is fully open, so ask first.

Jimmy Keane's [list of selected weekly sessions](https://jimmykeane.com/sessions) (updated April 2026) is the best single guide. Keane himself leads:
- the **Galway Bay "Tunesday" session**, Tuesdays 8-10 in a downstairs dive bar at 500 W Diversey, started in 2022 with Kavan Donohoe and reviewed by [The Trad Tourist](https://the-trad-tourist.ghost.io/tunesday-tunes-at-galway-bay-a-night-with-irish-music-legends-jimmy-keane-and-kavan-donohoe/);
- the **Kerryman Sunday Trad Session**, 5-7pm in River North, where the musicians sit "in a small nook underneath a staircase" with room for about three guests, and the sound is piped through two floors and out to the Clark Street sidewalk ([The Session](https://thesession.org/sessions/7408));
- **"The Sunday Seisiun"**, 1-3pm at 901 W Kinzie (the venue name isn't given anywhere I could find);
- **Trad Thursday** at Chicago Gaelic Park (see below).

Other North Side and close-in sessions:
- **[The Wolfhound](https://thesession.org/sessions/7539)** (Avondale): Tuesdays from 7:30, led by owner Brendan Byrne, intermediate level.
- **[Mrs. Murphy & Sons Irish Bistro](https://thesession.org/sessions/5910)** (North Center): two sessions. On Wednesdays at 7:30 flute player Larry Nugent and Frank Quinn lead an intermediate-to-expert session ("Larry is a lovely host", June 2026). On Sundays 1-3 the **[Irish Music School of Chicago](https://kathy-medic.squarespace.com/sessions)**, led by fiddler Sean Cleland, runs a family session where students are encouraged to start tunes. That is the nearest thing Chicago has to a slow or learners' session.
- **[Chief O'Neill's](https://the-trad-tourist.ghost.io/chief-oneills-sunday-session/)** (Avondale): Sundays 5-7, led by Pauline Conneely. An advanced but open session that The Trad Tourist described as all-women-led.
- **Fifth Province Pub at the IAHC**: Thursdays, led by James Conway and Mike Austin, who also teach at the Old Town School. A [2024 account](https://the-trad-tourist.ghost.io/a-night-at-the-5th-province/) counts **20-30 players aged from their 20s to their 80s**, mixing tunes, songs and poetry. It is "one of the largest sessions in Chicago" and openly beginner-friendly. Times differ between sources (7-9 or 8-10).
- **[The Harp and Fiddle](https://thesession.org/sessions/6859)** (Park Ridge): Thursdays 8-10 with Pauline Conneely.
- **[The Celtic Knot](https://thesession.org/sessions/1778)** (Evanston): Sundays 3-5 with John Williams. The pub closed on 30 April 2023 and reopened at 2022 Central St by January 2025, and the session came with it.
- **The Western Bar** (Lincoln Square area): announced as last Tuesday of the month from September 2026 after an irregular spring.

#### The South Side and south suburbs

The South Side was the historic heartland of Chicago Irish music, and it has thinned. **[Lanigan's Pub](https://thesession.org/sessions/2945)** in Mount Greenwood, which a 2022 poster called "the longest running continual session in Chicago", used to run weekly with piper Kevin Henry (a 1958 IMA charter signatory), flute player Albert Neary and bodhrán player Malachy Towey. After their deaths it became a **monthly** session (4th Sunday, 4-6pm, as of May 2026).

**[Clancy's](https://thesession.org/sessions/3505)** in Oak Lawn picked up the slack after 2013. In 2026 it has a Sunday afternoon session (Kell and Mike Chole, "very welcoming"), a Wednesday night and a Friday night with John Williams; the Saturday session ended in May 2024.

At **Chicago Gaelic Park** in Oak Forest, the old Thursday session led by flute player Pat Finnegan "fell victim to Covid-19" after the deaths of several regulars. [Jimmy Keane revived it](https://thesession.org/sessions/5842) in September 2023 as Trad Thursday (1st and 3rd Thursdays, 7-9pm, Carraig Pub).

#### The collar counties and Indiana

The suburban sessions are mostly monthly but growing:
- **[Quigley's](https://www.wsirish.org/content.aspx?page_id=4091&item_id=3029399)** (Naperville): 3rd Wednesdays, run by West Suburban Irish. It has been on The Session since 2003, when it met 1st and 3rd Mondays.
- **[McNally's](https://thesession.org/sessions/9191)** (St. Charles): 4th Sunday afternoons since 2025, aimed at beginners.
- **[Chicago Street Pub](https://chicagost.com/monday-night-irish-sessions)** (Joliet): weekly on Mondays with John Condron.
- **[The Cottage Pub](https://thesession.org/sessions/9351)** (Crystal Lake): every other Wednesday; moved from McGonigal's in Barrington.
- Two **McHenry County** sessions started in autumn 2024 by the same organiser, each a round-robin where notation readers are welcome: [Kishwaukee Brewing](https://thesession.org/sessions/9011) in Woodstock (4th Fridays) and [the Stanchion Pub](https://thesession.org/sessions/8967) in Harvard (2nd Fridays). Both organisers post the next date on The Session each month (October 2026 dates are posted).
- **Bobby's Irish Sessions** in Forest Park (3rd Sundays, hosts Quin Cunningham and Miriam Attia) appeared on a February 2026 calendar, but a July 2026 post asked whether it still runs.

I found no Irish session in the Indiana counties of the MSA. The nearest are in South Bend ([Fiddler's Hearth](https://thesession.org/sessions/495), outside the MSA) and Lake Geneva, Wisconsin (also outside).

#### Comhaltas, schools and lineage

The **[Irish Musicians Association of America](https://www.irishmusiciansassociation.org/history)** was founded on 19 August 1956 at the Midland Hotel on Adams Street, with musicians from Chicago, New York, Boston, Philadelphia, Detroit, Cleveland, Houston and Kansas City present. Its founders included pianist Eleanor Kane Neary and fiddler Jimmy Neary. [The jig "Eleanor Neary's"](https://thesession.org/tunes/15282) is one Liz Carroll learned from her, and the Trian II notes say the Nearys "spent a lot of time both encouraging and dazzling the younger musicians growing up in Chicago". The IMA now operates as the Comhaltas branch for NE Illinois, NW Indiana and SE Wisconsin from the IAHC. Chicago hosted the [Comhaltas North American Convention](https://cce-dv.com/event/6286043/738164049/comhaltas-ceoltoiri-eireann-north-american-convention) at the Northbrook Hilton on 9-13 April 2026. The IMA website lists no current public events of its own, so its weekly presence is through the IAHC sessions.

### Céilí, set dancing and Scottish country dance

- The **[Francis O'Neill Club](https://www.findglocal.com/US/Chicago/138134209614912/Francis-O%27Neill-Club---Chicago-Ceili-and-Set-Dancing)** meets at the IAHC: céilí practice on Fridays at 7:30 ($5, Room 111), set-dance lessons on Tuesdays, and on **first Fridays a Céilí Mór**, with free lessons 7-8 (Dan Cahill and Alan Beale) and dancing 8-11, usually to the band **Ceoltóirí Chicago**. The club also runs the **[Alan Beale Workshop Weekend](https://alanbealeworkshopweekend.org/)** (2-4 October 2026, with Ultan Mulcahy teaching).
- **[Chicago Gaelic Park](https://chicagogaelicpark.com/event/irish-set-dancing/2026-05-26/)** runs Tuesday-night set dancing, 7:30-9pm for **$2**, led by Brendan Houlihan ("Timing is not everything… it's the ONLY thing!"), plus occasional set-dance céilí mórs with live music (e.g. [August 2025, $10](https://www.facebook.com/events/chicago-gaelic-park/set-dance-ceili-mor/1440373713650839/)).
- **[RSCDS Chicago Branch](https://www.rscdschicago.org/welcome/)** holds one weekly Scottish country dance class, Tuesdays 7:30-10 at Dance Spot of DuPage in Naperville. The group began on 7 February 1980 in Palos Park and gained [branch status seven years later](https://my.strathspey.org/groups/group/759/). An older listing mentions a second, downtown class ("Loch Mich") that the current site no longer lists. The branch holds a Midwinter Ball and co-hosts a June Midwest Scottish Weekend with Milwaukee and Madison.

### Contra and barn dances

#### The Chicago Barn Dance Company (CBDC)

The [CBDC](https://www.chicagobarndance.org/) "sponsors and supports contra dance, traditional square dance and old-time music". Its anchor is the **Monday-night dance at the IAHC ballroom**, 7pm, $7-10. It runs about 48 Mondays a year, closing only when the IAHC closes for Labor Day, Memorial Day, St Patrick's Day and the like. A 2010 video shows it earlier at [Bethany Church Hall on Paulina](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ad5PJZKfpGY). The CBDC's Facebook page logged **93 dancers on 30 December 2024**.

The CBDC's [iCal feed](https://www.chicagobarndance.org/calendar/) is the region's master dance calendar, covering Chicago, the suburbs, Valparaiso and Wisconsin. Over the 12 months to October 2026 it lists **101 in-MSA dance dates** with roughly **45-50 distinct bands and 35 callers**. That is a large, home-grown band ecosystem, mostly old-time and contra string bands: Wabi Sabi, Birdhouse Hollow, Big Brandy, Hillbilly Diaspora, Wayward Potatoes, Ravenswing, Rare Privilege, Hot Coffee Breakdown, Ship of Fools, Cosmic Weather, the Resurrection Marys, Roasted and Salted, Old T-Blue & the J-87s, and **the Ladies Banjo Society of Illinois**. That last band includes former Chicago Tribune columnist **Eric Zorn** (who also calls) and his son Ben Zorn on fiddles, with Mary Tabatowski, Kathy Hirsh and Fred Campeau ([CBDC](https://www.chicagobarndance.org/ai1ec_event/contra-square-dance-13-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-4-2-2-3-2-2-2-2/)).

The most-booked callers are Meg Dedolph, Moose Flores, Georges Augustin, Jo Mortland, Wren Eisenhauer, Will Mentor and Lisa Sutton. Visiting national callers in the past year included Lisa Greenleaf and Bob Isaacs. The CBDC also runs occasional **advanced contra** nights and **squares workshops** "for tips" at St. John's Lutheran on Montrose. Its [first-timer page](https://www.chicagobarndance.org/your-first-contra-dance/) says callers use **larks and robins** unless a dance is advertised otherwise. On COVID: "masks are optional" and attendance is still registered for contact tracing.

#### Queer Contra Chicago

[Queer Contra](https://chicagoreader.com/city-life/queer-contra-chicago/) meets on the third Saturday of each month at Second Unitarian Church in Lakeview: lesson 6:30, dance 7-9:30, $10-20 suggested, no one turned away. It is a masked, sober space, and afterwards a crowd walks to Bamboo Garden on Clark for dinner. It **relaunched in 2017** after an earlier incarnation. Moose Flores tells the [Chicago Reader (May 2026)](https://chicagoreader.com/city-life/queer-contra-chicago/) that at his first dance the outgoing organiser announced the band "wouldn't play a single note until two volunteers stepped up". He raised his hand and has organised it ever since. In 2019 it drew [about 50-60 dancers a month](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2019/06/12/chicagos-queer-contra-practices-an-old-dance-think-jane-austen-think-oklahoma-made-new/). That year a CBDC board member predicted it was "only a matter of time" before the mainstream dance went gender-free, and the Reader now credits Queer Contra with being "among the first Chicago-area dances to drop 'ladies and gents' for the now-common 'larks and robins'".

#### Other contras and family barn dances

- **UChicago** contras ([Folk Arts Community](https://uchicagofac.wixsite.com/contra)): roughly monthly in term at Ida Noyes Hall, $5 for students and $8 for others. The same hall hosted contra dances and morris by the U of C Folk Dancers [in 1948](https://history.midwestmorrisale.org/index.php?title=Chicago_Morris).
- **[Valparaiso Oldtime Dance Society](https://valpo.chicagobarndance.com/)** (Porter County, IN, inside the MSA): 2nd Saturdays at Trinity Lutheran's fellowship hall. The house band is usually the **Hoosier Recruits** (the most-booked band on the whole calendar, 7 dates). $8 adults, $4 children, $20 households.
- **[Fox Valley Folklore Society](https://www.chicagobarndance.org/regional-dances/) all-ages barn dances**: 2nd Sundays 2-5pm, September-April, at Grace Lutheran in Glen Ellyn, on a $5-20 sliding scale.
- **[Plank Road Folk Music Society](https://www.plankroad.org/BarnDances.html)** barn dances: seven a season at the Two Way Street Coffee House in Downers Grove, $5, with the volunteer **Plank Road String Band** led by Betsy Anderson. Anyone at intermediate level can join its monthly practices in Lombard.
- **Ridgeville Park District** in Evanston runs twice-yearly family barn dances, "All genders included — everyone dances!".
- The **Chicago Park District's Night Out in the Parks** series hosts free outdoor contras with Baba Yaga's Stew several times each summer and autumn.
- **[Breaking Up Thanksgiving](https://www.breakingupthanksgiving.com/)** (27-29 November 2026, IAHC) is the big annual weekend. It used to be open band, open caller and all-night; now it is pre-scheduled "because we no longer have the time and space to dance all night, as we once did."
- The Wisconsin dances ([MKE Contra](https://www.mkecontra.org/) on 2nd and 4th Fridays, and Delafield on 1st Saturdays) are outside the MSA but share Chicago's calendar, bands and callers.

**Rebound:** the Monday dance is back to weekly, crowds have returned (93 on a holiday Monday), and new series keep appearing (park-district contras, balfolk). Some pandemic habits remain: Queer Contra still requires masks and the CBDC still registers attendance for contact tracing. No published age data was found. Organisers' descriptions range from families with children (barn dances) to students (UChicago).

### English country, international, Israeli and Balkan dance

**[Chicagoland English Country Dancers](https://www.chicagolandecd.org/about-us)** (a CDSS affiliate) was founded in January 2006 by Mady Newfield and Tammy Bretscher. It runs two series:
- **Chicagoland West**, at Grace Lutheran in Glen Ellyn, usually two Sundays a month. International folk dance runs 1-3:15, then English country dance 3:30-6, for $5-10. The site gives both "2nd and 4th" and "1st and 3rd" Sundays; the November-December 2026 dates are the 8th, 22nd, 13th and 20th.
- **Chicagoland North**, on 3rd Thursdays at John's Park Field House in Glenview, called by Cheryl Yenter. In summer it moves "outside to a charming residential setting".
- It also runs the **Harvest Moon Dance Weekend** in St. Charles (3-5 October 2026).

Mady Newfield is a central figure. She founded **Fermilab International Folk Dancing** at the physics lab in 1988 and ran it for 32 years, directs the **June Camp** international folk dance weekend, and also teaches for RSCDS. She is a PhD linguist.

Other international dance:
- **[Evanston Monday Night Folk Dancers](https://www.evanstonfolkdance.org/)**: Mondays 7:30-10 at Lake Street Church, part in person and part on Zoom.
- **Chicago Israeli Folk Dancing** at Temple Beth Emet, Evanston, every Thursday. Its Facebook group calls it ["Chicago's longest running weekly Israeli Folk Dance session (50 years as of 3/74!)"](https://www.facebook.com/groups/chicagoisraelidancing/), i.e. 50 years in March 2024 after starting in March 1974. It is led by Phil Moss and Penny Brichta and is still on [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/dance-358/) for November 2026.
- **[Ensemble Balkanske Igre](https://www.balkanskeigre.org/classes-rehearsal)**: an open Thursday class (6-8pm, 6069 N Milwaukee Ave) with dances from Bulgaria, Croatia, Greece, Hungary, Macedonia, Romania and Serbia. It co-produces a Spring Festival of Balkan music and dance that it bills as its 60th.

The volunteer networks (Ethnic Dance Chicago's calendar, the Chicago Folk Dance Council's list, last updated in 2019) show a mature, ageing IFD community. The [Argonne IFD blog](https://argonneifd.blogspot.com/) recently mourned teacher Edie Spear, who danced on Zoom until her death at 97.

### Nordic, Scottish fiddle and other session traditions

- **Nordic**, the fastest-growing niche:
  - The **[Scandinavian Jam](https://swedishamericanmuseum.org/product/34768)** at the Swedish American Museum in Andersonville: monthly on Sundays, with tunes taught by ear 1-1:45 and the jam from 2pm ("Recording devices are encouraged"). It was started around 2010-11 by Nordic fiddler **Mary Allsopp** of the **Chicago Spelmanslag** ([Paul Tyler](https://drdosido.net/fiddleclub/)).
  - In October 2025 the Two Way Street Coffee House in Downers Grove added a **[Nordic Jam](https://artsdupage.org/event/nordic-jam/)** on 1st Thursdays (Allsopp again) and a monthly **Nordic Dance** night with a live band. The jam's tunes feed the dance, so musicians learn to play for dancers.
- **Scottish:** the **[Chicagoland Scottish Fiddle and Accordion Club](https://www.facebook.com/ChicagoSFAC/)** meets monthly at Seman Violins in Skokie (10 January and 11 July 2026 meetings listed). I found **no public Scottish or Cape Breton pub session**.
- **Multi-tradition fiddle:** the **[Fiddle Club of the World (Chicago Chapter)](https://www.findglocal.com/US/Chicago/535429539981782/Fiddle-Club-of-the-World---Chicago-Chapter)**, founded in 2008 by fiddler and folklorist Paul Tyler, holds about six artist meetings a year at Seman Violins. Each is a close-up concert followed by a jam where the guest teaches tunes. It held 44 such meetings with players from 8 countries in its first six years. In 2026 a new **Dunelands Chapter** in Miller Beach, Indiana hosted Danish fiddler Kristian Bugge.
- **Cajun:** the **[Chicago Cajun Jam](https://www.facebook.com/events/six-penny-bit/chicago-cajun-jam/2427955947707753/)** at Six Penny Bit (5800 W Montrose) calls itself "Chicago's only regularly-scheduled Cajun jam", on Sunday afternoons about monthly.
- **French and Breton:** **[Les Rats Mouillés](https://www.chicagobarndance.org/event/balfolk-with-les-rats-mouilles/)** play **balfolk** (fiddle, hurdy-gurdy and accordion, with teaching by Meg Dedolph) at Sketchbook Brewing in Skokie every couple of months, for tips, half of which go to an immigrant-rights coalition.
- **Not found:** any French-Canadian/Québécois session, and any open klezmer jam. Klezmer here is a performance scene: the Maxwell Street Klezmer Band (founded 1983) and the Klezmer Music Foundation's monthly showcase are for listening.

### Old-time and bluegrass

**Old-time.** The one weekly open old-time jam is **Banjo After Dark** at Borelli's Pizza, 2124 W Lawrence. The [Bluegrass Chicago calendar](https://events.bluegrasschicago.com/) describes it this way: "Every Thursday … the old time community meets for a short show at 7pm always followed by an open jam at 8pm", with featured guests on alternate weeks. ChickenFat Klezmer Orchestra opened on 3 September 2026. The first 6-7 tunes come from a set list, then anyone can call one. Otherwise old-time lives in the **dance bands**, in the **Plank Road String Band** practice (2nd Saturdays, Lombard), and in house parties (a "Chicago Sunday Old Time Music Jam" at a family music party appears on YouTube; not counted). Chicago's old-time fiddlers also travel to [Fiddler's Hearth in South Bend](https://www.banjohangout.org/archive/396757) on Tuesdays.

**Bluegrass** is more suburban and better organised. The **[Northern Illinois Bluegrass Association](http://www.nibaweb.org/index_files/jams.htm)** keeps a public jam calendar. Current jams:
- **Hailstorm Brewing, Tinley Park**: weekly on Wednesdays 6-8, hosted by Steve Haberichter of [Down Home Guitars](https://downhomeguitars.com/events/bluegrass-jams/), all ages, no cover.
- **Naperville Acoustic Pickers**: Saturday mornings on the Riverwalk, moving to Tobias Music in winter.
- **Oak Park Farmers' Market band jam**: Saturdays, held at the Friendly Tap in Berwyn in winter.
- **[Bluegrass Breakdown](https://dice.fm/event/py399l-bluegrass-breakdown-4th-oct-judson-moore-distillery-chicago-tickets)** at Judson & Moore Distillery in Avondale: 1st Sundays. Host Aaron Smith (Mulefoot, Paulina Hollers) puts together a house band and then opens the floor; the band calls "bluegrass standards, country songs, old time fiddle tunes".
- Monthly jams at Werk Force Brewing (Plainfield) and Black Lung (Grayslake).
- A potluck jam season at **Millbrook United Methodist** in Kendall County.

The NIBA calendar also shows heavy churn: eight suburban jams were given end dates between October 2024 and December 2025.

### Morris and sword

Chicago has one of the oldest morris histories in North America. Hull House dance teacher **Mary Wood Hinman** taught folk dance to immigrants' children and founded a folk dance school in 1904. By 1910 University High School boys were dancing "Bean Setting" at UChicago, a dance Hinman had found the previous summer kept alive for five generations by an Oxfordshire bricklayer's family ([Midwest Morris Ale history](https://history.midwestmorrisale.org/index.php?title=Chicago_Morris)). The 1934 World's Fair "Merrie England" village had morris and sword dancers. The U of C Folk Dancers danced morris in 1948, and the **Chicago Morris Dancers** formed in 1969 when Paul Collins' square dance group "ended up with far too many men".

Today **[Pullman Morris and Sword](https://www.findglocal.com/US/Chicago/230565453648101/Pullman-Morris-and-Sword)** practises weekly on Sunday nights (in Pullman in summer, Hyde Park in winter). It runs a public May Day dance with a Maypole at 7:30am in Arcade Park with **Mortarbordis Border Morris**, and some members dance the sun up at Promontory Point at about 4:45am. **Ravenswood Morris** is still listed by [NAMDO](https://morrisdance.org/midwest/), but its last published practice details date from 2001.

### Modern western square dance

The **[Metropolitan Chicago Association of Square Dancers](https://squaredancechicago.com/)** calendar lists **49 dances and workshops in October 2026 and 35 in November** from about 17 clubs across six Illinois counties and Hebron, Indiana. Clubs include Arlington Squares, NW Grand Squares (McHenry), MotivAters (Advanced/Challenge, Woodridge), Glenview Squares, North Shore Squares, Southside Squares, Swing'n Sugar Squares (Oak Lawn), Fox Valley Mixers (St. Charles), Happy Twirlers (Des Plaines) and the LGBTQ club **Chi-Town Squares**. By event count this club world is about six times the size of contra, but it is a separate, level-based world (Mainstream, Plus, Advanced) with recorded music, and it barely overlaps with the trad dance scene. The CBDC's "squares workshops" teach traditional squares, not MWSD.

### Surprises

1. The city's main contra dance is in the ballroom of the **Irish** heritage centre, which also hosts the Comhaltas branch, a session, céilís and the Thanksgiving contra weekend.
2. One pub, the Galway Arms, runs **four** free public sessions a week.
3. The Irish Musicians Association of America, founded in Chicago in 1956, is called the oldest Comhaltas branch in the US.
4. The only weekly open old-time jam is in a pizza parlour, and some weeks the opening act is a klezmer orchestra.
5. One accordionist (Jimmy Keane) leads five sessions and keeps the city's session list.
6. Queer Contra pushed "larks and robins" into the mainstream Chicago dance.
7. Modern western square dance clubs still out-schedule contra about six to one.
8. Chicago morris dates to 1910, and today's Pullman side dances up the May Day sun at 4:45am.
9. Evanston's Israeli dance has met weekly since 1974.
10. New Irish sessions are appearing in McHenry County breweries (2024), and a "Fiddle Club of the World" chapter opened on the Indiana dunes (2026).

## Part II. Song, listening rooms, traditional singing, instruction, radio and makers

*Thread: song_listening. Covers layers 2–3 plus instruction, radio/media and shops/makers for the Chicago-Naperville-Elgin, IL-IN MSA. Researched Oct 30, 2026.*

### Character

Chicago's song scene is built around one huge institution and a ring of small, stubborn suburban clubs. The [Old Town School of Folk Music](https://www.oldtownschool.org/aboutus/) dominates in a way no folk organization does in most American cities. In 2025 it taught 33,845 students, sold tickets to 63,325 concertgoers, had $17.6 million in revenue and employed 276 teaching artists ([2025 annual report](https://www.oldtownschool.org/media/doc/2026/OTSFM2025Annual%20Report.pdf); [audited financials](https://www.oldtownschool.org/media/doc/2026/OTSFM_AuditedFinancialReport_2025.pdf)). Its founding idea, that "anyone can learn to play" in a group and then sing together afterward, shapes the whole metro.

Around it, the participatory song life happens in the suburbs, mostly in church basements, village halls, libraries and a brewpub. Since 1975 the Fox Valley Folklore Society has held a sing-around every Wednesday. Plank Road (Downers Grove) has run sing-arounds since 1985, and the Lake County Folk Club in Grayslake dates from 1991. The city's Sacred Harp community is unusually literary and folk-revival in origin. Because Chicago is a Great Lakes port, sea-shanty singing is a living hobby here. The metro also has some of the best folk radio in the country, with about 13 hours a week of folk and Irish programming, and it was once the guitar-making capital of the United States.

### The Old Town School of Folk Music in depth

#### Founding (1957)

The school grew out of Chicago's progressive labor-theatre and People's Songs circles. Win Stracke, a classically trained bass singer who had been blacklisted from TV, had the idea and the money. Frank Hamilton, a young Californian who had learned group teaching from Bess Lomax Hawes, brought the method. Dawn Greening hosted Hamilton's first $2-a-lesson guitar class in her Oak Park living room, and Gertrude Soltker ran the books ([school history, part 1](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/1); [part 2](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/2)).

The school opened in the old Immigrant Bank building at 333 W. North Avenue. Its own history gives the date as Friday, November 29, 1957, "though there's some dispute about the date"; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_School_of_Folk_Music) and recent obituaries say December 1. George Armstrong played bagpipes to open the night. Big Bill Broonzy played a blues, and Hamilton transcribed Broonzy's right-hand pattern onto a blackboard and taught it to the room on the spot.

Within three months the school had 160 enrolled students and another 200 coming to informal "sings." By 1963 it had 16 teachers. The building was slated for demolition in 1967, and in 1968 the school moved to 909 W. Armitage, an 1896 building known as "the Aldine" ([part 3](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/3)).

#### The "Second Half" and the method

Hamilton's invention, the **Second Half**, was a combined singalong after every evening's group classes, with each student playing whatever their skill level allowed. It is still running. The school lists 14 free weekly Second Half slots across its three buildings, open to "currently enrolled students AND members of the public," plus three on Zoom. Each half hour mixes songs from the Old Town School Songbook with a weekly two-song "Twin Spin" chosen by a teacher ([jams page](https://www.oldtownschool.org/community/jams/)). The 2025 annual report calls it "the oldest musical tradition at the Old Town School." Other weekly jams include:

- a Wednesday noon Americana jam (4544 N. Lincoln)
- a Thursday 8pm "Blues Jam for All" at Armitage
- a Saturday noon family jam at Armitage

#### Decline and turnaround

Enrollment collapsed after the folk boom. The school's own history says it fell to about 300 students a week by 1981, down from "more than 1000 just a few years earlier," and the school was nearly $60,000 in the red ([part 4](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/4)). Wikipedia instead gives a 1975 peak of about 650 a week; the sources disagree.

Ray Tate resigned in 1982. His successor, Jim Hirsch, cut costs (some teachers worked without pay), won the school's first grants and sold a neighboring building. In 1984 Hirsch hired banjoist Michael Miles as program director. Miles rebuilt the curriculum by adding fiddle, voice, percussion, ensembles and the Wiggleworms early-childhood classes (1985). The student body doubled between 1984 and 1987 ([part 4](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/4); [part 5](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/5)).

The school then broadened its idea of "folk." It added a Festival of Latin Music (1985–2000) and the La Peña Wednesday concerts (1995), during which minority enrollment tripled. In 1990 Paul Tyler and Steve Rosen started an **Old Time String Ensemble** that taught tunes by ear.

#### Lincoln Square (1998)

In 1993 the city offered the school the derelict 1931 Art Deco Hild Library at 4544 N. Lincoln Avenue. The old reading room became a two-level concert hall where no seat is more than 45 feet from the stage. The school's history says it holds 425; the 2025 report calls the **Gary and Laura Maurer Concert Hall** "intimate 430-seat."

The school opened there in 1998 and ran its first Folk & Roots Festival in Welles Park the same summer. Revenue nearly doubled between 1997 and 1999, from about $3 million to $6 million, and by 2004 about 6,000 students were registered each week. Leaders debated more than once whether to drop the word "folk" from the name. The school's history also credits its arrival with speeding up Lincoln Square's gentrification ([part 6](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/6)).

#### The campuses today

- **4544 N. Lincoln (west building):** Maurer Hall, the Music Store, a gallery, a café and classrooms
- **4545 N. Lincoln (east building):** the Myron R. Szold Music & Dance Hall
- **909 W. Armitage (Lincoln Park campus):** the Richard Bers Armitage Concert Hall and classrooms
- **Wiggleworms branches:** Evanston, Glen Ellyn, Naperville, Park Ridge and Western Springs

Sources: [directions page](https://www.oldtownschool.org/aboutus/directions/).

#### The Armitage sale fight, the union and the first contract (2018–2022)

In October 2018 the board announced it would sell the Armitage building to fund a roughly $10 million endowment, citing falling enrollment ([Newcity](https://music.newcity.com/2019/02/20/folk-and-blues-the-tribulations-of-the-old-town-school/)). A group of students, teachers and alumni called **Save Old Town School** organized letter campaigns, petitions and rallies. Executive director Bau Graves resigned in January 2019, citing health. On January 16, 2019 the faculty voted to organize as the **Old Town Teachers Organization (OTTO)** under the Illinois Federation of Teachers.

In March 2019 CEO Jim Newcomb announced that the board would not sell and would focus on enrollment instead. "We argued from the beginning: Rather than sell the building they should fix the problems," said Rich Gordon of Save Old Town School ([Block Club](https://blockclubchicago.org/2019/03/22/old-town-school-will-not-sell-armitage-building/)).

Bargaining began in October 2019. Federal mediation started in March 2021, and the school's FAQ described a unit of "over 200 part-time teachers" ([negotiations FAQ](https://www.oldtownschool.org/community/negotiations/)). The first contract came in June 2022 after a nine-hour session. It included a 4% raise and wider health-insurance eligibility ([Block Club](https://blockclubchicago.org/2022/06/03/old-town-school-of-folk-music-teachers-reach-deal-for-1st-union-contract/); [Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/news/2022/6/3/23153591/old-town-school-folk-music-teachers-union-organization-contract-agreement-reached)).

#### Status in 2026

Armitage is still a working campus, and the school is growing again. In 2025 adult participation rose 13% and youth participation 5%. Revenue was $17.59 million against $16.86 million in expenses, for a small surplus after a $683,000 loss in 2024. Tuition brought in $7.27 million and concerts $3.29 million, and net assets were $24.9 million ([audit](https://www.oldtownschool.org/media/doc/2026/OTSFM_AuditedFinancialReport_2025.pdf)). Troy Anderson is executive director and CEO ([staff page](https://www.oldtownschool.org/aboutus/staff/)).

Other 2025 figures from the annual report:

- **Classes:** 24,542 people in group classes, 5,060 in private lessons and 4,243 in workshops
- **Group-class registrations by category:** guitar 22.3%, Wiggleworms 17.9%, dance 14.7%, ensemble 9.8%, piano 8.6%, voice 8.2%, string band 5.4%
- **Financial aid:** $207,468 to 397 enrollees
- **Square Roots** (July, Lincoln Avenue): a record 45,000+ attendees
- **World Music Wednesday:** 38 free concerts, 5,820 attendees
- **Fretboard Summit:** 472 attendees and 94 luthiers and vendors

The fall 2026 session began October 26. Upcoming shows included six nights of Lucinda Williams and the Jake Blount Band in Szold Hall ([concerts](https://www.oldtownschool.org/concerts/)).

#### Notable teachers and figures

Early faculty included Frank Hamilton, Ted Johnson, banjoists Fleming Brown and Stu Ramsey, and Brazilian singer-guitarist Valucha deCastro. Later anchors were Ray Tate, Mark Dvorak, Skip Landt (harmonica), Jimmy Tomasello (guitar program head from 2002), Paul Tyler and Steve Rosen (old-time), and Steve Levitt (ensembles).

The fiddle program in fall 2026 has Maggie Neatherlin, Colby Maddox, Jordan Wankoff and Sean Colledge, with Irish fiddle taught by Devin Shepherd and Sean Colledge ([fiddle classes](https://www.oldtownschool.org/classes/adults/fiddle/)).

**Ted Johnson** was at opening night in 1957 and became the school's second teacher. He later taught yodeling there, co-founded the Chicago Sacred Harp Singers, and was still taking Irish fiddle classes at the school in his 90s. He died on October 10, 2026 at 98, and a memorial is planned at the school ([Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/obituaries/2026/10/16/old-town-school-of-folk-teacher-ted-johnson-obituary-music-chicago-history); [WBEZ](https://www.wbez.org/obituary/2026/10/17/old-town-school-of-folk-teacher-ted-johnson-obituary-music-chicago-history)).

### Listening rooms, coffeehouses and folk clubs

**Two Way Street Coffee House (Downers Grove, since 1970).** This is the region's longest-running folk coffeehouse. It began as a weeknight drop-in center for teenagers at First Congregational United Church of Christ, founded by Dave Humphreys, and is still run by volunteers as a church outreach ministry. The Friday concerts have run for more than 50 years; the suggested donation went from $1 to $2 in the 1990s and is $10 today.

The room seats 60. It is a member of Local 1000, the traveling musicians' union, and guarantees performers scale pay. It recorded concerts for local cable from 1982. The basement flooded in April 2013 and reopened after renovation, and in 2020 the room went online and set its single-concert donation record ([history](https://twowaystreet.org/history/); [about](https://twowaystreet.org/about/)).

Two Way Street also hosts:

- the revived **Last Thursday** open mic for high-school and college performers (begun 2001)
- a **5th Saturdays** workshop series (for example, "Murder Ballads: From Song to Story" with the Fox Valley Folklore Society on October 31, 2026, $25)
- a **Heritage Matinee** series of Sunday afternoon concerts
- **Cultural Connections**, a 2025 program of Nordic jams and dances run with Chicago Spelmanslag
- **Tunes and Tales** songwriter rounds, planned for 2026

**The Plank Road Folk Music Society (1985)** is a separate, partner club that runs most of Two Way Street's participatory events ([about](https://www.plankroad.org/AboutUs.html)):

- **Folk sing-around**, 1st and 3rd Saturdays, 2–4pm, with songbooks provided and led by former Old Town School guitar teacher George Mattson since the mid-1990s
- **"Bring Your Own Song" circle**, 2nd Tuesdays (2nd Thursdays in summer), since 2011
- **Country & Western sing-around**, 4th Saturdays, led by club president Bob O'Hanlon
- **Barn dances**, seven a season, with the all-volunteer Plank Road String Band

Membership is $20 a year.

**Lake County Folk Club (Grayslake, 1991)** runs a full monthly cycle ([calendar](https://thelakecountyfolkclub.org/calendar.html); [concerts](https://thelakecountyfolkclub.org/concerts.html)):

- open stage on the 2nd Sunday
- concert on the 3rd Sunday at the Grayslake Village Hall ($20, or $15 for members; 2026 acts included Joe Filisko & Eric Noden, Patty & Craig and Kray Van Kirk)
- song circle on the 4th Sunday
- a "virtual song share" on some fifth Sundays

**Acoustic Renaissance Concerts (Hinsdale)** is a monthly series at the Unitarian Church of Hinsdale, now in its 31st season, so it began in the mid-1990s. The 2026–27 season includes John McCutcheon ($30), Claudia Schmidt, Richard Shindell, Vance Gilbert, House of Hamill, Lucy Kaplansky and Cosy Sheridan; tickets are $20–30, or $188 for the season. Several shows are broadcast live from 8 to 9pm on WFMT's Folkstage ([schedule](http://acousticren.com/html/schedule.htm)).

#### City and inner-suburban rooms

- **FitzGerald's (Berwyn, 1980).** The FitzGerald family's roadhouse is the region's roots and Americana club. It is credited with bringing Clifton Chenier and zydeco to Chicago audiences and hosts the American Music Festival over Fourth of July weekend (43rd edition in 2026). Will Duncan and Jess King bought it in March 2020, days before the pandemic shutdown. In 2025 it became the first music venue in Illinois on the National Register of Historic Places; the building was a 1911 dance hall and, as Club Ritz, a Capone-gang haunt ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FitzGerald's_Nightclub); [Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/music/2025/01/14/fitzgeralds-nightclub-berwyn-national-register-of-historic-places)).
- **SPACE (Evanston).** A listening room behind Union Pizzeria. Its promotion arm, SPACE Presents, launched the **Evanston Folk Festival** at lakefront Dawes Park in 2024. The first year drew 4,500 people on Saturday and 4,000 on Sunday, plus about 500 free children a day ([Chicago Tribune](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2024/09/09/first-ever-evanston-folk-festival-draws-crowds-to-the-lakefront/)). The 2026 lineup included the Indigo Girls, Trampled by Turtles, Josh Ritter, Valerie June and Sierra Hull, and 2027 is set for September 11–12 ([festival site](https://evanstonfolkfestival.com/)).
- **Hey Nonny (Arlington Heights, 2018).** A listening room and bistro for up to about 150 people. It went up for sale in October 2026 while still booking shows into 2027 ([Suburbtalk](https://www.suburbtalk.com/2026/10/23/the-beat-goes-on-at-hey-nonny-despite-being-listed-for-sale/)).
- **The Hideout.** A bar licensed since 1934 in the Elston industrial corridor, run since 1996 by Tim and Katie Tuten with the Hinchsliff brothers. It was a home of the 1990s–2000s "insurgent country" scene around Bloodshot Records (Jon Langford, Kelly Hogan, Devil in a Woodpile with Honeyboy Edwards; Jimmy Martin played its block party). It is mostly indie today ([history](https://hideoutchicago.com/history)).
- **Uncommon Ground (Lakeview).** Hosts what it bills as Chicago's longest-running open mic, a weekly Sunday competition (sign-up 4pm, start 5pm) running for more than 25 years ([open mic](https://www.uncommonground.com/openmic)). Its Edgewater branch closed in October 2022 and was demolished in 2024 ([Block Club](https://blockclubchicago.org/2024/08/02/edgewaters-uncommon-ground-demolished-to-make-way-for-12-apartments/)).
- **Davenport's (Wicker Park).** Not a folk room. It cancelled its cabaret shows in April 2025 to rebrand as an "ultra lounge" ([Block Club](https://blockclubchicago.org/2025/04/23/davenports-piano-bar-abruptly-cancels-cabaret-shows-as-owners-rebrand-to-ultra-lounge/)).
- **City Winery** (West Loop) books a "Chicago folk" stream in its dinner-and-show room.

**Northwest Indiana.** **Front Porch Music** in Valparaiso (1991) is a shop with a downstairs room. It holds an open stage on the 3rd Wednesday at 7:30pm ($5 for performers, $6 for listeners), with a guest host who plays a 30-minute set ([concerts](https://www.frontporchmusic.com/concert)).

#### House concerts

No traditional folk house-concert circuit with published prices turned up. The visible model is **Undertow**, a Champaign company that books touring Americana and indie songwriters into fan-hosted living rooms and posts "hosts needed" calls for Chicago-area dates (Lydia Loveless, Murry Hammond and Pedro the Lion, 2026–27) ([undertowmusic.com](https://www.undertowmusic.com/)). Crain's covered the trend on January 20, 2026 ("Living room concerts heat up in Chicago"). Church-hall series such as Acoustic Renaissance and Two Way Street fill the house-concert niche here.

### Song circles, sing-arounds and folk open mics

The participatory core of the scene is suburban:

- **Fox Valley Folklore Society, Wednesdays.** A weekly sing-around "since 1975," now 6–9pm at Two Brothers Roundhouse, 205 N. Broadway, Aurora, plus a Monday Zoom sing (6:30–9:30pm) ([FVFS events](https://www.fvfs.org/events); [older events page](https://foxvalleyfolk.com/events/)).
- **Plank Road**, Downers Grove: the sing-arounds, BYOS circle and country sing-around listed above.
- **Lake County Folk Club** song circle and open stage, Grayslake.
- **Old Town School Second Half:** 14 short weekly slots, open to all. The school also runs a monthly Teen Open Mic at Armitage Hall.
- **Open mics:** Uncommon Ground (weekly Sunday), Two Way Street (last Thursday, young performers), Front Porch Music (3rd Wednesday).
- **Chicago Circle Singing**, a monthly improvised group-singing session at Grace Place in the South Loop. It is not folk repertoire, but it is a growing participatory singing practice ([site](https://chicagocirclesinging.com/)).

Excluding the Second Half, the recurring song circles, sing-arounds and singalongs total about 18 a month and the folk-leaning open mics about 8 a month.

### Folk-song festivals

- **University of Chicago Folk Festival (1961).** Student-run every February since 1961; the 66th edition was February 20–21, 2026, with Mandel Hall concerts and free workshops ([history](http://www.uofcfolk.org/history.shtml)). The first lineup included the Stanley Brothers, the New Lost City Ramblers, Elizabeth Cotten, Roscoe Holcomb and Willie Dixon. Organizers then combed record anthologies and knocked on doors to find forgotten rural musicians. A hungry young Bob Dylan was reportedly not chosen for the student hootenanny and slept in a student's cupboard for a few days ([UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/beyond-music-folk-festivals-rich-tradition-uchicago)).
- **Fox Valley Folk Music & Storytelling Festival** (Labor Day Sunday and Monday, Wheeler Park, Geneva). The 49th edition was in 2025, so 2026 was the 50th. The 2026 lineup included Anne Hills, Bryan Bowers, Claudia Schmidt, Chris Vallillo and Bill Robinson ([fvfs.org](https://www.fvfs.org/)).
- **Woodstock Folk Festival** (since 1986, third Sunday in July, Woodstock Square, McHenry County). Free. The 2026 main stage was hosted by WFMT's Marilyn Rea Beyer and WDCB's Lilli Kuzma, and the day ends with an all-sing finale. It gives Lifetime Achievement Awards (Art Thieme, Ella Jenkins, Bonnie Koloc, Tom Paxton and others) ([history](https://woodstockfolkfestival.org/the-festival/history/)).
- **Evanston Folk Festival** (2024–) and **Square Roots** (Old Town School, July, 45,000+ in 2025). Square Roots continues the Welles Park Folk & Roots Festival begun in 1998.


### Traditional singing

#### Sacred Harp / shape-note

Chicago has one of the oldest and densest Northern Sacred Harp communities. The Chicago Sacred Harp Singers formed in the early 1980s, founded by Ted and Marcia Johnson, Judy Hauff and others who came to the music through the folk revival rather than church choirs ([sacredharp.com](https://sacredharp.com/2017/12/31/building-community-harmony-thirty-three-years-illinois-state-sacred-harp-conventions/)).

At first the group had a choir director, Phil Trier. A month after Chicago singers returned from Paxton and the National Convention, he asked, "Do you want to sound like a group of backwoodsmen?" and the singers answered with a spontaneous "yes!" The Chicago singers met a downstate group from Eastern Illinois University in Charleston when that group performed at the Libertyville School of Folk Music and noticed people in the audience holding Sacred Harp books. Hugh McGraw then phoned Marcia Johnson and Judy Hauff ("Y'all are going to have a convention?") and brought Georgia singers to the **first Illinois State Sacred Harp Convention** in Paxton on May 25, 1985. It was reputedly the first such convention in Illinois since the Civil War. Ted Johnson's tune "New Agatite" is in the 1991 Sacred Harp.

The weekly and monthly singings in 2026 ([local singings](http://shape-note.uchicago.edu/singings.html)) add up to about 6.4 a month:

#### Sea shanties and maritime song

Chicago treats itself as a port city. **Chicago Shanties** is a 282-member groups.io community that meets monthly in room 212 at the Old Town School, which hosts it informally; it used to meet at the Welles Park gazebo. Contact is Kathy Whisler, and Tom Kastle, David Yondorf and John Wohlers are listed with the group. Its latest post at research time was an "Extra Spooky" October 2026 sing ([seashanties4all listing](https://seashanties4all.com/venues-and-events/chicago-shanties/); [groups.io](https://groups.io/g/chicagoshanties)).

The **Fox Valley Folklore Society** runs a second shanty sing on 1st Sundays, 2–4:30pm, at the Batavia Public Library ([FVFS](https://www.fvfs.org/events)). The Old Town School has offered a "Sea Music and Shanty Sing" class on songs sung "around the great inland seas."

The **Chicago Maritime Festival**, organized by Tom and Chris Kastle and usually held at the Chicago History Museum in late February, ran from 2003 to 2020 and resumed in 2022. Wikipedia calls it the only wintertime maritime-music festival in the US and says over 500 people often take part ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Maritime_Festival)). By 2026, though, the festival's old domain hosted spam, and the Chicago Maritime Museum's "Festival" was an October 29 fundraiser with shanties at $100 a ticket ([museum](https://www.chicagomaritimemuseum.org/chicago-maritime-festival)). Whether the February festival still exists is unclear. Great Lakes balladeer Lee Murdock still plays maritime Christmas shows at the museum.

#### Ballad and unaccompanied singing

No standing ballad or unaccompanied-singing club was found. Ballads mostly survive inside the sing-arounds (Fox Valley, Plank Road), the Old Town School's songbook culture, and occasional workshops such as the Halloween 2026 "Murder Ballads" workshop at Two Way Street.

#### Gospel as a community practice

Chicago calls itself "the birthplace of gospel music." Thomas A. Dorsey co-founded the National Convention of Gospel Choirs and Choruses here in 1932 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Dorsey)). The city runs the free **Chicago Gospel Music Festival** in Millennium Park. The 2026 edition, July 24–25, featured Tasha Cobbs Leonard, Hezekiah Walker and Ricky Dillard, plus a Saturday community showcase of local church choirs and youth groups (Chicago Youth United; Daniel X Smith & New St. Matthew MBC) broadcast by Inspiration 1390 ([city press release](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/dca/provdrs/chicago_festivals/news/2026/june/cgmf.html)). Traditional "Dorsey-style" gospel choirs are still active, such as Dr. Lou Della Evans Reid's Traditional Gospel Choir and the Gospel Music According to Chicago Mass Choir ([Chorus America, 2018](https://chorusamerica.org/conducting-performing/sing-skillfully-unto-lord-modern-gospel-chorus-movement-chicago)). An organization called Gospel Quartets of America appears in directory listings on W. 79th Street, but quartet "anniversary" programs were not documented in this research; they are a lead for the heritage thread.

- **Academy of Irish Music.** The legacy of flute player and historian Noel Rice, who died in 2019 ([Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/2019/5/15/18626893/influential-irish-music-teacher-historian-noel-rice-dies-at-84)). - **Plank Road String Band.** Monthly open rehearsals, 2nd Saturdays, September–April, at First Church of Lombard, for intermediate players, with downloadable tune and chord sheets ([string band](https://www.plankroad.org/StringBand.html)).
- **Workshops:** Two Way Street's 5th Saturdays series.
- **Front Porch Music** lessons in Valparaiso.
- **Chicago School of Violin Making** (1975), founded by Kenneth Warren & Son, for luthier training.

### Shops and makers

**Historic makers.** Chicago was once the country's fretted-instrument factory:

- **Lyon & Healy** (1864) made guitars, mandolins, banjos and ukuleles under the **Washburn** name (1883) and still builds concert and lever (folk) harps at its Chicago headquarters ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_%26_Healy)).
- **Harmony** (1892–1975) became the largest US instrument maker and mass-produced about ten million guitars between 1945 and 1975, including Sears Silvertones and Stellas ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony_Company)).
- **Kay** (1931–1960s) grew out of Stromberg-Voisinet, whose 1928 "Stromberg Electro" may have been the first commercial electric guitar ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kay_Musical_Instrument_Company)).

**Violin shops.** Kenneth Warren & Son has been in the Loop since 1926 ([kwsviolins.com](https://www.kwsviolins.com/)). Carl Becker & Son was opened in 1968 by Carl G. and Carl F. Becker; the family line goes back to a maker whose shop burned in the 1871 Great Chicago Fire, and Paul Becker runs it now ([legacy](https://www.carlbeckerandson.com/our-legacy)). Bein & Fushi deals in rare violins.

- **Chicago Music Exchange**: a vintage guitar showroom; its owner launched Reverb.com in 2013 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Music_Exchange))
- **Front Porch Music** in Valparaiso

## Part III. Heritage and diaspora traditions

*Thread: heritage. Metro: Chicago-Naperville-Elgin, IL-IN MSA (Cook, DeKalb, DuPage, Grundy, Kane, Kendall, Lake, McHenry and Will in Illinois; Jasper, Lake, Newton and Porter in Indiana; Kenosha County, WI was dropped in the 2023 delineation). As of October 2026. The structured data is in `heritage.json`.*

### Character

Chicago's living folk traditions are mostly not Anglo-American. They are carried by the city's immigrant and migrant communities, and many of them are organized like a parish or a fraternal lodge: a cultural center with a hall, a weekly rehearsal, a youth group, an annual festival and a long memory. The signatures are Polish (both the fading polka world and the very lively górale Highlander scene on Archer Avenue), Puerto Rican bomba and plena in Humboldt Park, Mexican son, mariachi and folklórico in Pilsen and Little Village and across the suburbs, Lithuanian folk dance in Lemont, South Slavic tamburitza across the Calumet region into Indiana, and the African American traditions the city made famous, blues and gospel. The metro is also unusually rich in [NEA National Heritage Fellows](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/list?state=IL): seventeen fellowships listed under Illinois (sixteen in the metro) plus one in Schererville, Indiana. They span uilleann pipes, polka, tamburitza, Romanian cimbalom, Delta and Chicago blues, gospel, rhythm tap, children's song and, newest of all, Mexican son (2026). For a visiting fiddler, the surprise is how much of this is participatory: you can take a $15 West African and bomba class on a Saturday, sit in at a blues jam on a Wednesday, or stand at the edge of a górale fiddle band at Dom Podhalan. Some of it, though, happens in languages and halls where nobody advertises in English.

### Puerto Rican bomba and plena: thriving

Humboldt Park and Hermosa have the most active participatory scene in this slice. Two schools teach bomba as a full curriculum.

- **[AfriCaribe](https://africaribe.net/)**, founded in 2000 by Evaristo "Tito" Rodriguez, runs an academy in six-week quarters, four times a year. Each 90-minute class starts with a short history lesson. Its [Fall 2025 schedule](https://africaribe.net/academy/) ran Saturday intermediate dance and maraca/cuá percussion, Sunday adult drum classes in sicá/yubá and cuembé/holandé, and Monday evening children's and adult beginner dance, ending in a recital on Nov 22, 2025. The curriculum moves through rhythms quarter by quarter, sicá and yubá first, then cuembé and holandé.
- **[La Escuelita Bombera de Corazón](https://www.bomberadecorazon.com/laescuelita)**, founded in November 2009 by Ivelisse "Bombera de Corazón" Díaz at 3505 W North Ave, offers semester courses at $150. The [current list](https://www.bomberadecorazon.com/classes) has bomba drumming (buleo I and II), dance I and II, plena güiro and pandereta, song and history, and Bomba Kids, and every one was sold out in October 2026. Its [event calendar](https://www.bomberadecorazon.com/events) shows free, open **bombazos** every two to three months: March 2024 (two), January and March 2025, a "Bombazo y Plenazo" in November 2025, February and May 2026, and a Bomba Kids bombazo in September 2026. It also runs free workshops with visiting artists such as Mariela Mendoza Solís. Its performing groups are Bomberxs D' Cora and Las BomPleneras, and in October 2026 it held a week-long anniversary, "Flamboyants en Chicago."

The **[Segundo Ruiz Belvis Cultural Center](https://www.srbcc.org)**, founded in 1971 and now at 4048 W Armitage in a 13,000 sq ft hall with on-site artist housing, calls itself the city's longest-standing Latino cultural center. It runs paid apprenticeships for ages 14 to 22 in Afro-Caribbean percussion. Its house ensemble, **[Los Pleneros de Don Segundo](https://segundoruizbelvis.org/srbcc-home/professional-artists/pleneros-poetas/)** (since 2016), plays original plena, and it has recently added bomba. It can scale from a four-piece to a full carnival with vejigantes, cabezudos and stilt walkers. Plena's history as protest song (the center calls Puerto Rico "one of the world's oldest colonies") is part of the pitch.

Bomba also crosses diasporas. [Muntu Dance Theatre's](https://www.muntu.com/muntucommunitydanceclass) weekly $15 community class at Kennedy-King College (Saturdays 12:00 to 1:30pm, walk-ins welcome) rotates Afro-Puerto Rican bomba in alongside West African, Afro-Brazilian and soca. The big public showcases are the June **[Puerto Rican Fest](https://www.puertoricanfest.com/)** in Humboldt Park, which advertises bomba, plena, salsa and merengue companies and the People's Day Parade on Division Street, and **Fiesta Boricua** on Paseo Boricua over Labor Day weekend. Sources disagree on Puerto Rican Fest's age: the site says "45th annual" (2026) and "47th annual parade" (2025), while histories date the first parade to 1966, the year of the Division Street riots.

**Verdict: thriving.** Weekly classes, sold-out semesters, regular open bombazos and a youth pipeline. Rough cadence: about 0.4 open bombazos a month at La Escuelita, plus weekly classes during sessions at two schools and Muntu.

### Mexican traditions: son jarocho, son, mariachi, folklórico and banda

**Son jarocho.** [Jarochicanos](https://www.jarochicanos.com/) began in 2008 as a youth workshop and grew into community projects: Talleres en la 18, Jarana All Stars, Son Chiquitos and Son y Arte. It founded the [18th Street Casa de Cultura](https://www.18casadecultura.com/) in Pilsen and has organized [fandangos and huapangos](https://www.jarochicanos.com/fandangoshuapangos) "for the last decade," starting with a "Huapango de Primavera" on April 4, 2015. The collective brings jaranas to marches, protests and fundraisers. A [May 2024 report](https://latinarepublic.com/2024/05/19/fandango-por-la-humanidad-jarochicanos/) confirms the talleres continue, and the Old Town School has hosted [jarana workshops](https://www.oldtownschool.org/classes/detail/?courseid=5699) with Patricio Hidalgo and Son de Madera from Veracruz, followed by a fandanguito. I could not find a dated 2025-26 fandango calendar (it probably lives on Instagram), so I rate son jarocho **active, evidence thin**.

**Mexican son.** [Sones de México Ensemble](https://www.sonesdemexico.org), founded in Pilsen in 1994, plays huapango, gustos, chilenas and son jarocho. It has six albums, including the GRAMMY-nominated *Esta Tierra Es Tuya*, runs a Mexican music school with guitar classes in Spanish, and does school outreach. Its founders' 2026 NEA fellowship is the strongest recent recognition of any Chicago heritage tradition.

**Mariachi.** This is a school-based tradition, and it is **thriving**:

- [Orozco Academy's after-school mariachi](https://www.pcccarts.org/youthprograms/p/orozco/mariachi), run by the People's Center for Cultural & Contemporary Arts, serves grades 3 to 8 on Tuesdays and Thursdays from 3:30 to 5pm across three terms in 2025-26, at $510 a year with scholarships.
- The Chicago Mariachi Project reportedly runs in-school and after-school programs across CPS, plus auditioned youth academy ensembles.
- [Mariachi Matters](https://www.ssipchicago.org/programs/music-program/) in Romeoville is free for ages 7 to 12. Mariachi veteran Juan Cervantes started it in 2011.
- The [UIC Latin American Music Festival](https://cada.uic.edu/schedule/latin-american-music-festival-5/) included a mariachi festival day for high school and community groups on Oct 9, 2025.

**Ballet folklórico** is also **thriving**. [Ballet Folklórico de Chicago](https://balletfolkloricodechicago.org/about-ballet-folklorico-de-chicago/), founded only in 2018 by three Latino professionals from the North Side, now reports more than 400 students at its studio at 3006 N Pulaski. It is open seven days a week for ages 2 through seniors. The older [Mexican Folkloric Dance Company of Chicago](https://mexicandancecompany.org/mexican-dance/mexican-dance-history.html) (1982) rehearses three times a week and has coordinated a Latino Horizons festival since 1984.

**Banda, norteño and jaripeo-baile** culture in Little Village, Cicero and the suburbs is probably the largest Mexican dance world by attendance. It is commercial, though, and documented mainly on social media and [ticket sites](https://www.eventbrite.com/d/il--cicero/chicago-jaripeo/). I found no stable venue calendar to count, so I rate it active with low confidence.

The big annual gathering is **[Fiesta del Sol](https://www.fiestadelsol.org)** in Pilsen, a free four-day festival at Cermak and Allport in late July. It calls 2026 its "55th annual," which implies a start around 1972 (its own FAQ numbers it differently).

### Polish Chicago: polka fading, górale thriving

**Polka.** Chicago invented the national polka convention. The [International Polka Association](https://www.ipapolkas.com) traces it to Chicago "moonlight dances" from 1960 that drew thousands, and to the first convention in 1963. The city also gave polka its "Chicago push" or honky style: [Li'l Wally Jagiello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%27l_Wally), a self-taught Chemnitzer concertina player, and Eddie Blazonczyk, raised in his parents' Pulaski Ballroom. Today the IPA still holds its **[Chicago Festival of Bands](https://www.ipapolkas.com/58th-annual-chicago-festival-of-bands-and-warm-up-dance-jan-15th-16th-and-17th-2027/)** every January for National Polka Month; the 58th is Jan 15-17, 2027 at Elements by the Odyssey in Orland Park. Lenny Gomulka & Chicago Push played the [2025 edition](https://chicagopush.com/ipa-january-18%2C-2025). But the [59th IPA Festival and Convention](https://www.ipapolkas.com/ipa-dances-and-festivals/) (September 2027) moves to Erie, Pennsylvania, flagged "NOTE: New Location." The IPA's national [calendar](https://www.ipapolkas.com/events/) for November 2026 to March 2027 is dominated by Florida, Ohio, Pennsylvania and New York; the only nearby event is a "Halfway to Dyngus Day" dance at St. Sava's hall in Merrillville, Indiana. I could not verify any weekly polka dance in Chicago. (The "Midwest Polka Association" that shows up in searches is a Twin Cities group.) **Verdict: fading.** The music survives in bands and an annual festival, but the ballroom circuit is largely gone. In Whiting, Indiana, [Pierogi Fest](https://www.pierogifest.net) (since 1993) keeps a polka dance contest in its July lineup.

**Górale (Polish Highlanders).** This scene is newer and fuller. The [Polish Highlanders Alliance of North America (ZPPA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Highlanders_Alliance_of_North_America), founded in 1929 and headquartered at Dom Podhalan, 4808 S Archer Ave, keeps a [calendar](http://zppa.org/index.php/kalendarz) that reads like a village year:

- January: caroling-group review
- March: highlander poetry and storytelling contest, and **Józefinki Muzykanckie**, the musicians' gathering (Mar 15, 2026)
- May: the 6th **review of Podhale soloists and bands in the USA named for Karol Stoch "Waka"** (May 3, 2026)
- July: the big ZPPA picnic
- September: a queen contest
- October: a **dance-pair competition** (Oct 18, 2026)
- November: the **43rd Festiwal na Góralską Nutę** ("On the Highlander Tune," Nov 7-8, 2026)

Masses at the Carmelite fathers' shrine in Munster, Indiana run alongside. The ensemble **[Siumni](https://www.siumni.com/about-us-o-nas)** (founded 2002, ages 18+, rehearsing at Dom Podhalan) is the alliance's representative group. It has a children's group, Mali Siumni, has toured Poland, Hungary, Bulgaria and Colombia, and has made a yearly trip since 2003 to a sheep *redyk* in Idaho. A ZPPA children's song and dance school and the group Holni also appear in the alliance's video archive. For a fiddler, this is the most interesting tradition in the city: górale music is fiddle-led string-band music with its own scales and bowing, and ZPPA runs competitions specifically for musicians. **Verdict: thriving.**

**Polish national folk dance.** The [Polonia Ensemble](https://copernicuscenter.org/polonia-ensemble-dancers/) has run weekly classes for 25+ years at the Copernicus Center, with Krasnoludki (ages 3-5) on Saturdays from 10am to 1pm. The center, a 1,852-seat former movie palace, also hosts **[Taste of Polonia](https://nadignewspapers.com/over-40-years-taste-of-polonia-festival-has-grown-into-popular-cultural-event-attracting-up-to-40000-each-labor-day-weekend-to-copernicus-center-in-jefferson-park/)** every Labor Day since 1979, drawing up to about 40,000.

### Eastern European and Baltic: Lithuanian, Ukrainian, tamburitza, Romanian, klezmer

**Lithuanian folk dance** in suburban Lemont is the most robust Baltic scene I have seen in these metros. At the [Lithuanian World Center](https://www.lcenter.org/organizations/dance-groups/), **Grandis** (founded 1953, led by Dalia Bilašytė DeMuth) runs **six age groups** that rehearse every Sunday evening. **Spindulys** (three age groups, led by Kastytis Šoliūnas) also meets Sunday evenings. **Suktinis** (2005) calls itself one of the most actively performing Lithuanian groups in North America, and there is a women's group, **Vaivora**. Grandis holds its annual concert in the center's R. Riškus Hall. The [Balzekas Museum of Lithuanian Culture](https://www.balzekasmuseum.com) (6500 S Pulaski) marks its 60th anniversary in December 2026. **Thriving.**

**Ukrainian.** [Hromovytsia](https://www.hromovytsia.com/about/) was founded in 1980 by Roxana Dykyj-Pylypczak, Yurij Cepynsky, Marta Horodylowsky-Kozyckyj and Ivan Pylypczak. Roxana has been sole artistic director since 1986, and her daughter Daniela has been assistant director since 2013. The company blends character, ballet and jazz training into Ukrainian dance. A Chicago Bandura Ensemble performed "Melodies of Resilience" with the [Elgin Symphony](https://www.elginsymphony.org/eso-news/chicago-bandura-ensemble) in February 2025. The [Ukrainian National Museum](https://www.ukrainiannationalmuseum.org) ("over 70 years of Ukrainian culture in Chicago") runs Petrykivka painting workshops and holds an archive. Dance is **active**; bandura is **niche**.

**Tamburitza.** This is a Calumet-region tradition: steelworkers' string bands from the South Chicago, Gary and Hammond mills. Its two NEA Fellows tell the story: Adam Popovich (Dolton) and instrument maker Milan Opacich (Schererville). Five Chicago orchestras and one from northwest Indiana were billed at the [2025 Tamburitza Association of America Extravaganza](https://www.tamburitza.org/2025-taa-extravaganza): Braća Orchestra, Drina, 5 Hot Guys, Joe Leno i Pajdaši and Sinovi of Chicago, plus Dunav from Schererville. [SerbFest](https://www.saintsava.net/serbfest/) at St. Sava in Merrillville (Jul 31-Aug 2, 2026) is the big public event. Junior tamburitza groups were reported (Sloboda Junior Tamburitzans, Hoosier Hrvati) but not verified. **Active.**

**Romanian lăutari music** in Chicago has a nationally honored bearer, cimbalom player [Nicolae Feraru](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/nicolae-feraru), but I found no current ensemble or class. **Niche.**

**Klezmer.** The [Maxwell Street Klezmer Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_Street_Klezmer_Band) was founded in 1983 by Lori Lippitz and is directed by violinist Alex Koffman. Its nonprofit [Klezmer Music Foundation](https://www.klezmermusicfoundation.org) (1994, Skokie) runs the **Junior Klezmer Orchestra** for grades 5-12. The JKO has met twice a month on Sunday afternoons since 1994 and plays the Skokie Heritage Museum's Firehouse Winterfest on Dec 13, 2026. The foundation also runs congregational klezmer bands and a Salaam-Shalom Music Project. The band plays a monthly no-cover ["Klezmer Night"](https://www.klezmerband.com/public-concerts) at Sabor a Café in Niles (Wed Nov 11, 2026, 7-9pm) and invented a line dance, the "Matzorena." **Active.**

### Native Chicago: powwow and drum

The [American Indian Center](https://aicchicago.org/), founded in 1953 for families who came under the federal Relocation program, calls itself the first urban Indian center in the country. Its **[73rd annual Chicago Powwow](https://chicagoaicc.com/events/73rd-annual-chicago-powwow/)** was Saturday, Oct 3, 2026 at Schiller Woods-East, Grove 13, with grand entries at 11am and 3pm. The [2025 (72nd) powwow](https://aicchicago.org/powwow-2025/) had Smokeytown as host drum and a Woodland & Grass memorial special. AIC's own description is telling: "Our specials recognize dancers, but competition does not define the powwow."

In July 2024 AIC started **[Powwow at the Lake](https://aicchicago.org/latest-news/american-indian-center-hosts-first-powwow-at-the-lake-announces-annual-tradition/7036/)** at Foster Beach, which drew more than 500 people. Cultural ambassador Ronnie Preston danced hoop and eagle dances, and the night ended in a round dance of more than 100. It also runs **Indigifest** (2025) and a free **[Drum & Dance](https://aicchicago.org/programs/drum-and-dance/)** class on Tuesdays and Thursdays from 5 to 7pm, with drum led by Shawn Canning Jr. and dance by Ronnie Preston. That page dates from 2023, so I count it as unverified.

In the southwest suburbs, the [Midwest SOARRING Foundation](https://www.midwestsoarring.org) (cultural center in Lockport) holds solstice and equinox ceremonies and beadwork classes. Its large **[Harvest Pow Wow](https://www.harvestpowwow.com)** (its first ten years were in Mokena) was cancelled for 2026 at the DuPage County Fairgrounds, with 2027 planned. **Active**, with the 73-year powwow as its anchor.

### African American traditions: blues, gospel and tap

**Blues** still works as a community practice in Chicago, but its jams have moved from neighborhood juke joints to clubs and institutions:

- **[Buddy Guy's Legends](https://www.buddyguy.com/calendar)** lists "Jimmy Burns – Host The Jam" every Wednesday at 9pm, for $16.99. It is a ticketed jam.
- The University of Chicago's **[Logan Center](https://www.logancenter.uchicago.edu/musiclogancenter/blues)** started a free **First Wednesday Blues & Beyond Jam** in October 2025. Kenny "Beedy Eyes" Smith leads a monthly house band of next-generation players, and musicians of all levels can sit in.
- **[Rosa's Lounge](https://www.rosaslounge.com)** lists a blues jam hosted by David Levit on Nov 18, 2026.

Older lists ([Chicago Blues Guide](https://www.chicagobluesguide.com/copy-of-clubs), [Low-reen's](https://lowreensliveblues.com/?page_id=1661)) show a much wider jam circuit in 2022-23, including Kingston Mines on Sundays, B.L.U.E.S. on Wednesdays, the Water Hole on Wednesdays, and many suburban bars from Tinley Park to Porter, Indiana. Those need re-checking. The **Chicago Blues Festival** (since 1984, created by Lois Weisberg) is billed as the largest free festival of its kind. NEA Fellows Sunnyland Slim, Honeyboy Edwards and Koko Taylor stand for the generation that brought the Delta north. **Active.**

**Gospel** is Chicago's other great migration tradition. [Thomas A. Dorsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Dorsey) arrived in 1919. NEA Fellow Albertina Walker belonged to West Point Missionary Baptist Church for more than 70 years, and the Staples family came out of Chicago churches. The [Chicago Gospel Music Festival](https://www.choosechicago.com/articles/festivals-special-events/chicago-gospel-music-festival/) (since 1985) runs Jul 24-25, 2026 at Pritzker Pavilion. The living practice is in hundreds of church choirs, which I did not count; the traditional singing thread may. **Thriving** (medium confidence).

**Rhythm tap** has two recent NEA Fellows, Reggio McLaughlin (2021) and [Bril Barrett](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/bril-barrett) (2024). Barrett "came up shedding wood on street corners" and teaches tap as a Black vernacular tradition rooted in the ring shout.

**African diaspora dance and drum.** [Muntu Dance Theatre](https://www.muntu.com/copy-of-mission) (1972) holds the weekly Saturday community class and held dancer and drummer auditions on Oct 24-25, 2026. The **[African Festival of the Arts](https://www.cnwmedia.com/article/the-35th-annual-african-festival-of-the-arts-returns-to-washington-park-labor-day-weekend)**, founded by Patrick Woodtor of Africa International House, returned to Washington Park for its 35th edition over Labor Day 2025 after a one-year hiatus. Its "Drum Village" is a participatory drum circle.

### Asian, Middle Eastern and other communities

- **Korean pungmul: active.** The [Korean Cultural Center of Chicago](https://kccoc.org/all-programs/performing-arts/poongmul-school/) in Wheeling runs a Chicago Poongmul School. In Albany Park, the [HANA Center](https://hanacenter.org/events/2025/6/18/ilgwanori-open-house) hosts the adult group IlGwaNoRi ("work and play"), which welcomes Korean Americans and adoptees (open house June 18, 2025), and the women's group Woori Sori. Pungmul drumming also shows up in immigrant-rights organizing.
- **Cambodian: active.** The [National Cambodian Heritage Museum & Killing Fields Memorial](https://www.cambodianmuseum.org/class-schedule) (2831 W Lawrence) teaches **pinpeat** ensemble music on Saturdays (9:30-11:30 and 12-2), Khmer traditional music for adults, and Khmer dance for children and teens, in 10-week terms at $115 for adults and $85 for students. It frames these as "culture and healing arts" for genocide survivors and their families.
- **Indian classical dance: thriving.** [Natya Dance Theatre](https://www.natya.com/our-story/) (Hema Rajagopalan, 1975) reached 50 years in 2025, and [Kalapriya](https://www.kalapriya.org) runs classes and a Diwali celebration.
- **Chinese instrumental music: niche.** The Chinese Music Society of North America (reported founded 1976), the Chicago Chinese Cultural Institute and UChicago's [Jade Chinese Music Ensemble](https://uchicagokunyu.wixsite.com/jade-chinese-music) (2022) are the main groups.
- **Assyrian: active.** Khigga line dancing fills the April Akitu (Assyrian New Year) parade on Western Avenue and the parties that follow. There were Assyrian dance performances at the [Skokie Festival of Cultures](https://allevents.in/skokie/skokie-festival-of-cultures-assyrian-dance-performance/200028107807538) in May 2025. Evidence comes from aggregators.
- **Greek folk dance: probably active, unverified.** Reported in Greek Orthodox parish troupes.
- **Bosnian: niche.** KUD Bosna Chicago and [BHACC](https://www.bhaccchicago.org/events), with no dated 2025-26 event found.
- **Filipino: niche.** I found no standing rondalla; folk dance is taught in parish youth groups such as the one at [Holy Child Jesus](https://hcjp.org/filipino-american-association-at-hcjp).
- **Ethiopian, Czech, Norwegian and Arab (non-Assyrian)** music and dance: no current ensembles found. The [Swedish American Museum](https://www.swedishamericanmuseum.org) (Andersonville, 50 years in 2026) programs language, crafts and holidays, not music.
- **Spanish dance.** [Ensemble Español](https://www.ensembleespanol.org/history) (Libby Komaiko, 1975) is in residence at Northeastern Illinois University and has a youth company.

### Irish community culture (heritage side)

The Irish thread covers sessions and céilí; here are the community institutions. The **[Irish American Heritage Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American_Heritage_Center)** (founded 1976; it bought the former Mayfair College on Knox Avenue in 1985) reportedly has more than 5,000 members and 200+ events a year, per a [2026 profile](https://donationmission.com/2026/07/a-group-of-irish-immigrants-had-a-dream-for-chicago-forty-years-later-it-is-still-going-strong/). It has the Mayfair Theatre, the Fifth Province pub, an Irish music school and the Francis O'Neill Club, named for the [police chief and tune collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_O%27Neill) whose 1903 *Music of Ireland* came largely from Chicago's immigrant players. **[Chicago Gaelic Park](https://www.chicagogaelicpark.org)** in Oak Forest hosts GAA sports, the Chicago Feis, Irish-language classes, Tuesday set dancing, a Thursday trad session led by Jimmy Keane and an [Irish Fest](https://chicagogaelicpark.com/irish-fest/) (Aug 21-23, 2026). **[Trinity Irish Dance](https://www.trinityirishdance.com)**, founded by Mark Howard in 1982, teaches at nine locations in Illinois and Wisconsin. Together with Flatley's 1988 fellowship, it makes Chicago one of the capitals of competitive step dance. **Thriving.**

### Historic: Uptown's Appalachian migrants

In the 1950s and '60s, Uptown's subdivided mansions and residential hotels filled with migrants from the South and Appalachia, and the neighborhood was nicknamed ["Hillbilly Heaven."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown,_Chicago) The Berea-based Council of the Southern Mountains opened a Chicago Southern Center there in 1963. I found no living successor scene; this is **historic only**, and a good archival question (see curiosities).

### What a visiting fiddler could do

- **Wednesday:** the Buddy Guy's Legends jam with Jimmy Burns ($16.99, 9pm). On a first Wednesday, the free Logan Center jam instead. On a monthly Wednesday, Klezmer Night in Niles (no cover).
- **Saturday:** Muntu's $15 class at noon (West African, bomba and more), or watch a Cambodian pinpeat class in Albany Park.
- **Sunday:** Lithuanian rehearsals in Lemont (members only, but concerts are public).
- **Calendar dates:** the górale Festiwal na Góralską Nutę (early November), the IPA Festival of Bands (mid-January), a La Escuelita bombazo (watch their calendar), and the Chicago Powwow (early October).

Costs are low: most community events and bombazos are free, classes run $15 a week or $85-150 a term, and festivals are mostly free.

## Part IV. History, people, festivals, archives and the fiddle angle

*Thread: history_festivals. Metro: Chicago-Naperville-Elgin, IL-IN MSA. As of October 2026.*

### Character

Chicago's folk history rests on three institutions. The first is a nineteenth-century **police chief's tune-collecting network**. The second is a 1920s **radio barn dance** that Nashville copied. The third is a **community music school** that grew from a living room into a 24,000-student operation. More than any single style, the city's signature is documenting and institutionalizing the music. The "Chicago Reel", "Chief O'Neill's Favourite" and "Sergeant Early's Dream" are played in sessions worldwide because Chicago policemen wrote them down between 1880 and 1910. The city also kept a singer-songwriter club scene alive long after the national folk boom collapsed: [the Earl of Old Town ran from 1962 to 1984](https://news.wttw.com/2013/04/29/remembering-earl-pionke). Its old-time fiddlers lean toward **Midwestern** (Illinois and Indiana) repertoire rather than Appalachian.

Geographically, the history and festival map splits into three zones:

- **North Side corridor:** Old Town, Lincoln Park, Lincoln Square, Mayfair and Evanston, home to the Old Town School, the IAHC and the old clubs.
- **South Side Irish belt:** Brighton Park, Bridgeport and Beverly, out to Gaelic Park in Oak Forest.
- **Suburban rim:** festivals in Geneva, Wheaton, Frankfort and Berwyn, and the David Adler center in Libertyville.

The festival year is lopsided toward summer: Blues in June, Square Roots and Gospel in July, Gaelic Park in August, Fox Valley on Labor Day. The oldest festival, the University of Chicago's, is held in February.

### A timeline from O'Neill to 2026

#### The Irish foundation (1870s-1930s)

[Francis O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_O%27Neill) was born in Tralibane, County Cork, in 1848. He settled in Chicago around 1871 after years at sea and joined the police in 1873. He rose to Chief of Police (April 1901 to July 1905) and was reappointed three times by two mayors.

O'Neill played flute, fiddle and pipes, and he hired musicians. Wikipedia lists Patrick O'Mahony, James O'Neill, Bernard Delaney, John McFadden and James Early among the traditional players he recruited to the force. The [Digital Chicago exhibit](https://digitalchicagohistory.org/exhibits/show/irish-music-in-chicago/the-music/the-music--who-contributed-) gives their stories:

- **Sgt. James O'Neill** (b. 1862, Co. Down) did most of the transcribing, from playing, humming and lilting. His Brighton Park home at 3522 S. Washtenaw became the session house after O'Neill's son Rogers died in 1904. He contributed 47 double jigs and 32 single jigs to the 1903 book.
- **Sgt. James Early** (Cloone, Co. Leitrim) came to Chicago in 1874. He took up the pipes after hearing a relative, James Quinn, and made reeds. In 1893 he hosted the Donegal piper Turlough McSweeney for the whole of the World's Columbian Exposition.
- **John McFadden** (b. 1847, Carrowmore, Mayo) was a fiddler from a fiddling family. He and Early played dances together for years and were known as practical jokers. They would "steal" Barney Delaney's pet jig and play it just before he went on stage.
- **Barney Delaney** (Tullamore) was a piper and O'Neill's brother-in-law. In an undated letter O'Neill asks a son-in-law on the force to get the Havana police to "dig Barney up" when he went missing in Cuba. The description reads "mustache usually dyed... false teeth, Roman nose".
- **Edward Cronin** (b. 1838, Limerick Junction) was a weaver-turned-fiddler who could read music. He composed tunes for the Chief's family: "Rogers O'Neill", "Caroline O'Neill's Hornpipe", "Doctor O'Neill" and the "Bantry Hornpipe".
- **John Ennis** (Kildare) was a piper and flute player and an officer of the Irish Music Club. His Chicago-born son Tom Ennis became a famous piper and is remembered in the jig "Young Tom Ennis".
- **Selena O'Neill** (no relation) was trained at the Chicago Musical College. She arranged O'Neill's later books (1910-1924) and a 1940 edition of the Dance Music.

The [Irish Music Club of Chicago](https://journalofmusic.com/opinion/irish-music-club-chicago-fl-c-1901-09) (flourished about 1901-09) grew out of these house sessions. It is the earliest known organization of Irish traditional musicians in North America. Members met monthly in a rented hall and ran concerts, dances and picnics. It "eventually broke up after bitter personal rivalries arose."

O'Neill's books came out of this circle:

- *O'Neill's Music of Ireland* (1903): 1,850 pieces.
- *The Dance Music of Ireland* (1907): "O'Neill's 1001".
- *Irish Folk Music: A Fascinating Hobby* (1910).
- *Irish Minstrels and Musicians* (1913), which prints the club's group photo on page 479.

O'Neill also made wax cylinders around 1904. They later turned up in Milwaukee and were remastered by the [Ward Irish Music Archives](https://archives.irishfest.com/dunn-family-collection/History/Musician-Biographies/Francis-ONeill.htm). O'Neill died in Chicago in January 1936. In 1931 he had given his library to [Notre Dame](https://rarebooks.library.nd.edu/collections/irish_studies/oneill.html).

The two world's fairs bracket this era. In 1893 Patsy Touhey and McSweeney played in the fair's Irish "castles". In 1934, at the Century of Progress, a teenage [Joe Shannon](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/joe-shannon) played the Irish Village with step dancer Pat Roche's céilí band, whose Harp & Shamrock Orchestra then recorded for Decca. In 1926 Chicago fiddler Patrick Stack and piper Edward Mullaney recorded "The Chicago Reel" ([irishtune.info](https://www.irishtune.info/tune/310/)). Mullaney was Shannon's uncle and first pipes teacher.

#### Hull House and the folk-dance movement (1889-1930s)

Hull House opened in 1889. From about 1898 [Mary Wood Hinman](https://sfdh.us/encyclopedia/hinman_m.html) taught immigrant folk dances there, as "recreative pleasure" for young factory workers and a wholesome alternative to dance halls and saloons. In 1904 (some sources say 1905) she opened the Hinman School of Gymnastic and Folk Dance in Hyde Park to train women teachers for schools, parks and settlements. She also taught at John Dewey's school and the Francis Parker School (1906-1919), where her pupils included the future modern-dance pioneer Doris Humphrey. Hinman later helped found New York's Folk Festival Council (1930). Chicago's recreational folk-dance tradition is therefore older than its English and contra dance revivals.

#### Radio barn dance (1924-1968)

WLS's Barn Dance first aired on [April 19, 1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Barn_Dance). Fiddler-folklorist Paul Tyler searched the Chicago newspapers of the day and found the first fiddler billed as "Timothy Cornrow from Ioway" ([DrDosido blog](http://drdosido.net/drdsblog/2014/04/the-national-barn-dance/)). That summer WLS ran an on-air fiddle-team contest:

- George Adamson of Kenosha won.
- Frank Hart of Aurora and William McCormick of Marseilles also placed. Their photo with guitarist J.B. Priest became the Barn Dance's early icon.

Tommy Dandurand, a retired Kankakee streetcar operator, led the house fiddlers from fall 1924 until at least 1930. In 1927 he recorded 14 sides for Gennett with fiddler Rube Tronson and caller Ed Goodreau (see the [Square Dance History Project](https://squaredancehistory.org/items/show/1357)).

Announcer George D. Hay left after about 18 months to start what became the Grand Ole Opry. Tyler argues the Opry "copied the National Barn Dance from the start and lagged behind it for twenty years." The show moved to the Eighth Street Theatre in 1931 and was reportedly the only radio program that charged admission. NBC carried it from 1933. WLS went Top 40 and dropped it in 1960, and it ended on WGN in 1968.

The broadcasts reached far beyond Chicago. Tyler's field recordings show downstate Indiana fiddlers who learned tunes off the air, especially from Slim Miller of the Cumberland Ridge Runners.

#### The Great Migration, gospel and blues (1910s-1950s)

The Great Migration from the Delta made Chicago the capital of electric blues. [Thomas A. Dorsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Dorsey) made it the cradle of the gospel-choir movement. Big Bill Broonzy hopped a train north in the early 1920s. He later crossed into the white folk revival through People's Songs, where he met Win Stracke in 1946 ([OTS history](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/1/)). The city still celebrates both traditions with free festivals: the [Blues Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blues_Festival) (1984) and the [Gospel Music Festival](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/dca/provdrs/chicago_festivals/news/2026/june/cgmf.html) (1985). (Detailed blues and gospel community practice belongs to the heritage thread.)

#### Field recordings

On October 25, 1938, [Alan Lomax recorded "Mrs. Hulan" singing with a kantele](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc1939007_afs02471a/) in Chicago during his Great Lakes trip. A Finnish zither is a fitting first entry in the city's Library of Congress file.

The big documentary effort came in 1977. The American Folklife Center and the Illinois Arts Council ran the [Chicago Ethnic Arts Project](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2016/05/chicago-ethnic-arts-project-goes-online/): 14 folklorists, 26 communities, about 344 tapes and 14,000 photos, put online in 2016. For a fiddler it holds:

- the Midwest Fleadh Cheoil at Bogan High School (79th and Pulaski);
- Kerry concertina player Terry "Cuz" Teahan;
- Frank Thornton on the Irish Musicians' Association;
- Pat Roche and Peggy Roche Boyle on Irish dance;
- Hardanger fiddler Jørgen Hyland in Evanston;
- Alabama-born fiddler [Nancy Buckalew](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc1981004_afs20779a/), recorded at Johnny's Tavern in Cicero. She had played with "Tennessee Slim", had quit at marriage, and bought a fiddle again in 1969 because her children wanted to learn.

#### Appalachian Uptown (1950s-1970s)

Through the 1950s and 1960s Uptown was nicknamed ["Hillbilly Heaven"](https://www.wbez.org/curious-city/2021/04/15/uptowns-moment-as-a-hillbilly-heaven), the densest and poorest concentration of white Southern migrants on the North Side. It supported honky-tonks and country bars. Urban renewal, including the building of Truman College, and gentrification scattered the community by the 1990s. Carol's Pub (1972) is usually cited as the surviving country bar. Recordings like Buckalew's are among the few traces in the folk archives.

#### The revival: Gate of Horn, WFMT, the Old Town School (1947-1967)

The revival began with a revue. In 1947 Win Stracke, Studs Terkel, Big Bill Broonzy and Larry Lane started *I Come for to Sing*, an early college-circuit folk show. Then came radio: WFMT, founded in 1951, launched **The Midnight Special** in 1953 with Mike Nichols as host. Then came the club. Albert Grossman and Les Brown opened the [Gate of Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_of_Horn) in 1956, in the 100-seat basement of the Rice Hotel at Chicago and Dearborn. It was probably the first nightclub in the country devoted to folk music. Odetta and Bob Gibson made their names there, Roger McGuinn wrote a song about it, and it ran weekly amateur hootenannies. In 1958 the *Chicago Daily News* reported that "most of the top performers from all over the country now either live in Chicago or can be found here a good deal of the time."

The [Old Town School of Folk Music](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/2/) grew out of a living room. Odetta took Oak Park host Dawn Greening to hear Frank Hamilton at the Gate of Horn. Greening set up a $2-a-lesson group guitar class at her house, and Win Stracke enrolled. The school opened on Friday, November 29, 1957 (some sources say December 1) in the old Immigrant Bank building at 333 W. North Ave.:

- George Armstrong's bagpipes opened the evening.
- Frank Hamilton watched Big Bill Broonzy play, wrote his right-hand pattern in tablature on a blackboard, and taught it to the room.
- Gertrude Soltker ran the books.

Hamilton brought Bess Lomax Hawes's group-teaching method from Los Angeles. He also invented the **"Second Half"**, the all-skill-levels sing after class that the school still holds. The school had 160 students by February 1958 and 16 teachers by 1963. Hamilton left in 1962 to replace Erik Darling in the Weavers.

#### The singer-songwriter era (1962-1987)

Earl Pionke opened the Earl of Old Town at 1615 N. Wells in 1962. After the Gate of Horn closed he kept booking folk because he "couldn't imagine Wells Street without folk music" ([OTS history 4](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/4/)). The Earl lasted until 1984 and launched or anchored three performers:

- [Steve Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Goodman), who wrote "City of New Orleans" about the Illinois Central train;
- [John Prine](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/4/), who started at the short-lived Fifth Peg in 1969;
- [Bonnie Koloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_Koloc).

[Fred Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Holstein), a baritone balladeer with "instant recall" of hundreds of songs, was resident from 1966. With his brother Ed he co-owned Somebody Else's Troubles (opened 1974 at 2470 N. Lincoln with Pionke, Goodman, Bill Redhead and Duke Nathaus; [Hoekstra](https://davehoekstra.com/2014/06/04/earl-of-old-town-estate-sale-3/)) and then Holstein's, which closed in 1987.

Other pieces of the scene:

- In Evanston the collective-run [Amazingrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazingrace_Coffeehouse) (1971-1980) topped the *Reader*'s club poll from 1973 to 1975.
- The Old Town School moved to 909 W. Armitage in 1968. It threw legendary all-night parties (a dollar to get in) and published the quarterly *Come for to Sing* from 1975.
- Its Thursday-night crowd moved from bar to bar: the Azteca, then the Saddle Club ("guys were falling down"), the Fifth Peg, and back to the Saddle Club until it closed in 1975.
- UChicago Folklore Society alumnus Bruce Kaplan founded [Flying Fish Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Fish_Records) in 1974.

By 1981 the Old Town School was down to about 300 students a week (from more than 1,000) and nearly $60,000 in debt. Jim Hirsch and program director Michael Miles rebuilt it on grants, a "world music" mandate and new fiddle, voice and percussion classes.

#### The University of Chicago Folk Festival (1961-present)

The [UChicago Folklore Society](http://www.uofcfolk.org/history.shtml) formed in the early 1950s. It staged its first festival in 1961, with the Stanley Brothers, the New Lost City Ramblers, Elizabeth Cotten, Roscoe Holcomb and Willie Dixon. It has run every winter since. Early years brought Bill Monroe, Frank Proffitt, Hobart Smith, the Staple Singers, Nathan Abshire, Dewey Balfa, Doc Watson, Clarence Ashley, Muddy Waters and Little Walter to Mandel Hall.

WFMT taped 35 years of it, 305 hours in all. Those tapes are now at the [Chicago Public Library](https://www.chipublib.org/archival_post/university-of-chicago-folk-festival-collection/), with 125 hours digitized. The [66th festival](http://www.uofcfolk.org/schedule.shtml) ran February 20-21, 2026, with High Fidelity, Gap Civil, Lissa Schneckenburger & Garrett Cameron, the Luke Huval Band, Jontavious Willis, Ethan Leinwand and Mariachi Flor de Primavera. Saturday workshops and dances at Ida Noyes Hall were free, and the concerts were streamed.

#### The Irish revival (1950s-2000s)

Flute player Kevin Henry came from Mayo in the mid-1950s, when "Irish music wasn't very popular". He and a committee set up what became the **Irish Musicians Association of America** ([Digital Chicago](https://digitalchicagohistory.org/exhibits/show/irish-music-in-chicago/the-music/trad-in-the-windy-city); 1956 per secondary sources). The South Side club Hanley's House of Happiness ran Sunday-night Irish radio shows, where a young Liz Carroll heard Joe Cooley, Eleanor Neary and the Murphy Brothers ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/liz-carroll)).

[Liz Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Carroll) learned from Johnny McGreevy, Joe Shannon, Seamus Cooley and Eleanor Neary. In 1975, at 18, she won the senior All-Ireland fiddle title, only the second American to do so, and the senior duet with accordionist Jimmy Keane.

Comhaltas came to North America in 1972. Chicago's branches belong to its Mid-West region, and the city hosted the Midwest Fleadh in 1977 and again in 2025. Other milestones:

- **Irish American Heritage Center:** formed 1976 out of the 1939 Shamrock American Club. It bought the derelict Mayfair School in 1985, outbidding rivals by $2,000 on Abbey Pub owner Tom Looney's suggestion, and opened in 1986 ([Block Club](https://blockclubchicago.org/2025/03/12/irish-american-heritage-center-celebrates-40-years-on-northwest-side/)).
- **Gaelic Park** (Oak Forest): land bought 1983, opened 1985. It now covers 53 acres and calls itself the largest Irish cultural center in North America ([Gaelic Park](https://chicagogaelicpark.com/about-us/)).
- **Irish Music School of Chicago:** founded 2003 by fiddler Seán Cleland ([IMSC](https://irishmusicschool.org/history)).
- **Abbey Pub** (opened 1973): the Northwest Side's Irish music hall, closed after a 2015 fire.

#### The old-time revival (1978-present)

Lynn "Chirps" Smith moved to Chicago in 1978. He had learned from downstate Illinois old-timers Harvey "Pappy" Taylor and Noah Beavers, and he became a regular at Chicago Barn Dance Company dances ([FRC](https://fieldrecorder.org/product/chirps-smith/)). From about 1984 to 2002 the [Volo Bogtrotters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volo_Bogtrotters), named for Volo Bog in Lake County, played 1920s string-band repertoire and Midwestern square-dance tunes with a twin-fiddle drive. Members included Smith, Fred Campeau, Steve Rosen, Tony Scarimbolo, Jim Nelson and, from 1993, Paul Tyler. Six members reunited in 2011. Tyler (DrDosido) went on to:

- serve as music supervisor and lead essayist for *The Hayloft Gang* film and University of Illinois Press book on the Barn Dance;
- teach at the Old Town School;
- record fiddle workshops in pubs and at Seman Violins, Skokie (2011-2019) for his [online archive](https://www.drdosido.net/drd30/ar-fiddlers-cgo.html).

#### The modern era, COVID and the rebound (1998-2026)

- **1998:** The Old Town School opened in the 1931 Hild Library in Lincoln Square, with a 425-seat hall where no seat is more than 45 feet from the stage. It launched the Folk & Roots Festival in Welles Park, which ran for about 13 years before the Square Roots street festival replaced it ([OTS history 6](https://www.oldtownschool.org/history/6/)).
- **1997-2010:** City-run [Celtic Fest Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Fest_Chicago) began as a two-day Grant Park event and ended as a one-day May event in Millennium Park.
- **2018:** The board announced the sale of the Armitage building. Protests followed, and filings show the school was still running "three facilities in Lincoln Square and Lincoln Park" in 2025.
- **2019-2022:** Teachers voted to join the Illinois Federation of Teachers in 2019. Jeff Tweedy backed their 2021 push for board seats, and the first contract came in June 2022 ([Block Club](https://blockclubchicago.org/2022/06/03/old-town-school-of-folk-music-teachers-reach-deal-for-1st-union-contract/)).
- **2020:** COVID moved classes online and cancelled the Blues Festival for the first time since 1984.
- **2025:** The school reported 24,542 students, more than 700 classes a week, 60+ ensembles, 200+ ticketed shows (63,235 tickets) and 45,000 people at Square Roots ([CauseIQ/990](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/old-town-school-of-folk-music,362373635/)).
- **2026:** The 66th UChicago festival, the 40th Highland Games and the 50th Fox Valley festival all went ahead.

**Gone:** Celtic Fest Chicago (1997-2010); the OTS Folk & Roots Festival (1998-c.2010); the OTS Festival of Latin Music (1985-c.2000), which drew audiences about half Hispanic and helped seed the long-running La Peña series.

**Camps:** No week-long residential fiddle camp for adults could be verified inside the 13-county MSA. Week-long camps mean travel to Wisconsin, Michigan or further.

### Fiddle contests

Chicago has a famous fiddle-contest moment but no current contest culture:

- **WLS on-air contest, 1924.** Won by George Adamson of Kenosha, with Frank Hart (Aurora) and William McCormick (Marseilles) placing ([DrDosido](http://drdosido.net/drdsblog/2014/04/the-national-barn-dance/)).
- **Midwest Fleadh Cheoil.** The one recurring competition with Chicago ties: the 1977 edition at Bogan High School is in the Library of Congress, and Chicago hosted again in 2025.
- **Old-time or Texas-style contests.** None was found inside the MSA for 2024-2026.

### The fiddle angle

#### Tunes named for Chicago and its people

Chicago's tune footprint is overwhelmingly Irish, and most of it is named for O'Neill's circle. From The Session (tunebook counts show how widely each is played):

**Named for O'Neill's circle**

- **"Chief O'Neill's Favourite"** (hornpipe): [1,123 tunebooks](https://thesession.org/tunes/13).
- **"The Chicago Reel"** ([649 tunebooks](https://thesession.org/tunes/840)): first printed under that name in the 1907 *Dance Music of Ireland*, but older; an 1883 Leitrim manuscript is cited via the Traditional Tune Archive, which could not be read directly. Recorded by Stack and Mullaney in 1926 and on *McGreevy and Cooley* around 1974.
- **"Sergeant Early's Dream"** ([430](https://thesession.org/tunes/1651)) and the jig "Sergeant Early's": for James Early.
- **"John McFadden's Favourite"** ([156](https://thesession.org/tunes/1466)).
- **"Doctor O'Neill"** ([393](https://thesession.org/tunes/1304)), plus "Caroline O'Neill's" and "Rogers O'Neill": all Edward Cronin compositions for the Chief's family.
- **"Young Tom Ennis"** ([534](https://thesession.org/tunes/131)).
- **"Patsy Touhey's"** ([277](https://thesession.org/tunes/1087)).
- **"Sergt. Stack's Favorite"**: probably for Irish Music Club member Garrett J. Stack (an inference).

**20th-century Chicago players and compositions**

- **"The Chicago" (jig)**, also called "Eleanor Neary's", and the hornpipe "Eleanor Neary's": named for the Chicago pianist ([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/15282)).
- **"McGreevy's"**: by fiddler Johnny McGreevy.
- **"Gaelic Park"** (jig) and **"Terry 'Cuz' Teahan's"** (polka): from concertina player Terry Teahan ([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/17757)).
- **"Lost in the Loop"**: Liz Carroll's reel named for downtown ([thesession](https://thesession.org/tunes/4791)).

**Outsiders' homages**

- Charlie Lennon's "The Windy City" (70 tunebooks)
- Leslie Harris's "The Boys from Chicago"
- Eoghan O'Brien's "Paddy's in Chicago"
- Carl Hession's "The Chicago Conneely"
- the waltz "Joe Murphy's Return from Chicago"

**Beyond Irish**

- **"Kostner Avenue Waltz"**: named for a Chicago street, heard at a Chicago Spelmanslag jam.
- **"Hell on the Wabash"**: recorded in Chicago in 1926 by Tom Owen's WLS Barn Dance Trio.
- **"Harrison's Folly"**: from Illinois fiddler Garry Harrison's circle, played at Chicago Barn Dance Company dances.
- **Chirps Smith's own tunes**: "Shades of Death Creek", "Lonesome Prairie" and "Week Before Christmas" ([FRC](https://fieldrecorder.org/product/chirps-smith/)).

#### Local fiddle styles

- **Chicago Irish.** Shaped by recordings and immigrant teachers more than by one regional style. Joe Shannon described the "American style" of heavily graced piping he learned from 78s by Touhey and Tom Ennis ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/joe-shannon)). The McGreevy-Cooley-Neary generation passed a dance-hall repertoire to Liz Carroll, whose compositions now circulate worldwide.
- **Old-time.** Leans Midwestern: Illinois and Indiana sources (Pappy Taylor, Lotus Dickey, Byford Tite), 1920s Barn Dance 78s and square-dance tunes, played twin-fiddle by the Volo Bogtrotters lineage.
- **Barn Dance house bands.** Dandurand's fiddles played "Leather Breeches", "Over the Ocean Waves" and "McLeod's Reel" for callers. It is a reel-and-quadrille, Northern repertoire, not a Southern one ([DrDosido](https://www.drdosido.net/drd30/ar-fiddlers-cgo.html)).
- **Immigrant fiddling.** Paul Tyler's archive is a reminder that Chicago's fiddle history is polyglot. It includes 78s of Polish village fiddle bands (Józef Kmiec, Franciszek Dukla, 1926-29), Polish Highlander string bands recorded 1988-2010, tamburitza players, Norwegian Hardanger and Swedish nyckelharpa players, Mexican son huasteco and planeco fiddlers (Juan Rivera, Los Pichardo), and Greek and Cajun fiddlers.

### Archives

- **[Library of Congress, Chicago Ethnic Arts Project](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2016/05/chicago-ethnic-arts-project-goes-online/)** (1977, online): the best single source for Chicago folk practice in the late 1970s.
- **Library of Congress, Lomax 1938 Chicago discs** (AFC 1939/007).
- **[Chicago Public Library](https://www.chipublib.org/archival_post/university-of-chicago-folk-festival-collection/)**, Harold Washington Library Center Music Information Center: the University of Chicago Folk Festival Collection (1961-1995, WFMT tapes).
- **[Hyde Park Historical Society Folk Fest project](https://www.hydeparkhistory.org/folk-fest-project/digitarchives)**: posters, schedules, Maroon clippings and oral histories. UChicago Library has also mounted the exhibit *Putting the Folk Back in Folk Music: Sixty Years of the University of Chicago Folk Festival*.

### Tuesday versus Saturday: leads for a visiting fiddler

Event specifics belong to other threads; these are the leads this research surfaced.

**Weeknights.** The Chicago Barn Dance Company's weekly contra, reported as Mondays at the IAHC's Erin Room, is the old-time musicians' anchor; verify. Seán Cleland's Wednesday session at Mrs. Murphy & Sons Irish Bistro was documented in the Digital Chicago exhibit; verify it still runs. The Old Town School's nightly classes end with the open "Second Half" sing.

**Weekends.** Summer weekends mean festivals: Square Roots in July, Frankfort Bluegrass in July, Gaelic Park in August, Fox Valley on Labor Day with jams and a barn dance. February brings the UChicago Folk Festival's free Saturday workshops. Thanksgiving weekend brings Breaking Up Thanksgiving in Evanston.

**Pilgrimages.** Chief O'Neill's Pub (Avondale), the old Brighton Park session house at 3522 S. Washtenaw, the Lincoln Square Old Town School, and the IAHC museum.

### Surprises

1. A police chief compiled the bible of Irish dance music and hired the players as cops. "Sergeant Early's Dream" is named for one.
2. O'Neill asked the Havana police, through a Chicago officer, to find his runaway piper brother-in-law. The description included "mustache usually dyed".
3. "The Chicago Reel" is not from Chicago; it took the name in O'Neill's 1907 book.
4. The Grand Ole Opry's founder learned his trade announcing Chicago's Barn Dance, which charged admission.
5. The first Barn Dance fiddler was billed as "Timothy Cornrow from Ioway". The house fiddle leader, Tommy Dandurand, was a retired Kankakee streetcar operator.
6. Lomax's 1938 Chicago recordings include a Finnish woman singing with a kantele.
7. The Old Town School began as $2 lessons in an Oak Park living room. On opening night Broonzy's fingerpicking went up on a blackboard.
8. The school now occupies a 1931 library, and its baby classes (Wiggleworms) help pay for its niche ones.
9. Liz Carroll was All-Ireland senior champion at 18 and worked as a nurse.
10. Joe Shannon, an NEA Heritage Fellow and a firefighter, learned "American-style" piping from 78s.
11. The free Frankfort Bluegrass Festival booked a Korean bluegrass band.
12. Chicago Scots (1845) claims to be the oldest nonprofit in Illinois.
13. A 1977 federal folklorist recorded an Alabama-born grandmother fiddling in a Cicero tavern.
14. Ella Jenkins, Chicago's children's-folk pioneer, lived to 100.
