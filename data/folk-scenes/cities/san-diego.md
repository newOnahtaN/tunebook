# San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad: the folk scene (dossier, October 2026)

*Metro: San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad MSA (CBSA 41740), population 3.28 million, which is all of San Diego County. Tijuana and Baja California, Orange, Riverside and Imperial counties are outside the MSA; cross-border things the scene treats as its own are flagged `outside_msa` in the JSON. Research used web search and fetch only, as of October 2026. Structured data: `san-diego.json`. Part 1 is the synthesis. Part 2 has four detailed chapters (trad sessions and dance, song and singing, heritage, history), with inline sources.*

## Scene in a nutshell

San Diego is a folk-dance town more than a session town, and a heritage town more than either. Balboa Park works like a city-run folk campus: international, Scottish, céilí, square, morris and Cajun dancing fill its exposition halls most nights, and the House of Pacific Relations' 38 cultural cottages stage about 55 free lawn programs a year. For a fiddler it is thin. There is one weekly Irish pub session (the Ould Sod, since 2000), a Comhaltas branch only since 2017, a well-organized bluegrass network, contra down to about twice a month, and no verifiable old-time jam. Its history is rich and unusually well taped: Sam Hinton, Lou Curtiss's San Diego State Folk Festival (1967–87) and Folk Arts Rare Records, and the Scottsville Squirrel Barkers. Its living depth lies in Kumeyaay bird singing, school mariachi, danza azteca in Chicano Park, Navy-linked Filipino and Chamorro troupes, and a son jarocho fandango played through the border fence.

## The 10 most interesting things

1. **A jam standard was born in Old Town.** "Waiting for Nancy", an old-time reel in D found in the *Portland Collection* and played at jams nationwide, was written in 1972 by San Diego fretless-banjo player Curt Bouterse. He composed it while waiting in Squibob Square for a friend named Nancy, who never came ([Tunearch](https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Waiting_for_Nancy); [history](https://dulcimerdownloads.com/pages/waiting-for-nancy-history)). Bouterse also co-founded the SDSU folk song society (1961) and led shape-note singing at Folk Arts Rare Records.
2. **Balboa Park is a city-run folk-dance campus.** The International Dance Association of San Diego County (12 clubs, backed by Parks & Recreation) fills the Balboa Park Club, Casa del Prado and War Memorial Building most nights. Central San Diego alone has about 31 modern Western square dance nights a month. Cabrillo International Folk Dancers charges $2.50 a class, the Pride of Erin céilí $2, and there is a free Sunday afternoon folk dance open to anyone ([IDASDC](https://www.idasdc.com/)). Moreton Bay Fig Morris (1984) is named for the park's giant fig tree.
3. **Son jarocho through the border fence.** Fandango Fronterizo (2008) holds a fandango on both sides of the fence at Friendship Park. At the first one, Border Patrol asked them to wrap up, so they played "La Bamba" for an hour, and the agents "couldn't believe it was still the same song." Since 2018 nobody has been allowed to play on the US side. Verses sung from Tijuana now go unanswered from the north ([San Diego Magazine](https://sandiegomagazine.com/everything-sd/arts-culture/fandango-fronterizo-music-festival/); [ACTA](https://actaonline.org/profile/fandango-fronterizo/)).
4. **The world's first mariachi degree.** Southwestern College in Chula Vista began a music degree with a mariachi specialization in 2004. School mariachi spread in the Sweetwater district in the 1990s partly to bring Latino parents to school nights: "if you have the mariachi playing on parent-teacher night, that changes the conversation." The Ed Marsh Mariachi Museum (appraised at about $3.4M) lost its home in June 2026 and now sits in a storage unit ([UT](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/07/12/the-one-of-a-kind-mariachi-museum-in-chula-vista-has-lost-its-home-now-what/)).
5. **A $350 festival that the Library of Congress archived.** The first San Diego State Folk Festival (1967) spent $250 of its $350 budget on Bill Monroe. His bus broke down in El Centro, so the Dillards backed him. Over 20 years the festival brought Tommy Jarrell, Kyle Creed, Benny Thomasson, Kenny Hall, the Balfa Brothers, Lydia Mendoza and Stan Hugill. The Library of Congress holds 420 tapes of the 1967–75 festivals and UCLA holds 400 reels; about 2,000 more tapes were still undigitized in 2011 ([AFC](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2018/07/lou-curtiss-san-diego-folk-promoter-1939-2018/); [OAC](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt8f59s1t5/admin/)).
6. **Rutabagas and Hare Krishnas.** The 1970s Balboa Park Old Time Banjo & Fiddle Contests cost $1 to enter and advertised bribable judges. Prizes were pies, used bowling trophies and bags of rutabagas. One year a tie award went to a gospel bluegrass band and the Hare Krishna chanters "for traditional dogma." Crowds reached several thousand. The contests ended only when the park stopped supplying electricity ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/fiddle-contests/)). The rutabaga prize echoes Berkeley's 1968 convention.
7. **A Navy port with no shanty sing.** San Diego is home to the *Star of India*, the world's oldest active sailing ship, and the Jackstraws (1979). Its Maritime Museum Sea Chantey Festival (1993–2019) produced the line that singer Loren Smith learned "Rolling Down to Old Maui" from Stan Hugo, "the last living chanteyman aboard the tall ships". The festival quietly died with COVID, and no public monthly shanty sing exists ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-sea-chantey-festival-a-lifeline-to-our-nautical-past/)).
8. **The best folk radio comes from a reservation.** No terrestrial San Diego station carries a dedicated folk show. The best is Joel Rafael's *Rockin' the Rez* (449 episodes) on Rez Radio 91.3, which the Pala Band founded after the 2007 wildfires as "the first indigenous-owned radio station in Southern California" ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/good-radio-is-out-there-you-just-have-to-know-where-to-look/)).
9. **Comhaltas arrived at a christening.** For decades the Irish scene had pubs and dance schools but no music instruction. In 2017 Barry Cogan of Carrigaline, Co. Cork, visited for his grandson's christening, saw the local players, and talked them into founding a branch. The branch now runs the Balboa Park House of Ireland Sunday sessions. Its founding board member notes, "I have no Irish heritage. I am Italian and Swiss German" ([Comhaltas SD](https://www.sandiegocomhaltas.org/comhaltas-in-san-diego-california/)).
10. **Kumeyaay bird songs, from "a thread" to civic protocol.** Singers say the songs "were almost lost, they were hanging from a thread" ([Viejas](https://viejasbandofkumeyaay.org/viejas-community/kumeyaay-history/bird-songs-dancing-and-songs/)). Now about a dozen bird-singing groups performed at the Safari Park on Indigenous Peoples' Day 2025. Bird singers opened SDSU's convocation and Chula Vista's 2026 proclamation of Kumeyaay People's Day, and powwows give them their own slot.

Runners-up:
- Tom Waits was the doorman at the Heritage coffeehouse in Mission Beach, now a surf bar.
- Bluesman Sam Chatmon of the Mississippi Sheiks wintered in San Diego for about 15 years, holding court from an armchair in Folk Arts Rare Records.
- The Scottsville Squirrel Barkers, the house band of a shop with "a partial dirt floor," launched Chris Hillman (Byrds) and Bernie Leadon (Eagles).
- Taylor Guitars (1974) and Deering Banjos (1975) were both founded in East County.
- An open mic in Santee died when SESAC demanded licensing fees.
- The Oaxacan Guelaguetza was cancelled in 2025 and postponed again in 2026 because of immigration enforcement.

## Character: what makes San Diego distinct

**Dance first, sessions second.** Most participatory folk activity here is dancing, and most of the dancing happens in a handful of city-owned buildings in Balboa Park, built for the 1915 and 1935 expositions. That public infrastructure, plus Parks & Rec support for IDASDC, keeps prices very low ($2–10). It may explain why square dancing, international folk dance and Scottish country dance held up while contra, which runs in rented halls (Liberty Station, and before that Trinity Methodist in North Park), shrank. Fees are coming, though: Balboa Park began charging for parking in 2026. The Folk Dance Center in La Mesa is being evicted for a Bass Pro Shop at the end of 2026, even as it organizes the 2027 Folk Dance Federation Statewide in Balboa Park.

**The border, the Navy and the reservations shape the heritage scene.**
- **Mexican and Chicano traditions** are the largest. Mariachi has been taught in schools since 1996. The 56th Chicano Park Day (2026) opened with danza azteca from several groups, and ballet folklórico companies are active. Son jarocho is anchored on the Tijuana side.
- **Navy migration** explains why Filipino troupes are among the county's oldest and busiest groups: PASACAT (1970) and Samahan (1974). The same goes for the Chamorro dance company Guma Imåhen Taotao Tåno', which spent years rehearsing in a police parking lot. The Pacific Islander Festival (1995) calls itself the largest in the US.
- **Kumeyaay, Luiseño and Cupeño communities** (Sycuan, Viejas, Barona, Pala, San Pasqual, Campo and others) keep bird songs, peon games and four long-running powwows: Barona's 54th, SDSU's 53rd, Balboa Park's 38th and Sycuan's ~35th.
- **Point Loma's Azorean fishing families** have held the Festa do Espírito Santo since 1910 (its 116th was in 2026).

**A revival that was taped, then mostly left behind.** San Diego's Anglo-American folk history is unusually well documented. Sam Hinton's 1947 Library of Congress session survives, and so do Lou Curtiss's reel-to-reel archive of the 1962–87 concerts and festivals and the *San Diego Troubadour*'s 25-year back catalog. But most living institutions of that lineage are small and aging:
- San Diego Folk Heritage runs about 20 concerts a year at around 65 people each, plus contra and storytelling.
- The 1957 Folk Song Society survives as one monthly library song circle.
- Folk Arts Rare Records survives under a new owner.

The younger energy is in songwriter formats. Writers Round, which meets Mondays at the Ould Sod, mixes Nashville-style rounds with an open mic. Songwriter Sanctuary is a monthly ASL-interpreted concert in a church that has quietly been San Diego's most important folk building for decades.

**Bluegrass is the fiddler's best bet.** Two clubs run the scene. The San Diego Bluegrass Society (1973; "$2 for a lifetime membership") and the North County Bluegrass & Folk Club together hold about 16 jam nights a month, mostly in pizza parlors and breweries, and co-founded Summergrass (2003) at the Antique Gas & Steam Engine Museum. Dwight Worden counts "more than 20 active bluegrass bands" and credits Appalachian-born military retirees. The lineage runs from the Squirrel Barkers through Dennis Caplinger (died 2021) to Nickel Creek, whose members played Vista pizza parlors as "Seldom Clean."

**Geography.** The folk-song world sits on Adams Avenue (Normal Heights and Kensington): the Ould Sod, Lestat's, Normal Heights United, Adams Avenue Unplugged, and Folk Arts nearby on University. Dance clusters in Balboa Park and La Mesa. Bluegrass is spread across North County (Escondido, Encinitas, Vista, Valley Center) and Miramar. Heritage life is centered in the South Bay (Chula Vista, National City, Barrio Logan), on the reservations of East and North County, in City Heights, and in El Cajon for the Chaldean community. Downtown and the beach towns barely figure.

**Who shows up.** No survey data was found. The Hinton/Curtiss-lineage institutions rely on older volunteers. The heritage troupes are intergenerational: Samahan teaches students "aged 5 to over 80." The songwriter rooms are younger and focused on community care and access.

## Curiosities for phase 2

1. Do metros with city-owned dance halls (San Diego's Balboa Park, run with Parks & Rec) keep more weekly folk dancing than metros that depend on churches and private venues?
2. San Diego's contra went from weekly (2016–21) to about twice a month, while square dancing and international folk dance held up. Is contra the most COVID-fragile folk dance nationally?
3. Why do some cities with deep old-time history (Bouterse, the festival tapes) have no regular old-time jam? What makes old-time jams take root?
4. Which metros' Irish scenes rest on a Comhaltas branch, and when did each arrive? San Diego's dates from 2017, SF's from the early 1970s.
5. How do border metros (San Diego–Tijuana, El Paso, Laredo) practice cross-border folk traditions, and how has 2025–26 immigration enforcement affected heritage festivals?
6. Does military migration seed folk traditions elsewhere (Norfolk, San Antonio, Tampa) the way Navy-linked Filipino and Chamorro troupes and Appalachian-born retirees did here?
7. Where did mid-century "folk song society" clubs survive, and what replaced them?
8. San Diego has one NEA Heritage Fellow; the Bay Area has about 15. How much is nominating infrastructure?
9. Rutabaga prizes appear in both Berkeley (1968) and Balboa Park (1970s) mock contests. Was this a shared West Coast joke, and who carried it?
10. Did the WPA and Lomax-era federal folk documentation systematically skip some big metros (San Diego has zero items in the WPA California collection)? Which ones, and with what consequences for later revivals?

Two things stand out. San Diego is a folk-dance town far more than a session town, and most of that dancing happens in a handful of city-owned buildings in Balboa Park. And for the Irish, Scottish and old-time fiddler, it is thin: one weekly Irish pub session, no Scottish or Cape Breton session, no verifiable old-time jam, and a contra dance that has shrunk from weekly to about twice a month.

### Irish sessions: one pub night, plus a Comhaltas engine in Balboa Park

The Session lists exactly three San Diego sessions ([nearby search](https://thesession.org/sessions/nearby?latlon=32.75,-117.15&radius=60&format=json)), and all three check out:

- **The Ould Sod, Tuesdays 7-10 PM** (Adams Ave, Normal Heights). San Diego's weekly pub session since 2000, hosted by Michael Eskin. It passed its 600th night in 2012, and Eskin posted "Still happening every Tuesday night at 7:00 PM!" in July 2025 ([The Session](https://thesession.org/sessions/759)). The original listing warns "Seating only for 6-8 players max so plan on getting there early!" The session alternates between the front room (2nd and 4th Tuesdays) and the back room. It's a bar, so 21+, and Eskin's page has unusually specific parking advice: don't use the liquor store lot, they tow ([michaeleskin.com](https://michaeleskin.com/theouldsod.html)). The Center for World Music describes "between five and ten musicians" in an alcove by the door or the beer garden, with uilleann pipes and concertina among the fiddles and flutes ([CWM](https://centerforworldmusic.org/2023/11/san-diego-irish-music-sessions/)). Visitors from Toronto, Brighton and Seattle have left thanks on The Session.
- **House of Ireland, Sunday mornings** (Balboa Park cottage). A Comhaltas slow, tune-learning session (now 9:00-10:30, with 9:00-9:30 the slowest), then an all-levels session 10:30-noon ([The Session](https://thesession.org/sessions/9123), updated Sept 2026). The slow session is led by Judy Carlstrom from a published class tunebook of about 120 common session tunes, with tablature for mandolin, banjo and GDAD bouzouki, compiled by Eskin in 2026.
- **Hooleys Public House, La Mesa, 4th Thursdays 7-9 PM**. A Comhaltas session started in 2024, with a 6 PM "learn a tune by ear with Michael Eskin" slot. It skips November and December ([The Session](https://thesession.org/sessions/8045)). I found no 2026 posting, so confidence is medium.

**Comhaltas came late.** The branch history by founding board member Judy Carlstrom is the best single document on the scene ([Comhaltas SD](https://www.sandiegocomhaltas.org/comhaltas-in-san-diego-california/)). The House of Ireland dates to the 1935 Exposition, and "Pub sessions and dance schools have thrived here for over thirty years," but "until 2017 the only missing element... was instruction in music." That year Barry Cogan of Carrigaline, Co. Cork, visited for the christening of his grandson. Seeing local players at the party, he talked them into founding a branch. ("Apparently, everywhere that Mairtín lived... his father had tried to get him to open a Comhaltas branch. This time, he caught me in his snare.") Carlstrom adds: "The irony is that I have no Irish heritage. I am Italian and Swiss German." The branch, Craobh Barry Cogan, now teaches fiddle, whistle, concertina, bodhrán, banjo, mandolin, guitar, singing, storytelling and sean-nós dance. It runs a Saturday Whistle Club and an annual céilí ([events](https://www.sandiegocomhaltas.org/events/)). A 2026 visitor from Ireland described a La Jolla house concert by Sligo-style fiddler Brian Conway that turned into a tight branch session around Máirtín de Cógáin ([mickgb.com](https://mickgb.com/en/comhaltas-barry-cogan/)).

**Irish dance.** The Pride of Erin Céilí Dancers, "one of the oldest Irish dance organizations in San Diego," hold an adult céilí and set dance class every Wednesday, 7-9 PM, in the Casa del Prado. It costs $2, or $5 a family, and is taught by Paul Wermuth TMRF ([Pride of Erin](https://www.pride-of-erin.com/ceili.html), [IDASDC](https://www.idasdc.com/ethnic-clubs)). The [Irish in San Diego pub list](https://irishinsandiego.com/pubs/) names The Field (Gaslamp), the Blarney Stone ("San Diego's original Irish Pub"), O'Sullivan's (Escondido), Hennessey's and others. Only the Ould Sod advertises trad sessions. I found no current Irish session in North County.

### Scottish and Cape Breton: dancers yes, a fiddle session no

The **RSCDS San Diego Branch** ("Dance Scottish San Diego") is healthy. It held its 50th-anniversary ball in spring 2025, which puts its founding around 1975 ([events](https://www.dancescottishsd.org/events/)). It runs three weekly classes: a Monday beginner series in the Casa del Prado (12 weeks for $70, first class free, taught by Jeanne Moody), a Tuesday social class at a Poway studio, and a Friday intermediate class ([classes](https://www.dancescottishsd.org/classes/), [beginner](https://www.dancescottishsd.org/classes/beginner/)). The San Diego Scottish Highland Games were incorporated in October 1974 and have had ten chieftains and eight sites ([SDHG](https://sdhighlandgames.org/)). But I found no Scottish or Cape Breton fiddle session or club in the county. The Scottish Fiddlers of California umbrella covers San Francisco and Los Angeles only ([SFC](http://scottishfiddlersofcalifornia.org/)). The one San Diego-titled tune I found is Scottish: **"Mr and Mrs McLaughlin of San Diego,"** a G-major reel by composer Muriel Johnstone ([abc](https://trillian.mit.edu/~jc/music/abc/by/Muriel_Johnstone/reel/MrAndMrsMcLaughlinOfSanDiego.abc)). Who the McLaughlins were isn't documented; Scottish country dancers are a plausible guess.

### Old-time: great history, no verifiable jam

This is the biggest gap for a visiting old-time fiddler. FolkWorks still lists a **San Diego Old Time Fiddlers Jam** (1st and 3rd Thursdays at a Tierrasanta Round Table Pizza) and a **Walt Richards SLO Jam** (3rd Fridays, San Carlos Rec Center) ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/old-time-jams/)). The freshest evidence I could find is a 2013 Banjo Hangout post: the location "has changed from week to week," that night at New Expression Music in North Park ([Banjo Hangout](https://www.banjohangout.org/archive/252424)). Both are logged as low confidence and left out of the counts. The California State Old Time Fiddlers Association has no San Diego district; the nearest is District 4's first-Sunday jam in Fullerton ([CSOTFA](https://www.csotfa.org/districts.htm)).

Yet the history is rich. Lou Curtiss's San Diego (State) Folk Festival, from 1967, brought **Tommy Jarrell, Kyle Creed, Benny Thomasson, Kenny Hall, Earl Collins, Byron Berline, Louis Boudreault, Kirk McGee and the Balfa Brothers** to town. The Library of Congress holds 420 of its tapes covering 1967-75 ([AFC blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2018/07/lou-curtiss-san-diego-folk-promoter-1939-2018/)). Curtiss's Folk Arts Rare Records (opened July 31, 1967) put on in-store concerts in the 1970s by locals including "**Fiddlin' Red Simpson and the Old Scratch Band**" ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-lou-curtiss-era/)). The **Julian Banjo & Fiddle Contest** started in 1973 and "saw crowds in the thousands" in the 1970s. Promoters came and went, and SDBS and NCBFC walked away in 2002 to start Summergrass ([SDBS history](https://www.sandiegobluegrass.org/PDFs/History-of-SDBS%20lr.pdf)). By about 2012 it had become a non-competitive "Julian Music Festival" ([page](https://julianbluegrassfestival.weebly.com/julian-music-festival.html)).

Two live old-time threads remain. Every May the **Julian Music Camp**, run by the California Bluegrass Association since 2011 and until now called Julian Family Fiddle Camp, teaches old-time and bluegrass fiddle and clawhammer banjo for ages "8 to 88." It moves to Camp Stevens on May 19-23, 2027 ([CBA](https://californiabluegrass.org/cbaevent/julian-music-camp/)). And SD-based old-time fiddler and banjo player **Clinton Davis**, a camp teacher who played the CBA Father's Day Festival main stage in 2025, runs a ticketed **North Park Square Dance** in Verbatim Books. The third one, on Nov 17, 2024, had Jeremy Horton calling ([Clinton Davis](https://clintonrossdavis.com/3rd-ever-north-park-square-dance/)). The bookstore now calls it "renowned" ([Verbatim](https://verbatimbooks.com/events/)). It's the closest thing San Diego has to a young old-time dance scene.

### Bluegrass: the best-organized jam network

Bluegrass is where the sessions are: about 16 jam-nights a month, run by two clubs that share members and newsletters.

**San Diego Bluegrass Society (SDBS).** Dwight Worden's 2014 history is a gem ([PDF](https://www.sandiegobluegrass.org/PDFs/History-of-SDBS%20lr.pdf)). The club began in 1973 "at the Straw Hat Pizza Hut on Fletcher Parkway" in La Mesa. Rick Kirby of Pacificly Bluegrass was first president, and dues were "$2 for a lifetime membership." For years, "If you weren't a member, you'd never know where the meetings were," since the only publicity was Wayne Rice's *Bluegrass Special* on KSON. Membership held at 300-350. The club met at Organ Power Pizza, Mrs. T's Pizza and the College Grove food court; "six bands played each meeting, 30-minute sets each". It ran Tuesday nights at Fuddruckers in Grossmont and Chula Vista; the Chula Vista night left "until noise from car clubs became too much". It incorporated as SDBS in 1998. Today's schedule ([SDBS](https://www.sandiegobluegrass.org/), [events](https://www.sandiegobluegrass.org/events.html)):
- 2nd Monday open mic and jam, and 4th Sunday jam with pickup bands, both at **Duck Foot Brewing, Miramar**
- An instructed **Slow Jam**, 4th Tuesdays at Mission Valley Library, led by Mary Jane Cupp with Janet Beazley. NCBFC's list says 3rd Mondays instead; I used SDBS's own page.
- **Emma's Gut Bucket Band**, Friday late mornings at Mission Bay RV Resort. It's an outreach band named for founder Emma Radcliffe that plays schools, senior centers and homeless centers.
- Free library concerts, Bluegrass Day at the Del Mar Fair, an instrument-lending library. Dues are $25 per family.

**North County Bluegrass & Folk Club (NCBFC)** ([about](https://northcountybluegrass.org/about-us/), [jams](https://northcountybluegrass.org/camping/)) runs First Tuesday Music Nights at Round Table Pizza in Escondido, with a patio jam from 6 PM, an opener at 7 and the featured band at 8, all free. It also runs a 3rd Wednesday jam there with an hour of instrument instruction first, a 3rd Monday jam at Duck Foot Leucadia, and two campouts a year (Potrero, Sept 24-27, 2026). It lists the 4th Wednesday jam at All Things Music in Valley Center and the weekly **Thursday Night Pickers** at Round Table Pizza, Encinitas. There's also a weekly Sunday **Bluegrass Brunch** with local bands at TERI's café in San Marcos, a vocational nonprofit for adults with special needs ([NCBFC](https://northcountybluegrass.org/teri-inc-bluegrass-brunch/)).

**Summergrass** (Vista, third weekend of August; Aug 14-16, 2026, "Celebrating 23 years") was founded in 2003 by SDBS and NCBFC as 50/50 owners at the **Antique Gas & Steam Engine Museum**. NCBFC officer Corky Shelton suggested the site. It has a Kids Music Academy (since 2004), an adult camp and an "Easy Jam for Beginners." The museum took over sponsorship in 2022 ([Summergrass](https://summergrass.net/), [NCBFC](https://northcountybluegrass.org/about-us/)). The 2026 festival sold a two-club membership for $40. "The Compound" at the north end of the RV lot is where the club jams happen ([SDBS](https://www.sandiegobluegrass.org/)).

**People and lineage.** The **Scottsville Squirrel Barkers** were the house band of the **Blue Guitar**, a Midway Drive shop "with a partial dirt floor." Its owners took the merchandise home at night for lack of security ([bcbrown.net paper](http://bcbrown.net/bluegrass/chronicles/articles/ICMC2007/SSB_paper.pdf)). The band launched Chris Hillman (Byrds), Bernie Leadon (Eagles) and Kenny Wertz (Flying Burrito Brothers, Country Gazette). *Sources disagree: Wikipedia says the band formed in 1961 with Wertz on banjo, while Worden in the San Diego Troubadour says 1962 with Wertz on guitar and Gary Carr on banjo* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottsville_Squirrel_Barkers), [Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/a-little-bluegrass-history/)). Wayne Rice's band Brush Arbor won the 1974 ACM Vocal Group award. Fiddler and banjo "monster" **Dennis Caplinger** of Vista joined Bluegrass Etc. at 13, studied violin at UCSD, recorded with Clapton and Vince Gill, and mentored Nickel Creek. He died Aug 14, 2021 ([Bluegrass Today](https://bluegrasstoday.com/dennis-caplinger-passes/)). **Sara and Sean Watkins** of Vista and Chris Thile played SD pizza parlors as "Seldom Clean" before forming **Nickel Creek** in 1989 ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/a-little-bluegrass-history/)). Wayne Rice's *Bluegrass Special* now runs Sundays 4-7 PM on Alpine Mountain Country 107.9 after decades on KSON ([SDBS](https://www.sandiegobluegrass.org/events.html)).

### Contra: weekly in 2016, about twice a month in 2026

**San Diego Folk Heritage (SDFH)** runs contra. Its main dance is the **First Saturday Contra at the Dorothea Laub Dance Place, Liberty Station** (lesson at 7, dance 7:30-10). It costs **$15-25 sliding scale**, with gender-neutral Robins and Larks ([SDFH](https://sdfolkheritage.org/contradance/), [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/events/ongoing/contra-dance-at-liberty-station)). Bands in fall 2026 included Too Many Notes (Parks Parks on fiddle), Pete Polansky & Barbara Magone, Dan Sankey & Joe Payne, and the Cactus Lickers. Callers were Martha Wild, Diana Livada and Ellie Konitshek. The Marley floor brings a long shoe policy, and barefoot dancing is encouraged ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/san-diego-folk-heritage-contra-dance/events/315898720/)). A second dance runs on "some Wednesdays" at the Balboa Park Club, hosted by the International Folk Dance Club (e.g. Nov 18, 2026, with Audrey Jaber & friends). There's a New Year's Eve dance and potluck in Spring Valley, and an open "San Diego Community Band" for musicians.

**Decline.** In October 2016 the San Diego Contra site said "we... come together each week to contra dance," on Friday nights at **Trinity United Methodist Church, 3030 Thorn St., North Park** ([archive](http://web.archive.org/web/20161006091556id_/http://sandiegocontra.org/)). The Troubadour that year: "Almost every weekend, there is a contra dance in North Park" ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/san-diego-folk-heritage-still-folkey-after-all-these-years/)). The site still said "each week" in February 2021 ([archive](http://web.archive.org/web/20210227082114id_/http://www.sandiegocontra.org/)). Now there are about two a month, and none in North County. Even in 2016 SDFH warned that "our dance and concert income has begun to fall behind our expenses" as venue rents rose. The **Labor Day Dance Away** contra weekend at Camp Cedar Glen near Julian, which appeared in SD Contra's promo video, is reportedly postponed indefinitely over venue costs ([Federation South](https://socalfolkdance.org/camps/labor_day_dance_away.htm); hiatus status from search summaries only).

*Source disagreement on SDFH's founding:* the Troubadour says nonprofit "since 1987" and "30 years old in 2017." A filing aggregator lists incorporation on July 2, 1979 ([bizprofile](https://www.bizprofile.net/ca/san-diego/san-diego-folk-heritage)). In 2015 SDFH ran 20 concerts averaging about 65 people. It also runs the free **Sam Hinton Folk Heritage Festival** (Old Poway Park, June 6, 2026) ([KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/events/2026/06/06/sam-hinton-folk-heritage-festival)) and the Train Song Festival (Nov 14, 2026).

### English country dance

**San Diego English Country Dancers** was founded in October 2000 by Ellen Riley, a Philadelphia transplant who, finding few outlets, "practiced ECD figures by spreading towels on the floors and using them as surrogate partners" ([SDECD about](https://www.sdecd.org/about.html)). It has "over 80 regular members," drawing dancers from Carlsbad to Chula Vista. Dances are 1st, 3rd and 4th Sundays, 4-6:30 PM, at the Rancho Bernardo Swim & Tennis Club. The norm is a local caller with **recorded music for $10**; live music or a guest caller costs $15 ([SDECD](https://www.sdecd.org/)). Since 2008 it has run the **May Faire Ball** (2026: Gene Murrow calling, with Audrey Jaber, Chip Prince and Laura Osborn, $55), and a New Year's Day dance (2027: Raven and Goose, $45). The International Folk Dance Club's Wednesday nights in Balboa Park also rotate through English country and contra, at $5+ pay-what-you-can ([IDASDC](https://www.idasdc.com/intl-folk-clubs)).

### Balboa Park: a city-run folk-dance campus

The **International Dance Association of San Diego County** (IDASDC) is a nonprofit backed by San Diego Parks & Recreation. Its 12 clubs fill Balboa Park's halls ([IDASDC](https://www.idasdc.com/)):
- **Balboa Park Club**: San Diego Folk Dancers on Mondays (kolo and line dances, intermediate-advanced, teacher Kin Ho). **Cabrillo International Folk Dancers** on Tuesdays ($2.50 a class, $10 a year; teachers Georgina Sham and Chin Yu). International Folk Dance Club on Wednesdays. A **free Sunday dance, 12:30-4:00**, open to dancers and spectators ([Cabrillo](https://sites.google.com/site/cabrillodancers/other-folk-dance-class)). The Harvest Festival (Sept) and the Round Dance Festival (Nov 6-8, 2026) are also here.
- **Casa del Prado**: RSCDS classes and the Pride of Erin céilí.
- **War Memorial Building and Recital Hall**: modern Western square dance most nights of the week ([SDSDA clubs](https://www.sdsda.org/clubs.asp)). **Moreton Bay Fig Morris** (founded 1984, named for the park's famous fig) practices Tuesdays, 7:30-9; Adderbury is in the repertoire ([MBFM](https://sites.google.com/moretonbayfig.org/moreton-bay-fig-morris/home)). The **Bon Temps Social Club** (first Cajun dance Valentine's Day 1991; runs Gator By The Bay) holds live-band Cajun/zydeco dances "often on 2nd Saturdays" for $10-15 ([IDASDC](https://www.idasdc.com/american-clubs), [Bon Temps](https://www.icajunzydeco.com/)).

In 2026 the park started charging for parking. Morris practice notes that street parking after 6 PM is still free.

**Square dance count.** The SDSDA calendar is live: the Circulators' Halloween party night was listed for Oct 30, 2026. Its November 2026 listings show seven weekly club dances: Lighthouse Squares (Mon), Finest City Squares (Wed), Single Spinners (Thu), the Callers Association's Advanced night (Thu), Circulators (Fri), and Ruffles 'n Beaus and Sundown Squares in La Mesa (Sun). The Callers Association adds a 4th-Saturday dance in Lemon Grove ([calendar](https://www.sdsda.org/get_dates.asp)). That is about **31 square dance nights a month in central San Diego alone**. The Palomar (North County) and Back Country (East County) associations aren't counted ([about](https://www.sdsda.org/aboutSDSDA.html)). A dance usually costs about $5.

**Folk Dance Center, La Mesa.** This dedicated international folk dance group meets in the Oasis Wellness Center at Grossmont Center. It holds weekly Saturday dances ($10), some with the Balkan band **Tzidia** or the Greek band **Parea**, and a Wednesday morning class. It's being displaced by a coming Bass Pro Shop: "we will be out of the Wellness Center at the end of the year," with a goodbye party set for Dec 19, 2026. It is also organizing the **2027 Folk Dance Federation Statewide** in Balboa Park (May 14-16) ([FDC newsletter](http://folkdancecenter.org/newsletter.html)).

**Israeli dance** is surprisingly plentiful. Galit's Thursday Rikudei Am night in central San Diego costs $15-30 suggested and has a young-adults night on 2nd Thursdays ([site](https://sites.google.com/view/galits-sd-israeli-dancing/home)). Barbara Birenbaum's "Israeli Folk Dance Fun" runs about four weekly-equivalent sessions in Poway, La Jolla and Carlsbad, plus chair dancing on Zoom ([site](https://israelifolkdancefun.com/)). I found **no Scandinavian dance** group.

### Shops, makers and archives

- **Taylor Guitars**, El Cajon: founded 1974 by Bob Taylor and Kurt Listug, about 750 employees ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Guitars)).
- **Deering Banjo Company**, Spring Valley: founded 1975 by Greg and Janet Deering, now run by daughter Jamie. It also makes Vega and Goodtime banjos and is described as North America's largest banjo maker ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deering_Banjo_Company)).
- **The Blue Guitar**: founded in the early 1960s, later in Old Town, on Mission Gorge Rd per the 2014 and 2016 sources. **Folk Arts Rare Records**: since 1967, now at 3610 University Ave under Brendan Boyle ([about](https://www.folkartsrarerecords.com/about/)). **Buffalo Brothers** (Carlsbad), **Moze Guitars** (La Mesa), **All Things Music** (Valley Center, hosts a jam). The **Old Time Music / New Expression Music** store in North Park hosted SDBS jams and closed in 2012.
- Violin shops: **San Diego Violins** (formerly Hammond Ashley) and **Lawrence Violins** in San Marcos ([sdviolins](https://sdviolins.com/), [Lawrence](http://lawrenceviolins.com/)).
- Archives: the LoC/AFC **Lou Curtiss San Diego Folk Festival Collection**, also at UCLA ([OAC](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt8f59s1t5/admin/)), and SDBS's history PDF and *Live at the Carlton Oaks* CD.

Other: the **San Diego Accordion Extravaganza & Band Camp** (May 13-16, 2026, El Cajon) ([KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/events/2026/05/16/san-diego-accordion-extravaganza)); the **San Diego Mandolin Orchestra** (Wednesdays, Mesa College) ([SDMO](https://www.sandiegomandolinorchestra.org/)); ukulele strums, mainly Hau'oli Strummers on Tuesdays, plus the Coronado Ukulele Club ([Hale Ukulele](http://haleukulele.com/localukeclubs)).

### Fiddle angle: tunes named for the region

The Session's tune search found no tune titled for San Diego, La Jolla, Coronado, Point Loma, Escondido, Cuyamaca, Palomar, Julian or Del Mar. Tunearch blocked automated access. On abcnotation the only hit was the Muriel Johnstone reel above (also in John Chambers' contra collection). San Diego's real fiddle footprint is in recordings and people: the 1967-75 festival tapes, Caplinger, Sara Watkins, Clinton Davis's stringband (2025 album *Ever Returning*), and the Comhaltas tunebook.

### What a visiting fiddler should do

- **Tuesday:** the Ould Sod Irish session (arrive early for one of 6-8 seats). Or Moreton Bay Fig Morris practice if you want to play for dancers; musicians are welcome.
- **Wednesday:** céilí in the Casa del Prado. Or a bluegrass jam in Escondido (3rd Wed) or Valley Center (4th Wed).
- **Thursday:** Thursday Night Pickers in Encinitas.
- **Saturday:** the first-Saturday contra at Liberty Station, or the Folk Dance Center (Balkan live music some weeks).
- **Sunday morning:** the House of Ireland slow and all-levels sessions in Balboa Park, then the free Sunday folk dance next door at the Balboa Park Club from 12:30. Bluegrass at Duck Foot Miramar on 4th Sundays.

For old-time, bring your own friends, or time the trip for Julian Music Camp in May or a North Park Square Dance.

## Chapter 2: Song, listening rooms, traditional singing, radio and festivals
## San Diego: folk song and listening scene, traditional singing, radio and festivals

*Thread notes for the san-diego dossier (San Diego County only). As of October 2026.*

### In short

San Diego's folk-song world is small, old, warm, and built around three things: one long street, Adams Avenue; one nonprofit, San Diego Folk Heritage; and the memory of two men, Sam Hinton and Lou Curtiss. Few listening rooms have outlived their founders. There is no folk show on terrestrial radio. The city's longest-running festival of sea songs seems to have quietly died with COVID. What remains is lively but scattered:

- two Monday-night songwriter rooms on Adams Avenue;
- a monthly ASL-interpreted songwriter concert in a progressive church;
- three monthly Sacred Harp singings spread from South Park to Fallbrook;
- a single monthly library song circle, all that is left of a folk song society founded in 1957;
- free festivals in a railroad park in Poway.

The San Diego Troubadour, a free monthly founded in 2001, is still the scene's newspaper of record, and most of the history below comes from its archive.

### Roots: Sam Hinton, the Folk Song Society and the coffeehouse years

The story starts with a marine biologist. [Sam Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Hinton) (1917–2009) came to San Diego in 1944, ran the Scripps aquarium from 1946 to 1964, and recorded 56 songs for the Library of Congress in 1947. He made Decca and Folkways LPs, hosted a 13-part folk-music series on National Educational Television, and taught folklore at UCSD. Wikipedia credits him with founding the **San Diego Folk Song Society in 1957**. Lou Curtiss, who first went to a meeting around 1958, says Hinton "helped to start the organization" ([Troubadour, 2011](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/what-i-remember-about-the-san-diego-folk-song-society/)).

The society met in members' homes. Curtiss remembered Johnny Walker, Ed Cormier and Reed Moran from that first meeting. Around 1961 he and Curt Bouterse started an SDSU branch, whose first president was the "folk songster" Corky Woerner. At its peak the society "split into about three groups that met three times a month," so with the SDSU group there were four meetings a month. In 1965 it put on a festival at Cal Western University in Point Loma, with Hedy West, Hinton, Stu Jamieson, Bess Lomax Hawes and the Kentucky Colonels.

For a generation of San Diegans, Hinton was simply "the man who played guitar and sang funny folk songs" in their classroom ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/remembering-sam-hinton/)).

The revival-era coffeehouses come alive in a long-lost 1990s interview with Curtiss, published only in July 2026 ([Troubadour, "Lou Curtiss: The Lost Interview"](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/lou-curtiss-the-lost-interview/)). He played with the John B. Trio, "Kingston Trio impressions," at:

- the **Upper Cellar** (70th and El Cajon);
- **Circe's Cup** by San Diego State;
- the **Ballad Man** on Carl St. in La Jolla;
- the wonderfully named **Zen Coffee House and Motorcycle Repair Shop**.

The Kingston Trio connection is real. Two of the trio's members, [Nick Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Reynolds) and [John Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_(musician)), were born in San Diego, and both died there in 2008.

The center of the scene was **the Heritage**, at 3842 Mission Blvd in Mission Beach, which was owned for most of its run by Bill Nunn. Its stage backdrop read "where we've been and who brought us along," and nobody knew what it meant. Players there ranged from Sam Chatmon and Big Joe Williams to the Scottsville Squirrel Barkers (with a young Chris Hillman) and Jack Tempchin. A young **Tom Waits** "started out as doorman" and became a regular performer. Suzanne Shea remembered playing Friday and Saturday nights there in 1969–71 "for $25 bucks" ([Troubadour, "Reunion at the Heritage"](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/reunion-at-the-heritage/)). The building is now the Single Fin Surf Bar.

Other rooms of the era included the **Bifrost Bridge** in La Mesa (Lightnin' Hopkins, Brownie McGhee), **In the Alley** in Escondido, the **Candy Company**, and the **Sign of the Sun** bookstore's "Old Wine in New Bottles" series ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-lou-curtiss-era/); [Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/why-i-love-the-adams-avenue-roots-festival/)).

### Lou Curtiss, Folk Arts Rare Records and the festivals

Lou Curtiss (1939–2018) is the central figure of San Diego folk. As a teenager he scoured used-record bins with his friend **Frank Zappa** ([Troubadour obituary](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/lou-curtiss-rip-july-2-1939-july-8-2018-the-king-of-vinyl/)). He opened **Folk Arts Rare Records** on July 31, 1967, at India and Washington. The shop later moved to Hillcrest, then 3611 Adams Ave in 1977, then 2881 Adams in 2004.

In 1967 he also launched the **San Diego (State) Folk Festival**. His telling of its origin is the best anecdote in the city's folk history ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/a-little-history-of-the-san-diego-folk-festival-and-how-it-got-started/)):

> "In the beginning there was a committee, and the Associated Mens Students had $350 to do something with."

They spent $250 on Bill Monroe, who showed up with only a guitarist; Doug Dillard, Mitch Jayne and Dean Webb filled out the band. Festival No. 2, in 1968, had Skip James get off his plane in Chicago and take the bus home. Merle Travis vanished to Tijuana with Smokey Rogers after his afternoon workshop.

The SDSU festival ran until 1987 ([Troubadour, "The Lou Curtiss Era"](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-lou-curtiss-era/)). In 1994 the Adams Avenue Business Association brought Curtiss in to book the Street Fair, and he added a spring **Adams Avenue Roots Festival**, which he booked for about 15 years. He numbered it as a continuation of his festivals: the 28th in 2001, the 29th in 2002, the 36th in 2009. The 2009 promoters claimed "over 40,000 visitors" the year before, with more than 50 acts on six stages ([KlezCalendar listing](https://www.klezmershack.com/calendar/007982.php)).

Curtiss's festivals mixed folk revivalists with traditional players. U. Utah Phillips first played in 1971. Mary McCaslin started at the third festival. Del Rey, "only 13 years old and working in San Diego under an alias," was introduced by Curtiss to Memphis Minnie records and to **Sam Chatmon** ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/why-i-love-the-adams-avenue-roots-festival/)).

Chatmon, of the Mississippi Sheiks, spent long stretches in San Diego between 1966 and 1982. A transcribed conversation with Curtiss is gold for a fiddler. Chatmon's father, Henderson, "was a fiddler in the slavery time" who played "just those old breakdowns: 'Old Grey Mule,' 'Chickens in the Breadpan Kickin' Up Dough'" with Milton Bracy, "two fiddles and no other instruments." He "had whiskers down to his waist and sometimes he'd have to tie them to the side with a cord to hold them off the fiddle." ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/sam-chatmon-san-diego-bluesman-talks-with-lou-curtiss/))

**Fiddle contests.** The 1970s brought serious contests, "the long-running contests in Julian that eventually evolved into a bluegrass festival," and the silly ones. The Balboa Park Old Time Banjo & Fiddle Contests ran about 15 times ([Troubadour, "Fiddle Contests"](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/fiddle-contests/)):

- $1 to enter, and the judges were announced as bribable;
- prizes were pies, used bowling trophies, or a bag of rutabagas for first place and two bags for second;
- crowds grew to several thousand in Gold Gulch;
- entrants included norteño and mariachi bands and Hare Krishna chanters;
- one year a tie award went to a gospel bluegrass band and the Krishnas "for traditional dogma."

The contests ended when park officials stopped providing power for the sound system. The Julian event reached its 35th annual edition as the Julian Bluegrass Festival at Frank Lane Park in September 2005 ([Village News](https://www.villagenews.com/story/2005/08/25/entertainment/35th-annual-julian-bluegrass-festival/4126.html)), which implies roots around 1971. I found no recent editions.

**Radio and archives.** Curtiss started on radio in 1968 (KPRI underground radio at 2 a.m., then KGB, until "I accidentally played a song with a bad word in it"). He later hosted **Jazz Roots** on KSDS 88.3 on Sunday nights ([Troubadour, "Radio Stories"](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/radio-stories/)). Sources disagree on its start: the 2026 interview preface says 1985–2015, while Curtiss wrote in 2011 that it was his 25th year, and his 2014 retirement piece places its start after 1987.

The American Folklife Center digitized **420 of his tapes covering the first nine festivals (1967–1975)**, plus 57 tapes mostly from Sam Hinton's radio show ([LoC Folklife blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2018/07/lou-curtiss-san-diego-folk-promoter-1939-2018/)). Performers on them include Tommy Jarrell, Kyle Creed, Benny Thomasson, Kenny Hall, Seamus Ennis, A.L. Lloyd, Stan Hugill, Bessie Jones, the Balfa Brothers and Los Madrugadores.

Curtiss retired in 2014. **Brendan Boyle** reopened Folk Arts in North Park and still runs it at 3610 University Ave, with in-store shows and Record Store Day sets ([Folk Arts](https://www.folkartsrarerecords.com/)). Curtiss died on July 8, 2018. The Troubadour's own obituary gives both July 8 (in Liz Abbott's note) and July 9; the LoC says July 8.

### San Diego Folk Heritage: the scene's backbone

**San Diego Folk Heritage (SDFH)** has been a 501(c)(3) since 1987 and is run by volunteers ([Troubadour, 2016](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/san-diego-folk-heritage-still-folkey-after-all-these-years/)). It is unusual in pairing three folk arts: acoustic concerts, contra dance and storytelling ([SDFH About](https://sdfolkheritage.org/about/)).

**People.** Dick Jay has been "chairfolk" for more than 20 years; he MCs, runs sound and writes the quarterly *Folk Notes*. JT Moring booked concerts from 2014 to 2025, and Cara Cormier, a Kansas-raised singer and nonprofit lawyer, has now taken over booking ([Officers](https://sdfolkheritage.org/about/officers/)).

**Scale.** In 2015 SDFH presented 20 concerts averaging about 65 people, split between Poway and Encinitas/Carlsbad. Today the calendar is thinner. As of late October 2026 it lists only the Train Song Festival (Nov 14) and the Tannahill Weavers at Pilgrim UCC in Carlsbad (Nov 15) ([events](https://sdfolkheritage.org/events/)).

**Venues.** Templars Hall in Old Poway Park, Pilgrim UCC in Carlsbad, Good Samaritan Episcopal, San Diego Oasis in Rancho Bernardo, and the Jazz Lounge on El Cajon Blvd ([locations](https://sdfolkheritage.org/directions-maps/)).

**Prices.** Kids 17 and under are free. A 2023 Java Joe's reunion show cost $30, or $25 for members ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/back-with-a-bang-java-joes-reunion-at-san-diego-folk-heritage/)). Sponsorships run from $25 ("Individual") to $300 ("Folk Legend") ([Join](https://sdfolkheritage.org/join-sdfh/)).

When print advertising collapsed around 2020, "every single advertiser, except Folk Heritage dropped out" of the Troubadour ([Troubadour 25th-anniversary piece](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/hey-everybody-the-san-diego-troubadour-is-about-to-celebrate-its-25th-anniversary-and-publisher-liz-abbott-is-here-to-tell-you-about-the-upcoming-celebration-and-fundraiser-along-with-a-bit-about-i/)).

**Festivals.** SDFH runs two free festivals in Old Poway Park:

- **The Sam Hinton Folk Heritage Festival** (June 6, 2026) has music on the gazebo, storytelling in the Porter House with the Storytellers of San Diego, and a family contra dance in Templars Hall ([KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/events/2026/06/06/sam-hinton-folk-heritage-festival)). It began as the San Diego Folk Heritage Festival, which existed by 1990. Hinton's last public appearance was at the 2002 edition, at the Children's School in La Jolla, and it was renamed for him.
- **The Train Song Festival** (Nov 14, 2026) puts six local acts on the gazebo while Poway-Midland Railroad engines give rides and model trains run in the Porter House ([SDFH](https://sdfolkheritage.org/events/train-song-festival-in-old-poway-park/)).

### Listening rooms and house concerts: rise and loss

**Java Joe's (1991–2018)** was the incubator of the 1990s San Diego songwriter boom. Founder Joe Flamini (also spelled Flammini) had worked at UPS. The first room in Poway was "tiny," with a grand piano and room for about 40 people next to the Chicken Pie Diner ([Jeff Berkley, Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/java-joes-one-shot-showcase/)). John Katchur's **One-Shot Showcase** put 20 to 30 songwriters on stage for one song each, including Jewel, Steve Poltz, Gregory Page, Robin Henkel, Lisa Sanders and Joel Rafael. "It was like church for us."

The club moved about ten times ([San Diego Reader, 2018](https://www.sandiegoreader.com/news/2018/oct/18/say-goodbye-java-joes/)): Ocean Beach in 1994 and again in 2000, Hillcrest, Mission Valley, Sunset Temple in North Park, the Ideal Hotel, an El Cajon Blvd pub, Normal Heights in 2014, and Old Town in 2016. That last room became the Backdrop in October 2018. Berkley announced a One-Shot Showcase revival at the Jazz Lounge for April 2026.

**Acoustic Music San Diego (AMSD, later AMSDconcerts), 2003–c.2017.** The founder was **Carey Driscoll**, not "Mike Alvarez" as the research brief guessed. Driscoll began with Jack Williams at an El Cajon music store, then moved to the Normal Heights Community Center and then "the Methodist Church in Normal Heights." He presented "more than 630 shows in all," including Tom Russell, Mary Gauthier, Ray Wylie Hubbard, Eliza Gilkyson, Geoff Muldaur, Ellis Paul, Judy Collins, Tom Paxton, and Martin and Eliza Carthy.

He was famous for scolding emails to his list. Tom Russell, from the stage in 2009: "You gotta be drinking to read those emails!" George Varga of the Union-Tribune called him "a quintessential grassroots concert promoter." Driscoll died in May 2023 at 72 ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/carey-driscoll-a-passionate-advocate-for-overlooked-music/)). Sources differ on when the series ended: the article gives the run as 2003–2017 but also says restart talk continued after the pandemic shutdown.

**Dark Thirty house concerts** started on May 3, 1998, in Jimmy Duke's custom-built knotty-pine great room in the **Eucalyptus Hills of Lakeside**, not Lemon Grove as the brief guessed. The room had 20-foot ceilings, a balcony, a heated deck, a stage, follow spots, and a Taylor house guitar for traveling artists. Tickets were $10–15, later $15–20, with a potluck. Performers included John Stewart, Chris Smither, Leo Kottke and Eliza Gilkyson ([Troubadour, 2017](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/jimmy-duke-impresario-dark-thirty-celebrates-20-years/)). Duke, now in his 90s, came to a June 18, 2026 reunion show at Vision Center for Spiritual Living ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/would-you-please-welcome-joe-rathburn-live-at-dark-thirty-reunion-show/)). I found no current Dark Thirty schedule.

House-concert economics, per Jeff Berkley in 2023: "most house concerts I play are $25–$35 per ticket," and "they are the only way we are able to tour" ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/house-concerts/)).

**Open mics died by a thousand cuts.** The Wood 'n' Lips Open Mic ran for close to six years at Kaffee Meister in Santee. It ended when SESAC demanded fees "about equal" to ASCAP and BMI combined. The café then banned SESAC-registered songs, Dylan's included, and the open mic. The same article lists 13 other dead East County and South Park coffeehouses ([Troubadour, "Kiss Open Mics Goodbye"](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/kiss-open-mics-goodbye/)).

### What is running now (October 2026)

**Adams Avenue is still the spine.** Within a block of each other in Normal Heights:

- **Writers Round San Diego**, every Monday 6–9pm at **The Ould Sod**, an Irish pub marking 35 years. It mixes Nashville-style rounds with an open mic: two open rounds drawn from a hat, three artists playing two songs each, and a booked feature round ([writersround.org](https://writersround.org/san-diego)). It is hosted by Lauren Leigh. It was founded after COVID by Ben Grace Gilmore and Karyn Thurston Gilmore, who asked, "Are the days of intimate listening rooms, like Java Joes & Lestats West, that launched artists like Jason Mraz & Jewel just… over?" A Jan 5, 2026 date is listed on [Sara Petite's site](https://sarapetite.com/event/6364308/745629121/sara-petite-writer-s-round-san-diego).
- **Lestat's West**, next to Lestat's on Adams (which opened in 1997), still "hosts weekly comedy and open mic nights" ([Lestat's](https://www.lestats.com/lestats-on-adams)). Current listings show a Monday 6pm sign-up that is mostly comedy ([Joinue](https://www.joinue.com/regulars/880e03e3-8938-42c1-b481-dab7244068ce)).
- **Songwriter Sanctuary San Diego** is a monthly listening-room concert at **Normal Heights United**, 4650 Mansfield St. It costs $10 suggested, with ASL interpretation and a "Pre-Show Pantry Jam" food and clothing drive. It began in January 2023, has been curated by Lindsay White (formerly of the Lovebirds), and became a nonprofit in October 2025 ([site](https://www.songwritersanctuary-sd.com/); [about](https://www.songwritersanctuary-sd.com/about.html)).

This one church is quietly the most important folk building in town. Driscoll's AMSD concerts met at "the Methodist Church in Normal Heights." Adams Avenue Unplugged puts its ticketed main stage in "the historic Normal Heights United Church" ([Times of San Diego](https://timesofsandiego.com/arts/2026/04/28/adams-avenue-unplugged-community-music-artists/)). Lou Curtiss also held concerts at "the United Methodist Church" from 1978, probably the same congregation, though I could not confirm it.

**Elsewhere:**

- **San Diego Folk Song Society** song circle: still alive as a monthly themed circle at the Serra Mesa–Kearny Mesa library. Sources disagree on the day: the [North County Bluegrass & Folk Club jam list](https://northcountybluegrass.org/camping/) says 2nd Saturday 1:30–4:30, and [FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/miscellaneous/folk-music-jams/) says 4th Sundays. In 2009 it met 2nd Sundays at the Old Time Music store as a bardic circle, with a volunteer leader picking the theme and guests encouraged to "look up a song within the theme" ([2009 listing](https://dulcimerdigest.blogspot.com/2009/08/san-diego-folk-song-society-information.html)). Its old domain now forwards to a defunct Meetup group.
- **North County Bluegrass & Folk Club**: free 1st-Tuesday concerts at Round Table Pizza in Escondido (opening band at 7pm, feature at 8pm, patio jam from 6pm), plus a September campout at Potrero ([NCBFC](https://northcountybluegrass.org/music-nights/)).
- **The Jazz Lounge** (6818 El Cajon Blvd) is a nonprofit jazz room that also hosts SDFH shows and Jeff Berkley's songwriter series. It has been alcohol-free since September 22, 2026 ([site](https://www.thejazzlounge.live/)).
- **Music at the Marston**: three summer songwriters-in-the-round afternoons in a historic rose garden, $10–15 ([SOHO](https://www.sohosandiego.org/enews/0526musicatthemarston.htm)).
- **Belly Up Tavern** in Solana Beach is the touring roots club (Ramblin' Jack Elliott, Richard Thompson, Greensky Bluegrass), but it is a general club, not a folk room.

### Traditional singing

**Sacred Harp.** **SD FaSoLa** sings from the 1991 Sacred Harp and holds three monthly practice singings, each on a Sunday from 2 to 4pm ([2025 update](https://www.sdfasola.org/posts/2025-singing-update/)):

- 1st Sundays in South Park (1536 Felton St);
- 3rd Sundays in Fallbrook (232 N Wisconsin St);
- 4th Sundays in Serra Mesa (9212 Fermi Ave).

It keeps loaner books, runs periodic "Learner's Groups," and its website is run by Juniper Grato ([about](https://www.sdfasola.org/about/)). A 2nd-Sunday singing at Graham Memorial Presbyterian in Coronado began in February 2024, praised for "a very bright sound," and was cancelled that July ([posts](https://www.sdfasola.org/posts/)). The fasola minutes show **no annual singing hosted in San Diego**. San Diego singers appear in the minutes of the All-California Convention in Altadena (LA County) ([fasola minutes search](https://fasola.org/minutes/search/?q=San+Diego)). That makes 3 singings a month for 3.3 million people.

**Sea chanteys.** The Maritime Museum's **Sea Chantey Festival** grew from a 1992 idea by Joe Ditler and Dave Kendall of **the Jackstraws** (founded 1979), and SDFH's Chuck Bernstein organized it every year. A 2018 Troubadour piece has the best line in this whole thread. Loren Smith learned "Rolling Down to Old Maui" from **Stan Hugo**, "the last living chanteyman aboard the tall ships." Sam Hinton, Johnny Walker and Ken Graydon were regulars ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-sea-chantey-festival-a-lifeline-to-our-nautical-past/)).

The 24th festival was held aboard the Star of India in 2016, with $16 adult museum admission ([The Log](https://thelog.com/traditional-songs-of-the-sea-take-center-stage-at-sea-chantey-festival/)). The 27th was in 2019, folded into the new Port of San Diego Festival of the Sea ([There San Diego](https://theresandiego.com/event/festival-of-the-sea-maritime-museum/)). The second Festival of the Sea was "postponed to a future date, yet to be determined" ([Maritime Music Directory](https://seashanties4all.com/venues/festival-of-sail-in-san-diego/)), and nothing appears on the museum's 2026 special-events page. I found no public monthly shanty sing in the county. In a navy port with the world's oldest active sailing ship, that is the scene's most surprising gap.

**Gospel as community practice.** The **Martin Luther King Jr. Community Choir San Diego** grew out of a 1990 commemorative choir of about 200 voices that sang with the San Diego Symphony. Pastor Ken Anderson took it over in 1996, reorganized and renamed it in 1997, and gave it a September–June concert season. By 2008 it had given 54 arts scholarships ([bio](https://www.bodhitreeconcerts.org/bio-mlk-jr-community-choir)). Anderson also directs the UCSD and Grossmont College gospel choirs. In a 2021 KPBS feature he explained the coded spirituals of the Underground Railroad ([KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/midday-edition/2021/09/22/summer-music-martin-luther-king-jr-community-choir)). I could not verify a living gospel-quartet circuit in Southeast San Diego.

**Open community singing** is thin. The Choral Consortium of San Diego runs **World Singing Day** (Oct 17, 2026, at Liberty Public Market) ([Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/world-singing-day-san-diego-2026-tickets-1998644644935)). A "Community Voices" series of by-ear folk-harmony sings is described on [choralsd.org](https://choralsd.org/program-community-voices.html), but I could not corroborate it.

**Storytelling** is a living folk art here in a way it is not in many cities, because SDFH treats it as a pillar. The Storytellers of San Diego run a room at every Hinton festival, ending with a storytelling open mic.

### Radio and media

- **No San Diego terrestrial station carries a dedicated folk show** that I could find. KSDS (Jazz 88.3) dropped folk-adjacent programming when *Jazz Roots* ended. It now has only Saturday blues shows: Roger Stolle's *Crossroads Delta Blues Hour* and Ron Dhanifu's *Saturday Night Blues Ball* ([schedule](https://www.jazz88.org/on-air-schedule.asp)). I found no KSDT or KCR folk show, and KPBS has none.

Joel Rafael, the Woody Guthrie interpreter, has produced *Rockin' the Rez* there with his wife Lauren for about 14 years: 449 episodes, airing six times a week across Rez Radio and Pacifica affiliates ([Troubadour, 2026](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/good-radio-is-out-there-you-just-have-to-know-where-to-look/); [schedule](https://rezradio.fm/shows/rockin-the-rez/)).

- **The San Diego Troubadour**: first issue September 2001, timed for the Adams Avenue Street Fair. ### Character: what a visiting fiddler should know

- **Geography:** concentrated on Adams Avenue (Normal Heights, University Heights, Kensington), with North County outposts in Poway, Escondido, Carlsbad, Encinitas, Vista and Fallbrook. Downtown and the beach towns barely figure.
- **Monday night:** Writers Round at the Ould Sod, with Lestat's West next door.
- **First Tuesday:** NCBFC in Escondido.
- **A Sunday afternoon:** Sacred Harp.
- **Whenever you can:** a pilgrimage to Folk Arts Rare Records.
- **Who shows up:** the Hinton/Curtiss-lineage institutions skew older and rely on volunteers. The newer formats (Writers Round, Songwriter Sanctuary) are younger, original-song-focused, and explicitly about community care and access.
- **What is missing:** a club that presents traditional song for its own sake, and a shanty sing.

## Chapter 3: Heritage and diaspora traditions
## San Diego: heritage and diaspora traditions (thread notes, Oct 2026)

Scope: San Diego County (San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad MSA). Tijuana items are flagged as outside the MSA. Vitality ratings use thriving / active / niche / fading / historic_only. Founding years marked "inferred" are worked out from edition numbers and may be off by a year or two because of COVID gaps.

### The short version

San Diego's living heritage scene is strongest where three things meet: the **Kumeyaay homeland**, the **Mexican border** and the **Navy**. Kumeyaay bird singing, an a cappella song tradition accompanied by gourd rattles, has gone from "hanging from a thread" to civic protocol. Mariachi is taught in public schools across the South Bay, and Chula Vista has the world's first college mariachi degree. Danza azteca fills Chicano Park every April. Navy migration explains why Filipino and Chamorro dance companies are some of the oldest and busiest groups in the county. Older European enclaves are still here too: Point Loma's Azorean Festa has run since 1910. The single best place to see many traditions in one week is the **House of Pacific Relations lawn programs** in Balboa Park, which runs about 55 free programs a year. The big negative story of 2025-26 is fear: the Oaxacan Guelaguetza was cancelled in 2025 and postponed again in 2026 over immigration enforcement, and the Somali community is under political pressure.

Only one **NEA National Heritage Fellow** has been based in San Diego County: Chicano musician **Ramón "Chunky" Sánchez** (2013). I checked every California fellow on [arts.gov](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/list?state=CA) for San Diego residence.

### 1. Kumeyaay and other Native traditions

#### Bird singing (the most important living tradition here)

Kumeyaay bird songs are an allegorical cycle of [roughly 300 songs](https://www.kumeyaay.com/kumeyaayhistory/233-traditional-origins-of-southern-ca-bird-songs-public-performance-and-text-by-ron-christman-ipai-tipai.html) about animals and right living. Men sing them with gourd rattles, and women dance in lines. The singer Ron (Ral) Christman told an audience at California Indian Days in Balboa Park (2000) that the songs probably survived the mission era because their allegories didn't look heretical to the padres. The Viejas Band's history page quotes him: ["The songs were almost lost, they were hanging from a thread but we kept them."](https://viejasbandofkumeyaay.org/viejas-community/kumeyaay-history/bird-songs-dancing-and-songs/) The same page tells of a late-1800s Campo singer, Chi-hoow, who lived "the poor life" in a hut and purified himself in the Jacumba mud hot springs. It also reproduces a flyer for a 1930 fiesta at Capitan Grande that drew city visitors to the mountains.

**Vitality: active and growing.** Evidence from 2025-26:

- **San Diego Zoo Safari Park, Indigenous Peoples' Day (Oct 13, 2025).** The San Pasqual Band hosted, and ["about a dozen bird singing groups performed during the day"](https://www.times-advocate.com/articles/tribe-celebrates-indigenous-peoples-day-at-safari-park/). The event was [first held in 2021](https://www.daylightsandiego.org/san-diego-honors-native-culture-and-history-at-indigenous-peoples-day-events/).
- **Kumeyaay New Year (Kupiihaaw), Sycuan Cultural Resource Center, Sept 22, 2025.** At this small equinox gathering, women danced to bird songs that tell the creation story. The director, Charlene Elliott, said facilities like this didn't exist when she was young. Bernice Paipa said the songs teach "how to live in a good way… it's all about community with the Kumeyaay Nation" ([Daylight San Diego](https://www.daylightsandiego.org/kumeyaay-new-year-celebration-at-sycuan-cultural-center2025/)).
- **Civic protocol.** In 2018 bird singers [opened SDSU's convocation for the first time](https://thedailyaztec.com/90425/news/campus-ceremonial-event-embraces-kumeyaay-tradition/). At the January 16, 2026 proclamation of Chula Vista's **Kumeyaay People's Day** (second Sunday of November), [Blue Eagle Vigil (Viejas), Jamie LaBrake (Sycuan) and Dakota Jacome (Mesa Grande)](https://www.daylightsandiego.org/newsletter-chula-vista-establishes-kumeyaay-peoples-day/) sang. The park where it happened used to be Discovery Park, home to a Columbus statue.
- **Powwows.** Kumeyaay bird singing has a slot of its own at the [38th Balboa Park Powwow (May 2026)](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/05/07/powwow-in-balboa-park-celebrates-native-american-music-dance-and-art/). The [53rd SDSU powwow](https://www.daylightsandiego.org/indigenous-community-dances-sings-and-serves-up-frybread-tacos-during-53rd-annual-sdsu-powwow/) named a head bird singer, Chris Alvarado Waipuk of San Pasqual, alongside its northern and southern host drums.
- **A loss.** Dr. Harry Paul "Junior" Cuero Jr., a former Campo chairman whose life was "dedicated to the preservation and promotion of the Bird Songs", received an honorary SDSU doctorate in 2019. His visitation was at Campo on Dec 12, 2025 ([obituary](https://www.dignitymemorial.com/obituaries/el-cajon-ca/harry-cuero-12660663)).

Bird singing is mostly not a public "session" you can drop in on. It happens at wakes, fiestas, powwows and civic events. As SDSU's Margaret Field put it, being a bird singer with a group is ["a humongous responsibility where you have to go, especially if it's for a traditional event or a death."](https://thedailyaztec.com/90425/news/campus-ceremonial-event-embraces-kumeyaay-tradition/)

#### Rarer song cycles and the ACTA lifeline

The **Alliance for California Traditional Arts (ACTA)** apprenticeships keep alive some of the more endangered cycles:
- 2001: [Jon Meza Cuero](https://actaonline.org/profile/jon-meza-cuero/), born in Potrero and raised in Baja, possibly one of the few US Kumeyaay who know the **Wildcat** cycle, taught **Stan Rodriguez**.
- 2017: Quechan/Kumeyaay elder [Preston Arrow-weed](https://actaonline.org/profile/preston-arrow-weed/), also an actor and playwright, taught Rodriguez the **Shuluk (Lightning)** cycle.
- 2009: Kumeyaay healer Jane Dumas taught ethnobotany to Luiseño practitioner [Richard Bugbee](https://actaonline.org/profile/richard-bugbee/). ACTA also profiles Kumeyaay basket weaver [Eva Salazar](https://actaonline.org/profile/eva-salazar/).

**Kumeyaay Community College**, set up by the Sycuan Band in 2004, teaches Kumeyaay language and culture, and in 2023 won an [ACTA grant for a Summer Solstice Week](https://actaonline.org/profile/kumeyaay-community-college/).

#### Luiseño and Cupeño: Cupa Days

The Pala Band's [**51st Cupa Days** (May 2-3, 2026)](https://cupa.palatribe.com/2026/03/06/announcement-51st-cupa-days/) included cultural performances and **peon games**, the Southern California hand game whose songs are sung partly to unnerve the guessing side. Cupa Days commemorates the May 1903 removal of the Cupeño from their village at Warner's hot springs. *Source conflict:* the Cupa Cultural Center says it has been held "every year since 1974", but a 51st edition in 2026 points to 1976.

### 2. Mexican and Chicano traditions

#### Chicano Park Day and danza azteca: thriving

On April 22, 1970, Barrio Logan residents and Brown Berets occupied the land under the Coronado Bridge to stop a CHP station from being built there. The occupation produced [Chicano Park](https://chicano-park.com/) (a National Historic Landmark since 2016), the Logan Heights clinic and the **Centro Cultural de la Raza**, which was [planted in 1970 at the Ford Building and inaugurated July 11, 1971](https://www.centroculturaldelaraza.org/). The **56th Chicano Park Day** (April 25, 2026) drew ["thousands"](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/04/25/this-is-culture-thousands-come-together-in-barrio-logan-to-celebrate-chicano-park-day/), and "Aztec dancers from multiple danza Azteca groups" performed the opening blessing. The Centro's president, Maria Figueroa, told the crowd: "We are not simply historic relics, but living entities." The steering committee called the 2024 danza ["one of the most largest performances of Aztec Indigenous dance"](https://chicano-park.com/) (coordinated by Calpulli Mexihca). ACTA describes **Danza Mexi'cayotl** as one of the oldest danza groups in the United States. Its heredero [Andrés Ehecatl Aguilar](https://actaonline.org/profile/andres-ehecatl-aguilar/) makes regalia and is collecting oral histories from the founders of US danza groups. ACTA also lists Danza Mixcoatl and the Congregación de Danzas Azteca de EECU.

#### Mariachi: thriving, but institutionally fragile

San Diego's mariachi story is about education:
- In the early 1990s, Sweetwater Union HSD board president **Bob Griego** noticed that Latino parents (49% of the district's students were Latino) didn't come to school events. He pushed mariachi into the curriculum, and by **1996** it was at Sweetwater High, backed by his new [Mariachi Scholarship Foundation](https://www.10news.com/news/local-news/mariachi-program-hits-high-note-in-san-diego-schools-empowering-latino-students-through-music). Jeff Nevin explained the logic: parents skip parent-teacher night, "but if you have the mariachi playing on parent-teacher night, that changes the conversation" ([UT 2026](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/07/12/the-one-of-a-kind-mariachi-museum-in-chula-vista-has-lost-its-home-now-what/)).
- In **2004**, **Southwestern College** (Chula Vista) became the first college in the world to offer a music degree with a mariachi specialization. Its ensemble is **Mariachi Garibaldi**, and Nevin also leads **Mariachi Champaña Nevin**. In 2026 the foundation gave a record $52,000 in scholarships.
- **Keith Ballard** built the mariachi and steel-drum programs at Montgomery and Southwest Middle and was named to the [National Teachers Hall of Fame in 2024](https://www.kpbs.org/news/arts-culture/2024/04/04/sweetwater-union-music-teacher-named-to-the-national-teachers-hall-of-fame). At his last concert (Dec 17, 2024), with roughly 80 middle schoolers in charro suits behind him, he warned parents that the district might try again to cut the program: ["I fought the system… I stayed the course."](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/01/03/beloved-mariachi-teachers-departure-puts-spotlight-on-school-districts-arts-cuts/)
- **Mariachi Juvenil** (La Jolla Music Society Community Music Center, Logan Heights, since 2001; director Serafin Paredes) won first place at the [California State Fair Youth Mariachi Competition in July 2024](https://www.sdcoe.net/about-sdcoe/news/post/~board/news/post/san-diego-county-students-invited-to-join-inaugural-mariachi-honor-band). In fall 2026 the society started a county-wide **Mariachi Honor Band**.
- **The fragility:** the Ed Marsh Mariachi Museum, which opened in 2022, holds an 1895 dictionary defining "mariache" as a fandango and a Jorge Negrete charro suit, and is appraised at about $3.4M. It [lost its library space in June 2026](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/07/12/the-one-of-a-kind-mariachi-museum-in-chula-vista-has-lost-its-home-now-what/) and is now in a storage unit.
- Festivals: the San Diego International Mariachi Summit (July), the [12th Route 78 Rotary Mariachi Festival](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/02/19/community-events-in-san-diego-county-from-chinese-new-year-fair-to-mariachi-festival/) (Escondido, March 2026), and the touring Mariachi USA at the Rady Shell. The Chula Vista International Mariachi Festival reached its [8th edition in 2020](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/chula-vista-hosts-8th-annual-international-mariachi-festival/2281332/), and I found no later evidence of it.

#### Son jarocho: active, but anchored in Tijuana

**Fandango Fronterizo** was [founded in 2008 by Jorge Castillo](https://sandiegomagazine.com/everything-sd/arts-culture/fandango-fronterizo-music-festival/) as a fandango played through the border fence at Friendship Park. At the first one, Border Patrol asked them to wrap up around 4:30 pm. "Just one more song," Castillo said, and they played "La Bamba" for an hour: "[the agents] couldn't believe it was still the same song." Since 2018 Border Patrol hasn't allowed anyone to play on the US side, so the verses now go unanswered from the north. A longtime participant recalls that in 1999 "you could buy mangos across the border." The 15th anniversary was October 2023. *Outside the MSA* (Tijuana side), but in 2024 the collective ran a [youth apprenticeship in Barrio Logan and community fandangos in Sherman Heights](https://actaonline.org/profile/fandango-fronterizo/) with an ACTA grant. Back in 2012, KPBS reported son jarocho ["thriving in Tijuana and San Diego"](https://www.kpbs.org/news/border-immigration/2012/05/30/son-jarocho-creates-community-both-sides-border-an), with at least three Tijuana jarana makers and Eduardo Garcia's monthly house fandangos and free workshops in San Diego. I found no current public fandango schedule on the US side.

#### Ballet folklórico: active
[El Tapatío de San Dieguito](https://actaonline.org/region/southern-border/) (2000, 60+ students at Bayside and Encinitas), Nadia Arámbula's Ballet Folklórico San Diego ([UT profile, Oct 30, 2026](https://article.wn.com/view/2026/10/30/Folkl_u00f3rico_dance_leader_keeps_the_spirit_of_Mexico_aliv/), via a syndication mirror), Tierra Caliente Academy of Arts, and Ballet Folklórico Xochipilli at Chicano Park Day.

#### Oaxacan Guelaguetza: fading for political reasons
The Coalition of Indigenous Communities of Oaxaca (COCIO) has run Guelaguetza San Diego for about [26 years](https://cocioguelaguetza.com/) (the 20th was in 2015 at CSUSM). It was **cancelled in 2025** and **postponed in October 2026** after immigration arrests. "There's this fear of insecurity, and it all stems like from racial profiling," said board member Cuetz Rodríguez. COCIO estimates [~40,000 Oaxacans live in the county](https://www.kpbs.org/news/border-immigration/2026/10/15/oaxacan-festival-postponed-second-year-in-a-row-because-of-immigration-crackdown), mostly in North County.

#### Chicano protest song: historic_only
**Ramón "Chunky" Sánchez** (NEA Heritage Fellow 2013) learned boleros from his mother while she washed dishes, then [joined La Rondalla at San Diego State](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/ramon-chunky-sanchez), sang strike songs with César Chávez, and led Los Alacranes Mojados. He died in 2016. A trivia note: NEA Fellow Lalo Guerrero did [war work at Convair in San Diego](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/eduardo-lalo-guerrero) during WWII and entertained at military camps at night.

**Banda, norteño and tamborazo:** I found these only as commercial concerts, not as community practice.

### 3. Navy-linked Pacific communities

#### Filipino: thriving
- **PASACAT** grew out of a 1969 Philippine Fiesta at the Balboa Park Organ Pavilion for San Diego's 200th, led by Navy veteran Delfin Labao. About 70 performers took part, and the troupe [formed on May 23, 1970](https://canvasrebel.com/meet-anamaria-cabato/) (pasacat.org says [November 1970](https://pasacat.org/): *a source conflict*). It bought its National City building in 1973 and added a rondalla and indigenous-instrument ensemble. Its sections are a Senior Company, Junior Dancers, Rondalla/Percussion and the Harana Singers ([ACTA](https://actaonline.org/profile/pasacat-philippine-performing-arts-company/)). Studio hours are Tuesday and Wednesday evenings.
- **Samahan** (1974) teaches students [aged 5 to over 80](https://actaonline.org/profile/samahan-filipino-american-performing-arts-education-center-inc/) and runs **SamaFest**, the Philippine Cultural Arts Festival, in Balboa Park ([38th year, Aug 2025](https://fox5sandiego.com/san-diego-guide/things-to-do/balboa-park-filipino-festival/)).
- **Bernard Ellorin** learned Sama and Tausug tagunggu' gong music in Tawi-Tawi and [mentored Kim Kalanduyan through ACTA in 2019](https://actaonline.org/profile/bernard-barros-ellorin/).

#### Chamorro: active
Chamorros are "the most widely dispersed U.S. Pacific Islanders", with about two-thirds living off-island ([ACTA on CHE'LU](https://actaonline.org/profile/chamorro-hands-in-education-links-unity/)). **Rosemary Mantanona** founded **Guma Imåhen Taotao Tåno'** in 1999. It started as 3-5 students in her Skyline Hills front yard, then for years practiced 20+ dancers [in the SDPD Southeastern Division parking lot](https://obrag.org/2026/08/one-womans-mission-keeping-the-chamorro-culture-alive-even-after-years-of-colonization-of-their-island-guam/). She harvests neighbors' palm fronds to teach weaving. Her standing derives from Guam's **Frank Rabon**, a [2026 NEA Heritage Fellow](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/frank-rabon) who is also a master artist for CHE'LU's free weekly youth workshops. Other events: the CHE'LU Chamorro Cultural Festival (Oceanside, [March 2025](https://ca.headtopics.com/news/chamorro-festival-returns-with-focus-on-honoring-ancestors-67428268)) and the 9th Håfa Adai Festival at the House of Chamorros (Aug 22, 2026).

#### Pacific Islander and Hawaiian: thriving
The **Pacific Islander Festival** (PIFA, founded 1995) held its 32nd edition at Ski Beach on Sept 26-27, 2026, and calls itself ["the largest Pacific Islander festival in the United States"](https://www.pifasandiego.com/). In ACTA's 2025 cohort, Kumu **Ilima Kam Martinez** (Carlsbad) is teaching [hula kahiko to Tiffany Tom](https://actaonline.org/2025apprenticeships/) (Oceanside). On the ukulele side, the Ramona Ukulele Jammers draw crowds to [free sing-and-strums](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2026/04/03/ramona-ukulele-jammers-get-ready-to-put-on-free-beatles-sing-and-strum-along-crowd-expected/).

- **Portuguese (Azorean), Point Loma: active.** The [116th Festa do Espírito Santo](https://obrag.org/2026/05/portuguese-community-of-point-loma-celebrates-the-116th-festa-do-espirito-santo/) (May 22-24, 2026) is "the oldest ethnic European religious celebration in San Diego". ### 7. The best recurring heritage stage: House of Pacific Relations

The [House of Pacific Relations](https://www.sdhpr.org/) is a consortium of **38 cultures** housed in 1935 Exposition cottages, and it turned 90 in 2026. ## Chapter 4: History, people, archives, defunct venues and tunes
## San Diego: history, people, archives, defunct venues and tunes

*Thread: san-diego / history. As of October 2026. Companion to `sd-history.json`. Researched with web search and fetch only; a large share of the revival-era detail comes from the San Diego Troubadour's archive of Lou Curtiss's "Recordially" columns, so read the 1957–2014 story as largely Curtiss's account.*

### The short version

San Diego's documented folk history has three layers. The deepest is Kumeyaay song: bird-song cycles that are still sung today and were nearly lost. The second is a Spanish, Mexican and Pacific-maritime layer of missions, fandangos, Hawaiian sailors' songs and Portuguese festas. The third is a remarkably well-taped Anglo-American revival. That revival had two anchors. One was a marine biologist, **Sam Hinton**, who founded the San Diego Folk Song Society in 1957. The other was a record-store owner, **Lou Curtiss**, who ran the **San Diego State Folk Festival** from 1967 to 1987 and **Folk Arts Rare Records** from 1967 to 2014. Thanks to Curtiss's reel-to-reel habit, San Diego's revival may be one of the best-documented in the West: more than 400 reels went to UCLA, digital copies went to the Library of Congress, and samples stream online. San Diego also produced the bluegrass band that launched a Byrd and an Eagle, a jam-standard old-time tune, and a rare cross-border son jarocho fandango played through the border fence.

### 1. Before the revival: Kumeyaay, missions, hide-droghers, fishing families

**Kumeyaay song.** The Kumeyaay (Ipai-Tipai/Kumiai) homeland straddles today's US–Mexico line. The Barona Cultural Center & Museum on the Barona Reservation in Lakeside holds objects dated to about 10,000 years ago and has "listening alcoves featuring sounds and songs" ([Barona](https://www.baronamuseum.com/about)). The Viejas Band's history pages explain that bird songs are "given to us by the Creator." Bird singer Paul (Junior) Cuero says the songs "belong to all the people," and educator and singer Ral Christman says "the songs were almost lost, they were hanging from a thread but we kept them" ([Viejas](https://viejasbandofkumeyaay.org/viejas-community/kumeyaay-history/bird-songs-dancing-and-songs/)). The same page cites ethnographer Constance Goddard DuBois (1906): Hatakek of Manzanita knew all the old songs, including those for the image ceremony. It also reproduces a 1930 flyer inviting the public to a multi-day Kumeyaay fiesta at Capitan Grande.

The ACTA profile of **Jon Meza Cuero** notes that besides bird songs, "other cycles, which include Wildcat and Salt Dances and funeral songs, are now more rare." Meza Cuero, born in Potrero and raised in Baja California, is "possibly one of a few" who still know the Wildcat cycle. He taught it to Stan Rodriguez in a 2001 ACTA apprenticeship ([ACTA](https://actaonline.org/profile/jon-meza-cuero/)). Retired engineer Ron Christman tells the origin story: the Creator stripped the crow's colors for trying to sell the songs and gave them instead to the humble *tu cuk* bird ([kumeyaay.com](https://www.kumeyaay.com/traditional-origins-of-southern-ca-bird-songs.html)).

**Early recordings.** The earliest sound documentation found is T.T. Waterman's UC Berkeley wax cylinders of Kumeyaay/Diegueño speakers and songs (1907–09), now at the Phoebe A. Hearst Museum. Waterman published *The Religious Practices of the Diegueño Indians* in 1910 ([LOC record](http://www.loc.gov/item/a10000645/); [Hearst descendant-community resources](https://hearstmuseum.berkeley.edu/descendant-community-media-resources/)). Medium confidence: the Hearst catalog portal blocked direct verification. I found no Lomax, Juan Rael, Helen Heffron Roberts or BAE recordings made in San Diego County.

The WPA California Folk Music Project (Sidney Robertson Cowell, 1938–40) recorded "mostly the northern part of the state." A search of its LOC online collection for "San Diego" returns zero items ([LOC blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2024/07/sidney-robertson-and-her-california-gold/)). San Diego was effectively skipped by 1930s federal folk documentation.

**Mission and Californio era.** Mission San Diego de Alcalá was founded in 1769, the first in Alta California. Kumeyaay converts "picked up skills in Western musical instruments, and joined the Mission choirs and orchestras" ([Wikipedia, Kumeyaay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay); [calindianmissions.org](https://calindianmissions.org/mission/basilica-san-diego-de-alcala/)).

The best eyewitness of the hide-trade coast is Richard Henry Dana's *Two Years Before the Mast* ([Gutenberg text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2055/pg2055.txt)). He spent months curing hides on the San Diego beach in 1835–36 alongside Hawaiian ("Kanaka") sailors. He describes "Mr. Mannini, who was the most noted improvisatore among them," singing for an hour at a time about the men he worked beside, to his countrymen's laughter. That may be the earliest written description of a folk-song practice on San Diego soil. Dana also reports that violin and guitar were the only instruments he heard at Californio fandangos, which usually lasted three days. The great fandango he describes was a Santa Barbara wedding, where San Diego's Don Juan Bandini was the star dancer.

**Fishing communities.** Azorean Portuguese families settled La Playa and Roseville on Point Loma from the 1870s, joined by Madeirans and by Italian fishermen in Little Italy. Their **Festa do Espírito Santo** (first held 1910) is often called San Diego's oldest ethnic festival: a spring procession up Avenida de Portugal followed by free *sopas* in the United Portuguese S.E.S. hall ([Atlas of Home](https://atlasofhome.com/roots/portuguese-in-point-loma-san-diego/); [festival listing](https://www.festivalsinamerica.com/festival/festa-do-espirito-santo-san-diego)). Medium confidence: these are secondary sources.

**1915.** The Panama-California Exposition in Balboa Park built a "Painted Desert" Native village where Hopi, Zuni and other Pueblo people lived and performed dances ([LOC photo](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/92500164/); [San Diego History Center](https://sandiegohistory.org/archives/amero/1915expo/ch3/)). Its anthropology exhibit became the San Diego Museum of Man. On Aug 2, 2020 it was renamed the **Museum of Us**, and its Kumeyaay exhibit was developed with the Mat'taam Naka Shin intertribal committee ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Us)).

### 2. Navy town, ballrooms and country music (1920s–1950s)

Lou Curtiss's 2003 column "San Diego Gems That Are Past and Gone" is the best map of the pre-revival scene ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/san-diego-gems-that-are-past-and-gone/)). He claims, without sources, that Jelly Roll Morton and Kid Ory played San Diego dance halls by 1917, that Blind Lemon Jefferson busked the streets in the early 1920s, and that Woody Guthrie and Lefty Lou played local radio in the 1930s. Treat those as low confidence.

The center of gravity was the **Bostonia Ballroom**, built around 1929 in the country between El Cajon and Lakeside. It hosted big bands in the 1930s and became the county's country-music showcase from the 1940s: Bob Wills, Spade Cooley, Ernest Tubb, Lefty Frizzell, Johnny Cash and owner-bandleader Smokey Rogers. The best anecdote is from a column by 12-year-old-at-the-time Lou Curtiss. In September 1952 the Santa Ana winds stranded Hank Williams's Drifting Cowboys east of the Laguna Mountains, so Chet Atkins stayed onstage after his opening set and backed Williams for 90 minutes. Williams died three months later ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/when-hank-williams-came-to-town/)). The building survives as the Royal Palace Banquet Hall, where Curtiss's memorial was held in 2018.

In National City, the **Maddox Brothers and Rose** owned and played the 21 Club around 1958–59 ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/about-old-records-and-what-people-collect-and-what-they-dont/)).

### 3. Sam Hinton and the Folk Scare (1944–1966)

**Sam Hinton** (1917–2009) came to San Diego in 1944 for wartime research on Point Loma. He was curator of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography aquarium from 1946 to 1964 and later taught biology and folklore at UCSD. He recorded 56 songs for the Library of Congress in 1947, cut LPs for Decca and Folkways, wrote "The Ocean World" column for the *San Diego Union*, and founded the **San Diego Folk Song Society** in March 1957 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Hinton); [SDFSS archived page](https://web.archive.org/web/20110525060330/http://members.cox.net/sdfss/samhinton.htm)). He could play the harmonica without his hands. Members recall him singing "The Trusty Lariat" in their elementary schools. Curtiss wrote in 2002 that Hinton "has played all 28 of the previous San Diego Folk/Adams Ave. Roots Festivals" ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/why-i-love-the-adams-avenue-roots-festival/)).

**Coffeehouses** ([Curtiss, Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-beat-movement-and-coffeehouses/)):
- 1957: the Zodiac, on the street level of the Pacific Ballroom building.
- About 1958: the Zen Coffee House and Motorcycle Repair Shop at Broadway and India, where Curtiss saw Judy Henske.
- About 1960: the **Upper Cellar** (6557 El Cajon Blvd), owned by Bob Stane, who later ran Pasadena's Ice House. Curtiss first saw 12-string player Fred Gerlach there and sang at its open mic around 1960.
- **Circe's Cup** near SDSU, with bordello murals whose faces were SDSU professors. It later became a pool hall, "Circe's Cue."
- The **Ballad Man** on Pearl St in La Jolla.

The most important early-1960s presenter was bookseller **Harold Darling**, whose **Sign of the Sun** at Adams and College ran San Diego's only regular traditional-music concert series. It presented Mississippi John Hurt, Jean Ritchie, Ramblin' Jack Elliott, Rev. Gary Davis, Bessie Jones, Skip James, the New Lost City Ramblers and others. Artists stayed in a Hillcrest boarding house; Bessie Jones sometimes stayed a month. Darling ended the series in the mid-1960s, believing traditional music "had enough support," and moved on to art film ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-sign-of-the-sun-2/)). Tapes from summer 1962 survive, including what Curtiss believed to be the only recording of Rev. Gary Davis playing five-string banjo ([Lou Curtiss Sound Library](https://www.folkartsrarerecords.com/library/)).

**Bluegrass begins.** In 1961, Ed Douglas, Larry Murray and Yuris Zeltins opened the **Blue Guitar** music shop, named after a Wallace Stevens poem ([Double Eagle archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20110716231606/http://thedoubleeaglestore.com/squirrel.html)). Its house band, the **Scottsville Squirrel Barkers**, was named for Douglas's hometown of Scottsville, Kentucky. Members were Douglas, Murray, Kenny Wertz, Gary Carr and a teenage **Chris Hillman**, who grew up on a ranch in rural north San Diego County ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hillman)). **Bernie Leadon** replaced Wertz on banjo when Wertz joined the Air Force; Leadon's family had moved to San Diego in 1957 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Leadon)). The band made one LP, *Blue Grass Favorites* (1963), and broke up. Its alumni went on to the Byrds, the Flying Burrito Brothers, Country Gazette, Hearts & Flowers and the Eagles ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottsville_Squirrel_Barkers); [Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/a-little-bluegrass-history/)).

*Sources disagree.* Wikipedia dates the band 1961–63 and puts Wertz on banjo. Dwight Worden's Troubadour column says the band formed in 1962, with Wertz on guitar and Carr on banjo. Hillman is called both "16" and "17" (he was born in December 1944). Douglas's own shop page calls him a San Diego native, while the Troubadour names Scottsville as his hometown.

The other early landmark was the **Heritage** coffeehouse at 3842 Mission Blvd in Mission Beach, active roughly from the early 1960s until 1973. Bill Nunn owned it for most of its run, and guitarist Bob Webb at the end. A sign behind the stage read "where we've been and who brought us along," and nobody knew what it meant. Acts paid $25 for two nights. Its doorman was **Tom Waits** ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/reunion-at-the-heritage/)). The building is now the Single Fin Surf Bar.

Two or three years before 1967, the Folk Song Society staged a festival at Cal Western University on Point Loma with Hinton, Hedy West, the Kentucky Colonels and Bess Lomax Hawes ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/asdf/)).

### 4. Lou Curtiss and the festival years (1967–1987)

**Lou Curtiss** (July 2, 1939 – July 8, 2018) arrived from Seattle around 1951. His father was an IWW member who taught him to ride the rails. As a teenager he combed record bins with his schoolmate Frank Zappa. He was jailed during civil-rights voter registration in 1964 ([Reader](https://www.sandiegoreader.com/news/2018/jul/09/lou-curtiss-kept-music-alive/); [Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/honoring-the-legacy-of-lou-curtiss/)). In 1961 he and Curt Bouterse started a folk song society with weekly jams at San Diego State ([Troubadour, "Lost Interview"](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/lou-curtiss-the-lost-interview/)).

The **1st San Diego State Folk Festival** (May 11–13, 1967, Peterson Gym) had a $350 budget from the Associated Men Students. The organizers spent $250 of it on **Bill Monroe**. Monroe's band bus broke down in El Centro, so Doug Dillard, Dean Webb and Mitch Jayne of the Dillards came down to back him ([Folk Arts](http://www.folkartsrarerecords.com/library/1st-san-diego-folk-festival/)). A fan kept shouting for "Earl's Breakdown," and Monroe politely declined ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/a-little-history-of-the-san-diego-folk-festival-and-how-it-got-started/)). At the 2nd festival (1968), Skip James got off his plane in Chicago and took the bus home, and Merle Travis disappeared to Tijuana with Smokey Rogers.

On July 31, 1967, **Folk Arts Rare Records** opened at 3753 India St. Curtiss gives that address; other accounts say "Washington and India" ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-lou-curtiss-era/)). It moved to 3743 Fifth Ave in Hillcrest, then to 3611 Adams Ave in Normal Heights in April 1977, then to 2881 Adams Ave in 2004 after a Curves gym outbid it. The store ran hoot nights and shape-note singing led by Curt Bouterse. Mississippi bluesman **Sam Chatmon** wintered in San Diego for about 15 years and held court in an armchair at the front of the shop ([FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/milestone/louis-f-curtiss/)).

On Nov 17, 1973, just after the Heritage closed, Tom Waits played one of the first in-store concerts to about 150 people at no more than $4 a ticket. He previewed songs including "San Diego Serenade" ([Reader](https://www.sandiegoreader.com/news/2018/jul/09/lou-curtiss-kept-music-alive/)).

The festival's guest list reads like a who's-who of old-time and traditional music: Tommy Jarrell, Kyle Creed, Benny Thomasson, Kenny Hall and the Sweets Mill String Band, Sam and Kirk McGee, Dennis McGee and Sady Courville, the Balfa Brothers, Lydia Mendoza, Santiago Jimenez, Stan Hugill, Silly Wizard, the Boys of the Lough and Louis Boudreault ([AFC](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2018/07/lou-curtiss-san-diego-folk-promoter-1939-2018/); [Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/a-long-time-here/)). Curtiss brought the first Cajun band to San Diego (Jo-el Sonnier and Doc Guidry, April 1972). One FolkWorks writer remembers "Dewey Balfa jamming with Aly Bain; Lydia Mendoza playing with Kenny Hall." Promoters called it "the largest folk festival west of the Mississippi"; the Library of Congress called it "the best traditional music event in the Western United States." It ended after the 20th edition in 1987.

**Fiddle contests.** Folk Arts also ran about 15 **Balboa Park Old Time Banjo and Fiddle Contests** in the 1970s. They cost $1 to enter, the judges were advertised as bribable, and one year first prize was a bag of rutabagas (two bags for second). Byron Berline, Earl Collins, Taj Mahal and Don "Sugarcane" Harris turned up. The contests ended only because the park stopped supplying electricity ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/fiddle-contests/)). Julian's cash-prize contests ran alongside them and evolved into the Julian Bluegrass/Music Festival.

**Irish music.** In 1974 the Dublin uilleann piper **Dave Page** (born 1906), a Leo Rowsome pupil who had played in the 1920s–30s Siamsa Gael Céilí Band, retired to San Diego. Within months he was sitting in at a potluck for musicians from the weekly New England-style dances, so contra dancing was already established in San Diego by 1974. He revived the Siamsa Gael band with young locals, ran house sessions ("plenty of beer, and always 'a nice cup of tea'"), and played the 1975 Smithsonian Folklife Festival ([UilleannObsession](https://www.uilleannobsession.com/article_dave_page)). He was the grandfather of singer Gregory Page.

**Instrument makers.** Bob Taylor bought into the Lemon Grove shop American Dream in 1974, creating Taylor Guitars ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Guitars)). Greg and Janet Deering founded Deering Banjos in Spring Valley in 1975 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deering_Banjo_Company)).

**Chicano music.** Ramón "Chunky" Sánchez arrived at SDSU in 1969 with La Rondalla Amerindia de Aztlán. His "Chicano Park Samba" became the anthem of the 1970 takeover that created Chicano Park. He became an NEA National Heritage Fellow in 2013 and died Oct 28, 2016 ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/heritage/ramon-chunky-sanchez); [Times of San Diego](https://timesofsandiego.com/life/2016/11/05/1000-mourn-chunky-sanchez-icon-changed-lives-of-thousands/)).

### 5. From Roots Festival to Unplugged (1987–2019)

San Diego Folk Heritage became a nonprofit in 1987. By 2015 it ran contra dances, storytelling and about 20 concerts a year averaging 65 people ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/san-diego-folk-heritage-still-folkey-after-all-these-years/)).

North County became a bluegrass nursery. The 40-seat **Old Time Cafe** in Leucadia, whose owners Bill and Pearl "tolerated no talking," hosted teenage Alison Brown and Stuart Duncan ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/bluegrass-happenings/)). The San Diego claim is disputed: Wikipedia has Brown born in Hartford and Duncan raised in Santa Paula. Dennis Caplinger joined Bluegrass Etc. at 13 ([Bluegrass Today](https://bluegrasstoday.com/dennis-caplinger-passes/)). Chris Thile and Sara and Sean Watkins played Vista pizza parlors as "Seldom Clean" before forming Nickel Creek in 1989. Amos and Emma Radcliffe hosted Sunday bluegrass and Wednesday old-time fiddle jams at home for about 30 years ([CBA](https://californiabluegrass.org/sweet-emma-of-san-diego/)).

Former tuna fisherman Joe Flammini opened **Java Joe's** in Poway in February 1991. Jewel was a waitress there. It moved to Ocean Beach in 1993 (the Troubadour's date; other accounts say 1994), and Jason Mraz held Thursday nights there in the 2000s ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/java-joe-splendid-times-still-guaranteed-for-all/)).

In 1994, after a five-year break, Curtiss launched the free **Adams Avenue Roots Festival**. The Reader quotes him saying 1994; some accounts say 1995. It kept the old numbering: the 2002 edition was "Number 29." Curtiss left the organization in 2008, and in 2012 the festival became Adams Avenue Unplugged, which ran on April 25, 2026 ([Reader](https://www.sandiegoreader.com/news/2018/jul/09/lou-curtiss-kept-music-alive/); [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/events/2026/04/25/adams-avenue-unplugged)).

The **San Diego Troubadour** began in fall 2001 with Curtiss's column in the first issue. A photo caption says September and the film team says October ([Troubadour @ 20](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-troubadour-twenty/); [open letter](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/an-open-letter-to-the-san-diego-music-community/)).

Other milestones:
- **2002:** Hinton's last public appearance; the festival was renamed for him.
- **2003:** **Summergrass** began in Vista.
- **2008:** Jorge Francisco Castillo started **Fandango Fronterizo**, a son jarocho fandango played on both sides of the border fence at Friendship Park ([ACTA](https://actaonline.org/profile/fandango-fronterizo/)).
- **2009:** Hinton died in Berkeley.
- **2014:** Curtiss retired; Brendan Boyle reopened Folk Arts on El Cajon Blvd on Sept 1. The store has since moved to 3610 University Ave and is still open ([Folk Arts](https://www.folkartsrarerecords.com/)).

Curtiss died on July 8, 2018. The AFC and NAMM give that date; one Troubadour tribute says July 9. The Library of Congress's tribute quotes archivist Michael Taft: "Lou was a 'keeper of traditions'… The world needs people like Lou Curtiss" ([AFC](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2018/07/lou-curtiss-san-diego-folk-promoter-1939-2018/)).

### 6. COVID and the rebound (2020–2026)

- **2020:** Summergrass was cancelled and the local Tuesday jams were suspended ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/dealing-with-covid/)). In September, KSON's corporate owner cancelled Wayne Rice's *Bluegrass Special* after 44 years, "San Diego's longest-running radio show," just as IBMA honored him. It reappeared in December on Mountain Country 107.9 in Alpine ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-end-of-an-era-2/)).
- **2021:** Summergrass returned. Dennis Caplinger died that August.
- **2022:** The Troubadour headlined "Live Bluegrass Events are Back!" San Diego Folk Heritage held the "Loulapalooza" benefit.
- **2023:** Yale Strom and Elizabeth Schwartz's documentary *Recordially Yours, Lou Curtiss* premiered. Its makers say the COVID pause gave them time to finish it ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/the-music-film-you-need-to-see-in-2023-recordially-yours-lou-curtiss/)). Summergrass passed from its founding clubs to the Antique Gas & Steam Engine Museum.
- **2024:** ACTA funded Fandango Fronterizo to train Chicano youth in son jarocho.
- **2026:** Worden writes that San Diego has "more than 20 active bluegrass bands" and a jam nearly every day, which he credits to Appalachian-born military retirees ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/bluegrass-music-and-culture-in-san-diego/)).

### 7. Archives: where the sound is

- **UCLA Ethnomusicology Archive, Lou Curtiss San Diego Folk Festival Collection (2007.04):** 400 reels covering 1962–87 ([OAC](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt8f59s1t5/admin/)). Russ Hamm counts more than 860 tapes processed by 2014.
- **Library of Congress, American Folklife Center:** 420 tapes of the 1967–75 festivals plus 57 tapes largely from Sam Hinton's radio show ([lccn 2015655368](https://lccn.loc.gov/2015655368)). Listening is on site only.
- **Lou Curtiss Sound Library samples** online at Folk Arts ([library](https://www.folkartsrarerecords.com/library/)). Curtiss said in 2011 that about 2,000 tapes remained undigitized, and he lamented that "no one… from" SDSU or any other San Diego institution came forward to house them ([Troubadour](https://sandiegotroubadour.com/asdf/)).
- **Sam Hinton:** the 1947 LOC session (Bear Family CD, 1999), Folkways LPs, and papers at UC San Diego Library ([Calisphere](https://calisphere.org/collections/26175/)).
- **Kumeyaay:** Waterman cylinders at the Hearst Museum (restricted), the Museum of Us, and the Barona Cultural Center & Museum.
- **San Diego Troubadour** back issues since 2001: the best narrative archive of the scene.

**Gaps.** Curtiss repeatedly asked for tapes from the Old Time Cafe, Drowsy Maggie's, the SDSU Back Door, the Cal Western festival and early Folk Heritage concerts. None are known to have surfaced.

### 9. Tunes and songs tied to San Diego

- **"Waiting for Nancy"** (old-time reel in D, 1972). The headline find for a fiddler. Curt Bouterse wrote it on fretless banjo while waiting in Squibob Square, Old Town, for a friend named Nancy who never came. It is now a jam standard: in the *Portland Collection* (1997), recorded by the Reed Island Rounders, and on the North Atlantic Tune List. Bouterse recorded it in 2008 with the Heritage's old owner, Bob Webb ([history](https://dulcimerdownloads.com/pages/waiting-for-nancy-history); [Tunearch annotation](https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Waiting_for_Nancy)).
- **"Mr and Mrs McLaughlin of San Diego,"** a reel in G by the Scottish composer and pianist Muriel A. Johnstone ([abc](https://trillian.mit.edu/~jc/music/abc/by/Muriel_Johnstone/reel/MrAndMrsMcLaughlinOfSanDiego.abc)). It is the only fiddle tune found that literally names the city. Who the McLaughlins were is unknown.
- **"San Diego Serenade,"** Tom Waits (1974).
- **"Chicano Park Samba,"** Chunky Sánchez.
- **"Sweet Emma,"** a bluegrass song by Mike and Yvonne Tatar for jam hostess Emma Radcliffe.

**Searches that found nothing.** The Session's API and abcnotation returned no tunes named for Coronado, La Jolla, Escondido, Cuyamaca, Point Loma, Julian, Balboa, Palomar or Del Mar. Tunearch (403) and Slippery-Hill (410) could not be searched directly.

### 10. Source disagreements and caveats

- **Squirrel Barkers:** formed 1961 or 1962; Wertz on banjo or guitar; Hillman 16 or 17.
- **Curtiss's death:** July 8 (LOC, NAMM) or July 9 (one Troubadour tribute).
- **First Roots Festival:** 1994 (the Reader quotes Curtiss) or 1995 (implied by the festival numbering).
- **Troubadour's first issue:** September or October 2001.
- **Java Joe's:** moved to Ocean Beach in 1993 or 1994; the 2018 closing date is secondary.
- **Brown and Duncan:** called San Diegans by the Troubadour but not by Wikipedia.
- **Mark O'Connor Fiddle Camp at Point Loma Nazarene:** c.1999–2004 per one summary; one teacher placed his teaching there within 2006–2014.
- **Earlier claims:** the 1910 festa date and the Waterman cylinders rest on secondary or blocked sources.
- **Bias:** the revival narrative leans heavily on Curtiss's own columns.
