# Denver-Aurora-Centennial, CO: the folk scene (as of Oct 2026)

Metro #19 of 20 (CBSA 19740; 3,092,037 people in 2025). The MSA is ten counties: Adams, Arapahoe, Broomfield, Clear Creek, Denver, Douglas, Elbert, Gilpin, Jefferson and Park. **Boulder County is a separate MSA**, and so are Larimer (Fort Collins) and El Paso (Colorado Springs). The Denver scene treats all three as part of its own circuit, so this dossier counts them separately and flags them *outside MSA*. That split is one of the main findings: much of what people call "the Denver scene" happens in Boulder County.

Synthesised from four research threads (trad, song, heritage, history) plus the merged data file `denver.json`. Headline numbers come from `denver.json`'s computed `headline_stats`.

---

## 1. Scene in a nutshell

Inside the ten-county MSA, Denver is a bluegrass-every-night town (47 jams a month, many in breweries) and a Sunday-afternoon Irish town (20 sessions a month; the biggest moves around Broncos kickoffs). Old-time (4 a month) and contra (2, both in Wheat Ridge) are thin. Dance life centres 25–30 miles northwest at Boulder's Avalon Ballroom: Boulder County, a separate MSA with a ninth of the population, adds 66 events a month, and Fort Collins and Colorado Springs add 32 more. The spine is Harry Tuft's Denver Folklore Center (1962), still a shop, and its offspring Swallow Hill ($6.4M revenue, about 3,500 students, 219 concerts in 2025). Around them sit a Western layer (a Texas-style fiddle contest inside the stock show, a 1987 cowboy gathering, Lloyd Shaw's square-dance legacy), a large urban Native community (the 50th Denver March Powwow), a mariachi boom and a Mongolian horsehead-fiddle festival.

## 2. The 10 most interesting things

1. **The dance scene lives in Boulder County.** The MSA has **2 contra dances a month**, both at the Wheat Ridge Grange; the shared Front Range adds **7.3** (Boulder ~4.3, Fort Collins 2, Colorado Springs 1). The City and County of Denver had about four a month before 2020 and has none now ([CFOOTMAD](https://www.cfootmad.org/calendar?month=2026-11); [Dancing the Web](https://www.dancingtheweb.com/coloradocontra/ccovenue.htm)). ECD and Scandinavian: 0 inside, 1 and 4.3 a month at Boulder's Avalon.
2. **Bluegrass beats Irish about 2.3 to 1:** 47.1 jams against 20.3 sessions a month inside the MSA, plus 35.5 jams in Boulder County. Bill Monroe talked a new society that had voted *against* a festival into the 1973 Henderson festival (about 6,500 people, inside the MSA). It was sold in 1993 for **$10,000** and became RockyGrass ([CBMS](https://www.coloradobluegrass.org/history); [Bluegrass Today](https://bluegrasstoday.com/50-years-of-bluegrass-in-colorado-cbms-celebrates-silver-anniversary/)).
3. **The biggest Irish session runs on the Broncos schedule:** the Abbey Tavern, 3 pm, 6 pm in NFL season, cancelled for playoffs ([thesession 7312](https://thesession.org/sessions/7312)).
4. **A 1962 guitar store became a $6.4M nonprofit, and the store is still open.** Swallow Hill ran 219 concerts, taught about 3,500 students and lost $699k in 2025 ([annual report](https://swallowhillmusic.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Swallow-Hill-Music-Annual-Report-2025.pdf)). Harry Tuft (born 1935) plays his namesake theatre on 19 Nov 2026.
5. **The state fiddle contest is held in the Beef Palace** at the National Western Stock Show: Texas-style, with a Bob Wills division and a Jake Jabs accompanist division; players warm up "behind the sheep" ([COTFA](https://www.coloradofiddlers.org/about-us-2/); [KUNC](https://www.kunc.org/news/2025-01-14/colorados-fiddle-family-holds-a-swinging-reunion-at-the-national-western-stock-show)).
6. **Denver hosted the 4th Morin Khuur Festival in the USA** (May 2026, DU), a Mongolian horsehead-fiddle competition. A local ensemble has existed since 2024, and the US Embassy in Ulaanbaatar is on "Denver Street" ([Denver Sister Cities](https://denver-sister-cities.ghost.io/denver-welcomes-the-soulful-sound-of-the-mongolian-steppe/)).
7. **The 50th Denver March Powwow** (about 55,000 people, 1,600+ dancers, ~100 tribes) is run by **two staff**, a grandmother and granddaughter ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/arts-culture/inside-the-denver-march-powwow-after-fifty-years-2026-40855904/)).
8. **Denver's "grandfather of folk" was a Black singer, Walt Conley,** who put up a young Bob Dylan in 1960. Dylan flopped here but found Jesse Fuller's harmonica rack at the Exodus ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/music/passin-through-bob-dylans-ill-fated-summer-in-denver-8007398/)).
9. **Colorado's state folk dance is legally "square dancing"**, defined to include contra and the Virginia reel ([C.R.S. 24-80-909.5](https://www.dancingtheweb.com/coloradocontra/crs.html)). Revival founder Lloyd Shaw was Denver-born; the metro has **19 modern western square dance clubs** to 2 contra series, and the National Square Dance Convention comes to Loveland in June 2027.
10. **The gaps matter to a Celtic fiddler:** no Comhaltas branch, no full RSCDS branch, and no Scottish or Cape Breton session, fixed céilí, shanty sing or (inside the MSA) English country dance. The Scottish Fiddle Club and Rocky Mountain Fiddle Camp have parked domains.

- **Heritage practices** are mostly rehearsals of performing groups, some with only 2025 or undated evidence.
- The total includes a 2.17/month Swallow Hill sing-along the song thread flagged as unverified. Unverified items (Burns Pub, the DFC song circle) and the online Clawhammer Corner are excluded.
- Fort Collins bluegrass jams were never itemised, so the Larimer column undercounts.

### 4.2 Irish sessions (20.3 a month inside; 8.7 Boulder Co.; 18.3 Fort Collins + Colorado Springs)

thesession.org's `sessions/nearby` API returns 15 sessions in range: 10 in Denver and Boulder, 3 in Fort Collins and 2 in Colorado Springs. `events/nearby` returns none. Each listing was checked against its comment thread.

**Inside the MSA**

- **The Abbey Tavern, East Colfax, Sun 3 pm** ([7312](https://thesession.org/sessions/7312)). The biggest standing session, continuing the ~18-year Irish Snug session that COVID closed (first Abbey session 20 Feb 2022).   - **Tue ~7–10:** song-friendly, turn-based, "learners to masterful pros". Last firm evidence Aug 2024 ([6545](https://thesession.org/sessions/6545)).
  - **Wed 6–9:** tunes only, experienced players; confirmed "still on" 29 Oct 2026 ([2798](https://thesession.org/sessions/2798)).

### 4.6 Squares: the statute, Shaw, and 19 clubs

Colorado's official folk dance is "square dancing", defined to include "squares, rounds, clogging, contra, line, the Virginia reel, and heritage dances" ([C.R.S. 24-80-909.5](https://www.dancingtheweb.com/coloradocontra/crs.html)). **Lloyd "Pappy" Shaw** (born in Denver, 1890–1958) ran the Cheyenne Mountain School in Colorado Springs; his *Cowboy Dances* (1939) and touring dancers seeded the national revival ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Shaw_(educator))). The Library of Congress recorded his dancers at the 1938 National Folk Festival with fiddle and piano, including a Mexican varsoviana ([LOC](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc9999005.25610/)), and his foundation's archives are at the University of Denver.

Today the Denver Area Square & Round Dance Council lists **20 modern western square dance clubs, 19 inside the MSA** (Denver, Aurora, Wheat Ridge, Lakewood, Littleton, Englewood, Westminster, **Broomfield**, Bennett, Conifer and more), including the **Rocky Mountain Rainbeaus**, an LGBTQ club ([list](https://coloradosquaredance.com/clubs/dasrdc-clubs)). That is about ten clubs per contra series. The **76th National Square Dance Convention** is in Loveland, 23–26 Jun 2027 ([CSSDA](https://new.coloradosquaredance.com/)).

**Scandinavian:** weekly at the Avalon, **Mondays 7:30–10, $7** (polska, hambo; "learn to pivot") ([BDC](https://boulderdance.org/)); nothing inside the MSA.

**International folk dance** ([denverfolkdancing.org](https://www.denverfolkdancing.org/)): Denver Area Folk Dancers (first three Thursdays in winter, Cameron Church, $5), Washington Park Folk Dancers (May–Oct Thursdays, free) and Maple Grove Grange, Wheat Ridge (1st & 4th Sun, $3). **Klezmer:** **KlezKolorado** (idea 2022, per [JCC](https://www.jccdenver.org/2026/09/15/built-by-community-for-community-the-story-of-klezkolorado/)) held its first two editions at the JCC's Ranch Camp (including 16–18 May 2025). **Balkan:** Planina's Boulder Balkan Bash (§6). **French-Canadian:** none found.

**Morris:** only an unnamed Boulder group teaching free Fieldtown classes on Wednesday evenings at St. Aidan's, Sep–Nov 2025 ([listing](https://happeningnext.com/event/free-intro-morris-class-eid3a0ctqsw6q)). No Denver morris, sword or rapper team was found.

## 5. Song and listening scene

### 5.1 The Folklore Center and Swallow Hill

**Denver Folklore Center (DFC)**
- **Founding.** Harry Tuft opened it on **12 Mar 1962** at 608 E 17th Ave, in the Swallow Hill district. He had spent his $900 savings on stock from Izzy Young, and Exodus owner Hal Neustaedter suggested the idea ([Wikipedia: Harry Tuft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Tuft)).
- **1960s–70s.** Sunday hoots and square dances; Rev. Gary Davis, Elizabeth Cotten, Doc Watson and Mike Seeger came through. A 1971 concert hall booked Taj Mahal, Ry Cooder and Bonnie Raitt ([DFC history](https://www.denverfolklore.com/denver-folklore-center--history.htm)). Hot Rize, Grubstake, Otis Taylor and Tim and Mollie O'Brien came out of its orbit.
- **1980–93.** Tuft lost the lease in 1980, closed the store in 1983 and reopened it at **1893 S Pearl St** in 1993.
- **Ownership.** Sold in 2016; since **2023** owned by long-time manager **Ian Dehmel**.
- **Today.** Acoustic instruments, repairs, rentals and a teacher-referral list. Hours: Mon–Fri 10–6, Sat 10–5, Sun 1–5 ([DFC](https://www.denverfolklore.com/)).

**Swallow Hill Music.** Volunteers formed it in 1979 to keep the DFC's concerts and lessons going ([about](https://swallowhillmusic.org/about-us/)). Autoharpist **Julie Davis** built the school (named for her since 2002); under Seth Weisberg (1987–95) enrolment reached 2,700, and under Chris Daniels it moved to **71 E Yale Ave** in 1999 ([CMHOF](https://cmhof.org/inductees/swallow-hill-music/)). Rooms: **Daniels Hall** (299), **Tuft Theatre** (100) and **Quinlan Cafe** (~95), home of the jams ([tech specs](https://swallowhillmusic.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Swallow-Hill-Music-Tech-Specs-2022.docx-1.pdf)). CEOs since 2022: Aengus Finnan (ex-Folk Alliance International), then Jessy Clark (2024, medium confidence). It gets SCFD money (the seven-county cultural sales tax) as a Tier II organisation.

COVID cut its earned program revenue from **$4.83M (FY2019) to $0.76M (FY2020)**, about 84% ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/840781725)). It also programs the Denver Botanic Gardens concerts (since 2010), a Northglenn series and Englewood's **Indiewood** festival.

Fall 2026 bookings show the folk core is intact: Alasdair Fraser (15 Nov), BanjoFest (14 Nov, with Kaia Kater, Allison de Groot and Nic Gareiss) and **"Harry Tuft & Friends" in the Tuft Theatre on 19 Nov** ([Nov guide](https://swallowhillmusic.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/November-2026-Swallow-Hill-Concert-Guide.pdf)).

**Swallow Hill's recurring community events** (71 E Yale; jams $10 suggested): the **Monday Night Jam** (8 pm, Spencer Crawford: "zydeco sing-alongs into Prince"); the **Tuesday Night Jam** (8 pm, Mike Jagel: "decades of 'Hill lore", charts, bluegrass and **cowboy classics**; in effect Denver's song circle); monthly Irish (1st Sun), jazz (2nd Sun), old-time (3rd Sun) and bluegrass (4th Sun) jams; **Circle Singing** (3rd Sat 2–3:30, Roy Willey, "like a drum circle for voices"); the Denver Uke Community (3rd Sat); a **Tabla Jam** (Nabin Shrestha); and an open mic (2nd Thu). A fortnightly sing-along, Teen Jam and "Loop 2 Loop" vocal jam appear on the [jams page](https://swallowhillmusic.org/jams/) without dates; treat them as unverified.

### 5.2 Listening rooms, house concerts, open mics

Denver has **no second dedicated folk listening room** of Swallow Hill's size, and venues have been closing since 2016. The Walnut Room (South Broadway) closed in 2016 after a rent rise. The **Soiled Dove Underground** closed in Feb 2024 and reopened as **The Dove Underground** on 15 Aug 2026 ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/music/underground-dove-lowry-grand-opening-40909035/)). The **Mercury Cafe** (Five Points, 1975; open mics, poetry, swing) closed in Mar 2025; its successor, The Pearl, closed in Apr 2026 when the building was seized for taxes, and in Sep 2026 the public-benefit corporation CoCreate agreed to buy it and revive the Mercury ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/arts-culture/mercury-cafe-under-new-cocreate-ownership-40935194/)). Others: the **Buffalo Rose** (Golden), Parsons Theatre (Northglenn), and outside the MSA eTown Hall, the Gold Hill Inn and Planet Bluegrass Ranch.

**House concerts:**
- **Songs at Summerstead, Lakewood:** the duo Alright Alright hosts it in a creekside meadow, pass-the-hat (2026: 6 Jun, 18 Jul, 22 Aug, 26 Sep). It exists "as listening rooms, small venues and breweries in Denver continue to struggle or close" ([site](https://www.alrightx2.com/songs-at-summerstead)).
- **Five Points Live!** (Doug Gertner and Maggie Miller, since 2016, 40+ shows); its 2nd Jazz & Roots Celebration (10 Oct 2026) sold out at $15 ([site](https://www.fivepointslive.com/)).
- **El Rancho House Concerts**, Evergreen. No recurring series was found in Arvada, Highlands Ranch, Centennial or Parker.

### 6.2 Cowboy music and poetry

Cowboy songs also survive informally in Swallow Hill's Tuesday jam. No Western Music Association chapter or Western swing society was found.

**Native American (thriving).** In 1951 the BIA opened its first relocation field offices in **Denver, Salt Lake City and Los Angeles** ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/native-americans/bia/urban-relocation)). Denver Native Americans United (1970) led to the Denver Indian Center (incorporated 1983; an ACF sheet says 1975) at 4407 Morrison Rd ([DICI](https://www.denverindiancenter.org/home-1)). The **Denver March Powwow** (1974, Denver Coliseum) claims 1,600+ dancers from about 100 tribes, 38 states and 3 provinces ([site](https://denvermarchpowwow.com/)); 2025 had 31 drums and 1,200+ dancers ([Buffalo's Fire](https://www.buffalosfire.com/the-daily-spark/denver-march-powwow-marks-50th-year-with-special-contests)). Executive director Grace "SwaHuux" Gillette has served 37 years. Other powwows: DU New Beginnings (May), the Denver Art Museum's 37th Friendship Powwow (Sep), the 4th Welcome Home Powwow (Sep) on the **70-acre Tall Bull Memorial Grounds** in Daniels Park, reserved for Native use since 1977 ([CPR](https://www.cpr.org/2026/09/09/indigenous-youth-tallbull-groups-welcome-home-powwow/)), and a Black Light Pow Wow at áyA Con (Oct). No currently active named host drum could be confirmed.

**Hispano, Chicano, Mexican (thriving).** **MSU Denver** launched Colorado's first **mariachi performance degree** in fall 2025, out of the student club Mariachi Los Correcaminos ([Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2025/08/17/mariachi-colorado-schools/)). The **Colorado Youth Mariachi Program** grew from 7 backyard students (2016) to 80+ a year with a studio in Aurora, and Westminster schools' mariachi raised **$57k+** in a 9NEWS campaign (Sep–Oct 2026). Violinist **Dr. Lorenzo A. Trujillo** is the culture bearer ([site](https://www.lorenzotrujillo.com/)). Grupo Huitzilopochtli (1969, Raúl "Tlaloc" Chávez Portillo, d. 2022) founded Denver's danza Azteca scene ([Denverite](https://denverite.com/2022/05/11/raul-chavez-portillo-who-elevated-aztec-dance-in-denver-has-died/)). **Su Teatro** (1972) held its 30th Chicano Music Festival in 2026 and has run a Chicano Music Hall of Fame since 2002 ([Su Teatro](http://suteatro.org/hall-of-fame/)). Norteño and banda thrive commercially, for instance at Stampede, a country-western club in Aurora; one promoter bills "norteña de **sax** y acordeón". Son jarocho and alabados were not found as living metro practices.

**Japanese American.** The Tri-State/Denver Buddhist Temple (1916) anchors Sakura Square. **Denver Taiko** (1976) was the **first taiko group outside California** and is "full" in its 50th year ([site](https://www.denvertaiko.org/)). **DBT Minyo Kai**, founded by Issei women in the 1960s, practises weekly and leads Bon Odori (8 Aug 2026) ([TSDBT](https://tsdbt.org/announcements/2026/obon-2026/)).

**Mongolian (a Denver signature).** The community began in 1989 with three Mongolian engineers at the Colorado School of Mines. **Ajnai Morin Khuur Center** (2024, Ariunbold Mijiddorj) co-hosted the national **Morin Khuur Festival** at DU in May 2026 ([Ajnai](https://www.ajnaicolorado.com/)); Naadam was 11 Jul 2026.

**Hmong.** HMAAC runs a youth dance academy and, from 2026, a **year-long beginners' qeej course**. Two New Years were held three weeks apart: Brighton (29–30 Nov 2025, the 41st) and the Jeffco Fairgrounds (20 Dec 2025) ([HMAAC](https://hmongassociationco.org/)).

**Others, briefly** (details in `denver.json`): Ethiopian/Eritrean (about 30,000 in the metro in 2013; Taste of Ethiopia in City Park); Korean (festival on Havana St, Aurora, 3,000+); Filipino (FACC, 1954); Greek (**60th Greek Festival**, Hellenic Dance Academy); German (the Denver Kickers soccer club houses the metro's **Schuhplattler** troupe ([site](https://schuhplattlers.com/))); Polish (**Krakowiacy**, Fridays 6–9, Globeville, undated listing); Italian (Mount Carmel's **131st feast**, 2025); Ukrainian (Belmar Park festival). **Five Points jazz** is fading: its festival (from 2003, 22 years) became a $225k/yr grants fund in Nov 2024 ([Denverite](https://denverite.com/2024/11/21/denver-five-points-jazz-festival-ending/)). **Black banjo:** a 2026 state grant funds Align With Purpose's **gourd-banjo building intensive** with the Black Banjo Reclamation Project ([CCI 2026](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/supporting-colorados-cultural-legacy-25-new-folk-and-traditional-arts-initiatives-awarded)).

**State support.** Colorado Creative Industries funds 25 folk-arts grants of $3,000 a year. The Colorado Folk Arts Council was founded by folk-dance pioneer Vyts Beliajus (its site says both 1968 and 1969); its International Heritage Festival (first held 1976) has no evidence after 2024 ([CFAC](https://internationalheritagefestival.org/about-us-news/)).

## 13. History

### Narrative

**Camps and immigrants (1858–1930).** The 1851 Treaty of Fort Laramie recognised the whole metro as Cheyenne and Arapaho territory. Auraria was founded in Oct 1858, and the earliest Denver-region tune found comes from 1859: a period caption says a fiddler called "Tosso" played gold diggers "an original melody 'Pike's Peak Gallop'" ([Colorado Magazine, Apr 1959](https://www.historycolorado.org/sites/default/files/media/document/2018/ColoradoMagazine_v36n2_April1959.pdf)). Linking him to Joseph Tosso of "Arkansas Traveler" fame is plausible but unverified. Welsh and Cornish miners built the Central City Opera House (1878). Globeville's (1891) Slovene, Croat, German and Polish smelter workers and North Denver's Italians (feast from about 1894) left traditions that survive. The first St. Patrick's parade (1883 or 1889) ran until anti-Catholic feeling stopped it in 1921 ([DPL](https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/irish-colorado-part-two-st-patricks-day-parade)). The Stock Show began in 1906, and violinist **George Morrison Sr.**, son of a champion fiddler, recorded for Columbia in 1920; the Klan burned a cross at his house in 1921 ([CMHOF](https://cmhof.org/inductees/georgemorrisonsr)).

**The Western dance revival (1930s–50s).** Denver-born Lloyd Shaw's Cheyenne Mountain Dancers brought cowboy squares and Hispano couple dances (La Camila, the varsoviana) to the 1938 National Folk Festival. His *Cowboy Dances* (1939) went national. From 1947, CU folklorist **Ben Gray Lumpkin** taught folksong in Boulder and at CU's Denver Extension, and taped more than 200 Colorado informants ([AMRC Pages vol. 24](https://www.colorado.edu/amrc/sites/default/files/attached-files/amrc_pages_vol._24.pdf)).

**The coffeehouse boom (1958–66).** Denver was a waystation for acts touring between Chicago and the West Coast. The Little Bohemia (1958) and Hal Neustaedter's **Exodus** (1959, 1999 Lincoln St, 3.2 beer by the bucket) made **Walt Conley** a local star; the Exodus's 16 Oct 1959 festival came out on LP, and by 1961 the club had booked Judy Collins (East High), Josh White, Cisco Houston, Sonny Terry & Brownie McGhee, Jimmy Driftwood and the Weavers ([kimsloans](https://kimsloans.wordpress.com/2026/08/10/the-exodus-appearances/)). Dylan came in summer 1960 and flopped ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/music/passin-through-bob-dylans-ill-fated-summer-in-denver-8007398/)). Tuft opened the Folklore Center in 1962; future governor Dick Lamm later fronted him $5,000 for a Mamas & the Papas show, and Lamm's share of the profit financed his first state House race ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/music/harry-tuft-makes-denver-music-history-age-90-40919683/)).

**Bluegrass and old-time explosion (1970s).** This happened mostly in Boulder County. CBMS formed in 1972, and Bill Monroe, then playing Tulagi's in Boulder, pushed it into the 1973 Henderson festival. Pete Wernick settled in Niwot in 1976; Hot Rize and KGNU both started in 1978; Swallow Hill followed in 1979. The same decade produced the heritage institutions that still anchor the calendar: Su Teatro (1972), the Denver March Powwow (1974), Denver Taiko and the Trujillo family's Southwest Musicians (both 1976).

**Institutional era (1980–2019).** The DFC lost its lease (1980) and closed (1983), then reopened on Pearl St (1993). The 1980s and 90s built most of today's organisations: the cowboy gathering (1987), Planina (1988), the Sacred Harp convention (1990), eTown and the Folks Festival (1991), CFOOTMAD (1992), the sale of RockyGrass (1993), Swallow Hill's move to Yale Ave (1999) and COTFA (2002). The 2010s brought a transplant-led bluegrass boom (Trout Steak Revival won Telluride's band contest in 2014) and the start of venue attrition (the Walnut Room, 2016).

**COVID and rebound (2020–26).** Swallow Hill's earned revenue fell 84%. El Chapultepec (1933) closed after house bandleader Freddy Rodriguez Sr. died of COVID-19. The Irish Snug session lost its pub, and Denver-proper contra shrank to zero. By 2023 CFOOTMAD's revenue had passed its 2019 level, and sessions had re-formed at the Abbey (2022) and later Fiction Beer (2026). Tuft's papers went to CU in 2025, and 2026 brought the 50th powwow and Denver Taiko's 50th, while the Soiled Dove and the Mercury Cafe closed and began to come back.

Songs include "The Colorado Trail" (Sandburg, 1927), Conley's "Colorado, Queen of the West" (1959) and the state songs. thesession.org searches for a dozen other Colorado place names found nothing. **For the tune book:** "Up Against the Flatirons" is the one real Front Range tune in session circulation, and the valse de los paños and La Camila link Shaw's 1938 program to the living Trujillo band.

### Local style

There is **no documented, named Colorado fiddle style**. Four strands overlap: (1) **Texas-style contest fiddling**, which is COTFA's stated emphasis, with national-circuit judges (Luke Price, Carl Hopkins in 2026); (2) **Lloyd Shaw's cowboy-dance fiddling** (fiddle and piano for squares, quadrilles and rounds, 1930s–50s); (3) **Hispano fiddling** from the San Luis Valley and northern New Mexico (valses, polkas, cuadrillas), kept alive by the Trujillo family; and (4) **Boulder newgrass/jamgrass** (Tim O'Brien, Leftover Salmon, Yonder Mountain, Elephant Revival, Trout Steak Revival). The Lumpkin tapes (1950–70) are the best chance of documenting an older Anglo ranch-dance repertoire, but the instrumental tracks are only partly catalogued.

### Contest

The **Colorado Fiddle Championships** (COTFA, formed Mar 2002) run on the opening weekend of the National Western Stock Show in the Ames Beef Palace Auction Arena; next 9–10 Jan 2027, billed as the **22nd**. Divisions run from Small Fry to Senior-Senior, plus Jake Jabs Accompanist, Bob Wills, Twin Fiddle and a three-round Championship. A jam follows at the Holiday Inn Denver East ([schedule](https://www.coloradofiddlers.org/2026-schedule/)). KUNC counted 70+ fiddlers in 2025. For a non-Texas player it's worth watching, and the hotel jam is the social payoff.

## 16. Character

**What makes it distinct**
- **A split across a county line.** Denver proper holds the institutions (the DFC, Swallow Hill, CBMS, COTFA, the Stock Show, the powwow, the Sacred Harp convention). Boulder County holds much of the playing (the Avalon's dances, the oldest Irish session, the Lyons jams, Hot Rize, KGNU, eTown, Planet Bluegrass, Ome Banjos).
- **Western, not Appalachian:** a fiddle contest inside a stock show, a 39-year cowboy gathering, square dancing as the legal state folk dance, and Hispano couple dances older than statehood.
- **Centralised:** Swallow Hill does what three or four organisations do elsewhere (listening rooms, school, community jams).
- **Breweries as well as pubs:** bluegrass meets in brewpubs and taprooms (Monolith, Snowpack, Launch Pad, Vine Street), taverns, a church and a cinema; Denver Celtic Night says Colorado sessions are "often" at "breweries and bars".
- **Transplants:** the 2010s bluegrass boom was led by arrivals from Michigan and Nashville ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/music/trout-steak-revival-on-the-denver-bluegrass-boom-9668920/)).
- **Altitude and weather:** 5,280 ft, with jams at about 8,200 ft (Conifer, Nederland). Snow cancels sessions and football moves them.

**Who shows up.** No published demographic data exists. Contest entrants run from "Small Fry" to "Senior-Senior". The dance scene is explicitly inclusive (gender-neutral Hands Across, larks/robins at every CFOOTMAD dance, an LGBTQ square dance club, queer céilís), and klezmer is newly multigenerational.

**Geography.** Three inside-MSA clusters: (1) **Wheat Ridge / W 38th Ave** (Clancy's; the Wheat Ridge and Maple Grove granges); (2) **South Broadway / Platt Park** (Swallow Hill, Cameron Church, BurnDown, Monolith, Trashhawk, the DFC on Pearl); (3) **East Colfax** (Velvet Banjo, Abbey Tavern, Fiction Beer). Bluegrass also reaches Arvada, Castle Rock, Conifer and Bennett. The Avalon in east Boulder is the regional dance hall, and Lyons, Fort Collins and Colorado Springs (45–75 minutes) are treated as part of the circuit. Heritage life concentrates in Aurora (Ethiopian, Korean, Nepali/Bhutanese, mariachi), Five Points, Sakura Square, Globeville, North Denver and Federal Blvd.

## 18. Surprises

1. **The Abbey Tavern session moves for Broncos kickoffs** (3 pm, 6 pm in NFL season) and cancels for the Super Bowl.
2. **The City and County of Denver has zero contra dances**: the metro has 2 a month, both in Wheat Ridge, against 7.3 a month in Boulder, Fort Collins and Colorado Springs.
3. **Bill Monroe talked a society that had voted against festivals into one** (Henderson, 1973, about 6,500 people); it was sold for $10,000 in 1993 and became RockyGrass.
4. **The state fiddle championship is in the Beef Palace**: Texas-style, with a Jake Jabs accompanist division, and warm-ups behind the sheep.
5. **A Mongolian horsehead-fiddle competition came to DU in May 2026**, and Ulaanbaatar's US Embassy sits on "Denver Street".
6. **The 50th Denver March Powwow (about 55,000 people) is run by two staff.**
7. **Swallow Hill grosses $6.4M and ran a $699k deficit in 2025**, while the 1962 guitar shop it came from is still open.
8. **Colorado's official folk dance is square dancing, legally including contra and the Virginia reel**; the metro has 19 square dance clubs to 2 contra series.
9. **Denver's grandfather of folk, Walt Conley, was Black**, sheltered Dylan, and ended his career fronting an Irish pub band.
10. **Bluegrass jams outnumber Irish sessions about 2.3 to 1**, several on a 3-mile stretch of South Broadway.
11. **No shanty sing anywhere on the Front Range**, despite a big Irish pub scene.
12. **There's an old-time jam in a movie theatre** (Alamo Drafthouse, Westminster, 4th Fridays).
13. **The Colorado Springs Sacred Harp singing meets in a coffee shop**, and the Rocky Mountain Convention (since 1990) ends with a hike-and-sing at Gold Hill.
14. **The earliest Denver tune on record is the 1859 "Pike's Peak Gallop"**; the "Pike's Peak" breakdown is from Arkansas and "Denver Belle" from Kentucky.
15. **Colorado's three NEA Heritage Fellows include no musicians**; the one who lived in the metro, colcha embroiderer Josephine Lobato of Westminster, died in Apr 2026.
16. **No Comhaltas branch and no full RSCDS branch**; the nearest RSCDS group is 334 miles away.
17. **The first taiko group outside California (Denver, 1976) is full** in its 50th year.
18. **Future governor Dick Lamm bankrolled a Folklore Center concert**, and the profit funded his first state House race.

## 20. Data-quality notes

**Method.** Headline numbers are `denver.json`'s computed `headline_stats`: inside-MSA, in-person, recurring, 2024–26 evidence, not flagged `unverified_current`, weekly = 4.33. I computed the Boulder, Larimer and El Paso columns from the same records by the same rules. The total is high by about 6 a month: international folk dance counts two seasonal series at full-year rates (9.3; about 5.7 annualised), and a Swallow Hill sing-along that the song thread flagged unverified (2.17) is included. Modern western square dance (19 clubs) and Fort Collins bluegrass are undercounted. Bluegrass relies on the undated CBMS list (medium confidence), and heritage "practices" are mostly rehearsals with thin date evidence.

**Disagreements, and how they were resolved**
- **Colorado Irish Festival:** threads said it ended c.2020 or c.2022. **Last held 2019**: closure announced Oct 2019, the organiser's revenue collapsed in 2020, and nothing was filed after. Founded c.1995 (25 years); 1997 is the organiser's IRS year. **Irishfest Denver** is a separate, active event (14 Mar 2026, verified).
- **KlezKolorado:** start year and month disagreed. Per JCC (Sep 2026): idea 2022, Ranch Camp in 2024 and May 2025, 3rd edition 23–25 Oct 2026 at JAAMM.
- **Cowboy Gathering:** c.1990–92 vs older. **1987**, Arvada Center (its own history; Fence Post).
- **Fiddle Championships:** "since 2002" is COTFA's founding (Mar 2002, verified). The contest is billed 22nd in Jan 2027 (verified), implying a first contest around 2005–06.
- **Sacred Harp convention:** **1990** (the 1995 minutes call it the 6th annual).
- **CFAC:** its own site says both 1968 and 1969. The International Heritage Festival was first held in **1976**.
- **Josephine Lobato:** San Luis Valley vs Westminster; both are right (from San Luis, living in Westminster when honoured). She died Apr 2026.
- **Unresolved:** Denver Indian Center 1975/1983; Greek Festival 1965/66; Cherry Blossom 1972/73; first St. Patrick's parade 1883/1889; Shaw at the National Folk Festival 1938/1939; powwow dancers 1,600+ vs 1,200+; Swallow Hill teachers 51 vs 82.
- **Geography checks:** Club Tico is in Fort Collins (FoTD's new home), not Colorado Springs. Broomfield items are correctly inside the MSA.

**Could not verify:** Clancy's Tuesday session since Aug 2024; Caitríona's céilí dates; Rocky Mountain Fiddle Camp and the Scottish Fiddle Club since 2023; a named host drum; several heritage rehearsal times; the Tesoro powwow, CFAC festival (after 2024) and WaltFest (after 2016).

**Not found:** Scottish/Cape Breton, French-Canadian or regular klezmer sessions; morris, sword or rapper teams; shanty sings; ECD inside the MSA; son jarocho; living alabados; a Colorado fiddle contest before 2002; a named Colorado fiddle style.

**Limits:** search was rate-limited for all four threads, so many items rest on single primary pages; tunearch.org (Wayback used), the DPL and CU archive databases and pptdcontra.com blocked access. No audio or video was played.
