## Who this is for

Nate is a fiddler (old-time, Irish, Scottish/Cape Breton, contra; Pacific Northwest based) who keeps a tune book. He
wants a fun, deep, data-rich picture of what "folk" looks like in each big American metro: what you could actually
go do on a given week, where it came from, who carries it, what is unique, and what is surprising. Depth and
specificity beat generic travel-guide prose.

## Scope: what counts as "folk"

Use a broad, participatory definition, in four layers. Cover all four for every city.

1. **Trad instrumental and dance scene:** Irish sessions, old-time jams, bluegrass jams, Scottish / Cape Breton
   sessions, Scandinavian, French-Canadian, Klezmer, Balkan and other session/jam traditions; contra dance, English
   country dance, squares, céilí, Scottish country dance, morris / sword / rapper teams, international folk dance.
2. **Folk song and listening scene:** folk clubs and societies, coffeehouses and listening rooms, house-concert
   series, song circles, open mics oriented to folk/acoustic, singer-songwriter rooms, folk radio.
3. **Traditional singing:** Sacred Harp / shape-note, sea shanty sings, ballad and unaccompanied singing groups,
   gospel/quartet traditions where they function as community folk practice.
4. **Heritage and diaspora traditions:** living community traditions of the metro's own peoples, e.g. son jarocho,
   mariachi, conjunto/norteño, Cajun/zydeco, bomba/plena, Cuban son/rumba, Haitian twoubadou/rara, Polish/Slovenian
   polka, Greek, Armenian, Arab/Chaldean, Hmong, Vietnamese, Chinese, Filipino rondalla, West African, Ethiopian,
   Native American powwow and drum groups, Hawaiian, Appalachian migrant music, blues where it works as a
   community tradition. Find out which are genuinely alive and practiced (classes, community events, ensembles),
   not just name-checked.

## What to find (go wide AND deep)

For each city:

- **Recurring events census (as of Oct 2026).** Every recurring participatory event you can verify. Weekly =
  4.33/month; biweekly = 2.17; monthly = 1. Require evidence from 2024–2026 (event page, calendar, social post,
  listing) and record the date of the freshest evidence. Things that stopped go in `defunct`.
- **Venues** (listening rooms, pubs that host sessions, dance halls, coffeehouses, house-concert series).
- **Organizations** (folk societies, dance associations, CDSS affiliates, bluegrass associations, Comhaltas
  branches, Sacred Harp groups, heritage cultural centers), with founding years and membership counts if published.
- **Festivals and camps** (name, founded, month, typical attendance, still running?), fiddle contests.
- **Instruction:** folk music schools, community classes, camps (e.g. enrollment, number of classes).
- **Radio and media:** folk/trad radio shows (station, host, hours/week, on air since), podcasts, labels.
- **Shops and makers:** acoustic/folk instrument shops, luthiers of note.
- **Archives:** collections, field recordings made in the metro (Lomax/Library of Congress, WPA), museums.
- **History:** a real timeline from earliest documented folk activity to now: migrations, the 1930s–60s revival,
  key venues, the Great Folk Scare, dance revivals, closures, COVID impact, post-2020 rebound. Who built the
  scene and when.
- **People:** tradition bearers, NEA National Heritage Fellows connected to the metro, notable fiddlers and
  singers past and present, organizers who keep things running.
- **Fiddle angle:** tunes named after or associated with the city/region (check The Traditional Tune Archive,
  The Session, Slippery-Hill), local fiddle styles, notable local fiddlers, fiddle contests.
- **Character:** what makes this scene distinct from others; its signature tradition; who shows up (age, demographics,
  if documented); what a visiting fiddler should do on a Tuesday night vs. a Saturday; cost of a typical dance or
  concert; geography (is it concentrated in one neighborhood or scattered across suburbs?).
- **Surprises:** anything unexpected, counterintuitive, or delightful. Collect these deliberately.
- **Curiosities:** at least 8 questions your research raised that would be worth answering with more research or
  data, especially cross-city comparisons (e.g. "Does X happen in other cities?", "Why did Y die here but not
  there?"). These seed the second phase.

Good sources (not exhaustive): thesession.org (it has a JSON API: e.g.
`https://thesession.org/sessions/nearby?latlon=40.75,-73.99&radius=50&format=json`), CDSS (cdss.org) and
contradancelinks.com, fasola.org singings listings, bluegrass association jam lists, folk society calendars,
Meetup, Facebook event pages (as evidence), local newspapers and alt-weeklies, NEA Heritage Fellows pages, Library of
Congress / American Folklife Center, state folklife programs and state arts councils, university archives,
Wikipedia (as a lead, not a final source), Mudcat Café, Folk Alliance, Fiddler Magazine, radio station schedules.
Follow leads into specifics: event pages, organization histories, newsletters, oral histories.

## Rules

- Cite a URL for every non-obvious fact. Give a confidence (high/medium/low) for counts and contested claims. Say
  where sources disagree. Never invent events, people or numbers; "not found" is a valid, useful answer.
- **Never play audio or video.** Don't use the Edge browser tools at all (they drive Nate's real browser and
  speakers). Use web search and fetch only.
- Do not edit, commit or push anything in the tunebook repo. This is research only.
- Write everything as files (see Outputs). Keep JSON valid (check it with `node -e "JSON.parse(require('fs')
  .readFileSync(process.argv[1],'utf8'))" <file>`).
